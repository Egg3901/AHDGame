## What changed

- Economy bills in countries that start the era with a planned economy can carry an **Economic system** provision with three choices: reform socialism (plan and market), market economy, or central planning.
- Once enacted, the economy moves toward the chosen system over the following turns rather than all at once. Once the economy leaves full central planning, private enterprise becomes legal, the currency is no longer pegged, and insolvent state enterprises can fail.
- When the change is complete, the chosen system becomes the economy's resting point.
- In a one-party state, reform toward the market costs the leader confidence within the ruling party and raises popular legitimacy. A return to the plan does the reverse.
- Computer-run legislators vote on these bills by their economic views: market-leaning members support liberalization and planning-leaning members oppose it.
- Fixed a bug where a planned economy that had reached the market band could be pulled back to central planning on the next turn.
