## What changed

- When a primary wave votes, its results are no longer all in at once. For the next eight hours the wave plays out live: each state closes its polls on its own, in real poll-closing order, and returns climb as the count comes in.
- States are called once enough of the vote is in for their margin: a landslide is called early, a close race waits for most of the count. Until a state is called its result, its delegates and its county map stay hidden.
- On the primary map, a state with polls still open or no returns yet is greyed out, a counting state is hatched in its leader's colour with the share reporting, and a called state turns solid. The calendar marks the wave as "Counting now", and the results ticker runs while the night is on.
- The page refreshes the count every minute during a primary night.
