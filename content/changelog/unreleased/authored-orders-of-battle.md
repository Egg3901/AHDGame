## What changed

- Six countries that had never had a written starting force now have one: the
  United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, Ireland and China. Every
  country that can field an army in any era is now authored.
- The rosters are written per era rather than carried forward from 1953. The
  Bundeswehr grows to twelve divisions by 1979 and is cut to seventeen units by
  2019 before the Zeitenwende rebuilds it. Japan starts as an occupied state's
  seven-unit National Safety Force and becomes the JSDF in 1954. The Soviet army
  collapses after 1991 and recovers through 2023. Ireland stops flying combat
  jets in 1999, because it did.
- The Soviet Union no longer drifts. Its 1999, 2007 and 2023 forces were partly
  random, because a table written for 1953 cannot name a rocket force founded in
  1959 or a space force founded in 1992, and an unnamed branch falls back to
  invention.
- Aircraft carriers, marine divisions, guided-missile destroyers, strategic
  bombers and drone commands now appear in the forces that actually operated
  them. None of them had been used by any written roster before.

## What it means at the table

- The United States starts as the strongest military power in every era, ahead
  of the Soviet Union by about eight percent in 1953 and by more later. It was
  previously seeded at a quarter of Soviet strength.
- Small neutral states are small. Ireland fields four units in 1953 rather than
  fourteen, which is the right number for eight thousand soldiers and three
  corvettes.
- No country's defence budget changes. Upkeep is measured against a nation's own
  starting force, so a larger written roster costs the same share of the defence
  line on day one; only growth beyond it costs more.
