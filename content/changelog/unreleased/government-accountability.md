## What changed

- NPP prime ministers face post-election confidence votes. Coalition support and explicit minority tolerance count toward survival.
- Governing-party records follow the sitting executive. Old appointment votes cannot protect a government that loses confidence.
- Autonomous one-party leaders receive confidence, popular legitimacy, faction splits and real succession.
- Brazil has recurring presidential selection, with era-specific rules and fresh modern runoff ballots.
- Poor government performance affects responsible parties at national and regional level. Continuous party tenure survives a fresh nominee, while successful government keeps its performance treatment.
- Regional executives earn registration advantages through approval. Severe disapproval continues to reduce their re-election support.
- Custom parties with genuine regional presence receive autonomous challengers after maturing in game turns.
