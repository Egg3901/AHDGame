## What changed

- The first time a country outside the player law book refreshes its budget,
  the gap between its spending envelope and the cost of its laws is fixed as a
  share of GDP and kept as ongoing spending. Russia already worked this way.
  Law changes still move spending on top of it.
- The envelope is the smaller of the country's seeded spending and what its
  receipts afford after debt service with the usual small opening deficit, so
  a stale seeded figure cannot open a huge deficit.
- In the live 1991 world this restores German spending from about 12% to 33%
  of GDP and China's from about 7% to 16%, and lifts Brazil's and Nigeria's to
  roughly balanced budgets. Germany's 27% of GDP surplus shrinks to a
  believable level.

## What players will notice

- Those countries' budgets show realistic spending again, which feeds their
  deficits, debt and borrowing.
