## What changed

- GDP growth is now the measured growth of real output: what each region's sectors produce at constant prices. If output keeps growing fast, growth stays high; if production stalls, growth falls. Nothing pulls it back toward a target rate.
- Each region now tracks an output trend learned from its own recent growth. The output gap is how far output sits above or below that trend. Steady growth at any pace has no gap.
- Inflation's demand pressure now comes from the output gap instead of from growth itself, so a fast but steady economy is not treated as overheating.
- Unemployment and migration now compare growth with the region's output trend instead of its potential growth.
- Potential growth is still shown as a supply-side indicator but no longer sets the headline rate.
- On the live world, growth figures move from the old values to the new ones evenly over 24 turns, so nothing jumps in a single turn.
- The GDP card on the Metrics tab now shows growth against the region's trend.
