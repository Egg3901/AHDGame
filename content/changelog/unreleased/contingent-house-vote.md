## What changed

- Repeated ballots: the House ballots again every turn while the vote is open. The moment a candidate holds a majority of state delegations, that candidate becomes president and the acting president becomes vice president. If the window ends with no majority, the acting president keeps serving.
- Votes can change: any sitting House member can back one of the three candidates, and change their vote at any time while the vote is open. The latest vote counts on the next ballot.
- Whips: a party chair can whip their party's House members for a candidate or a free vote, and a coalition chair can whip every member party without its own whip. NPP members follow their whip, otherwise they vote along coalition lines. Players are never forced, and the panel shows which members defied their whip.
- Candidates who leave: a candidate who withdraws, is deleted, or retires drops off the ballot. A lone remaining candidate still needs a majority of delegations.
- Live board: the election and results pages show the acting president, turns left, each candidate's delegations against the majority needed, the state by state grid, whips in force and the ballot history for each turn.
- Notices: House members, the candidates and the chairs are told when the vote opens, members who have not voted get a short reminder when the board moves, and everyone is told how it ended.
