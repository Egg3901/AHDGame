## What changed

- When a company's supply priority is set to its own plants, its spare output gets first claim on freight capacity to reach its own plants in other states of the same country, ahead of open-market buyers.
- The company pays normal freight for those shipments itself, billed to its own plants in the destination state rather than shared among other buyers there, and its plants receive only what the network actually hauled.
- Plants in the same state as the supplier are still served first. Shipments only draw on what the supplying state has spare after its own local demand, stay within one country, and take effect from the turn after the policy is set.
