## What changed

- 1991 lower-house races award seats in candidate vote order. A named or player candidate wins at most one seat, so no candidate can take several.
- NPP entries stand as bounded slates of individual candidates, enough to fill the 512 seats beyond the 44 named seed entries without creating hundreds of persistent people. A player can displace a slate candidate.
- Polling, snapshots, election-night projections and resolution use the same allocation. Regular and snap elections store the method when they open, and 1991 races that were already open get it filled in once.
- Modern Japan and the House of Councillors keep their existing methods.

The political reform decision and the 500-seat mixed system that follows it are not part of this change.
