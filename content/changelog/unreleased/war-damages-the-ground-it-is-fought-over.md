## What changed

- A war being fought across a country damages its road network, in proportion to
  how far the front has moved from where it started. A border skirmish barely
  registers. A front that sweeps the whole country wrecks it.
- The damage is felt in the economy, not just on a page. Road condition feeds
  transport efficiency, which is one of the inputs to national productivity, which
  sets how fast the economy can grow.
- Damage stops the turn the fighting does, and roads recover with continued
  spending. Destruction is faster than neglect; recovery is not instant.

## Who this hits

Only the country the war is being fought **on**. An army fighting abroad pays in
money and in men, which the defence budget and the public's patience already
charge it for. It does not come home to broken roads.

Where a war covers more than one country, as a war over both Germanies does, every
country whose soil is in the theatre takes the damage. The map anchor is not the
only one being fought over.

## What it looks like

A well-funded region holding at 61 road condition, across a war of the length of
the war for Germany:

| how far the front moved | road condition after                         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| a quarter of the way    | 55                                           |
| halfway                 | 48                                           |
| the whole country       | 36, reached about a third of the way through |

A country at peace is completely untouched by this.
