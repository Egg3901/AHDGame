## What changed

- East Germany's monetary decisions were also being made from an inactive shared bank record. That record read its own small reserve balance instead of the State Bank's, judged East Germany's banks short of reserves, and asked for an emergency liquidity injection every turn. Five turns in six it was refused because East Germany's bank was still on its six-turn cooldown.
- A refused operation ended the whole policy review, so every central bank reviewed after it skipped its bond buying, bond selling and liquidity decisions on those turns. A refusal now skips only the bank it applies to.
- Each country's committee now decides from, and acts through, its own central bank, so East Germany's liquidity decisions use the State Bank's own reserves.
