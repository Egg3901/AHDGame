## What changed

- A supply agreement premium (or discount) is now charged on one turn of the volume the buyer actually uses, not a full day's worth every turn. Premiums that used to eat most of a buyer's operating profit are about a twenty-fourth of their old size.
- Premiums and breach damages are priced at the same damped market price that sales and input bills already use, so a price spike no longer inflates them beyond the real cost of the goods.
- The cap on breach damages shrinks with the same correction.
