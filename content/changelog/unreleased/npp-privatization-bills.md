## What changed

- Once a planned economy reaches the dual-track stage, its computer-run government periodically sponsors a privatization bill that floats part of one to three state holdings as new listed companies.
- Fully market economies that came out of the plan privatize larger batches, and more often.
- Loss-making and low-margin holdings go first. Defence production is never put up for sale, and strategic sectors are sold last with the state keeping a golden share.
- These bills go through the normal vote and still need a two-thirds majority, and no single new company may end up controlling more than 30% of its market.
- Countries still under a full command economy, and market economies that were never planned, do not sponsor these bills.
