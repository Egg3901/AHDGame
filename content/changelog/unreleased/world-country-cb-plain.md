## What changed

- The world page lists nations in one table: nation, status, government, leader and leading party. Status is a word instead of a coloured badge, and on a phone the government, leader and party sit under each nation's name.
- Links to world events, conflicts, trade and the hall of fame move to a side column, and the grid pattern behind the map is gone. The map's filters, legend and view switch are easier to read.
- A country's overview keeps its photo, with the country's name below it and its government and status on one line. Leaders and approval sit under the name, and approval only turns red below 40%.
- The country directory is four plain columns of links with live figures beside them, and national ideology reads in words, such as "Drifting left".
- The central bank has a simple header with the bank's name, its chair, the prime rate and the key figures. The overview puts the chair, rate controls and nominations in a side column next to the rate corridor, trends, credit ratings and lobbying.
- The rate corridor now says where the bank stands in a full sentence, for example "The prime rate is 1.15 points above inflation, a restrictive stance."
- Every central bank tab uses readable labels and plain sections. The savings and loan accounts, borrowing and repaying keep a single panel each.
