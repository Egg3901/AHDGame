## What changed

- Coalitions in contingent elections: House members and senators treat candidates from their coalition partners as allies, second only to their own party, so a coalition votes as a bloc.
- House deadlock: if no candidate reaches a majority of state delegations, nobody is elected president. The vice president chosen by the Senate serves as acting president, and the House keeps voting for about a day.
- The contingent election warning, the election night board and the results page explain who is acting president and how long the House vote stays open.
