Cut from development.

## Fixed

- The delivery-limited figure behind a sector's freight warning was worked out
  by dividing all the world's unmet demand by all the world's unsold output and
  applying that single ratio to every state. The result carried no local
  information at all: measured on production at turn 361 it came out at 98.9% in
  New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Texas and California alike. A state with idle
  trucks and a plain oversupply was blamed at exactly the same rate as a state
  whose network was genuinely full.
- It is now accumulated where the shortfall happens, against the origin state
  whose freight capacity turned the haul away. A state with spare capacity and
  no buyers reports zero, which is correct: freight was never its problem.
- Demand that went unfilled because the buyer would not pay the landed price no
  longer counts as a delivery failure. That is a price outcome, and folding it
  in meant the warning told sellers to buy trucks that would not have sold the
  unit anyway.

## Changed

- The freight pill on a sector row now reads `BULK FREIGHT 32% STUCK` instead of
  a bare `BULK FREIGHT`. In warning colours next to a healthy fill chip, the
  class name on its own read as a category label, or as a light saying the
  sector needed freight. The share and the verb are now on the face of the pill,
  where a touch device can see them without a tooltip.

## Guarded

- Tests cover the three cases the old rule confused: a state whose own network
  refused the haul is attributed, a state that lost its buyers to price is not,
  and two states holding identical unsold output get different answers when only
  one of them has a freight problem.
