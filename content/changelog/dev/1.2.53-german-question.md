Cut from development.

## Added

Branch `feature/german-unification-question`, flag `settlementCrisisEnabled`
(default off). A new crisis KIND — a standing, game-wide contest over one
nation's constitutional settlement, fought across four weighted institutions
rather than on a single meter. Design in `ahd-german-question-design`; the
field-by-field mockup audit in `ahd-german-question-live-data-contract`;
measured balance in `ahd-german-question-balance-findings`.

Two collections, `settlementCrises` and `settlementPlays`, both classified
`runtime` in the seed manifest. Positions are integer HUNDREDTHS throughout, for
the same reason `alignment/normalize.ts` is: `crisis.position` is a weighted mean
of the four institutions and on a float grid it drifts away from the cards it is
derived from. `recomputePosition` is the only writer, which is what makes the
masthead figure and the four cards incapable of disagreeing.

- **Turn phase** `settlement`, registered after `alignment` because seat
  direction is read from live bloc membership that the alignment and
  international-organisation phases write. Claims the whole tick on
  `lastTickedTurn` and each play on `resolvedTurn` through
  `claimStatusTransition` — this project has had overlapping turn runs from
  rolling deploys, and without the tick-level claim the losing runner computes a
  drift-only result from the same snapshot and overwrites the winner's write,
  discarding every play that landed that turn.
- **Bonn is an NPC.** West Germany is unplayable, so its politics are mean
  reversion toward an authored anchor plus seeded noise, one rng per tick
  advanced in stored institution order so a replay reproduces the rolls.
- **Four seats** — DD primary at 2.0x, US/UK/RU secondary at 1.0x — resolved from
  office (`electedOfficials` plus `governmentFormations.pmCharacterId`), not from
  a stored roster. Each seat is exercisable by its country's primary executive
  AND its foreign minister, who share one budget: the seat is rate-limited, not
  the person.
- **Direction is not configured.** Official plays take their sign from
  `loadBlocMembership` at commit time, so a country that changes bloc changes
  which way its seat pushes, and a country in neither bloc cannot play at all.
  Personal plays choose freely — domestic dissent is the point of the street.
- **Commit** is a guarded increment claim on the seat's budget, then the treasury
  debit, never the reverse: charging first would let a losing race still spend.
  `requireHumanSessionWithCharacter` plus a 20/minute limit, because
  `requireAuthWithCharacter` carries no same-origin assertion and this route
  spends a national treasury.
- **Ladder** of five rungs. Coercive plays cap at rung 4 and decay a rung on any
  turn none lands; rung 5 is a deliberate press by an authority seat and only
  ARMS the war, which a second press then declares. Holding the top rung levies
  2% of each seat country's treasury and a point of approval per turn, so the
  brink is a position you pay to hold rather than a latch.
- **War** freezes the crisis and opens a real NATO–Warsaw Pact conflict with both
  Germanies as hosts. Whoever wins it takes the settlement outright, wherever the
  meter stood. Freeze-claim first, conflict second: a failure inside
  `createConflict` leaves a recoverable frozen crisis rather than an orphan war.
- **Actuation** on a reunification win runs `mergeCountry(DD into DE)`, then gives
  DE the winner's `governmentType` and Warsaw Pact membership. A Western win is
  the plain status quo plus a 96-turn re-open cooldown.

### Country retirement and merge

New generic capability, in `src/lib/country/mergeCountry.ts`. `registeredBase`
and `getRegisteredCountryIds` were add-only: a country compiled into
`COUNTRY_ORDER` could be hidden from players but never taken out of the engine.
Both now filter on a new `CountryGameState.dissolvedTurn`.

`mergeCountry` is NOT a loop over `transferRegion` with the old arguments. That
contract assumes the source survives, with evacuated NPPs retreating into another
of the source's regions and the source keeping its national layer. A merge
inverts both, which is why `relocateToRegionId` grew a null case. Regions first,
retirement last: a half-run merge leaves a country with fewer regions but still
simulated, which re-running fixes, whereas the reverse order strands regions in a
country nothing enumerates. A merge that fails part-way stops WITHOUT retiring
and reports the failure.

**DE is the surviving shell, not DD.** The owner's ruling was "winner's shell
survives", which would mean renaming DD to Germany and deactivating DE. A
country's name is seed data read at roughly ninety synchronous sites, so a
runtime rename is not a field to set. `governmentType` IS runtime-overridable via
`countryState` and bloc membership is a document, so merging into DE produces an
identical player-visible result — one country called Germany, one-party, in the
Warsaw Pact — and differs only in which document id survives.

### Opening path, rule switches, AP banking

- **Nothing opened the crisis, and by design nothing automatic ever will.** The
  turn phase only ever read a document nothing created; it now stays that way
  deliberately — the phase advances a live crisis and never creates one.
  `openSettlementCrisis` is called from `/api/admin/settlement` alone, so an
  operator decides when the question is asked and may ask it at any point in a
  world's life. No era window, no re-open cooldown, no reset-time seed. Three
  refusals only: a crisis already live, a resolved one not yet enacted (a merge
  still pending, whose challenger the actuation sweep is about to dissolve), and
  the two Germanies no longer being separate states — checked against the country
  registry, so a reunification by any route closes the door on its own. A UNIQUE
  PARTIAL INDEX on `settlementCrises { kind }` where `status: "open"` stops two
  operators opening two; the loser catches a duplicate key and no-ops. Opening
  does not tick, so the board is visible at its authored figures for one turn
  before the first drift lands.
- **The source design's three "Rules" switches** (`openLog`, `driftRevealed`,
  `escalationEnabled`) land as a per-crisis `rules` block, not as gameState
  flags: two of the three default ON and the feature-gates panel reads every
  boolean fail-closed, which would invert both. Read through `settlementRulesFor`,
  which falls back per key so a partial admin write cannot reset the others.
  With the ladder off, heat is driven to ZERO rather than frozen — freezing would
  leave a crisis stuck at DEFCON 2 that nobody could arm or stand down.
- **Seat AP is now a bank**, shaped exactly like capital: accrue per tick, clamp
  at `SEAT_ACTION_BANK_TURNS = 3` turns' worth. Four authored plays cost more AP
  than their seat earns in a turn and nothing carried AP forward, so they were
  unpressable buttons. One of them, `pressure`, is Moscow's only lever on the
  Allied Garrison — and since the index is a weighted mean, a garrison pinned at
  its anchor of 16 capped the East at 74.8 against a carry threshold of 85.
  Reunification was unwinnable by construction. Two config invariants now hold
  the line: every seat play fits inside its seat's bank, and every institution is
  reachable by both blocs.
- **`DRIFT_K_PCT` 6 to 3.** Measured, not guessed: at 0.06 mean reversion scales
  with the distance already travelled and swallows any sustained push. At 0.03
  an uncontested West locks in 28 turns, an uncontested East carries in 39, and
  contested play stalls — which is the pressure that sends a bloc to the ladder.
- **Admin surface** at `GET/POST /api/admin/settlement`, under World > German
  Question: open, close, force-resolve, set one institution's position, flip one
  rule. Open and close both take no options — there is no gate left to force
  past, so a second weaker set of preconditions would only be a way to get them
  wrong.
- **Close** ends a live crisis as though it was never asked: no winner, no
  absorption, no country-history entry, nothing for the actuation sweep to
  enact, and it can be reopened on the same turn. A new terminal `cancelled`
  status rather than a delete, because deleting would strand every
  `settlementPlay` row pointing at a document that no longer exists, and those
  rows are the record of what players really spent. Cancelled is inert: every
  sweep and read filters POSITIVELY on open/frozen/resolved, and the unique
  partial index is on `status: "open"`, so nothing picks it up and nothing is
  blocked by it. A turn-phase test holds that inertness, since it is a property
  of the whole query set rather than of any one line.
  Two things it does NOT do, both surfaced rather than hidden: it does not
  refund (resolved plays spent real treasury at the rates of the day, and
  unwinding that is a separate operation), and closing a FROZEN crisis does not
  cancel the war it declared — that conflict has its own combatants and history,
  so the response names it rather than letting someone find it later. A forced resolve writes the outcome and STOPS —
  `cooldownUntilTurn` stays null, which is the marker the actuation sweep looks
  for, so a forced reunification runs through the same merge and the same guards
  as one the players earned. Setting a position writes the institution and
  re-derives the index; `position` is never written directly.

### World News dispatches

Five kinds, following the `eraCrossing` pattern — a system news post in the
in-game feed plus an embed on `discordNewsWebhookUrl`.

- **Every six turns, a sentiment briefing** (`SETTLEMENT_WIRE_INTERVAL_TURNS`):
  where the settlement stands, which way it swung since the last dispatch and by
  how much, each institution with its weight and Bonn's own drift, and the
  ladder once it is hot. The public appears **only in aggregate** — how many
  people took a position over the period, never what any one of them did. Every
  character in the world holds a personal play, so a post per action would bury
  the channel and everything else in it. The count is a `distinct` over the
  whole period, served by the `{ crisisId, turn }` index.
- **Four one-off moments**, because each changes what the question IS rather
  than where it stands: opened, DEFCON 1, war declared, settled.

Emission is centralised in the turn phase and nowhere else. Emitting from
`armLadder` or `declareWar` would put a network call on a player's request path
and would fire again on a retry; from the tick, a post reflects a board that has
actually ticked.

**The stamp, not the state, makes a one-off post one-off.** A crisis sits armed
for as long as the heat holds and frozen for as long as the war runs, so the
tick sees those states on every turn. `postedWireEvents` is claimed with a
guarded `$ne` write BEFORE the post goes out: two overlapping turn runs cannot
both post, and a crash between claim and send loses a dispatch rather than
repeating one — the right way round for something that cannot be unsent. The
briefing's cadence write is guarded the same way, on `lastBriefing.turn`.

The settled dispatch fires only when actuation reports success. Announcing a
reunification whose merge then failed is the one lie this wire could tell.

Failure is non-fatal twice over — `sendNewsEvent` swallows its own errors and
`postSettlementWire` catches around the news post as well. A Discord outage must
never fail a turn, and a test holds that.

Copy follows the project rules: no calendar years, so the same text reads
correctly in any era, and no anchor-unit figures, which are not a player-facing
currency. Both are asserted rather than trusted.

The admin panel shows the turn the next briefing is due and which moments have
been filed, so an operator can tell the wire is running without reading the
channel.

## Changed

### It runs over in-game years now

The board is a first-order system: plays add a push `P` per turn, drift pulls
back by `k x (distance from anchor)`, so it settles at `anchor + P/k` and
approaches that with a time constant of `1/k`. Where it lands and how long it
takes are set by DIFFERENT things, which is why shrinking the plays alone put
both thresholds out of reach and slackening the brake alone barely changed the
pace. Scaling both together dilates time and nothing else.

`SETTLEMENT_TEMPO` is that divisor — over every authored magnitude, the noise
band, the personal cap, the wire's swing vocabulary and `DRIFT_K_BPS` (basis
points now; a whole percent has no resolution left once divided). Verified
balance-neutral rather than assumed: the index-per-AP ranking is identical at
tempo 1, 4 and 8. The numerator of the brake is the BALANCE dial; the tempo is
the SPEED dial.

Measured: an unopposed West locks independence on turn 156, an unopposed East
carries reunification on 294, and a contested question is still unresolved and
flat-topped at 57.0 after 480 turns. At a turn to the hour that is six real days
against twenty. Sentiment briefings moved from every 6 turns to every 12, which
would otherwise have filed up to eighty dispatches into one channel.

### The ladder is an instrument of last resort, and two-sided

Three findings from `scripts/debug/gq-war-path-sim.ts`, which exists because the
balance harness never armed and so left the whole escalation half unmeasured.

- **`LADDER_UNLOCK_TURNS`** — two in-game years before the brink is available at
  all. Nothing gated a declaration on the index or on the crisis's age, and the
  opening capital banks buy a coercive play on each of the first three turns, so
  Moscow stood at the coercive cap on turn 3 and could declare on turn 4 of a
  question tuned to run 156 to 294. The design's own logic is that the stall is
  what sends a bloc to the ladder; this makes it literal. Heat still accumulates
  from turn one — only the press is held back, and the panel counts it down.
- **Decay is slower than the climb.** A rung was lost on every quiet turn, which
  set a hidden threshold nobody intended: a bloc had to land coercion on more
  than half of all turns to stand still, and none can. So the ladder collapsed to
  zero and rung 5 could not be HELD — the opposite of "a position you pay to
  maintain rather than a state you reach". One counter now paces every rung:
  three quiet turns cost one. A standoff can last, and the mobilisation levy
  finally runs long enough to bite — 24 turns of DEFCON 1 costs 38% of three
  treasuries.
- **Both authority seats can feed the ladder they can arm.** Washington held the
  authority to escalate and could never satisfy the precondition: `station` was
  its only coercive play at 2 AP and 14 capital against an income of 1 and 3, and
  London has none at all, so Western heat oscillated between 0 and 1 forever.
  Moscow had the same problem one step later, once East Berlin's treasury ran dry
  around turn 60. Both are repriced to 1 AP, and both magnitudes are HALVED
  alongside, so the change buys escalation and not settlement.

## Fixed

- **The personal cap deleted the public tier when the tier got crowded.**
  `resolvePlayBatch` scaled each personal row independently and rounded it, so
  once every row's share fell below half a hundredth every row rounded to zero
  and a thousand characters moved the board by nothing at all. It needed about
  1,200 characters to bite at the old cap and about 200 at the new one, and it
  silently defeated the design's headline that every character in the world holds
  a play. Apportioned by largest remainder now, so the stamps sum to exactly the
  cap however many rows share it.
- **`Call a Joint Referendum` was worth three times what it looked like.** A
  settlement-level magnitude is not in the same unit as an institution one:
  adding d to all four moves the weighted mean by exactly d, so the authored 5.0
  outweighed `border`'s 8.0 on a weight-2 institution by more than three to one,
  and it decided every simulated crisis on its own. Rescaled to 2.5.
- **London could not man the Allied Garrison.** `rhine` cost more action points
  than the seat earns, so the West's only lever on the weight-3 institution the
  East can barely touch was unaffordable. Now 1 AP.
- Play magnitudes display to two decimals; positions stay at one, so the two
  halves of a split still read 100.0.
