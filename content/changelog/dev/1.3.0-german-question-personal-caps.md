Cut from development.

## Fixed

- The open floor panel announced that the tier "was scaled to +0.0" on turns
  where nothing had been scaled at all. It was adding up a figure that does not
  exist until the turn resolves, so the live turn always totalled zero. It now
  reports what the floor will actually get, and says so in the future tense,
  because the scaling happens when the turn resolves.

## Changed

- Each character may use each personal play once per turn. The button closes
  once used and says when it comes back. Repeat clicks previously ate into the
  shared pool, so one determined player could quietly drown out everyone else on
  the board.
- The floor's ceiling now grows with turnout instead of being a fixed number. It
  sat at a level roughly six aligned characters could fill, past which nobody
  else's contribution moved anything. A bigger crowd is now genuinely louder,
  with diminishing returns, so the street can never overrun the four delegations
  however many people take part.
- The panel names the ceiling this turn's turnout has earned, rather than
  quoting a standing rule, and says that a larger turnout raises it.

## Balance notes

Measured across the full run of the crisis, against the same scenarios before
the change:

- An unopposed West still locks independence on the same turn, and an unopposed
  East still carries reunification on the same turn. Neither involves the
  public, so neither should move, and neither does.
- At maximum turnout the crisis lands within half a point of where it did
  before. The street's ceiling rises tenfold there, but the street and the
  Bundestag are the only two institutions the public can reach and both
  saturate, so past a point a larger ceiling buys the crowd nothing.
- What does change is each person's share. With around eight hundred people
  pushing the street, one character now takes roughly ten times the slice of the
  ceiling they used to.
