Cut from development. Raised as player suggestion S#308.

## Fixed

- A country carrying national debt could not make any German Question play that
  cost money. The treasury reading is a signed cash position, so a national debt
  shows as a negative balance, and the check asked whether that balance covered
  the cost. Once a country was in debt it never did. Delegations now spend into
  debt here, as they already do for organisation dues, aid and every other
  national payment, and the borrowing shows up as debt and interest the usual
  way.
- The same check was refusing national extraction surveys for the same reason,
  and is gone there too.

## Changed

- Every delegation play that costs money can now be bought with political
  capital instead. Each one shows two buttons, priced separately, and each is
  live or dead on its own budget. The four plays that never cost money are
  unchanged.
- The treasury button now says when a play will be paid for by borrowing, and
  how much it will add to the national debt. Since a delegation can always
  borrow, that note is the only thing separating a spend from a loan.
- Delegations bank political capital faster: the primary seat gains eight points
  a turn instead of six, the others four instead of three. Measured across a
  full crisis, the plays that cost capital rather than money were going
  completely unused, because a delegation always had something better to do with
  the turn than save for them.

## Balance notes

Measured over the full run of the crisis, comparing against the same scenarios
before this change:

- An unopposed West still locks independence, and an unopposed East still
  carries reunification. Both arrive sooner, because delegations can now afford
  the capital plays they were never reaching.
- Playing at full tilt used to drain East Berlin's treasury in about ten turns
  and London's in twenty-six, against a question that takes well over a hundred
  turns to settle. Most of the crisis was being played by delegations that could
  no longer act.
- A delegation that refuses to borrow at all can fight the whole question on
  political capital, but not win it from the Western side: the West gets within
  a fraction of a point of locking independence and stalls there. Holding Bonn
  without borrowing is not currently possible.
