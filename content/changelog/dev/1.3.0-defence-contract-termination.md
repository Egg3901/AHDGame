## Terminating a contract has terms

Awarding a defence contract to a company you own was already disclosed and
priced. Cancelling somebody else's was free, instant and silent, and it handed
the quarter's contracting room straight back so it could be re-awarded elsewhere
in the same window. Ministers used it on rivals. That gap is closed.

A cancellation is now one of three things, and the game tells them apart.

**Withdrawing an offer** the supplier has not accepted yet costs nothing. No
fee, no disclosure, and the lots go back into the tranche. Nothing was promised.

**Terminating for cause** costs nothing either. A plant that has missed three
delivery turns in a row for a reason of its own making can be dropped for free
and the tranche is returned. A buyer who has run out of appropriation does not
qualify: underfunding your own defence budget is not the supplier's fault.

**Terminating for convenience** is everything else, and it has a price:

- The supplier is paid its margin on every lot it was ordered to build and will
  now never build. If the appropriation cannot cover that, the termination is
  refused rather than leaving the company holding the bill.
- The cancelled lots stay charged to the contracting window. Cancelling does not
  create room to award a replacement this quarter, to anyone.
- The cancellation is posted publicly and costs the minister favorability,
  scaled by how much of the quarter's procurement the order was. If the minister
  holds a stake in a different domestic defence supplier, the wire says so and
  the penalty doubles.

Breaking a contract is still cheaper than letting one you do not want run to
term, so a country can always get out of an order it no longer needs.

## Both sides can see it

The minister's Arsenal board quotes the break fee on the cancel button before
the click and states the terms underneath it. Suppliers are notified when an
order is withdrawn or terminated, with the reason and the fee, and a terminated
contract now says on the corporate order book which of the three it was and who
signed it.
