## What changed

Procurement has no overdraft: a country that cannot pay this turn takes fewer
lots and the contract waits. That part was right. What was wrong was where the
unpaid output went.

Delivery banked only the sub-lot remainder of what a plant produced. Whole lots
were expected to ship the turn they were finished, so anything the appropriation
could not cover was simply dropped. For a large plant that costs a turn of
output. For a small one it is fatal: a plant producing about a tenth of a lot
per turn spends ten turns accruing a single lot, and if the ministry is short of
cash on the turn that lot completes, the whole thing is binned and the plant
starts again from nothing. The contract never advances past zero, no matter how
long production runs. Reported in ticket-1099, where a naval supplier had been
producing for more than forty turns with an untouched progress bar.

The delivery carry now banks everything the plant built and did not ship,
finished lots included, capped at what the order still needs. Materiel already
built waits in the yard for funding instead of evaporating, and the contract
fills as soon as the appropriation can cover it. The cap means a stalled order
cannot stockpile past what it was billed for.

No change to what a country pays or to the no-overdraft rule. Contracts that
were already delivering behave exactly as before.
