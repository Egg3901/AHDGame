## Changed

- Support and attack actions move a character's reputation by one point each.
  That is unchanged, but there is now a limit of three points of net movement
  against any one person per turn. Individual costs never limited what a group
  could do together, so a coordinated push could move a candidate across most of
  the reputation scale between two turns. Reputation multiplies a candidate's
  entire vote, so a swing that large decided races on its own. Groups still move
  a target faster than any one person can, and can still shift someone a long
  way over a campaign; it now takes a campaign rather than a turn.

- Campaign media spending and travel presence still raise reputation every turn,
  and now give less the closer you are to the ceiling, with a small guaranteed
  amount so the spending is never wasted. Previously the per turn gain from a
  fully upgraded media tree was larger than the natural decline at the top of the
  scale, so a funded campaign held the maximum permanently. A fully invested
  campaign now settles a little below the ceiling and has to keep working for it.

- Presidential primaries now weigh a candidate's public standing at 25 points
  instead of 10, with party influence at 20 and national reach at 15. Public
  standing is the single biggest factor in the general election, while party
  influence does not enter the general election vote at all, so the nomination
  now rewards roughly what winning in November rewards. Policy alignment with
  the party stays the largest factor at 40, so parties still pick candidates who
  agree with them.
