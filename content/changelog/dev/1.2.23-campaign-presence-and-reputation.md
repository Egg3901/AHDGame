## Changed

- **State Organization is now Campaign Presence.** Same map, clearer name: it is
  the ground game you build for a presidential run.

- **Campaign Presence is paid for by the campaign, not by you.** Each level costs
  3 campaign actions and cash from the campaign treasury, so it competes with
  media, ground game and opposition research for one budget. You need an active
  campaign to build it.

- **The level cap is gone.** You can keep investing in a state as long as you can
  afford it. The price rises about a third with each level, and the bonus curve
  flattens as it climbs, so level 10 delivers roughly three quarters of the
  maximum and the levels above it buy steadily less. A large treasury can
  dominate a handful of chosen states rather than the whole map.

- **The price is real now.** The old flat $50,000 was a rounding error against a
  presidential treasury. The first level starts near $250,000 and escalates from
  there.

- **The map shows the whole race.** Pick any candidate to see where they have
  built. Previously it only ever showed your own presence, which was confusing if
  you had never run for President.

- **Campaigns can appoint up to three managers.** Appointing someone who is
  already a manager removes them, so one control handles both.

- **Reputation is slightly less decisive in the vote.** It remains the strongest
  single factor; the gap between a very popular and an unpopular candidate just
  no longer swamps everything else.

- **Support and attack actions cost 6 action points, up from 2.** Moving someone's
  reputation is now a real use of your turn rather than loose change. There is
  also an upper bound on how far one person's reputation can be moved in a single
  turn, set high enough that ordinary campaigning never touches it.

- **Presidential primaries weigh electability more.** Public standing counts for
  25 points instead of 10, party influence 20 and national reach 15. Policy
  alignment with the party remains the largest factor at 40.
