Cut from development.

## Fixed

- The Separate Peace section of a conflict record named the sender of the offer
  as the country that left the war. A peace offer runs in both directions: a
  government can propose to leave itself, or ask an enemy to withdraw and stay in
  the war itself. For the second kind the record printed the two countries the
  wrong way round, crediting the exit to a country whose army was still at the
  front. It now reads the leaver off the deal itself, so both kinds of settlement
  are written up correctly.
- The War for Germany is the case this was found on. Its record said East Germany
  left the war on turn 532 settling with the United Kingdom, when it was the
  United Kingdom that withdrew on East Germany's terms. Sixteen of the seventeen
  offers filed in that war are of the kind that was reported backwards, so the
  same line would have misdescribed nearly every settlement still to come out of
  it. No stored data was wrong and nothing needed correcting in the world: the
  fault was only in how the record was written, and every affected line now reads
  correctly on its own.
