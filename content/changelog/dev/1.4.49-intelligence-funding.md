Cut from development.

## Added

- Every country with a law book now carries an intelligence appropriation: the Secret Vote in the United Kingdom, an Intelligence Authorization Act in the United States, and an allocation to the security organs in the Soviet Union and the German Democratic Republic. It has five levels and every country starts at Unfunded, which is a real position rather than an absence: the service exists, keeps its files, and mounts nothing. Raising it is an ordinary bill.
- The intelligence office gains an Appropriation panel showing the enacted line, what the service is holding, what it accrues each turn, and how much of that is already committed to networks. When upkeep outruns the line it says so, and names what stops first.
- The office also gains the three controls it was missing. A director can fund a network in a target country, order a collection or covert action, and set the counter intelligence posture. Each one says plainly why it is unavailable when it is: no money, no slots left this turn, or no network to work through.

## Fixed

- Nothing could be funded at all. Every service in the world was holding a zero balance and every operation checked that balance before running, so the whole system was unreachable from the moment it shipped.
- Operation prices were the same flat number for every country, but national accounts are kept in each country's own currency. At an identical share of national income that price bought the United Kingdom three operations a turn and the Soviet Union two hundred. Prices are now a share of the ordering country's own economy, so a funding level means the same thing everywhere.
- Networks were being built for free. Their upkeep was written down and then never charged to anyone, so funding a network cost nothing and there was no reason not to run every network at full tilt. Upkeep is now taken out of the appropriation each turn, and a network the service cannot pay for goes quiet until it can. It keeps what it has built and owes nothing.

## Changed

- The director can no longer move money out of the defence budget. That was the only funding route that existed, it needed no vote and had no ceiling, and it has been removed. Intelligence money now comes from the legislature or it does not come at all.
- A service that runs out of money stops working rather than going into debt. Unlike the armed forces, which must be sustained whether or not they are paid for, an unfunded service simply does less.
