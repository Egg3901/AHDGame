## What changed

- Bicameral democracies now weight both elected chambers equally when measuring concentrated party control.
- A party cannot hide overwhelming upper-chamber control behind a narrower lower-chamber majority.
- Continued control adds pressure over time, including consecutive presidential terms when the same party also leads the legislature.
- Divided government interrupts presidential continuity pressure instead of treating rival-party control as one entrenched bloc.
- Unicameral and appointed-upper-chamber systems keep their existing behavior.
- Political direction remains separate: concentrated control weakens democratic health without moving the country left or right.
