## NPP corp cash rails re-based for 1953 (PR #73)

`CASH_FLOOR`, `SAFE_CASH_FLOOR_MIN` and `EXPANSION_MIN_CASH` in
`nppCorporationBehavior` gate every discretionary decision an NPP-run
corporation makes: dividends, sector expansion, and the growth leg of capacity
reinvestment. They were authored against a modern-era money scale and never
re-based for the 1953 worlds in production.

Measured on prod at turn 79, over a 200-corp sample of the 476 NPP-run corps:
median liquid capital ₳1,724,110 against a ₳2,000,000 floor, with 105 of 200
below it. A corp under the floor cannot spend, so it cannot earn its way back
over it — the lockout is permanent. The visible symptom is a corporation whose
sectors are all healthy (20-35% margins, selling out every turn) sitting on idle
cash while its share price falls. Meyer Logistics (corp 446) is the worked case:
₳1,199,942 of cash, six sectors at 21.9-37.6% margin, all clearing, and a share
price down from 17.35 to 4.38 across turns 59-79.

The floor drops to ₳250,000, the safety rail to ₳125,000 and the expansion
surplus requirement to ₳625,000. The whole family moves by the same factor, so
the design ratios are unchanged and only the scale differs. On the same prod
sample the frozen count falls from 105 of 200 to 36 — the remainder are corps
that are genuinely out of money, which is what the rail is for.

The safety rail had to move with the floor. It is a `MAX()`, so leaving it at
₳1,000,000 would have clamped the new floor back up and made the change inert.
A guard test pins that case specifically.

This is a calibration, not a cure. The cohort's cash spans four orders of
magnitude (p25 ₳398,719, p75 ₳52,833,977), so no single absolute number fits
both tails. The durable fix is to derive these rails from the corp's own revenue
and the world's era unit scale, as `computeBuildCost` already does.
