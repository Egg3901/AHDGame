Patch cut from development, 2026-08-13. All entries below shipped today.

## Interstate haul is now real freight demand (ticket #1039, PR #63)

The Logistics map read haul TEU from `sourcingNetworkLoad`, the record-only
landed-price sourcing ledger, while logistics sold % cleared against freight
demand that never included that haul. A state could show thousands of TEU of
haul while its logistics sectors sat in a glut. The sourcing pass's consumed
TEU per origin state is now booked as freight demand before clearing, at the
state leg (state prices and the map's stored `stateDemand`), the national
aggregate (trade clearing), and the global balance (price and sold %). Freight
is never itself shipped, so there is no same-turn feedback; higher freight
prices damp next turn's haul through shipping cost. Money wiring (who pays
shipping, plan step 5) is still off. Map copy updated to match.

## Money wiring for interstate sourcing (plan step 5, flag off, PR #69)

The landed-price sourcing pass stops being purely observational. Behind a new
gameConfig flag `interstateMoneyWiringEnabled` (default off, everything byte
identical while off):

- The sourcing pass now records, per destination state and commodity, the
  delivered units and the extra cost (shipping plus tariff) they carried, and
  per importing country the total tariff paid and import value. Both persist
  on the `sourcingNetworkLoad` doc as `landedPremiums` (per-unit surcharge)
  and `importAggregates`.
- Plants pay the landed premium: `computeInputsCost` adds the sector state's
  per-unit surcharge on top of the global lagged price ratio, so out-of-state
  sourcing costs real money. One-turn lagged like the price ratios.
- Tariff revenue becomes flow-aware with netting: the sourced import value is
  netted out of the GDP-proxy import base before the rate applies, then the
  sourcing pass's real tariff take is added on top, so sourced trade is not
  double counted. Wired through budget refresh, fiscal year, tariff bill
  enactment, tax-rate bills, and the budget detail recompute; the client-side
  estimate helper stays on the proxy with a pointer comment.

Logistics corps already earn the haul through demand wiring (previous entry),
so no separate haulage transfer exists. Rollout per the interstate-logistics
plan: flip the flag in sandbox first, then prod.

## Campaigner NPP Management was a dead letter (ticket #1067)

PR #4318 admitted confirmed campaigners to `canUseNationalPartyInfluence`, so
the national NPPs tab and the influence GET/POST route let them in. The
executor (`validateNationalPartyInfluence`) still required chair / vice-chair /
admin, and that is the check that actually runs the action. Campaigners saw
"Only the National Chair or National Vice Chair can use party influence."

The executor now uses `isNationalCampaigner`, matching the route. Recruitment
is still chair / vice / admin. State NPP Management is unchanged.

## State Overview pool legends carry party identity (PR #67)

The Org Pool and Registration Pool legends listed bare abbreviations against a
colour dot, so a state page gave no way to read a party's actual name without
leaving the tab. `PartyOrgRow` and `registrationPool.parties` now carry the
party's full `name` from `politicalParties`, and both legends render through a
shared `PoolLegend` component: party logo, full name, abbreviation as a muted
tag, then the share. Non-party buckets (Unaffiliated / Independent /
Unregistered) have no party doc, so they keep the plain colour dot. The name
truncates with a `Name (ABBR)` tooltip on narrow cards.

## Whips no longer sign as Admin (ticket #1062, PR #58)

State-party whips never stamped issuedByRole, and the list UI resolved issuers
against the national chair, so a state chair's NPP whip rendered as "HARD FOR
by Admin". Chamber-leader roles also collapsed to Admin in the player-whip and
defiance panels. A shared issuerRole helper now infers the issuing org's chair,
and Admin only shows for a stamped admin override.

## USD equivalents on non-dollar budgets (ticket #1065, PR #62)

Soviet outlays printed as руб566B with no FX note, so a US player read it as
$565B. Alphabetic currency prefixes get a space and national budget surfaces
show an approximate USD figure from the era denomination rate.

## NPP corp operators actually run their companies (ticket #1060, PR #59)

NPP caretaker CEOs sat on idle cash (the Meyer Logistics report) and never
touched wages, supply agreements, or prospecting: the operator passes the 1953
all-flags world runs were simply missing. New turn passes under
`src/lib/turn/npp/`: treasury (surplus parked in high-coupon home-currency
bonds via the player buy path), supply agreements (negotiated with capacity
checks through the shared `supplyAgreementCapacity` helper), prospecting, and
wage adjustment in `nppCorporationBehavior`. Wiki pages updated to match.

## State Elections is one click from the nav

The state menu listed Overview, Economy and Legislature, but nothing for the
state's races: reaching them meant opening the state page, then Politics, then
the Elections sub-tab. A State Elections row now sits between Economy and
Legislature and goes straight to `?tab=politics&sub=elections`. It is separate
from My Election, which still jumps to your own race. Added on all four nav
surfaces (classic and focused, desktop and mobile) with English and German
labels, and a test pins the link's URL to the tab it is supposed to open.

## The status bar covered modal buttons on mobile (ticket #1061, PR #74)

The fixed bottom status bar and modal overlays both sat at `z-50`. At an equal
z-index the later element in the document wins, and the root layout renders the
status bar after the page, so the bar painted over the bottom of every modal.
On desktop the modal centres well clear of it. On a phone a tall form like
Propose Legislation fills the viewport, and its Cancel and Propose row landed
underneath the bar, unreachable: the bar is fixed, so scrolling does not move
it out of the way. The status bar drops to `z-40`, below the overlay level, and
a test now pins that ordering. This covers all 50 modals that use the standard
overlay, not just bill submission.

## Borrowing no longer counts as bank capital

`assessCapital` measured capital as `postedCapital + liquidCapital`. Money drawn
on the discount window is credited straight into `liquidCapital`
(`discountWindowCommands.ts:80`), and so is a CB margin draw. Both are somebody
else's claim on the bank and both counted as the bank's own capital, so
**borrowing raised the capital ratio**: a bank one draw from breaching the
minimum could cure the breach by drawing on the emergency facility it needed
because it was in trouble.

Capital is now `postedCapital + liquidCapital − discountWindow − cbMargin −
interbank`. A draw moves cash and the matching liability together and leaves the
ratio where it was.

Deposits are deliberately NOT in that line. In this engine deposits are not
cash: phase (b) of `bankingTurn` moves `npcDeposits` against the central bank's
`externalBroadMoney` and never touches `liquidCapital`, and deposit flight in
`bankSolvencyTurn` does the same in reverse. The deposit base is a liquidity
test (`reserveRatio × deposits`), not a capital test. Subtracting it here would
have reported every honestly-run bank in the world as insolvent the moment it
took a deposit.

Side effect worth knowing: loans are funded by the deposit base, so originating
one now grows the denominator and leaves equity alone. Lending costs ratio
instead of creating it.

`BankBalanceSheet.borrowings` is required rather than optional. An omitted
liability silently inflates equity, which is the bug being fixed; making it
required turned the compiler into the thing that found all four call sites.

## The reserve floor

`bankCharter.reserveFloor` (`totalDeposits × reserveRequirement − postedCapital`,
recomputed each banking turn) is enforced inside
`atomicallyDebitCorpLiquidCapital`, in the same atomic filter as the balance
check via `$expr`. A chartered bank's depositors and its shareholders share one
`liquidCapital` field, and nothing stopped the CEO spending the depositors' half
on capacity, shares, bonds or payouts. The reserve ratio was measured after the
fact, so it recorded the raid rather than preventing it.

Posted capital is netted off the floor because it is already cash held aside
behind the charter — without that, posting capital would raise the floor by the
same amount it lowered the balance and a recapitalization could lock a CEO out
of their own treasury.

The turn engine deliberately does not route through this guard: deposit
interest, insurance premiums and loan servicing move bank cash directly. Those
are the bank meeting its obligations, not the corporation spending its
depositors' money.

## Charter type switching (PATCH .../bank/charter)

Previously the only route from a retail charter to an investment one was to
persuade the central-bank chair to revoke your charter and then charter again
from scratch, posting capital twice — which is to say there was no route, and a
player who picked wrong at founding was stuck.

Switching to a charter that cannot hold deposits returns the whole book: player
savings pointers flip back to the central bank (balances untouched, pointer
only) and NPC deposits go back to `externalBroadMoney` with conservation. That
is the real price; `CHARTER_SWITCH_COOLDOWN_TURNS` (24) only exists to stop a
bank flipping every few turns to arbitrage whichever facility is cheaper.
Refusing the switch until the CEO had shed deposits by hand was the alternative,
and there is no mechanism for them to do that — they cannot evict depositors —
so it would have reproduced the original complaint with extra steps.

Facilities that do not survive the target charter must be settled first: an
outstanding discount window blocks a move to investment, outstanding CB margin
blocks a move to retail. Loans are left in place and keep amortizing, the same
rule `unwindBank` uses.

## The household book is no longer one number

`bankLoans` gains `creditBand`. The NPC household book was a single `npcBulk`
loan with one rate and one blended default rate, which is why the console could
only say "NPC bulk outstanding (implied)" — there was genuinely nothing else to
say. It is now one document per band (AAA through CCC, `creditBands.ts`), so the
same servicing loop produces a book that can be read by rating and shocked by
composition.

`bankCharter.lendingProfile` (conservative / balanced / aggressive) selects which
bands are originated into from the next turn. It never reprices, recalls or
re-rates a tranche already on the book: bands outside the stance get a target of
zero and wind down at the ordinary flow cap. A pre-split book carries no band and
is serviced as its own tranche at its own stored rate with a target of zero, so
it runs off while the banded tranches build; total book size lands in the same
place and only the composition changes.

Aggressive is a trade, not a free win. At an 8% base rate a conservative book
nets ~6.8% on 45% of household demand and an aggressive one ~6.2% on 100% of it:
more absolute income, thinner margin, and a stress loss of ~15% against ~3%.

## Stress is now band-weighted

`STRESS_LOSS_FRACTION` was flat at 15% for every bank regardless of what it had
lent into. `stressLossFraction` derives the shock from the book's band mix at
`STRESS_LOSS_MULTIPLIER` (5×) expected defaults, calibrated so an all-in
aggressive book lands on the same ~15% it always got. Aggressive banks see no
change; conservative banks stop being punished for a book they never held. A
mostly-legacy book keeps the flat fraction, blended on the banded share, so one
new tranche cannot re-score the whole book as prime.
