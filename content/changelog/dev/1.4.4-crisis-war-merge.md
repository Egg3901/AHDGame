Cut from development.

## Changed

- A war's record now opens with a list of who is fighting it, above the momentum
  bar: attackers and defenders, each country by name, and a column saying what put
  it there. A country that declared says so; the country whose soil is the theatre
  says home ground; an ally an alliance dragged in names the alliance. Until now a
  side was a row of two-letter codes under its banner, so a war could have a second
  great power in it without a reader being able to tell. Attackers and defenders
  are read from which side the host country is on. A war fought over ground neither
  side calls home, which is every proxy war, keeps each side's own name instead of
  naming an aggressor the record cannot actually identify.

## Fixed

- A war the German Question attaches to opened with East Germany holding the
  entire theatre. That is the right opening for a war fought over one country,
  and the wrong one the moment the question widens the ground to both Germanies:
  the West had to fight all the way across the Federal Republic simply to draw
  level on territory it already held. The front now starts even, so each side has
  the same distance to travel. A war that was already being fought when the
  question attaches keeps its front exactly as it stands, because that ground
  belongs to whoever took it.
- The front map drew only one country when a war was fought over several. A
  conflict can be hosted in more than one place, and the line across it is placed
  as a share of the ground the map can see, so drawing half the theatre put the
  line in the wrong place on the half that was drawn. The map now takes in every
  country in the conflict zone, and the line falls where the two sides actually
  meet. This shows up most clearly on a war for Germany, where the line now sits
  on the border between the two Germanies instead of somewhere inside one of them.
- Reunification left Germany in NATO and the Warsaw Pact at the same time. The
  settlement added the Pact membership but never removed the old one, and with a
  country in both alliances at once, which bloc it counted as was decided by
  nothing more than the order two records came back in. A reunified Germany now
  leaves NATO as it joins the Pact, and is in one alliance only.
- East Germany stayed on the Warsaw Pact's member list for ever after being
  absorbed. The merge moves regions and retires the state but never touched
  alliance rolls, so a country that no longer existed went on being listed as a
  member, and could be left holding a chair nobody could vacate. It now comes off
  both rolls when it is absorbed.
- In an era with no eastern alliance to join, the settlement no longer strips
  Germany out of NATO. Leaving it aligned with nobody is not an outcome the
  question has, so nothing moves at all.
