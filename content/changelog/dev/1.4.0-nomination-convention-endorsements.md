## Changed

- A presidential nomination with no delegate majority now resolves through an
  explicit multi-ballot convention: the lowest candidate is released each
  ballot, their delegates flow to whoever is closest to them (and to anyone
  they endorsed), until someone holds a majority. It is fully deterministic.
  Elections already in progress keep the old behavior.
- Endorsements can now lend organization and campaign credibility. The amount
  is set to zero for now, so this ships as wiring and is tuned later.
- A suspended campaign that endorses another candidate transfers a share of its
  support scaled by how aligned the two are, instead of a flat quarter. A
  well-aligned successor still inherits the same amount as before; a poorly
  aligned one inherits less. The two old transfer paths are unified into one.

## Guarded

- Every change is gated to the next election cycle; a race in progress is
  byte-identical (plurality nomination, flat transfer, no endorsement effect).
- Down-ballot nominations are unaffected. Tests cover the convention
  resolution, the alignment transfer, the zero-value endorsement no-op, and the
  in-progress-race identity guarantee.
