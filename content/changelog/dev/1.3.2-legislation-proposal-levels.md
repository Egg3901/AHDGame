Cut from development.

## Fixed

- A state or region bill recorded nothing about the law it was replacing, so its
  detail page worked the current law out afresh every time it was opened. Once
  the bill passed, the law it found was the bill's own outcome, and the proposal
  and the current law read as the same thing. Bills looked as though they had
  re-passed a law that was already in force. The law in force is now frozen at
  the moment the bill is proposed.
- The same applied to bills a governor introduced through the legislation queue.
  Those now record the law in force at the moment the bill reaches the chamber.
- A bill list could name the wrong option. Where a law offers more than one
  option pushing in the same direction, the card picked whichever came first
  instead of the one the bill actually proposed.
- Where a policy option's description contained a colon, its title was dropped
  and a fragment of the description was shown in its place. Bills already on
  record are corrected.
- Region bills resolved the older names of the corporate tax laws as unknown, so
  no current law was shown for them at all.

## Changed

- National and region bill provisions are worked out by one shared piece of code,
  so the two pages cannot drift apart again. A test now compares both and fails
  if they disagree.
- Region bill provisions show the cost and revenue detail that national ones
  already did, priced against the region's own budget.
- Region bills show projected effect chips for laws that name a single headline
  metric, which previously only appeared on national bills.
