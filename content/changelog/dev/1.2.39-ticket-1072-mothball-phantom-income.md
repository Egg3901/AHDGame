## What changed

- A National Corporation's holdings reported operating profit as nameplate revenue times last turn's margin, floored at zero. Under the plants tier none of those three terms survives a plant being switched off, and all three failed in the same direction.
- Nameplate revenue is capacity times the price of what the plant makes. Mothballing stops the plant, drives what it sells to zero, and leaves the nameplate exactly where it was.
- The margin was worse. A sector that sells nothing has no margin to derive, so the engine keeps reporting what its margin would be if it ran. Multiplying a nameplate that did not move by a margin that was never earned invented income out of an idle building.
- The zero floor finished the job. A plant that was running and losing money reported a flat zero, so switching it off could only ever make the number go up. One East German manufacturing corporation with five of six plants cold reported over four million per day of profit while the engine booked it a loss every turn.
- Operating profit is now the engine's own figure: what the holding actually sold, at the margin it actually earned, minus the upkeep the cold capacity keeps paying. It is signed, so a loss reads as a loss.
- The national budget's state enterprise line and the treasury remittance sweep also stopped booking a mothballed plant's nameplate as earnings, and now charge it the cold-storage upkeep rate the turn charges instead of the idle rate.
- The treasury's price-control subsidy line is on the same basis. A plant that sells nothing forgoes nothing, so price control on it costs the treasury nothing.

## Why

Mothballing exists so you can park capacity you cannot afford to run and pay a reduced standing cost until conditions turn. It is meant to be a way to lose less. Reported the old way it was the most profitable thing a state-owned corporation could do, and the player who found it said the obvious thing out loud: anyone could use this to make huge profits. Nothing in the engine ever paid that money out, but every number a minister used to decide what to build, run or sell was quoting it.
