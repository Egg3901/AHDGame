Cut from development.

## Changed

- Above every Aye/Nay button (congress bill page, both chambers; regional bill
  page; bill cards in lists) two lines now show what an Aye and a Nay would do to
  your economic and social positions, for example "Aye: Economic 0.25 toward
  Left, Social no change". The numbers are computed server-side by the same code
  that applies the shift, so what is shown is what happens.
- The shift now targets the bill's average position across its policy
  provisions (provisions that take no stance on an axis are left out of the
  average) instead of counting each provision separately.
- An Aye moves you toward the bill by at most 0.25 per axis and never past it;
  if you already hold the bill's position on an axis you do not move, since you
  are voting on your stances. A Nay moves you 0.25 away.
- Changing a vote you already cast is measured from the position you held
  before that bill first moved you, so an Aye followed by a Nay lands one step
  the other side of that baseline rather than two steps from where you are, and
  repeatedly flipping a vote can never net more than 0.25 per bill. Switching to
  Abstain reverts the shift that bill applied.
- Each bill now records, per voter, the baseline and the movement it applied
  (`policyShiftLedger` on national and regional bills). Votes cast before this
  change have no record, so changing one of those votes does not move you.
- A vote written onto you by a party whip is not your stance: it never moves
  your positions, and it does not count as a prior vote, so your first personal
  vote on that bill still shifts you normally.
- Bills with no ideology at all (a war declaration, a tariff-only bill) say so
  in one line instead of claiming you already hold the bill's position.

## Fixed

- Added regression tests that run the real shift through both vote commands and
  assert the write to the character; the previous route test mocked the shift
  out, so nothing proved a cast vote reached the character.
