Cut from development.

### Added

- **Vote suppression.** Spend $70,000 from the war chest and 5 actions to take
  10% off a rival's vote in one state for 8 turns. It applies to the projection
  and to the wave from the same rule, so the board never shows a lead the night
  is about to remove. However many rivals converge on one candidate, suppression
  can never take more than 15% of a state's vote off them.
- It sits behind the opponent row in State operations, alongside the local
  attack. Rapid Response blunts both.

### Fixed

- **A local attack is now local.** It fed the target's national favourability, so
  an attack bought in Iowa lowered them in all fifty-one states, and buying it in
  five states stacked five times over. A balance simulation of whole primaries
  had a single $40,000 purchase flipping a close race. It is now a standing
  penalty in the state it names and nowhere else, applied through that state's
  own approval maths, which is what its price and its button have always said.
  It is also a flat penalty while live rather than a drain that accrued for ever.
- **The primary board now reads turnout modifiers.** Party GOTV and player
  canvassing have always moved the actual result, because the turn engine reads
  the turnout modifiers they write. The projection never did, so their effect
  was invisible on the board and the numbers you were looking at were the ones
  from before your canvassing landed. Both now read the same channel the same
  way. Expect projected primary numbers to shift in states where turnout has
  been worked.

### Cut before release

- **Demographic turnout suppression.** A third attack was built and pulled. It
  took points off one group's turnout in a state, and it was indirect by design:
  the group's turnout fell for everyone there, the buyer included, so it only
  paid when aimed at a group a rival depended on disproportionately. Simulated
  across two- to five-candidate primaries, no group is that concentrated. It
  moved the leader's delegate share by 0.00 points at 1.5, 4 and 8 points of
  suppression, and reached 0.03 points only at 15, in a field already decided.
  A $50,000 purchase a player could not detect is worse than no purchase, and
  the fix is a sharper per-state demographic spread rather than a bigger number.
