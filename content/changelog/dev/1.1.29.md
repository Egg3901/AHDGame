## The last inert valve

The iron curtain (1.1.28) exposed the West's structural dependence on bloc
inputs: fertilizer at 2.3x base with 83% of world supply on the far side of
the curtain, freight at 3.3x with a 5:1 US deficit. A working market prices
that scream correctly — but nothing on the NPC side could hear it. The
expected-revenue re-strategize pass (t899) covered extraction sectors only;
fertilizers is a chemical-industries STRATEGY, freight a logistics sector,
and 34 US chemical plants sat on pharmaceutical and standard strategies with
no mechanism that could ever move them.

A third pass now runs the same decider over every NPP-run sector whose type
offers two or more era-available strategies. The scorer needed no changes —
it already prices non-extractables at neutral headroom and the deposit veto
self-skips. Tech-locked strategies are never auto-adopted, the 12-turn
transition blend and −5% margin penalty apply as ever, the 24-turn cooldown
prevents churn, and the same per-run cap paces the whole economy's response.
Player sectors are never touched.

At current prices the decider moves standard industrial-chemicals sectors
onto the fertilizers strategy at roughly 3x expected-revenue improvement —
covered by a regression test pinned to the observed prod ratios. Western
fertilizer supply should begin recovering within a few transition windows,
and with it agriculture margins. Freight relief follows the same mechanism
through logistics strategy choices plus NPP founding into the (large) unowned
logistics pool as its price ratio sustains.
