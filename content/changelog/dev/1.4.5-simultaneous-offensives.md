Cut from development.

## Fixed

- Casualties from both engagements of a turn now stand. Offensives are grouped
  by front and attacking side, so an attack and the enemy's counter-attack are
  two separate battles resolved in the same tick, and the same formations fight
  in both. Each battle wrote personnel, readiness and equipment as an absolute
  value computed from the roster as it was at the start of the turn, so the
  second write did not merely ignore the first battle's losses, it undid them. A
  division that lost two thousand men holding the line got them back an hour
  later by attacking.

- The front now moves for both engagements instead of only the last one. The
  territorial step read the conflict as it was loaded at the top of the tick and
  never saw its own earlier write, so the second battle started from ground the
  first had already taken and its result replaced it.

- Nothing fights on after the war is over. When an engagement drives the line to
  a pole the conflict is stood down and every unit at the front is sent home, but
  anything still queued for that turn went ahead anyway: it fought with a roster
  that had already left, took casualties for it, and could drag the front back
  off the pole of a war that had ended. Those offensives now stand down with it.

- The order engagements are resolved in is fixed rather than left to whatever
  order the database returned the declarations in. It decides who fights at full
  strength and who takes ground first, so it is now settled the same way the
  attacking roster already was: the earliest declaration goes first.

- The battle report's opening position names the front the engagement was
  actually fought over. For the second report of a turn it repeated the first's,
  which also meant the conflict record double counted ground that had moved once
  when it added the reports up.

- A country enrolled as a belligerent partway through a turn is stamped with the
  front as it stands when it joins, and the starting line a conflict measures its
  progress from is pinned once per conflict rather than once per engagement.
  Both feed war approval, and both were being read off a stale document.
