Cut from development.

## Changed

- Entering a conflict at a bloc's call now requires unanimous consent from every
  member entitled to vote. Committing an ally's soldiers is the one resolution
  that spends something a member cannot get back, so any member can refuse it by
  declining to consent. Free trade agreements and admitting a new member already
  worked this way and are unchanged. Mutual defence is untouched: a member who is
  attacked still brings its allies in automatically, with no vote at all.

- Every other resolution, sanctions, directives, aid packages, dues, posture,
  agency funding and joint statements, now needs more than half the members
  entitled to vote. It previously needed only more yes votes than no votes, so a
  lone yes against an otherwise silent bloc carried. Leadership elections now
  answer to the same majority instead of a simple majority of whoever turned up.

- Abstaining and never voting now withhold consent exactly as a "no" does, on
  every instrument. The threshold is measured against the members who hold a
  ballot rather than against turnout, so a member who sits an issue out no longer
  quietly lets it through.

- A resolution that fails, and a leadership candidate who is not elected, are now
  written to the country history. Falling short is ordinary under a threshold
  measured against the roll, and until now a failed vote simply vanished from the
  pending list without ever saying what became of it.

## Fixed

- Every voting panel was measuring its tally against the whole membership,
  including client states that hold no ballot and were never going to vote. A
  NATO entry vote read "1 / 34 members in favour" when the roll it would actually
  be judged against was two. Admissions, chair elections, trade agreements and
  all seven resolution panels now show the roll the resolver uses, and say how
  many yes votes are needed rather than naming a threshold that was not the one
  being applied.

- A country that changed its vote could be counted twice. The turn processor has
  always folded those older duplicate rows down to a country's latest vote, but
  no panel did, so a tally could be inflated, the roster could list the same
  country twice with two different votes, and the vote buttons could highlight
  the choice a minister had already moved away from.

- The vote buttons were offered to members that hold no ballot, and to a trade
  agreement party that had since withdrawn from the host organization. The server
  refused both. They are now shown as unavailable, with the reason.

- A trade agreement vote from a party that had left the organization, or lost its
  ballot, was accepted and then silently discarded when the agreement resolved.
  It is now refused at the point of voting.

- The progress bar filled toward the full membership rather than toward the
  votes actually needed, so a resolution one vote from carrying could look
  almost untouched.

- The international organizations wiki page described neither who holds a ballot
  nor what carries a vote. It now states both.
