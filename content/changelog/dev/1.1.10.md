Fixes ticket #1093.

## What was wrong

A supervised revoke and an ordinary revoke did two different things with the
bank's money, and only one of them was right.

The ordinary revoke path (`revokeCharter`) returns the bank's whole cash balance
to the corporation once its depositors are paid off, and archives the charter.
The supervisory revoke, which fires when a bank stays below the minimum capital
requirement past its recapitalization deadline, did not call that path. It only
stamped the charter `revoked`. The bank's ring-fenced `cashReserves` were left
stranded, and for an investment bank, whose value sits in its proprietary
trading book rather than in deposits, the entire book was written off with the
licence.

For the reporter that meant a charter revoke erased about 1.2M of value that was
never at risk to any depositor: the owner was trading their own money, missed a
capital deadline, and lost the money instead of the licence.

## What changed

Supervisory revoke now flattens the proprietary book back into the bank's cash
at its last marked value (the mark the solvency pass already computed this turn,
so it is an orderly close-out, not a fresh hit to the market), then calls the
same `revokeCharter` path as every other revoke. That path refunds the full cash
balance to the corporation when deposits are zero, zeroes the ring-fenced
reserves, and archives the charter.

Banks that still owe depositors are unchanged: they revoke without a shareholder
refund, exactly as before, because the depositors are paid first.

A `bank_prop_trade_sell` transaction records the book close-out so the money
trail is complete.
