## Changed

- NATO and the Warsaw Pact enforce mutual defence. Both alliances already
  promised it in their charters, and neither delivered it: a war declared on a
  member opened a conflict between exactly two countries and no ally was ever
  asked. Now, when a member is attacked, every player-led member of that
  alliance enters the war immediately. There is no vote and no window to opt
  out. Withdrawing from the alliance beforehand is the only way to stay out of
  its wars.

- Only defence is automatic. A war a member starts still pulls nobody in, which
  is what the charters actually say: an armed attack against one is an attack
  against all. Allies can still be called into an offensive war the way they
  always could, by an alliance resolution followed by a vote of their own
  legislature.

- An ally brought in by treaty cannot sign a separate peace while the country it
  came to defend is still fighting. Without that, an attacker could buy the
  coalition apart one member at a time and the guarantee would mean nothing.

- The same ally leaves the war automatically when the country it defended makes
  peace. The war it was brought into is over for the country it came for, and
  leaving it to fight on alone would be a promise that outlived its reason.

- Allies arrive with troops already deployed and are counted in the war's
  opening strength, rather than joining an empty front.

- The government of a country taken to war this way is told, and the entry is
  recorded on the alliance and on the conflict page, so nobody discovers their
  own belligerency from a battle report.
