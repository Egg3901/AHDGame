## What landed in this release

102 changes shipped.

### Elections and campaigns

- **A more responsive electorate model for future worlds**
  Admins can select Demographics v2 for a future 1991 reset. It connects the live population to voter age composition and lets race closeness, issue contrast and ballot access shape turnout through an explainable ledger.
- **Enter race works in the iOS app**
  Entering or withdrawing from a race in the iOS app now shows a confirmation in the page instead of doing nothing.
- **Party geography checks during signup and relocation**
  Starting party choices now respect nearby party presence. Relocating outside your party's reach requires confirmation that you will become Independent.
- **Party NPP controls open during the founding window**
  In a world that opens with no parties, new parties and their members can use NPP controls such as slating for the first 240 hours instead of waiting 48 hours.
- **Show regional registration pools for new parties**
  Regional overviews now show existing registration pools even when new parties have no registered voters. Independent and Unregistered shares no longer disappear when the leading party has no stored registration value.
- **Actions no longer ask for more money than they cost**
  In historical worlds, polls, campaigning, ads and donor building could be refused for lack of funds even though you had enough for the listed price. The funds check now uses the same era price you are shown and charged.
- **Fix JP Shugiin proportional seat allocation**
  Japanese House of Representatives races now award every regional seat proportionally. Parties need at least 10% of the vote to qualify, and existing 1991 races are corrected automatically.
- **Keep party influence request limits separate from unrelated actions**
  Unrelated requests no longer consume the party influence request allowance.
- **Leave party confirmation works in the desktop and mobile apps**
  Leaving a party now asks for confirmation in an in-game dialog instead of the browser's pop-up, which the desktop and mobile apps could not show. Cancelling keeps your membership; confirming leaves the party once.
- **Party chairs can upload a logo again**
  Some party chairs were refused when uploading a party logo even though the party page showed them as chair. Character creation also no longer leaves a stray extra character behind when a step fails partway.
- **Party mergers require neighboring presence**
  Party mergers now require overlapping or directly neighboring regional presence.
- **Withdrawing from a race now keeps you out of it**
  The withdrawal warning always said you could not re-enter a race you left, but the game let you file again. It now holds to that.
- **Fix country approval page loading**
  Fixed a loading error on country approval pages so national metrics and regional rankings can be displayed again.
- **Stop free challenger recruitment in player countries**
  Empty elections no longer create free NPPs for parties in countries open to players, including during Founding.

### Government and legislation

- **Brazil's 1991 budget opens at a realistic size**
  New 1991 worlds now open Brazil with public programs sized to its tax receipts and split according to the 1991 scenario, instead of a tiny program book and a huge surplus.
- **Clarify the 1991 Russian legislature page**
  The Russian legislature now introduces its 1991 institutional and federation decisions before showing the controls. Shared assets have readable labels.
- **Funded coupon payments settle in one step per country**
  When a Treasury can pay its bond coupons, it now pays them together in one recorded payment per country and turn instead of one per bond and turn, so a backlog of owed coupons no longer stalls the turn.
- **Quarterly bond auctions no longer stop the turn in funded Treasury worlds**
  In worlds where the Treasury spends only cash it has actually raised, a quarterly bond auction the market could not fully buy no longer stops the turn. The unsold part stays on offer instead of being bought with new money.
- **Resumed turns keep completed bond settlement results**
  A turn resumed after a restart now reuses the bond settlement it already completed, so treasury cash settles once from the real bond flows.
- **State enterprise profit transfers no longer stop the corporation turn**
  In worlds where the Treasury spends only cash it has actually raised, a state enterprise whose transfer is limited by its cash no longer asks for a fraction of a unit more than it holds, and a refused transfer no longer stops the turn.
- **State enterprise remittances survive a replayed turn**
  A corporation turn that is replayed after a crash no longer stops when a state enterprise already sent its profit to the Treasury earlier in the same turn. The original payment stands, at its original amount and exchange rate.
- **Unpaid coupon arrears no longer slow the Treasury step**
  Unpaid sovereign bond coupons are now kept as their own records instead of on each country's budget. The Treasury step of the turn stays the same size as arrears build up.
- **Germany, China, Brazil and Nigeria keep their government spending**
  Countries outside the player law book carry only a few laws that price part of their government. Their budgets now keep the rest of their spending as a fixed share of GDP, up to what their receipts can afford, so spending no longer collapses to the law book alone and their budgets stop showing impossible surpluses.
- **Faster Treasury processing for unpaid bond coupons**
  Treasury processing no longer slows down every turn while a government cannot afford its outstanding sovereign bond coupons.
- **Unfunded state capex grants no longer stall the corporation turn**
  A state enterprise's replacement capex grant is now paid only from spendable Treasury cash. A country that cannot cover it skips the grant for that turn instead of stopping corporation processing for everyone.

### Economy and corporations

- **Share prices stop counting the era's free technology as company value**
  Every company is handed the technology of the decades its world has already passed. That shared baseline no longer counts as a company asset in its share price. Only research a company chose and paid for adds tech value, so stock prices now separate leaders from laggards.
- **1991 household incomes for Nigeria, China and Turkey**
  New 1991 worlds now open Nigeria, China and Turkey with household incomes in the same 1991 currency as their GDP. Nigeria and China no longer start far above their own economies, and Turkey no longer starts with 1979 lira. Small yearly wage changes now move incomes in every currency.
- **1991 household incomes for seven Western European countries**
  New 1991 worlds now open Austria, Spain, Finland, France, Greece, Italy and Sweden with household incomes in 1991 national currency. Their regions no longer all start with an income score of 0 or 100.
- **Logistics sectors no longer pay the sprawl penalty**
  The logistics division always said depots offset sprawl, but they paid the penalty like every other sector. Logistics sectors are now exempt on their own margins.
- **AI companies now trade on the supply exchange**
  AI-run suppliers accept your buy proposals when they have spare capacity and the discount is no deeper than 10%, unless an embargo or the iron curtain stands between the two countries. AI companies also post standing sell and buy offers on the board, marked as AI. Accepted AI contracts now start on the accept turn and run for their agreed duration.
- **New AI-founded companies go where the market is short**
  Wealthy AI politicians who found companies now favour sectors whose products are scarce in their home market, and avoid sectors their country is already crowded with. They used to pick a sector at random.
- **Ownership chart and headline figures on the corporation page**
  The corporation overview shows who owns the company as a chart you can tap, puts revenue, net income, margin and growth in four headline boxes, and shows revenue growth in the page header.
- **Supply Exchange, take offers and safer contracts**
  Posted supply offers are now standing offers you can take in one step, with an offers view on every commodity, amendable pending offers, and new guards against related-party and embargoed contracts.
- **The Market, one page for everything you can buy**
  The Stock Market page is now The Market. A collapsible stock market chart sits on top, with stocks, bonds, funds, sectors for sale, commodities, supply deals and currencies in tabs below.
- **The Treasury follows the central bank's advice when the government leaves rates alone**
  Where the government sets the policy rate, as in the UK before 1997, the Treasury now applies the central bank's standing advice once the government has left the rate untouched for a quarter. The government can still set the rate at any time, and its own decisions always win.
- **World resets now start exactly like a fresh world**
  Resetting an existing world no longer carries leftovers from the old one into turn 1, so a reset world matches a brand-new 1991 seed.
- **Corporate profit and freight cost breakdowns**
  Corporate income statements now include freight and the full recorded plant cost bill. Sector margin breakdowns explain losses with actual costs, and current estimates are clearly separated from last-turn income.
- **Corporate tax payments look up each treasury once**
  Each turn now looks up every national treasury once when settling corporate tax, instead of once for every tax payment.
- **Corporate tax settles across countries at once**
  Corporations now pay their turn taxes to different national treasuries at the same time, instead of working through one country before starting the next.
- **Faster settlement of payments with many recipients**
  Payments that go to many recipients at once, such as product advertising spread across media outlets, now settle in a handful of database calls instead of several per recipient. The corporation step of the turn is much shorter as a result.
- **Fewer database writes for money entering or leaving the world**
  Payments that create or retire money, such as realized corporate receipts and write-offs, record that step together with the payment's completion instead of with a write of its own.
- **Fewer database writes when payments record their receipts**
  Payments that record receipts now mark them done in a single write instead of one write per receipt. Index-fund payouts and other settlements finish with fewer database calls each turn.
- **Index fund bond purchases settle together again**
  Index funds no longer fall back to buying government bonds one at a time when a bond they planned to buy is close to maturity.
- **Index fund turns no longer stall on long redemption queues**
  When many index fund redemptions were waiting at once, the turn tried to pay a slice to every one of them every turn and could run out of time. Each turn now pays a bounded batch, and whoever was skipped goes first next turn.
- **One less database read per payment**
  A payment no longer reads back the record it has just written before moving money, saving a database call on every payment the turn makes.
- **Payment receipts are written together**
  When a payment records several receipts, such as ledger entries, transaction log rows and audit rows, they are now written at the same time instead of one after another.
- **Plants that sell out ramp up faster in short markets**
  A plant that sold everything it made into a market with buyers left unserved now steps up to meet that demand instead of climbing 15% a turn, and the sector page no longer calls it demand limited.
- **Show actual bond holdings in fund details**
  Fund pages now show their corporate and sovereign bond positions, including units, issuer and market value. Bond assets and open buy-order escrow appear in the balance sheet using actual holdings instead of estimates from NAV.
- **Turn logs time the banking and treasury steps in detail**
  Turn logs now record how long each part of the banking and treasury steps takes, so slow parts of a turn can be found and fixed.
- **1991 households no longer out-demand the whole economy**
  In 1991 worlds, household shopping demand is now sized to the goods the world actually starts with, so food, retail and services no longer read as tens of times short on day one.
- **Bond issues count in the right currency for financial services demand**
  Government and corporate bond issues add demand for financial services. That demand now reads each issue in anchor value instead of its own currency, so lira, yen, zloty and Turkish lira issues no longer count hundreds or thousands of times too large.
- **Central banks now hike faster when inflation runs far above target**
  Automatic central bank chairs, the committee, and player chairs or governments setting the rate by hand can now raise rates by up to 3 points in one move when inflation is at least 5 points over target, instead of being held to 0.75. Rates in high-inflation economies catch up in a few moves rather than years. The central bank page shows the range you can use this turn.
- **Clearer sector expansion and financing**
  Sector expansion now puts quantity, added capacity and buyer demand together, with a visible build total and a clearer split between bank funding and company cash.
- **Computer-run companies keep growing while maintenance orders are pending**
  Small maintenance orders no longer lock a company out of expanding a healthy plant. Growth is now limited by how much new capacity is already on the way, not by how many orders are waiting.
- **Corporate credit ratings are labelled as leverage ratings**
  A company's AAA-to-CCC rating measures how much debt it carries and how safely it can service it, not whether it is making money. The corporation pages now call it a leverage rating and explain that a company with no debt rates AAA even when it is losing money.
- **Economies no longer read as overheated while companies are still being founded**
  The growth signal behind the output gap now strips out inflation and ignores the jump from an almost empty economy to its first companies. Before this, every region looked permanently overheated, which pushed unemployment too low and added to inflation. Output gaps and unemployment will drift back toward normal over the following turns.
- **Freight bills no longer outrun the goods they ship**
  A sector's shipping bill for a commodity is now capped at 15 percent of the value of the goods it takes in. Sectors in states with scarce freight were being billed several times their own revenue for hauling cheap bulk cargo.
- **Governments can only buy what the world produces**
  Health and defense budgets buy health care and ordnance as commodities. Those purchases are now capped at one and a half times last turn's world supply, the same cap households already have, so the two goods stop sitting near three times their base price.
- **Households and governments now share one demand ceiling**
  Health care and entertainment no longer read three times their supply when households and governments both hit their purchase limit.
- **Ireland's 1991 budget opens within its receipts**
  Ireland has no spending laws, so it spends the budget it starts with. That budget ran to about 46% of GDP before interest against 42% in receipts, a deficit of 13% of GDP. New 1991 worlds now size it to receipts after debt service, keeping the same split between health, education and the rest.
- **Profitable corporations no longer flagged insolvent over pocket change**
  A corporation that is earning money is no longer put on the insolvency clock because its cash dipped a trivial amount below zero.
- **Sector commodity icons open the right market page**
  Clicking a commodity icon in a sector's inputs and outputs now opens that commodity's market page instead of a missing page.
- **Sector values now count shipping costs and haul income**
  A sector's going-concern value, and so its company's share price, now reflects what it pays for freight and what it earns hauling it. Companies whose freight bill ate their profit are no longer valued as if they had kept it.
- **Shipping costs are a small share of the goods again**
  Hauling goods between states used to eat a fifth to half of what the cargo was worth, which pushed delivered prices up and starved short states. A typical bulk haul now costs roughly 5 to 12 percent of its cargo at normal freight prices, and no haul can cost more than a fraction of the goods it carries, even when freight is scarce.
- **Corporate operating cash settles without stalling the turn**
  Each corporation's operating receipts were settled one corporation at a time, so a world with several hundred corporations could run the corporation phase past its time limit. Independent corporations now settle in a few lanes with the same receipts, the same amounts and the same order inside each corporation.
- **Freight bills follow each sector’s share of state demand**
  Corporate sectors now pay freight in proportion to their share of all state demand. Small sectors no longer inherit shipping costs belonging to other buyers.
- **Preserve freight billing when loading older snapshots**
  Corporate shipping charges and freight earnings use matching buyer and supplier records when loading older sourcing snapshots.

## Detailed release notes

### Share prices stop counting the era's free technology as company value

#### What changed

- The technology part of a company's book value now skips the baseline nodes
  of passed decades, which every company in a sector receives for free.
  Research in the current or future decades, and specializations, still count
  at full value with the usual 50% decay per decade.
- In the 1991 world this baseline was worth about 11 million anchor dollars at
  nearly every company, more than a third of all listed market value. A dormant
  company and a sector leader were priced almost the same.

#### What players will notice

- A one-time repricing of the stock market. Small and weak companies lose most
  of their price; profitable companies lose much less. Prices move at most 35%
  per turn, so the adjustment plays out over a few turns.
- Afterwards, market value follows earnings far more closely, the largest
  companies are worth noticeably more than the rest, and P/E ratios fall to
  believable levels.

### Tutorial updated for the 1991 world

#### What changed

- "What changed" has a 1991 edition, written for players coming from the 1953 world: the fresh start, the world in January 1991, player-founded parties, the founding round, 1991 prices and the newer money rules. Other worlds keep their existing cards.
- The "New to this iteration" option is now "Played before", and in a 1991 world it says what it skips.
- In a world that opened with no parties, the "Join a party" step becomes "Join a party, or found one" and explains the three-founder charter.
- While the founding round runs, the tour adds a card with the turns primaries and general elections close on.
- Closing the tour keeps your place, so "Resume the tour" on the tutorial page picks up where you stopped. Resuming no longer risks restarting the tour from the first step.
- The time estimate in the welcome flow now counts every chapter the tour actually runs.
- The empty parties page explains how parties are founded.

### Choose a modern or classic interface

#### What changed

- Appearance settings now offer a clear Modern or Classic selector.
- Classic mode restores the richer typography, color, depth, and navigation
  used before the October 3 redesign while keeping current game features.
- Modern mode gives cards and section labels clearer theme color on phones.

### 1991 household incomes for Nigeria, China and Turkey

#### What changed

- Nigeria, China and Turkey seed 1991 median household income from their 1991
  GDP per resident, household size, household income share and an inequality
  adjustment. Each region keeps its relative standing.
- Their resting income targets match the opening values, so the first turn
  carries no income pull.
- Newly seeded 1991 worlds score Nigerian, Chinese and Turkish income against that same
  1991 level. Worlds already running keep scoring their incomes exactly as
  before, and worlds that start in any other year are unchanged.
- The income ceiling now fits Turkish lira incomes, so the first turns no
  longer cut 1991 Turkish incomes down to 10 million lira.
- The seed changes above apply only to newly seeded 1991 worlds. Existing
  worlds keep their seeds.

#### Income growth precision

- Regional median income still shows in whole currency units, but its growth
  path now keeps the fractions between turns. A slow wage trend used to vanish
  each turn in currencies with small income numbers: a 1% yearly change on a
  1,884 yuan income never moved it at all. It now adds up to the full 1% over
  the year, the same as it does for incomes in the millions.
- This applies to every world, including running ones, from the next turn. No
  income jumps on the switch, and no money is created: an income with no wage
  change stays exactly where it is.

### 1991 household incomes for seven Western European countries

#### What changed

- Austria, Spain, Finland, France, Greece, Italy and Sweden used 1979 incomes
  in 1991 worlds. They now seed 1991 median household income from their 1991
  GDP per resident, census household size, household spending share and an
  inequality adjustment. Each region keeps its relative standing.
- Their resting income targets match the opening values, so the first turn
  carries no income pull.
- Newly seeded 1991 worlds score these countries' income against the same
  1991 level. Before, every region scored 100 in Austria, Spain, Greece, Italy
  and Sweden and 0 in Finland and France. A few regions well above their
  national average, such as Paris, Madrid and the Italian north-west, still
  start at the top of the scale.
- Worlds that start in any other year keep their income scoring as it was.
- Worlds already running keep their incomes and score them exactly as before.
  The new scoring applies only to incomes seeded with the 1991 values.

### A more responsive electorate model for future worlds

#### What changed

- The Admin Panel now has a reset-scoped Demographics v1 or v2 selector. Existing and newly reset v1 worlds keep today's behavior.
- V2 builds voter age shares from the same live age and sex population stock used by births, deaths and migration. Aging now changes who is in the electorate, not only how large it is.
- V2 turnout starts from each group's established participation habit, then records separate effects for issue salience, race competitiveness, ballot access, campaign contact and saturation.
- V2 makes strongly contested economic or social differences matter more to voter choice while keeping the existing ideology scale and bounded effects.
- Canvassing now appears as its own turnout effect in v2, with repeated contact gradually losing efficiency. The election page explains every turnout term, and the Campaign Room recommends whether to canvass or persuade a weak group next.
- Each country uses a named, versioned calibration pack, so balance changes can be simulated and reviewed without changing the portable rules.
- The reset validates the required live population vectors before certifying v2. Missing data falls closed to v1 rather than partially activating the new model.
- An explicit, dry-run-first migration can verify and promote the current active world, including inactive Ireland, to v2 without waiting for another reset. Scotland and Wales join v2 when independence creates and verifies their successor-region data.

### Logistics sectors no longer pay the sprawl penalty

#### What changed

- Logistics sectors no longer take the sprawl margin penalty, however many
  sectors the corporation runs.
- Depots still count toward the corporation's sector total, so other sector
  types keep the normal sprawl penalty and adding depots still raises it.
- The logistics budget works as before: it raises the sprawl threshold and
  softens the penalty for every non-logistics sector.
- The Sectors tab, CEO budget panel, logistics panel and wiki now describe the
  exemption. A corporation made up only of logistics sectors shows no sprawl
  penalty.

### Discord bot can read the public API

#### What changed

- `/api/public/v1` accepts the Discord bot's key as well as the public bot key.
  The bot's country, commodity, war and legislation commands use these routes.

### New AI-founded companies go where the market is short

#### What changed

- When an AI politician founds a company, the sector is chosen with weights
  instead of uniformly. A sector whose products sell well above their base
  price in the founder's market is more likely; a 2x-priced product is about
  four times as attractive as one at base price.
- Sectors that already have many companies in the founder's country are less
  likely, so new companies spread into gaps instead of piling up.
- Every sector keeps a minimum chance, so founding stays varied.

#### What players will notice

- Over time, more new companies appear in short sectors such as health care
  and consulting, and fewer in sectors that are already oversupplied.

### Ownership chart and headline figures on the corporation page

#### What changed

- The Shareholders list on the overview is now an Ownership chart. Tap or hover
  a slice or a name to highlight that holder and see their stake in the middle.
  The top six holders are named, smaller holders are grouped, and shares still
  on the market show in grey.
- Key statistics opens with four headline boxes: revenue, net income, net
  margin with a bar, and revenue growth with an up or down arrow. The full
  figures are still listed below them.
- The page header shows revenue growth next to market cap, in green or red.

### Plan simulation evidence against change risk and compute budgets

Presentation-only changes can avoid full worldsims. Runtime changes require
bounded baseline and candidate evidence; declared cycles extend the horizon.
Over-budget and unavailable aged-state plans remain explicitly blocked.
Accepted reports can be reused only with matching execution fingerprints.
Budgeted queued runs stop their owned engine process group on expiry and remain
unqualified. Existing release gates still apply.

### Supply Exchange, take offers and safer contracts

#### What changed

- Open supply offers are binding. Take one for any quantity up to what is left and an active agreement starts at the listed terms. The publisher and the taker are both notified.
- Each commodity has a Supply offers page listing sell offers and buy requests with quantity, price, term, the seller's country and credit rating. It is linked from the commodity page and the commodity market table.
- A sector's inputs panel now points to the offers for its costliest input when other corporations are selling it.
- You can amend your own pending offer instead of cancelling and proposing again.
- Corporations with the same owner, the same CEO, or a 5% shareholding in each other can no longer contract with each other.
- Supply agreements between countries whose trade lane is closed by an embargo or the iron curtain are blocked.
- Proposing, countering, amending, accepting and taking now respect the supply agreements setting and the corporation actions pause, and are rate limited.
- You are notified when a supply agreement is accepted, taken, cancelled or expires.
- Fixed a bug where buying a listed sector into a lane you already operate lost the seller's stockpiled inventory.

### The Market, one page for everything you can buy

#### What changed

- New `/market` page with the stock market chart at the top. Collapse it and

- Normal recruitment and slate invitation processing are unchanged. No existing NPPs are deleted by this fix.

### The audit anomaly scan no longer exhausts server memory

#### What changed

- The audit anomaly scan filters in the database to rows a detector can act on:
  rows with a player or character actor, player, NPP or admin actors, admin
  actions, and party funding rows during a pre-election window. The system
  copy of each turn money movement stays in the database.
- Rows stream into a compact form instead of loading every projected document
  at once, and names, references and metadata other than the agreement id are
  no longer read.
- The fan-in and fan-out finding still reports how many system settlement rows
  it excluded; those rows are now counted in the database.
- `scannedRows` on the scan summary now counts candidate rows, not every row in
  the window.

### Unfunded state capex grants no longer stall the corporation turn

#### What changed

- A state capex grant that the owning Treasury cannot fully cover from spendable cash is skipped for that turn. The enterprise receives no replacement capacity and the Treasury is not charged.
- Corporation processing continues past an unfunded grant, so other countries' grants, loss backing, remittance, taxes, salaries, dividends and history are no longer held up.
- A funded grant charges the Treasury and installs the replacement capacity together, and a retried turn does not charge or build twice.

### Preserve freight billing when loading older snapshots

#### What changed

- Preserve the saved commodity book's turn so older sourcing snapshots can use
  their matching demand and supply records.
- Continue leaving freight unapportioned when the available records belong to a
  different turn.
