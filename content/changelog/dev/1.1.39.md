  A provision's fiscal line listed the proposed cost before the current one,
  the reverse of the Current Law to Proposed cards directly above it, and
  labelled the result "Net change" without saying net change of what. The
  same bill could show a red minus on one view and a green plus on another
  because one figure was the treasury delta and the other was the cost
  delta. Both are now labelled for what they measure.

Cut from development.

Raised on ticket #1107. A DDR education bill raised programme funding from
M315M/yr to M751M/yr, and the fiscal line summarised that as
"Proposed: M751M/yr cost, Current: M315M/yr, Net change -M436M/yr" in red.

Three things were wrong with that, none of them the arithmetic. The pair was
listed proposed-first while the provision cards immediately above read left to
right from Current Law to Proposed, so the summary contradicted the layout it
was summarising. The sibling branch of the very same component already got this
right for tax sliders, which render "Rate 12% to 18%". And "Net change" never
said net change of what, so a funding increase looked like a cut.

The cost pair now reads "Cost M315M to M751M/yr", ascending, matching the cards
and the rate row. The net figure is now explicitly "to the treasury", which is
what it has always measured: revenue minus cost, so a spending rise is a red
negative because the treasury is worse off.

The bill-level rollup strip had the ordering right already but measured
something different, the change in cost, which carries the opposite sign for
the same bill. Its column is now "Net change in cost". Same bill, two figures,
two labels, no longer two unexplained signs.
