1.15 collects everything merged to development after the v1.14.0 tag. The headline is a rework of per-turn actions and national influence across every role, alongside subsidiary group hubs and own-company supply across state lines, a run of mining and plant economy fixes, Westminster devolution and UK confidence rules, independence lifecycle repairs, and many interface fixes.

Coverage: base v1.14.0 (6a45528727), source 1d92a71f06. Merged feature and fix work in that range has a note; dependency bumps share one entry and test-only or promotion merges are not listed.

## What landed in this release

102 changes shipped.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Actions and national influence rework**
  National influence now fades when it is not maintained and settles at a level set by the roles you hold. Chamber leaders and Supreme Court justices earn extra actions, office action pools refill a little every few turns instead of at midnight, and the governing party pays for holding power in home-state influence.
- **Endorsements, race detail and every party's primary on the presidential page**
  The presidential map page has the endorse button on primary candidates again, the race detail tabs are back under it, and the primary map can be switched between parties from above the map.
- **Links, dates and result notices back on the presidential page**
  The presidential page links to each party's full primary page again, shows the key dates, lets signed-out visitors see the primary board, and puts Enter race above the map on phones.
- **Election night header fits on phones**
  On narrow screens the election night title no longer overlaps the "States called" count and clock.
- **Whip room shows the cabinet seat for each nomination**
  Cabinet nominations in the party whip room now name the position each nominee was put forward for.
- **Government approval entries follow the current leader**
  Government approval contest entries now start when a player leader takes office and reset when leadership changes.
- **Keep desktop general election cards within rail**
  The presidential general card now puts Campaign links under party names on desktop, so long labels fit inside the narrow left rail.
- **Keep presidential primary field rows readable on phones**
  Candidate names and primary statistics stay readable in the field list on narrow screens. Campaign details wrap below the candidate information.
- **Presidential primary shares use the active delegate pool**
  Presidential primary share percentages now use delegate totals for the world's active apportionment preset and stay within 100% on standings.
- **Custom party leadership titles**
  Party chairs can set optional display titles for the chair, vice chair and treasurer. Blank titles keep the country's existing labels. Role permissions remain unchanged.
- **Protect the post-election Commons confidence vote**
  UK Prime Ministers cannot dissolve Parliament while the post-election confidence vote is active.
- **Sync chartered party affiliation to held seats**
  Charter founders who already hold seats now have the new party affiliation shown on those seats while keeping their offices.
- **Government formation no longer fails at the new year**
  On the turn the year changed, the automatic federation proposal for Czechoslovakia or Yugoslavia could fail and stop government formation for that turn. It now waits for the new year properly.
- **Party founders keep the chair through the first leadership cycle**
  A newly chartered party's founder can no longer lose the chair to an early election that opened before the party's first leadership cycle finished.
- **Preserve the world when UK regions change country**
  Independence and Irish reunification carry live economies, government records and enacted services through the transition.
- **Repair independence lifecycle handoffs and council maps**
  Scottish and Welsh independence now hands elections, government and trade into normal country processing. Their maps follow whole council areas.
- **Connect Hawaii to Pacific states**
  Hawaii now counts as adjacent to Alaska, Washington, Oregon, and California through reciprocal Pacific sea links, allowing adjacency-based party expansion.
- **Isolated regions are within every party's reach**
  Regions with no configured neighbours, such as MOL, are reachable by parties established elsewhere in their country.

### Government and legislation

- **Westminster sets a separate stance for each devolved nation**
  The UK Executive has a Devolution tab for Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. Westminster can set independent constitutional stances, and both the PM and First Ministers can track Desire, government stances and drift drivers.
- **Cabinet estates say why an action was refused**
  Opening, expanding or funding a departmental estate now shows the reason when the game turns it down, instead of looking like the button did nothing.
- **Cabinet budgets explain an intentional zero**
  When a cabinet department's legislated budget is zero on purpose, the cabinet page says so instead of looking like missing data.
- **Custom flavour-only bills in the guided composer**
  Choose Custom Bill to write a flavour-only proposal with up to 10,000 characters. Custom bills have no provisions, budget changes, or metric effects.
- **Estates show the actions they spend**
  The Estates tab now shows how many ministerial actions are left for opening or expanding estates, and when the next one arrives.
- **Acting cabinet members have full departmental authority**
  Acting cabinet members can use the same departmental powers as confirmed members. Their appointments still expire after 24 turns, with one acting appointment per office per presidential term.
- **Cabinet funding no longer gets stuck after a program law changes**
  Changing which department funds a program, or leaving it to the states, now moves or ends all of that program's funding, so the national cash step can keep settling Cabinet departments every turn.
- **Faster cabinet nominations**
  Submitting a cabinet nomination now sends the Senate and nominee notifications together, so the nomination saves faster.
- **Bill proposals work again**
  Proposing a bill through the guided legislative proposal failed with a server error for every country after the first turn on 1.14.0. Proposals work again.

### War and diplomacy

- **Defense administration while conflicts are disabled**
  Defense ministers and authorized admins can manage personnel, commands, and unit assignments while Conflicts is disabled. Naval and air state remains viewable, but mission orders stay unavailable.
- **Crisis growth shocks now fade after the crisis ends**
  Crises that hit GDP growth no longer leave a permanent drag once they resolve, and a crisis can no longer lift growth in a region that was already shrinking.

- **Subsidiary Group hub and voluntary supply coordination**

- **Clearer message when a corporation page can't load**

- **GDP growth now reports what the economy actually produces**
  Headline GDP growth follows real output instead of being pulled back toward a fixed potential rate. The change phases in over 24 turns.
- **Own-company supply crosses state lines**
  With freight on, a company that prioritizes its own plants can now supply its plants in other states, paying normal freight.
- **Parent company CEOs can see a private subsidiary's technology**
  The Tech tab of a private subsidiary now shows its research choices to the CEO of the controlling parent company, read only.
- **Subsidiary valuation and charts are visible to parent CEOs**
  Controlling parent company CEOs can see private subsidiary valuations in corporation listings and detail pages, and choose the brand loyalty chart.
- **Parent company CEOs can see the rest of a private subsidiary's pages**
  The CEO of a controlling parent company can now view a private subsidiary's history, commodity, portfolio and credit details, not just its main books.
- **Restore Subsidiary Group direction controls**
  Review strategic objectives, regional preferences and player responses with clearer subsidiary direction cards.
- **Restore Subsidiary Group financial comparisons**
  Compare selected corporations with financial totals, sortable figures and real multi-member history.
- **Restore Subsidiary Group overview insights**
  See completed-turn Group metrics, revenue trends, member contributions and richer corporation cards.
- **Restore Subsidiary Group supply insights**
  Preview supply policies, inspect estimated allocations and export authorized comparisons.
- **Sector input warnings name the bottleneck from the last production run**
  Sector pages no longer pair an applied output throttle with a different world-market shortage when regional delivery or internal supply changes the tightest input.
- **Bond repayment warnings look ahead**
  Bond repayment notices now estimate whether the company's cash will cover the repayment by its due turn, and only demand action when it will not.
- **Corporation banners show at their full shape**
  The corporation page banner was a very thin strip, so most uploaded banners were cropped down to a narrow band through the middle. It now shows at a 6:1 shape, matching the size the upload hint recommends.
- **Keep buyer demand visible after a competitor founds a sector**
  The Expand sector dialog no longer shows buyer demand as unavailable just because another company recently founded a sector making the same goods.
- **Parent CEOs can see their private subsidiaries' finances**
  CEOs of corporations that control a private subsidiary can inspect its financial statements, credit rating, and bond details. Subsidiary CEO actions such as bond issuance remain unavailable to the parent CEO.
- **Show regional build options when demand is fully served**
  Regional economy pages now link to the plant build planner when a market has no unserved demand. The page explains that a new plant will compete with current producers for sales.
- **Stabilize plant production and clarify expansion risk**
  Plant production responds to fresher sales feedback and shares shortage opportunities between suppliers. Expansion advice accounts for unused existing capacity and explains why current demand cannot guarantee profits.
- **Compensation payments now show up in your company's transactions**
  When the team repays a company for money a bug cost it, the payment now appears in the company's transaction history as a restitution credit.
- **Corporation controls appear only when you can use them**
  Corporation, bank and sector pages hide management controls and unavailable choices when you cannot use them, while keeping relevant financial information.
- **Energy producers keep the revenue from their productivity gains**
  Extra output from the 1991 energy productivity ramp (and from output-rate technology) no longer drags down the price each unit earns while the sector's plants revenue cap is still easing off.
- **Let subsidiaries contract with their parent**
  Formalized parent-subsidiary groups can use private supply agreements while related corporations outside the group remain restricted.
- **Media ownership limits now count the audience, not your rivals**
  The rule that limits how much of a state's advertising one owner can carry now measures against the state's advertising audience. A station with few or small competitors can sell up to the legal share of the audience, and ads the law blocks no longer shrink the station's output.
- **New sectors earn the market price from their first turn**
  Sectors that players build or split off now sell at the market price right away instead of being held near a starting rate for many turns.
- **New-sector market pricing can no longer cut revenue**
  The change that lets player-built sectors earn the market price from their first turn now only ever raises their revenue. Sectors whose local market pays less than their usual rate, such as media stations in smaller states, keep their usual revenue.
- **Plant pages quote the real shortage floor**
  The inputs panel and throughput tooltip now show the lowest throttle an input shortage can actually apply to a plant, instead of always saying 50%.
- **Plants that cannot sell their output now shrink over time**
  A plant that keeps making far more than its market buys no longer pays wages and running costs on the unsold capacity forever. After a few turns of oversupply, the extra capacity retires a little each turn until the plant is close to what it sells.
- **Rechartered banks start with a clean loan book**
  A bank that is chartered again no longer shows the previous charter's loans in its console.
- **Sector market support no longer drops all at once**
  A recent change shortened the market support window for every sector founded during play in a single turn, which cut revenue sharply for many companies. Each sector is back on its own support window, and market support will be phased out in small steps instead.
- **Splits no longer move market cap during the split turn**
  A stock split or reverse split now keeps the company's market cap level for the rest of the turn it happens in, instead of drifting back toward the old per-share price.
- **Sponsored funds select investments and account for seed money**
  Sponsored index funds now select companies from their published scope. Management fees count each asset once, and new sponsors receive units for the money they invest.
- **Tech nodes have a minimum cash price**
  Unlocking a tech node now always costs some cash, even for companies with little or no revenue.
- **Cost-plus pricing covers every operating cost**
  Plants on cost-plus pricing now quote enough to cover all of their running costs, so a plant that sells its whole output earns its markup instead of a loss.
- **Groundwork for shipping your own supply across states**
  Prepares own-company input priority to cover your plants in other states of the same country. No change to play until the follow-up ships.
- **Larger gas and timber deposits, and mines abroad produce fully**
  New York and Cork natural gas and London timber deposits are larger, and mines owned in currencies other than the dollar are no longer held back as if their deposits were nearly empty.
- **Mines on empty deposits stop earning for output nobody buys**
  Mines whose region holds none of what they extract no longer earn revenue or buy inputs for output that never reaches the market. The change phases in over 24 turns.
- **Mining companies respond to high prices**
  Computer-run mining companies now switch to and open mines for resources that are expensive, not only ones in extreme shortage, and move mines off deposits they cannot work.
- **New mines open where the ore is**
  Computer-run mining companies chasing a rare earth shortage now open and grow mines only in regions that actually hold rare earth deposits.
- **One country's treasury problem no longer stalls every other Cabinet treasury**
  National cash settlement on the Cabinet finance system now runs each country on its own, so bad data in one country holds back only that country's treasury and departments.
- **Petition contributions reach the Treasury**
  Filing an index listing petition while the committee seat is vacant no longer charges the corporation without paying the Treasury.
- **Rare earth mines need fewer inputs**
  Rare earth mining now uses about as many inputs as other focused mines, so rare earth miners can make money while rare earth is in short supply.
- **State companies shut mines on empty deposits**
  National state companies without an appointed chief executive now shut mines sitting in regions that hold nothing those mines can extract.

## Detailed release notes

### Actions and national influence rework

#### What changed

- National influence decays 1% of its value every turn, then gains the turn's influence. Each role settles at 100 times its per-turn gain, and a reputation fades by half over about 69 turns after leaving office.
- The state influence term is steeper at both ends: 0 state influence costs 0.5 national influence per turn, 25 is neutral, 50 earns 0.5, 75 earns 1.25 and 100 earns 2.0.
- Position tiers: heads of government 2.5; vice presidents, presiding officers, majority leaders and national party chairs 2.0; cabinet members, governors, Supreme Court justices, the central bank chair, minority leaders and the CPPCC chair 1.5; national legislators and ceremonial heads of state 1.0; acting cabinet members 0.75; state legislators and state party chairs 0.5.
- CEOs of the 10 largest listed corporations in their country earn 0.3 national influence per turn, and CEOs of other listed corporations in the top half of their country by market cap earn 0.15, after running the same corporation for 12 turns. CEOs earn no extra actions.
- Holding an international organisation's leadership seat adds 1.0 national influence per turn on top of your domestic position.
- Members of the party holding the national executive lose state influence faster: 1.5 times the normal decay at 50% government approval, 2 times at 0% and the normal rate at 100%.
- Chamber leadership earns actions on top of the seat: presiding officers +3; majority leaders, minority leaders and the Leader of the Opposition +2; the President pro tempore, CPPCC chair, whips and shadow cabinet members +1. Only the highest role counts.
- Supreme Court justices earn +2 actions per turn on top of any other office. Write an Opinion grants 0.5 national influence instead of political influence.
- Office action bonuses follow each country's own office table. Ceremonial heads of state no longer earn a head of government's +4 actions, Chinese and Russian premiers earn +4 and the French prime minister +3.
- Ministerial actions regain 1 action every 6 turns up to 4. Vice president and Supreme Court actions regain 1 every 12 turns up to 2. These no longer reset at midnight Eastern time.
- You can spend 10 actions to buy 1 office action (ministerial, vice president, Supreme Court or gubernatorial), once per pool per turn, up to the pool's cap.
- The head of an international organisation has an agenda pool of 2 actions per turn, banking up to 4, for proposals inside that organisation.
- Campaign actions bank up to 48 turns of the campaign's own income.
- The central bank chair earns the 1.5 national influence tier instead of a separate bonus. Above 25 infamy the chair still loses 1.5 actions per turn.

### Committee votes no longer count a ballot from a vacated seat

#### What changed

- A ballot cast by a seat that has since gone vacant (term expiry or removal) is dropped from the open meeting and ignored by the tally, so the vote count never exceeds the number of seated members.
- The central bank committee tab shows the corrected tally for an open motion.

### Endorsements, race detail and every party's primary on the presidential page

#### What changed

- During a presidential primary, each candidate in "The field" on the left of the map has an Endorse button (and an Endorsed state you can undo). One endorsement per race, never your own candidate, and in a primary only candidates in your own party. Candidates from other parties no longer show a button that the game would refuse.
- A "Race detail" section under the map brings back the tabs the map rebuild had dropped: Campaign presence, Trends, State drivers, Turnout (when the race has it) and Factor ledger. In a primary it shows only the tabs that have something to read yet.
- A party strip above the map lets anyone switch between each party's primary, so you can see their map and field, not just your own party's.
- Campaign presence on the map itself is unchanged: if you are running, switch the map to the Campaign presence view. Everyone else can read each candidate's presence in the Campaign presence tab.

### FOMC motions use the seated governor majority

#### What changed

- A rate motion passes with a strict majority of seated governors. Vacant seats do not count, while seated governors without a ballot abstain.
- The chair sets the rate directly only when no governors are seated. A committee with one seated chair can pass a motion with the chair's vote.

### Inbox alerts on the desktop client

#### What changed

- The desktop client checks for new inbox alerts once a minute while it is open and shows each one as a system notification with its own title, message and category.
- Desktop alerts follow the same rules as phone alerts: muted and snoozed alert types stay quiet, routine turn income stays in the inbox, and anything older than a day is skipped.
- Signing in on a new install starts from your current inbox, so you are not flooded with old alerts.

### Links, dates and result notices back on the presidential page

#### What changed

- Under "Parties" on the primary page, each party has a link to its full primary page (calendar, delegate leaders and the state-by-state view).
- A "Key dates" block shows how many turns remain until the primary ends and until the general election, with the election year.
- Signed-out visitors can see who leads each state in a primary. The board is the same one the party pages already showed publicly; nothing about a candidate's own campaign is included.
- Enter race and Withdraw now sit in a strip under the page title, above the map, so they are not buried below it on a phone.
- Phones show the field once, with each candidate's campaign figures, instead of twice.
- Signed-out visitors see "Sign in to see where you stand" instead of a message that they are not filed in a primary.
- The Campaign presence tab tells a player with no campaign in the race how to get one, rather than describing how to build.
- A concluded race shows the winner's banner, and a race waiting on the House ballot or the swearing-in says so again.
- The page no longer asks for the live wire when it has no ticker to show, which removes needless errors for signed-out visitors.

### Media companies sell advertising without picking a model

#### What changed

- Default media strategies resolve as newspapers for advertising eligibility, supplier display, and coverage.
- Legacy broadcast resolves as radio network before 1950 and broadcast television from 1950. Explicit available models keep their catalog identity.

### More figures for the phone widgets

#### What changed

- The status data the phone widgets read now includes your corporation's market cap and your national influence.
- It also includes the per-turn change for campaign funds, state influence, national influence, favorability, vote share, share price, market cap and liquid capital. Influence, favorability and funds use the same per-turn numbers as your profile page. Vote share, share price, market cap and liquid capital show the change over the latest turn.
- The website's status bar is unchanged.

### Westminster sets a separate stance for each devolved nation

#### What changed

- The UK Executive Devolution tab has separate Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland sections.
- The Prime Minister can change Westminster's stance for each nation for 2 national office AP, with a separate 72-turn cooldown per nation.
- Westminster's policy and approval effects are half as strong as the First Minister's. Popular governments advance their chosen stance; unpopular governments weaken it.
- Both offices show the same Desire breakdown, including Westminster's stance and its direct effect.
- Existing worlds default to anti-devolution until the Prime Minister changes the stance.

- Both offices project annual Desire movement and time to the referendum Desire requirement, assuming current conditions continue.
- Incoming Westminster governments can make one initial stance change per nation despite inherited cooldowns, for the usual AP cost.
- Referendum campaign displays explain that starting support is captured when consent is granted; later live Desire drift does not directly move the campaign poll.
- Stance changes validate current UK regional ownership, and failed dashboard requests require a fresh load before further changes.

### Add Group Chain discounts and agreement controls

- Automatic Group Chain discounts use the existing agreement price band, from 0% to 35% below market, defaulting to zero.
- Consumed reserved inputs receive matched supplier charges and buyer credits. Existing contracts, consent, finite cash and NPP-only supply safeguards remain in force.
- Forecasts reflect saved discount and participation choices per corporation and for the group, before tax.
- Player CEOs can manage their own negotiated agreements from the Group page, including under Commercial independence.
- Automatic coordination remains disabled by default pending economic qualification.

- Selected-policy estimates show per-corporation and combined sales and profit impact for Commercial independence or Group members first, with both-policy comparisons retained.

- Subsidiary Group screens now use game theme surfaces, active navigation, styled policy cards and clearer financial tables.

- The Navbar Subsidiary Group shortcut now appears only for a parent or member of a formalized Group.

### Bank actions stay visible in the bank list

#### What changed

- The private bank list now keeps Deposit and Borrow actions pinned to the right edge while you scroll the table.

### Bank funds with named company investments

#### What changed

- Bank owners can create a fund under Bank > Funds. Pick up to 10 public companies in the bank's home country. At least a quarter stays in cash, and each company can hold at most 15% of the fund value.
- Fund money stays separate from the bank's capital, deposits, reserves and investments. Retail banks cannot manage funds.
- Investors reserve money on the fund page and receive units at the next turn's value. Pending investments can be cancelled for a refund. Withdrawals use the existing fund settlement process.
- Unavailable shares and amounts above an investment limit stay in cash. The fund keeps its published company choices and fees.

### Bank row highlights stay even

#### What changed

- The pinned action column now uses an opaque version of the row's hover color, keeping the highlight even and hiding values underneath.

### Bottom of the page no longer hidden under the status bar on phones

#### What changed

- Pages now leave exactly as much room at the bottom as the status bar takes up. On a phone held upright the bar wraps onto two lines, and pages without a footer, like the moderation panel, ended with their last controls (such as Next page) stuck underneath it. They now scroll fully into view.

### Cabinet estates say why an action was refused

#### What changed

- On a minister's Estates roster, a refused open, expand or funding change (for example no ministerial actions left, an acting minister, or a host country that already has that installation) now shows the reason under the Open estate button.

### Clearer Match demand availability when expanding a sector

#### What changed

- When Match demand is unavailable, the Expand sector dialog now tells you whether it is because the company has loans, bonds or unpaid arrears, or because it needs a full turn of operating figures.
- A sector in your company with no plants and nothing under construction no longer stops Match demand from working on your other sectors.

### Each product's own sale price on sectors that make more than one thing

#### What changed

- On sectors that make more than one product (for example ordnance and industrial chemicals), each row under "Things you make" now shows the price that product actually sold for last turn.
- The "each unit sold for" figure in the market and money section stays as the overall average for the sector, and now says it blends every product you make.
- Sectors start showing per-product prices after their next turn.

### Election night header fits on phones

#### What changed

- On phones, the "States called" count and the election night clock now move onto their own line under the race title instead of sitting on top of it.

### Give your own plants first claim on internal supply

#### What changed

- Added an Internal supply policy in CEO Operations, with a link from sector pricing, next-turn timing, saved settings and a compact last-turn allocation receipt.
- Existing supply agreements reserve first. Own plants come next, then wholly owned group plants when enabled. Surplus remains on the open market, and existing market scope and delivery constraints apply.
- Internal discounts create matching supplier charges and buyer credits on consumed inputs. They remain visible in plant operating accounts and cancel within one corporation before tax.
- Cold plants no longer offer stale output into physical clearing.

### Index committee hidden for private corporations

#### What changed

- The index committee panel on the deals tab now appears only for public corporations. A private company has no shares on the market for index funds to buy, so it could never file a petition.

### Index listing panel only shows when you can act on it

#### What changed

- The index fund listing panel appears only for a company's CEO, and only when the company is below an index fund bar or already has a request or approval in place.
- Other viewers no longer see the panel at all.
- The panel explains in plain words why a company is left out of index funds, what the request costs and how it is decided.

### Promotional cash grants through the Discord bot
