Cut from development.

## Added

- Regions of the United States, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union and East
  Germany have a **Metrics** tab of their own, alongside Politics and Economy
  rather than buried under Demographics. It carries the same registry the
  country page has: the nine category scores, every metric behind them, and a
  drill-down into any one of them. Every figure is that region's own value, not
  a slice of a national one, which is the right way round because the national
  number is built by averaging the regions in the first place. Countries without
  a registry of their own keep their statistics where they were, now under
  Demographics and Statistics.
- A metric now shows what is moving it at region scope. The national laws that
  set its target, the region's own programmes on top of them at half weight,
  the standing conditions, the cabinet channels, and the labour relations term.
  That last one has been quietly moving every region's politics for a long time
  and appeared on no screen anywhere until now.
- Underlying statistics on a region's metric are that region's own figures.
  Rates set for the whole country, like the prime rate and inflation, are
  labelled as national so there is no confusion about whose number it is.
- Compare a region against up to three of its neighbours across all nine
  categories at once, expanding any category to see the individual metrics.
- Regions now keep a trend record of their own. The movement figures and trend
  lines start empty and fill in as turns pass, the same way the national ones
  did when they were introduced.
- The state Laws and Policy page gained a **Record** view: that region's own
  enactment timeline, with what each law moved and when. Regions that have not
  passed a law of their own say so plainly instead of showing an empty chart.

## Changed

- State Laws and Policy now uses the same layout as the national policy page.
  Numbered titles, the law's serif name and current setting, when it was enacted
  and under which act, its annual cost, and its position on the economic and
  social axes.
- The legacy economic and population boards that used to sit under Demographics
  are now under **Demographics and Statistics**. Old links to the metrics tab
  still work: they open the registry where there is one, and the statistics
  boards where there is not.

## Fixed

- State policy sections headed by a raw internal key instead of a name. Georgia
  showed sections called "tax" and "economy" because the state page carried its
  own shorter list of section names than the national page did.
- State tax rates listed twice on the same page, once as laws and once as a
  separate rates panel.
- A malformed law record could blank the whole statute book instead of the one
  line it affected.
