## Fixed

- An index fund is paid its share when a company it holds is wound down through
  a bond default. The wind-down split the company's remaining assets across its
  shareholders and counted the fund's block in that split, but the fund's slice
  was never actually handed over, so it was left in the failed company and
  destroyed. The fund now receives that cash.

- The failed company is removed from the fund's holdings. Because the payout
  step was skipped, the company was also never cleared from the fund's book, so
  it lingered as a position marked at its final price even though it no longer
  existed and could never be sold. The holding is now dropped when the company
  is wound down, matching how voluntary dissolution and nationalization already
  settle fund holders.
