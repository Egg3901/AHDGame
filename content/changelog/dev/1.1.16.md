## What changed

Most tax laws offer a ladder of preset options, and each option carries its own
rate. A newer class of tax law instead offers a slider: there is no options
ladder, and the rate the sponsor picked lives only on the provision itself.

Enactment collected the provisions of a passed bill into an internal list before
applying them, and that list left the slider rate behind. Everything else in the
pipeline still ran, which is why the failure was silent: the bill was recorded
as signed, the enacted-law row was written, the policy record was stored, and
the bill page showed the new rate. Only the national budget was untouched. The
step that writes the rate is guarded on the provision carrying a rate, and after
the rate was dropped that guard never passed.

The practical result, reported in ticket 1102, was a country that voted through
a purchase-tax rate and a customs duty rate and then collected nothing on either
line, turn after turn, with no error anywhere to explain it. Because the rate
never reached the budget, revenue stayed at the pre-bill rate of zero and the
Treasury quietly ran the deficit the bill was meant to close.

Enactment now carries the slider rate through, so a slider tax law moves the
national tax rate the moment it is signed, revenue is recalculated on the spot,
and the budget surplus updates with it. Ladder-option tax laws are unaffected;
they were always reading their rate from the selected option.

## Repairing rates already voted through

Bills that passed while this was broken did not apply their rates, so those
countries need their tax rates set to what the enacted law says before the
revenue lines come back. Re-passing the same bill also works and now takes
effect correctly.
