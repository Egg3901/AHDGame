Cut from development.

Builds on the acting secretary scope limits, which restrict what a caretaker may
do. This adds how one is appointed, and how long they last.

## Changed

- A President can appoint an **acting secretary** to a vacant cabinet seat
  directly from the cabinet page. There was previously no control for this
  anywhere in the game, which is why departments sat unhelmed for long stretches.
- Each President gets **one acting appointment per office per term**, and it
  lasts **24 turns**. When it lapses the seat falls vacant, and because the
  appointment is not given back, that office can only be filled by Senate
  confirmation for the rest of the term.
- An acting appointment now requires a **vacant seat**.
- An acting appointment no longer cancels a pending nomination, and confirmation
  always wins: when the Senate confirms someone, they replace the acting
  secretary at once.
- The cabinet page shows an Acting badge with the turns remaining, and the
  appoint control explains itself once an office's acting appointment is used up.
- A department run by an acting secretary shows how long the appointment has
  left, alongside the locked controls.
- The Acts of Government ledger marks an acting appointment as Acting instead of
  filing it alongside Senate confirmations.

## Fixed

- An acting appointment could remove a sitting confirmed secretary, which let a
  President replace a confirmed officer permanently without a vote.
- A nominee the Senate had already rejected could still be installed as acting
  for the same office. They no longer can.
- An acting appointment did not check that the office exists in the current era,
  so a department could be helmed centuries before it was founded, or filled
  using another country's cabinet post.
- Cabinet listings reported acting status from the wrong field, hiding genuine
  acting secretaries and mislabelling seats held by non player parties.
- A nomination could be refused for an office a create department bill had
  already brought into existence, even though the cabinet page offered it.
- Confirming a nominee could clear a cabinet seat in a different country that
  happened to share the same position name.
