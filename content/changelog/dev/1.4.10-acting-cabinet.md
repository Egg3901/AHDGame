Cut from development.

## Changed

- A President can appoint an **acting secretary** to a vacant cabinet seat
  directly from the cabinet page, with no Senate vote. This closes the long
  stretches where a department sat unhelmed and no action could be taken in it.
- An acting secretary is a caretaker. They keep the operational work of the
  department: deployments, recruitment, postures, standing orders, and funding
  for projects already under way. They cannot change department policy or
  budget allocations, appoint or dismiss commanders, adopt doctrine, enter a
  nuclear programme, or open new estates, infrastructure, or energy projects.
- Each President gets **one acting appointment per office per term**, and it
  lasts **24 turns**. When it lapses the seat falls vacant, and because the
  appointment is not refunded, that office can only be filled by Senate
  confirmation for the rest of the term.
- An acting appointment no longer cancels a pending nomination. Confirmation
  always wins: when the Senate confirms someone, they replace the acting
  officer at once.
- The cabinet page now shows an Acting badge with the turns remaining, and the
  appoint control explains itself when an office's acting appointment is spent.

## Fixed

- An acting appointment could remove a sitting confirmed secretary, which let a
  President replace a confirmed officer permanently without a vote. Acting
  appointments now require a vacant seat.
- An acting appointment reset the office's policy cooldowns, so repeatedly
  swapping acting officers reset department policy at will. Cooldowns now
  belong to the office rather than to whoever holds it.
- A nominee the Senate had already rejected could still be installed as acting
  for the same office. They no longer can.
- Cabinet listings reported acting status from the wrong field, hiding genuine
  acting officers and mislabelling seats held by non player parties.
- Confirming a nominee could clear a cabinet seat in a different country that
  happened to share the same position name.
