  A bond default now asks whether your company can cover its debt, not just
  whether your cash went below zero for a turn. Building with borrowed money
  leaves you cash-poor by design, and that alone no longer starts the default
  process.
tags: [corporations, economy]

## Changed

- A corporate bond default used to fire the moment cash went below zero, by any
  amount, for any reason. That reads a company mid-build as a company that has
  stopped paying: capital spent on plant is gone from the bank until the plant
  comes online, which is exactly what borrowing to build looks like. A default
  now also requires that your assets fall short of your bond debt.
- Your assets are valued the same way the system values them when it decides
  what to sell to cover a default. If selling could have covered the debt, there
  was nothing to default on.
- A company short of cash is still short of cash. It cannot fund new building or
  spending until it recovers, and a company whose assets genuinely do not cover
  its debt defaults exactly as before.
