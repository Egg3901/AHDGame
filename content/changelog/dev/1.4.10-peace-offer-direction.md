Cut from development.

## Added

- A peace offer now says which of the two countries leaves. It can still be you,
  which is what every offer meant before. It can now be them: you ask a country on
  the other side to withdraw, and you stay in the war. They still have to agree,
  because no government is removed from a war without its own consent, so in
  practice you are paying them to go. This is how an alliance comes apart without
  being beaten, which the guide has advised as strategy for longer than it has been
  possible.

- A withdrawal that would end the war outright is gated on the ground. If the
  country you are asking to leave is the last one on its side, or takes its treaty
  allies out with it, then its departure hands you the war. You may only ask for
  that once the front has moved well into their territory, at the point the war is
  already visibly winding down in your favour. Short of that you are not
  negotiating a settlement, you are buying a victory.

- A white peace is exempt from that gate, always. It records no winner and moves
  nothing, so there is nothing to buy: a war fought over an open question ends with
  the question still open.

## Changed

- Mutual defence no longer holds an ally in a war with no way out. Being dragged in
  by a treaty still happens, and still costs, but the country pulled in can now
  negotiate its own exit at whatever price the other side will take. It used to be
  held there until the member it came to defend settled, which meant a guarantee it
  never chose could keep it fighting indefinitely.

## Fixed

- A war's founding country is identified correctly when treaty allies were pulled in
  at the moment it was declared. Those allies join the opening roster rather than
  arriving later, so they carried no join record and could read as founders
  themselves. It resolved correctly in practice only because of the order the roster
  happened to be written in.
