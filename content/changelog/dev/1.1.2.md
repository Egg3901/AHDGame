## Defence contracts stop stranding small suppliers (ticket #1083)

A player with naval lines accepted a Ministry of Defence order and it delivered
nothing for eighteen turns. Not for lack of money — the appropriation held
billions — and not a re-tooled plant: the plant was certified naval and the
order was for naval.

Delivery output is `floor(revenue-derived lots)`, and their plants produced
about 0.17 lots per turn. `floor(0.17)` is zero, so every turn the plant's
fractional output was computed and then discarded, and the order never moved.
The mechanic worked only for large suppliers; any plant under one lot per turn
was frozen out of procurement entirely, permanently, with only a generic
"producing no output" note that reads as false to a CEO who built the line.

Deliveries now carry the sub-lot remainder from turn to turn (`deliveryCarry`,
always under one), so a plant producing a third of a lot per turn ships one lot
every three turns instead of never. Long-run throughput is unchanged — this
banks output that was being thrown away, it does not manufacture new materiel,
and whole lots the appropriation cannot pay for are still dropped rather than
stockpiled. The supplier and order-book panels now show the true fractional
rate, so a slow line reads as slow rather than broken.

## Reachable market scope (ticket #1077)

Design doc: ops-knowledge `reachable-market-scope-design`.

### The report

A US player saw the Commodities tab list Food Products at D/S 2.88 with the
copy "scarcer, which sells at a premium. Supply these", then found every market
in Build a new sector reporting "ROOM FOR 0 farms / 0 units/day open" and the
expand dialog saying the market was oversupplied.

### Root cause

Three surfaces read three different market scopes, none of them the scope the
engine clears on.

- The shortage panel read `nationalDemand / nationalSupply` (country, production
  only): 863,550.81 / 299,765.18 = 2.8808.
- Both build gates read `max(0, globalDemand - globalSupply)`, min over output
  legs. World food: 14,360,715.65 demand against 21,071,946.52 supply, so the
  gap was 0 and agriculture's single `{food: 0.5}` leg zeroed headroom
  everywhere on Earth.
- The engine clears each sector against its home country's book from
  `market/tradePartition`: `max(0, D - imports) + exports`.

The world aggregate sums three populations that cannot substitute for each
other: reachable supply, supply behind an embargo, and supply from countries
outside `COUNTRY_ORDER` that trade with nobody. Measured on prod at turn 97:

| commodity   | global D/S | old gate  | US-reachable D/S | untraded supply | blocked from US |
| ----------- | ---------- | --------- | ---------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| oil         | 0.82       | 0 room    | **1.82**         | 0               | 164,002         |
| steel       | 1.25       | 822,839   | **2.27**         | **1,412,598**   | 696,494         |
| electronics | 1.70       | 2,268,406 | 1.63             | 169,266         | 1,160,097       |
| plastics    | 1.68       | 308,298   | 1.18             | 57,130          | 62,240          |

Ukrainian steel alone is 41% of world supply from a country the clearing engine
never sees. The correction runs both ways: plastics room drops from 308,298 to
51,687 once unreachable demand is also removed. This is a correctness fix, not
a buff.

### Changes

- `market/tradePartition.reachableDemandUnits` is now the single definition of
  sellable demand, shared by the engine's clearing books and every read surface,
  so a quote and a settlement cannot drift.
- New `trade/reachableBook`: per-country, per-commodity books in units, carrying
  `supply`, `demand`, `imports`, `exports`, plus `blockedSupply` and
  `untradedSupply` for disclosure only. Those two are never added back into a
  ratio; they exist so the UI can name what it excluded instead of showing a
  bare 0 that reads as a dead market.
- `commodityPriceTurn` builds the books from the SAME pre-convergence balances
  the clearing ran on, immediately before `applyTradeConvergence` mutates them
  in place, and persists them on the per-turn `tradeFlowSnapshots` doc. Reading
  post-convergence would understate every importer's deficit by the k = 0.5
  convergence factor (verified: imports / stored deficit is exactly 2.0000 for
  all eleven net importers).
- New `market/sectorDemandGap.sectorDemandGapUnits` extracts the min-over-legs
  computation that `expand-suggestions/route.ts` and `queries/sectorDetail.ts`
  each carried their own copy of.
- Both gates now resolve the gap per candidate market's country. `sectorDetail`
  uses the sector's home country.
- A world with no persisted book (has not run a turn since 1.1.2) falls back to
  the previous global behaviour rather than reporting no room anywhere.
- Lazy `{turn: -1}` index on `tradeFlowSnapshots` for the latest-book read.

### Deliberately not in this change

- Commodities page map rework (commodity dropdown, choropleth, flow arcs). It
  belongs on top of a correct scope layer.
- The shortage heat map's country lens still quotes the stored national
  balance, which is a price-formation intermediate rather than physical unmet
  demand. Next PR on this branch.
- Adding UKR/BLR/BAL to `COUNTRY_ORDER`. Owner decision: it would put 6.1M
  units/day of Ukrainian grain and 1.4M of steel onto a live world's market.
  Scoping the read surfaces removes them from every player's signal without
  touching trade balance.
- Advertising at 43.70x oversupplied, healthcare_services 5.75x and natural_gas
  5.10x with 143.8 turns of stock cover look like seeding faults and still
  suppress the gate for any sector whose mix touches them.

## Player banking and funds gated to the basics for now

Two player-facing capabilities are held back behind config flags until they are
ready. Both apply only when a charter or fund is created or switched, so anything
already active keeps operating unchanged, and the flags can be turned on later
without a deploy.

- **Bank charters:** players may only charter, or switch to, a retail bank.
  Investment and universal charters are withheld behind
  `playerAdvancedBankChartersEnabled`. The gate lives in the one function both
  new charters and type switches consult, so a withheld type is refused
  everywhere with a single check.
- **Index funds:** a corporation cannot sponsor its own index fund. The default
  system funds still trade normally; player sponsorship is withheld behind
  `playerFundSponsorshipEnabled`.

## Reachable lens on the commodity world map

Stacks on the reachable-market-scope change. Design doc: ops-knowledge
`reachable-market-scope-design`.

### Reachable lens on the world map

The map could already colour countries by supply, demand, price and deposits.
All four are world-aggregate or raw-balance views, so none of them answered the
question a player actually asks before building: can I sell here.

Reachable paints each country by its own book, `demand / supply` where demand is
what that country's sellers can reach after imports and exports. Same book the
turn engine settles against, same book the build gates now quote.

#### Palette

The job is POLARITY (which side of demand = supply a country sits on), so the
scale is diverging: two hues and a neutral gray midpoint, never a rainbow and
never a hue in the middle.

Poles are the ones `ShortageHeatMap` already ships (#D64545 shortage, #3E7CB1
glut, #8A8F98 neutral) so the two surfaces read as one system. Each arm was
solved for even OKLCH lightness spacing and checked with the dataviz
`validateOrdinal` in BOTH light and dark mode:

- shortage arm `#e68f8f #dd6363 #c44040 #973535`
- glut arm `#8ab0d0 #6294c0 #3d79ad #325f85`

Both pass lightness-monotone, adjacent dL >= 0.06, light-end contrast and
single-hue. The CATEGORICAL validator fails a correct diverging ramp by design
(it spans the lightness band and a gray midpoint sits under the chroma floor),
so it is not the check that applies; `validateOrdinal` per arm is.

One validated mid-tone set serves every theme, matching the existing reasoning
in ShortageHeatMap: the app ships 10+ named data-theme surfaces, so a per-theme
override would mis-fire on the themes it does not name.

#### Details

- Steps escalate at 1.15x / 1.5x / 2x / 3x imbalance and the glut arm mirrors
  the reciprocal, so a 2x glut and a 2x shortage sit the same distance from the
  middle. A linear slice would have put nearly every real market in one bucket.
- A country with no book is left unpainted; one with a book but no activity on
  either side is painted "unknown", which is deliberately a different answer
  from "balanced".
- The legend names BOTH poles and the midpoint, because a polarity scale is
  unreadable if the middle is unlabelled. Its swatch is built from the ramp
  itself, so legend and fill cannot drift.
- The mode is hidden, not disabled, when no book is persisted. An always-greyed
  button on an older world is noise.
- Inside a country drilldown the per-state map falls back to demand colouring:
  sales clear nationally, so there is no per-state reachable ratio to invent.

#### Not in this change

- Blocked/untraded disclosure on the country stat card. The shortage heat map
  already names excluded supply; the map repeats the ratio but not yet the
  reason.
- Commodity dropdown on the map (the commodity is still the route).
- Bilateral flow arcs from `tradeFlowSnapshots`.

## Build room counts import displacement

Follow-up to 1.1.2. Design doc: ops-knowledge `reachable-market-scope-design`.

### What 1.1.2 got wrong

1.1.2 moved the build gates off the global aggregate and onto the per-country
reachable book. Correct scope, wrong quantity. OVO reported "still the same" on
ticket #1077 with the identical "room for 0 farms" screenshot.

`clearCommodity` trades only the NET residual: it nets domestic supply against
domestic demand first and sets `imports = D - S`. So the clearing book,
`max(0, D - imports) + exports`, collapses to `supply` for every net importer.
Prod, turn 113, food, to every decimal place:

| country | supply        | clearing demand | imports   |
| ------- | ------------- | --------------- | --------- |
| US      | 323,218.24676 | 323,218.24676   | 1,115,631 |
| UK      | 161,664.3504  | 161,664.3504    | 245,443   |
| JP      | 370,004.72352 | 370,004.72352   | 803,508   |
| IT      | 372,077.962   | 372,077.962     | 1,594,918 |
| CN      | 769,021.85    | 769,021.85      | 2,960,763 |

`demand - supply` off that basis is identically zero for any commodity a country
imports. That is algebra, not a market condition. It also means the earlier
reading that the shortage panel was "the liar" and the gate "directionally
right" was backwards: the panel's shortage signal was pointing at a real
opportunity all along.

### The correct basis

Domestic output clears before imports, so new domestic capacity displaces
imports one-for-one and the deficit shrinks to match. Room is the deficit:

    max(0, domesticDemand + exports - supply)

Effect on live turn-113 data:

| market                    | old room | new room  |
| ------------------------- | -------- | --------- |
| US food                   | 0        | 1,115,631 |
| UK food                   | 0        | 245,443   |
| JP food                   | 0        | 803,508   |
| IT food                   | 0        | 1,594,918 |
| CN food                   | 0        | 2,960,763 |
| US electronics            | 116,372  | 116,502   |
| US coal (self-sufficient) | 0        | 0         |
| PL food (real glut)       | 0        | 0         |

Surgical by construction: it only moves where imports exist. Genuine gluts and
self-sufficient markets are untouched.

### Changes

- `ReachableBookEntry.domesticDemand` persisted explicitly, because the clearing
  `demand` destroys the information a build decision needs. Books written at
  turns 106-113 lack it and are healed by inverting the clearing formula, so the
  first turn after deploy is not a cliff.
- `reachableDemandGap` switched to the deficit basis. `reachableDemandUnits` in
  `market/tradePartition` is UNCHANGED — it is the engine's order-book quantity
  and is correct for what today's producers fill. Only the build signal moved.
- New `reachableSellableDemand`, shared by the gap and the Reachable lens so the
  panel, the map and the gate rank on one number.
- The shortage heat map's Reachable lens ranks on sellable demand rather than
  the pinned clearing book, which previously rendered every importing market as
  exactly "balanced".

### Note for reviewers

The test `closes the book when imports actually arrive` previously asserted a
zero gap when imports arrive. That assertion encoded the defect, and is now
inverted to assert the displaceable volume.

## State enterprises can modernize again

Fixes ticket #1072.

### What was broken

A player set a high modernization (R&D) budget on a state enterprise and its
modernization chance stayed at 0% no matter how high the budget went. The
enterprise earned revenue in the millions per turn, but its score never moved.

R&D spend was gated on `liquidCapital`: a turn only advanced modernization if the
corp held enough cash to pay for it. But an SOE's operating profit is remitted to
the treasury each turn and is never banked as corp cash, so `liquidCapital` sits
at ~0 in steady state. The gate could therefore never pass for a normally
operating enterprise. The only corps that modernized were ones still draining a
seeded starting balance, and they would have silently stopped once it ran out.

### The fix

Modernization is funded from the enterprise's own revenue, which is what the
mechanic always intended: spend is capped at a fraction of the corp's per-turn
revenue, so it is affordable from operations by construction. The cost is charged
as an operating expense in the same estimate that drives both the treasury
remittance and the budget page's State Enterprises line, so the treasury funds
R&D by remitting less. It never touches `liquidCapital`.

### Changes

- `buildRdModernizationOps` drops the `liquidCapital` affordability gate and the
  liquid debit. A funded SOE advances its score regardless of cash on hand;
  spend is still capped at revenue and still decays to zero when unfunded.
- `estimateNationalizedOperatingIncome` subtracts the R&D charge as an operating
  cost, so remittance and the budget line both reflect it with one source of
  truth. The charge is capped identically to the turn math.

### State enterprises no longer pay tax on top of remitting profit (ticket #1072)

The same reporter noticed his defense enterprise withdrawing far more than it kept:
"it withdraws 115k, but after taxes it is just less than 50k." He was right.

A state-owned enterprise was charged corporate income tax like any private firm,
**and** remitted a share of its profit to the same treasury each turn. That is one
profit taken twice by a single owner. Because the operating-loss backstop
deliberately does not cover tax-driven holes, the drained enterprises did not just
sit at zero — several went negative and stayed there (East German Agriculture at
around −30,000, Chemical, Media, Healthcare, and Entertainment all below zero).

State enterprises now pay no corporate income tax. The profit remittance is the
state's return on an industry it already owns; taxing it on top was double-dipping.
Both the enterprise's cash debit and the government's tax-revenue credit are
zeroed in the same place, so the treasury simply receives its take as remittance
rather than as tax — no phantom revenue line, no reconciliation gap. Private
corporations are unaffected.

### Private-bank loans check the bank vault and income, not corp cash (ticket #1085)

Named loans used to cap a corporation at 50% of its own `liquidCapital` (and a
character at 25% of personal cash). That is the holding company's treasury, and
it is the opposite of why people borrow. After the ring-fence the bank's money
is `bankCharter.cashReserves`, a different number.

Origination now takes the lowest of: the bank's cash reserves, leftover deposit
headroom, and 35% of demonstrated per-turn income after existing private-bank
payments (stricter than the central-bank LOC's 70% DTI). The hub form prints
that maximum and says it is not bond issuance headroom, which is what made a
$95M Hunt Oil application look like it should have cleared.

## Reclaim a caretaker-run corporation; bank deposits, borrowing, and loan approval

Fixes tickets #1074 and #1088.

### What was broken

A player handed day-to-day operation of their corporation to an NPP caretaker,
which the feature says you can reclaim at any time, and then could not find the
button to reclaim it. Installing the caretaker made the CEO Office tab disappear
entirely, and the "Resume Control" button lives inside that tab.

The CEO tab is shown when the viewer is the corp's CEO. That check compared the
viewer against the _resolved_ CEO, which during caretaker rule is the NPP, not
the owner. Installing a caretaker sets `ceoType:"npp"` and points `ceoId` at the
NPP, so `isCeo` went false and the whole tab (and the reclaim button with it)
vanished. The owner was left with no in-game way to take the corporation back,
even though they still held `userId`, every share, and full server-side
authority to reclaim.

### The fix

The corp detail now surfaces the caretaker's underlying owner, and the page
treats that owner as CEO for tab visibility. So the CEO Office tab stays
reachable for the appointing owner throughout caretaker rule, and the "Resume
Control" button is where it always was.

While fixing this, we also added a reappointment cooldown: after you reclaim a
corporation you must wait 3 days (72 turns) before handing it to a caretaker
again. This stops a corporation from flipping between owner and caretaker
turn-to-turn to game whichever operator is momentarily better. The card explains
the wait and disables the hand-off button until the cooldown ends; the server
rejects an early hand-off with a 400.

### Changes

- `corporationDetail` returns `caretakerUnderlyingCharacterId` and
  `caretakerReappointCooldownTurnsRemaining`. The corporation page's `isCeo`
  gate now also matches the caretaker's underlying owner, so the CEO Office tab
  no longer disappears when a caretaker is installed.
- `dismissCaretakerCeo` stamps `caretakerCooldownUntilTurn` (turn +
  `CARETAKER_REAPPOINT_COOLDOWN_TURNS`, 72). `validateCaretakerAppointment`
  rejects a hand-off while that cooldown is active with a new `reclaim-cooldown`
  error. `CaretakerCeoCard` disables the hand-off button and shows the hours
  remaining.

### Banking from the bank's own page (ticket #1088)

- **Deposit and borrow in place.** A finance corporation that runs a bank now
  shows deposit and borrow controls on its own corporation page, for any viewer
  who is not the CEO. Depositing routes that currency's savings to this bank so
  it earns the bank's deposit rate; borrowing requests a personal loan in the
  bank's currency. Both previously lived only on the separate `/banking` hub.
- **Opt-in loan approval.** A bank CEO can switch the bank to require approval.
  With it on, new loan requests land as **pending** and the CEO approves or
  declines each from the loan book; the borrower is notified either way. With it
  off, loans auto-grant as before. Pending loans move no money and do not count
  against the loan book until approved, and are re-checked against headroom and
  the blacklist at the moment of approval.
- **Your funds are listed.** The sponsoring corporation's page now lists the
  index funds it sponsors, with a link to each. A chartered fund was previously
  invisible to its owner once the charter session ended.
- **Bank / fund separation.** A corporation may run a bank or sponsor an index
  fund, but not both. Enforced when chartering the second one; corporations that
  already hold both are unaffected.

## Small corporations can issue bonds again

Fixes ticket #1083.

### What was broken

Corporate bond issuance had a flat minimum (₳100,000) but a corp-scaled maximum:
you may not carry more than 2x your equity in debt, so the largest bond a small
corporation could issue was `2 x equity - existing debt`. When that ceiling fell
below ₳100,000 — a corp with roughly ₳48,500 of equity has a ₳97,000 ceiling —
the valid range was empty. Every amount at or above ₳100,000 failed the maximum,
and every amount below ₳100,000 failed the minimum, so the corporation could not
issue bonds at all. Neither threshold was era- or inflation-scaled, so this hit
small corporations hardest in early-era worlds where nominal values are low.

### The fix

The minimum now clamps to the corporation's own ceiling: the effective minimum
is `min(₳100,000, the corp's issuance headroom)`. A corp capped at ₳97,000 can
now issue up to ₳97,000, and larger corporations keep the ₳100,000 floor. Below
a small dust floor (₳10,000 of headroom) bonds are shown as unavailable with a
"too small to issue bonds yet" message rather than an impossible range.

### Changes

- `effectiveBondIssuanceWindow` (`src/lib/constants/bonds.ts`) is the single
  source of truth for the issuance window: `maxAllowed = min(per-issuance cap,
2x-equity headroom)`, `effectiveMin = min(MIN_BOND_ISSUANCE, maxAllowed)`, and
  an availability flag below `BOND_ISSUANCE_MIN_HEADROOM`.
- `POST /api/corporations/[id]/bonds` enforces the clamped minimum and the dust
  floor; `GET` returns `maxAllowedIssuance`, `minIssuance`, and `bondsAvailable`.
- The Bonds tab uses the server's effective minimum instead of a hardcoded
  ₳100,000, and disables issuance with a clear message when unavailable.

## Banks can only upstream real equity, and only anchor deposits they can back

### What was broken

A chartered bank keeps its cash in `bankCharter.cashReserves`, separate from its
holding company's `liquidCapital`. The owner can move surplus cash up to the
holding company through the bank's "upstream" action.

The cap on that move was the bank's reserves above its reserve requirement
(`deposits x reserveRatio`). That is the wrong ceiling. Reserves sitting above
the reserve requirement are still mostly depositor money, not the owner's. A
bank with a large deposit base therefore reported a large "surplus" the owner
could withdraw, even when the bank owed far more to depositors than it held in
cash. Borrowed cash (interbank, central-bank margin line, discount window)
inflated reserves the same way while being a liability the bank still had to
repay.

### What changed

Upstreaming is now capped at the lower of two figures:

- the reserves above the reserve requirement (unchanged), and
- the bank's book equity: cash plus outstanding loans, minus deposits and minus
  borrowed cash (interbank, central-bank margin, discount window).

Book equity is what is genuinely the owner's after depositors and lenders are
made whole. A bank whose deposits and borrowings exceed its cash-plus-loans has
zero equity and can upstream nothing, regardless of how far its reserves sit
above the requirement. The same ceiling is re-checked atomically inside the
write, so a stale read cannot slip a payout past it, and the bank page now shows
the corrected upstream capacity.

### Deposit base tied to capital

The NPC household deposits a bank attracts were sized purely from its financial
sector capacity, with no reference to the bank's capital. A bank with a large
financial sector but little or no capital could therefore anchor a deposit base
worth billions that it had nothing to stand behind.

The deposit ceiling is now the lower of the capacity figure and a leverage
multiple of book equity. A well-capitalized bank (whether its capital is posted
contributions or retained earnings) is unaffected; a bank with little or negative
equity has its ceiling pulled down toward zero, and the normal per-turn deposit
outflow then unwinds the excess gradually rather than all at once.

## See how close a nation is to joining your bloc

Fixes ticket #1091.

### What was confusing

A player watched a nation sit above 60 percent of the Western pole for a day and
never join NATO, and reasonably asked whether joining was automatic or bugged.
Neither. Reaching 60 percent is only the first of several steps, and every step
after it was hidden.

To join a bloc, a non-player nation has to hold at or above the 60 percent join
share for a sustained run of 24 turns. Only then does it file a membership
application, and the bloc's existing members still vote on it. The turn engine
tracks that run in a `joinReadySince` stamp that resets the moment the share dips
back below the gate. Nothing in the game surfaced it. The one screen that could
have shown a live "held 21 of 24 turns" counter showed it only for members
drifting toward the exit, never for a nation climbing toward accession.

### The fix

The Influence tab now reads the same sustain clock the turn engine keeps and
shows it where a player is already looking at a courted nation.

- The nation dossier states the countdown in plain terms: "It has held above the
  60 for 21/24 turns. If it holds, it applies to join in 3 more turns, then the
  members vote it in. Drop it back below the 60 and the clock resets." Once the
  run completes, it says the application is in and the members' vote now decides.
- In "Closest to joining," a nation over the gate now reads "applies in 3" rather
  than a bare "eligible," so the roster answers "when," not just "yes."

### Changes

- `loadOrgInfluence` computes a `joinCountdown` per courted nation from
  `joinReadySince` and the current turn, gated to non-members that are actually
  on the accession path. A player-run nation never auto-applies, so it shows no
  countdown that would never fire.
- `NationDossier` and `NationsInPlay` render the countdown. No mechanic changed:
  this only surfaces state the turn engine was already keeping.

## Sector worker counts read at a believable scale

### What changed

The Workers figure on a sector page was tied to revenue at a rate that made an
entire regional industry look like it employed a few hundred people. A top
sector pulling hundreds of millions a day reported around 600 workers, which
read as far too small for a whole industry in a whole region.

The headcount now scales one hundred times higher, so the same sector reads in
the tens of thousands and a busy state's industries add up to a believable
labour force. Capacity build previews use the same anchor, so the "workers this
capacity needs" figure moves up in step and still matches the sector total.

### What did not change

Nothing in the economy moves. The worker count is a display figure: labour cost
is driven by revenue, wage and automation effects use the headcount only as a
relative weight (so the scale cancels out), and national employment is never
built from these numbers. Wages, margins, strike thresholds, dividends, and
prices are all exactly as before. Existing sectors pick up the new figure on the
next turn with no migration.

## Corporation money reads per turn by default

### What changed

Money on the corporation pages was shown per financial day (24 turns), which
made it hard to line a figure up with what actually happens on a single turn.
The default view is now **per turn**, labelled `/turn`. The money toggle still
offers Day and Year, so nothing is lost, and the default is what most players
were mentally converting to anyway.

This covers the whole corporation surface: the Financials tab and its summary
band, the Overview and CEO budget and production views, the sectors list, the
bonds panel, the expand-market screen, and the deep per-sector financials. On
the per-sector Financials panel each row shows the per-turn amount with the
daily figure kept in its tooltip, so you can still see both.

### What did not change

This is a labelling change. The stored figures, wages, margins, growth costs,
taxes, and every underlying calculation are exactly as before; the display just
divides the daily rate by the 24 turns in a financial day. Growth rates are
still shown per game year, and physical capacity is still shown per day for now.

## The sectors list shows how many plants you own

### What changed

On the corporation Operations tab, the sectors list gave each sector's capacity
in output units but not the number of actual plants behind it, so there was no
way to see at a glance whether a sector was one large plant or a dozen small
ones. That count now appears as a line under the capacity number on every row,
using the right word for the industry (plants, stores, mines, and so on), and it
shows in the mobile card view too.

The number is derived from capacity the same way the per-sector Plant panel
already derives it, so the list and the detail page always agree. No data or
economy change: this is a readout that was missing.

## A revoked bank charter returns the bank's money

Fixes ticket #1093.

### What was wrong

A supervised revoke and an ordinary revoke did two different things with the
bank's money, and only one of them was right.

The ordinary revoke path (`revokeCharter`) returns the bank's whole cash balance
to the corporation once its depositors are paid off, and archives the charter.
The supervisory revoke, which fires when a bank stays below the minimum capital
requirement past its recapitalization deadline, did not call that path. It only
stamped the charter `revoked`. The bank's ring-fenced `cashReserves` were left
stranded, and for an investment bank, whose value sits in its proprietary
trading book rather than in deposits, the entire book was written off with the
licence.

For the reporter that meant a charter revoke erased about 1.2M of value that was
never at risk to any depositor: the owner was trading their own money, missed a
capital deadline, and lost the money instead of the licence.

### What changed

Supervisory revoke now flattens the proprietary book back into the bank's cash
at its last marked value (the mark the solvency pass already computed this turn,
so it is an orderly close-out, not a fresh hit to the market), then calls the
same `revokeCharter` path as every other revoke. That path refunds the full cash
balance to the corporation when deposits are zero, zeroes the ring-fenced
reserves, and archives the charter.

Banks that still owe depositors are unchanged: they revoke without a shareholder
refund, exactly as before, because the depositors are paid first.

A `bank_prop_trade_sell` transaction records the book close-out so the money
trail is complete.

## National Corporation profit page shows the real remittance (ticket #1072)

The National Corporation overview labelled a "treasury remittance" that was
actually the corporation's full operating profit. The same number appeared as
"operating profit" in one tile and as "remittance" in another. It applied
neither the CEO's retention split nor the on-hand-cash cap, so it disagreed with
the state-enterprise line on the budget page: a corporation could show a large
profit here yet contribute little or nothing to the budget, with no way to see
why. A player running an SOE spent a long support thread unable to reconcile the
numbers for exactly this reason.

The overview now shows the real path from operating profit to the budget: profit
across held sectors, the share the CEO retains as working capital, the share
remitted (capped at the corporation's cash on hand), assumed-debt service, and
the net that reaches the treasury. The remitted figure uses the same
`cappedRemittanceLocal` calculation the turn engine settles against, so the page
and the budget line now agree. When a corporation's cash caps its remittance
below its profit share, the page says so instead of showing a bare number. This
is a display fix only; the money the turn actually remitted was already correct.

## Union pages show total membership, not just per-employer density

A union's page listed a Unionization percentage for each sector it stood in, but
never rolled those up. There was no way to read how many workers the union
actually represented, or what share of its whole industry that was. "Strength"
looked like the closest thing, but that is banked organizing muscle for the
leadership contest and ballot weighting, not a headcount, so a big shallow union
and a small deep one could read the same.

The stats strip now carries a Members figure: the unionized headcount summed
across every sector the union stands in, with the density (that count as a share
of the total workforce) underneath. Each sector row also gains a Workers column,
so the per-employer Unionization percentage now has a base to sit against. Both
numbers come from data the sector documents already held (`workers` and the
per-sector `unionization` rate); this surfaces them and computes the rollup, and
changes nothing about how membership or bargaining works. The label reads as
coverage, kept distinct from Strength so raw size is not mistaken for power.

## National Corporation holdings use the sector money clock (ticket #1072 follow-up)

The National Corporation Holdings tab labelled each sector's stored daily
revenue and operating profit as `/turn`. The sector detail page correctly used
the financial-day basis, but mixed its daily totals, per-turn detail and
per-unit averages under short labels. That made two views of the same sector
look irreconcilable and encouraged players to read sector profit as the amount
sent to the treasury.

Holdings now labels both figures per financial day, and the SOE versus private
comparison uses the same basis. The sector money panel explicitly separates
average revenue and cost per unit produced, daily total revenue, daily sector
operating profit, per-turn revenue and game-year revenue. State-owned sectors
also explain that sector operating profit is not treasury remittance and link
back to the corporation's budget flow. Every holding gains a direct Open sector
link, removing the build-dialog navigation workaround described in the ticket.
No economy math or persisted value changed.

## Officer terms end when nobody stands (ticket #1100)

A national party leadership cycle that closed with no winner left every officer
exactly where they were. The resolver logged "no candidates, position unchanged"
and moved on, so an officer who never re-stood kept the office indefinitely and
the party had no way to remove them through the election mechanic. Observed on a
UK party treasurer: two consecutive treasurer cycles drew zero candidacies and
the incumbent simply carried over.

A mandate is now renewed only by standing. When a cycle closes with no winner,
the resolver checks whether the incumbent was among the candidates. If they were
not, the seat is vacated, the former holder gets a
`national_leadership_removed` notice explaining why, and the empty seat is
picked up by the usual missing-election sweep so the next cycle opens
immediately. If the incumbent did stand, nothing changes: an uncontested or
unvoted race is not a repudiation, and they keep the seat.

Chair vacancies follow the existing rules. The led coalition's
`chairCharacterId` is cleared through the same chair-change sync used by the
elected and auto-vacate paths, and the vice-chair inherits chair authority under
the acting-chair rule. Vice-chair and treasurer seats simply open up. A holder
who won a different office in the same batch is skipped, since the cross-office
auto-vacate has already cleared and notified that seat.

This applies from the next cycle to close. Officers currently seated are not
touched retroactively.
