## Added

- **Economic assessments.** Coverage in the economic domain reads out in the same
  four steps as the other two. Below the threshold you learn nothing. Past it you
  learn whether there is a corporate sector worth the name. Past that you get
  estimated company counts and capital. At the top you get the real figures.
- **Leaking a company's books.** A successful covert action in the economic
  domain puts one of the target country's listed companies fully in the open. For
  the next while anyone looking at that company sees its real financials rather
  than the fogged quarterly estimate outsiders normally get. The operation picks
  the largest listed company it can reach, and never touches a private one, whose
  books were never on show to begin with.
- **What happens next is the market's business.** The leak moves no number by
  itself. It changes what traders can read, and they do the rest.

## Notes

- The national picture and one company's books are deliberately separate. No
  amount of coverage quietly opens every set of accounts in a country; reading a
  specific company takes an operation aimed at it.
- Production sabotage and industrial theft are not built. Both would move real
  numbers in a live economy, and how far they should move is a balance question
  that has to be measured before it can be answered.
