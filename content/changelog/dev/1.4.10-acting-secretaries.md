  An acting appointment existed to stop a department going dark while the
  Senate got round to a confirmation vote, but it handed over the whole
  office. An acting secretary now runs the department day to day and leaves
  the decisions that outlast the appointment to whoever the Senate confirms.
  Each one on the books costs half a point of national approval, and that
  cost is now visible on the rating instead of silently applied.

Cut from development. From suggestion #315.

## Changed

- An acting secretary can no longer change their department's stance. Policy
  direction is the thing a confirmation vote decides, so it waits for one.

- An acting Secretary of Defense can no longer commission or dismiss
  commanders, adopt doctrine, move the nuclear programme, or award and cancel
  defence contracts. All of these outlive the appointment: a commission stands
  until it is revoked, a contract obligates money across a full procurement
  window, and a test cannot be taken back.

- An acting secretary can no longer open, close or expand estates, power
  plants or infrastructure projects, run debt operations, or reset the
  sovereign bond profile. An acting secretary funds what already exists; the
  confirmed secretary decides what exists.

- Everything a vacancy actually costs the country stays open. Ministerial
  orders, moving and assigning units, declaring and withdrawing offensives,
  command and formation structure, reinforcement mode, recruiting, funding
  levels and splitting an already-appropriated budget all still work. That is
  the reason to make an acting appointment in the first place.

- A locked control now says so where it sits, and names confirmation as what
  unlocks it, rather than looking available and failing when pressed.

## Fixed

- The approval cost of acting appointments now appears on the national rating
  as its own line. It has always been charged at half a point each, but it was
  applied after the rating was assembled, so a president could seat four
  acting secretaries, watch approval fall two points, and find nothing on the
  page accounting for it. An empty cabinet's penalty was invisible the same
  way and is now shown too.

- An acting appointment no longer clears the department's setting cooldowns.
  Those cooldowns belong to the position rather than to whoever holds it, so
  clearing them on every acting appointment let a president reset one at will
  by seating a caretaker and then confirming who they wanted all along.

- An acting secretary is now seated with a full ministerial action pool, like
  every other appointment. The acting path was the one route that skipped it.
