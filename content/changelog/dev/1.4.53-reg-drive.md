## Fixed

The voter registration drive did nothing. It could only convert a state's
unregistered and independent voters, and every one of those pools had been at
zero for a long time — in the United States since turn 155, Russia 176, the
United Kingdom 143. Parties kept funding drives and kept getting nothing back.
Drives now draw on the pool first and then, when it cannot cover the rest, on
the rolls of parties holding more registration than their organisation supports.
A party that funds a drive can no longer fund it out of its own surplus, and a
drive that finds nothing to convert still costs nothing.

Registration that lapsed used to pass entirely to rival parties, and only
reached the unregistered pool in a state where no party had any organisation at
all. That made the pool a one-way valve: registration drained out and never
came back, so every state trended toward a fiction in which every single voter
is registered to a party. Half of all lapsing registration now returns to the
pool.

Alaska and Hawaii were admitted as states but never finished being set up.
Admission matched parties by abbreviation and gave up the moment one did not
match, which happened as soon as a party had been renamed since the world was
seeded — so it stopped partway, after creating the state's party chapters but
before creating its registration pool. Without that pool both states sat outside
the registration system entirely: not moving slowly, not moving at all. Neither
could the District of Columbia, which is never admitted and so was never
reached. Admission now matches a renamed party by its name, and one unrecognised
party no longer halts the rest of the work.

## Changed

Registration in a contested state moves roughly three times faster for a party
that pays for it, which brings a determined campaign inside the intended window
for shifting a stronghold rather than well beyond it. Parties with wide
organisation lose a little of what they used to collect automatically as rivals'
registration lapsed; that share now goes back to the unregistered pool, where
anyone willing to campaign for it can reach it.
