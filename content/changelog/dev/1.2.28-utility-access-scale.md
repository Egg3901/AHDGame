Cut from development. Raised on ticket #1129.

## Fixed

- Grid reliability is a percentage that in practice only ever moves between
  about 97 and 100, because a national grid that is down more than three
  percent of the time is a national emergency. The conversion that turns a
  change in that percentage into a change on the 0-100 political board was
  spreading it over that narrow realistic window, which multiplied every effect
  roughly thirty-five times. A grid crisis authored to shave a few points of
  uptime per turn arrived on the board as a swing larger than the whole scale
  and pushed the family to the floor on its first turn. Any single effect on
  any board family is now limited to a bend rather than a takeover, matching
  the limits the economic and legislative channels already respect.
- Energy estates could only ever damage their own region's grid, no matter what
  they built. The mechanic compared a 0-100 quality score for the plant mix
  against the grid's own uptime percentage, so the gap was negative for every
  possible mix and the effect sat at its maximum penalty every turn, in every
  region with plants. Both sides now use the same units, so a firm and diverse
  mix raises reliability and an all-intermittent one lowers it.
- The board and the underlying metric now translate between each other using
  the same era-appropriate range in both directions. A 1953 grid was being
  measured against a modern standard on the way in and an early Cold War
  standard on the way out, which made the causal channel a constant instead of
  a signal.

## Not changed

- Existing region values are untouched by this change on its own. Regions
  already sitting at the extremes recover through the normal per-turn drift
  once the effects pushing them there are corrected.
