## Fixed

- A supply agreement is additive. The contracted volume is reserved for the
  buyer and guaranteed-sold; everything a supplier produces above the contract
  still clears on the open market like any other unit. Selling percentage now
  reflects real total sales, contracted plus open-market fill, never
  `contracted / produced`.
- Removed the exclusive offer cap. An "exclusive" agreement used to withhold all
  output above the contracted volume from the market entirely, at any price, in
  every country's book. A supplier that contracted a small share of a large
  output was pinned to a few percent fill across all of its sectors, including
  ones in countries the buyer could never receive from. That cap is gone.

## Changed

- The supply-agreement form drops the "exclusive" toggle and states plainly that
  the volume cap is units per day reserved for the buyer, with the rest still
  sold on the open market. Existing agreements keep delivering their contracted
  volume and now sell their surplus normally.
