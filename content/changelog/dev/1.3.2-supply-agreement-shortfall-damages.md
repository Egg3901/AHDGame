Cut from development.

## Fixed

- Supply agreement shortfall damages were measured against the contract's
  volume cap, which is validated at signing against your plants' **nameplate**
  capacity. Actual production sits below nameplate whenever inputs run short,
  a strike bites, a disaster lands, or the demand throttle pulls a plant back
  in a glutted market. The gap between the two was charged at half the market
  value of every missing unit, every turn, on a contract that was perfectly
  legal when it was signed. Suppliers had no way to close it and no way to see
  it: one corporation watched the same profit get debited back out every turn
  and reported it as their cash refusing to accumulate.

  Damages are now clamped to what the plants could genuinely have made. The
  levers you control are deliberately excluded from that ceiling, so turning
  the production policy down or mothballing a sector still owes the full
  contracted volume.

## Added

- Contract rows tell you when you fell short, by how much, and which of the two
  causes it was: a contract larger than your plants can supply, or your own
  production being throttled back. The first asks you to resize the deal, the
  second to turn output back up.
- The volume cap field now states, where you pick the number, that a cap is a
  commitment rather than a ceiling, and that missing it costs half the value of
  every unit you fall short by.

## Changed

- The supply agreements wiki page explains that damages track achievable output
  rather than the contracted figure, and advises sizing a cap to what your
  plants reliably make rather than to their best possible turn.
