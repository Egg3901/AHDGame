Cut from development.

## Fixed

- Supply agreement shortfall damages were measured against the contract's
  volume cap, which is validated at signing against your plants' **nameplate**
  capacity. Actual production sits below nameplate whenever inputs run short,
  a strike bites, a disaster lands, or the demand throttle pulls a plant back
  in a glutted market. The gap between the two was charged at half the market
  value of every missing unit, every turn, on a contract that was perfectly
  legal when it was signed. Suppliers had no way to close it and no way to see
  it: one corporation watched the same profit get debited back out every turn
  and reported it as their cash refusing to accumulate.

  Damages are now clamped to what the plants could genuinely have made. The
  levers you control are deliberately excluded from that ceiling, so turning
  the production policy down or mothballing a sector still owes the full
  contracted volume.

- When a supplier could not fill all of its agreements, the settlement's
  maximum-flow pass gave the first stored agreement everything it could and
  could leave the next agreement at zero. The same set of contracts could
  produce different deliveries when database row order changed. Scarce output
  is now shared proportionally by each agreement's demand-capped commitment,
  while still respecting every buyer's real consumption and preserving the
  maximum amount the network can deliver.
- Settlement used the corporation balance loaded before the rest of the turn.
  A supplier that earned cash earlier in the turn could still be treated as
  insolvent when damages fired, while a supplier that had spent cash could be
  charged against money it no longer held. Settlement now refreshes the live
  balances immediately before applying the contract cash legs.

## Added

- Contract rows tell you when you fell short, by how much, and which of the two
  causes it was: a contract larger than your plants can supply, or your own
  production being throttled back. The first asks you to resize the deal, the
  second to turn output back up.
- The volume cap field now states, where you pick the number, that a cap is a
  commitment rather than a ceiling, and that missing it costs half the value of
  every unit you fall short by.
- Buyer commodity cards explain why delivery can be lower than the cap and
  that a short supplier's output is divided across active agreements.
- Media sector pages now show commodity-ledger output beneath the run meter.
  The run meter measures total audience activity, while advertising contracts
  use the smaller quantity that reaches the commodity ledger.
- Commodity history marks the point where its calculation changed from a
  revenue estimate to measured plant output. A visible step at that marker is
  identified as a reporting correction instead of looking like vanished
  production.
- Per-commodity cards state that Net means the corporation's own output minus
  its own input use, not the global stockpile or a world shortage.

## Changed

- The supply agreements wiki page explains that damages track achievable output
  rather than the contracted figure, and advises sizing a cap to what your
  plants reliably make rather than to their best possible turn.
