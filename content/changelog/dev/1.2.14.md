Cut from development. Raised on ticket #1134.

## Fixed

- A lot's price band had a floor set by what the materiel cost to build and a
  ceiling set by a share of national output. Those are not the same kind of
  number, and on the live world they sat about two hundred thousand times
  apart: an air lot that cost 1,091 to produce could be contracted at
  383,748,809. Since a delivery pays the supplier the difference between the
  price and the cost, a contract written near that ceiling was not a purchase
  at all. It was the defence budget moving into one company's bank account.
- Both ends of the band are now set by the same thing: production cost. A
  contract must clear cost by at least 12% so nobody is made to build at a
  loss, and it may not exceed cost by more than 100% so no order can be turned
  into a private cash tap. Leaving the price blank now quotes a 35% margin,
  which is a fair deal rather than either extreme.
- The economy-wide anchor survives as a hard upper limit only. A country whose
  economy cannot bear the cost-based price still pays the lower of the two, so
  a poor buyer is never quoted a rich buyer's number.

## Not changed

- Contracts already awarded keep their agreed price. A contract records its
  price when it is signed and delivery bills against that record, so no
  supplier has a deal repriced under them after the fact. The new band governs
  new awards.
- The limit on how much a country may put under contract in a window, and the
  per-turn payout limit added in 1.2.10, are both untouched and still apply.
- The emergency procurement freeze remains available as a switch.

## What this means at the board

- A defence plant still makes real money on state orders. On the live world's
  numbers a supplier taking the default quote earns roughly 380 per lot on a
  1,091 lot, and a plant runs hundreds of lots a turn, so an arms programme is
  a solid line of business. It is simply a business now, not a windfall.
- Defence budgets buy far more materiel per unit of money than they did. That
  is the point. A country that spent its whole quarterly procurement allowance
  and received twenty-three lots of equipment for it was being charged for an
  army and delivered a warehouse.
