Cut from development.

This release folds twenty six patches (1.1.13 through 1.1.38) into one entry.
Individual dates and ticket references are dropped; the substance of every
change is kept below.

## Economy and trade

Most of this release is one connected arc: prices, scarcity, and NPC
decision-making moving from a single worldwide aggregate to each country's
own reachable trade book, plus the planned economy bloc being closed off from
the open world.

- **Tax rate slider bills now actually change the rate.** Tax laws that set
  their rate via a slider dropped that rate during enactment, so a passed VAT
  or customs bill collected nothing even though it showed as signed. Enactment
  now carries the slider rate through; countries whose bills passed while this
  was broken need the rate corrected once, or the bill re-passed.
- **Producer costs pass through to prices.** Commodity prices came from
  supply and demand alone, so an input cost squeeze (fertilizer, plastics and
  freight all trading well above base) could push a whole sector class
  permanently negative with no price path out. Producer cost pressure now
  lifts the commodity's own price, damped and capped so gluts still clear.
- **Share price discount rate is smoothed.** Share valuations discounted
  earnings by the spot central bank rate, so every FOMC meeting whipsawed
  every listed company's valuation within the hour. Valuation now discounts by
  a smoothed multi-turn average; loans and bond coupons still reprice on spot.
- **Prices come from the market you can reach.** The wide leg of every state
  price was a planet-wide aggregate, so supply that could never cross a border
  (6.3M units of untraded Eastern bloc food) still dragged prices down
  everywhere, including in food-short Western countries. It now prices each
  country's own reachable trade book.
- **The iron curtain closes.** Planned economies (the USSR, China, and
  Eastern Europe) now trade only among themselves, zero affinity across the
  curtain in both directions, membership taken straight from the historical
  marketization schedule. Inside the curtain, administered pricing now
  governs the whole state price, so the bloc runs on the plan instead of
  cratering under its own surplus.
- **NPCs answer shortages in every industry.** NPP strategy switching used to
  exist only for extraction (mining) sectors, so no NPC could ever respond to
  a shortage in a chemical, logistics or other strategy-produced commodity.
  The re-strategize pass now covers every sector type; chemical plants began
  retooling toward fertilizer immediately.
- **NPCs build critically short single-source inputs regardless of their own
  industry.** Freight is a recipe input to six sector types but made only by
  logistics, so a freight shortage had no builder pool unless an NPC happened
  to already be a logistics firm. A tier-0 override now lets any cash-healthy
  NPC found a critically short essential input outside its own type. The 1953
  US logistics seed weight was also raised from 4 to 8.
- **Scarcity, margins and realized prices all move to the reachable
  market.** Three remaining consumers of price data (the scarcity-drift
  integrator, sector margin modifiers, and clearing's price-realization
  factor) still read the worldwide aggregate even after clearing and the wide
  price leg were partitioned. All three now read each country's own reachable
  book.
- **NPCs retool bleeding sectors instead of riding them down.** A sector
  losing money on expensive inputs had no way out: mothballing destroys
  capacity and nothing ever changed the recipe. NPC-run sectors with sustained
  negative margins now re-score every available strategy against real
  reachable-market prices and switch to the best one.
- **Profitable NPCs chase shortages too.** The prior retooling only fired for
  bleeding sectors, missing the biggest shortage-relief opportunities sitting
  inside sectors that were already comfortably profitable. A second, higher
  advantage bar now lets a profitable sector retool when the payoff is large.
- **Plants buy inputs at home-market prices.** The physical input bill still
  priced every purchase at the world price ratio, the last wire still reading
  the global aggregate. Inputs are now billed at the sector's own country's
  reachable price level, falling back to the world ratio only where no
  reachable book exists.
- **Auto-retool keeps operating costs honest and reads the local market.**
  NPC auto-retool used to rescale a plant's size without moving its
  calibrated operating-cost residual, inventing a large extra bill on the
  next turn. It also scored miners on world prices instead of the local
  market. Both now match the behavior the player command already had.
  Player-run sectors are still never auto-retooled.

## Corporations and finance

- **Defence contracts stop discarding output the ministry can't yet pay
  for.** A defence plant that finished a lot on a turn the government could
  not afford had that lot thrown away, so small suppliers sat at zero
  delivered forever. Built output is now banked and ships once funding
  arrives.
- **Corporation income reads the same on every page.** The masthead and
  Overview tab could show a profitable corporation as losing money while the
  Financials page correctly showed a profit, because of a double-counted
  dividend and a stale projected figure. All three surfaces now share one
  income basis, and the market-cap tile explains why a debt-funded expansion
  can lower valuation even while revenue rises.
- **The production adjustment slider now moves real output.** Throttling a
  plant's production changed the money reported but not the actual tonnage,
  and the units it claimed to cut were removed a second time on the way to
  market, so a throttled plant built goods it could never sell and paid to
  hold them. The slider now gates production once, on the curve the panel
  advertises.
- **Acquisition search finds player-run companies missing a CEO-type
  flag.** Older player-founded companies whose CEO-type field was never
  written were invisible to the acquisition search even with a human CEO able
  to accept an offer. They are now treated as player-run, matching how
  onboarding and stat allocation already read a missing field.
- **The corporate income statement adds up.** The income waterfall left out
  wages and regulatory compliance while still reporting a bottom line that
  included them, so the numbers on screen could not be reconciled by reading
  them. The total is now derived from the visible rows, with any unexplained
  remainder shown as an explicit Other line instead of hidden. The dividend
  tile now shows the effective rate actually charged, including
  legal-structure minimums.
- **Corporation money screens report the engine's real margins.** The corp
  page and the credit system priced every sector on a recomputed theoretical
  margin instead of the one the engine actually applied, so loss-making
  sectors could display as strong earners and balance sheets could overstate
  sector value by millions. Both now read the engine's stored margin, and
  bond collateral is priced the same way.
- **The sector page uses the same margin as the corp page.** The sector
  detail page had its own separate margin computation, so a sector could show
  opposite signs of profitability one tap apart from the corporation page. It
  now derives from the same stored engine margin.
- **Borrowers are not priced as bond funds.** The share-price formula
  measured bond reliance as gross coupons against net earnings, so a
  leveraged operator paying more interest than it received had its real
  operating earnings clamped toward zero and took the maximum bond-fund
  valuation penalty. Reliance is now measured on net coupon income, coupons
  received minus interest paid.
- **Unlocked tech is worth more on the balance sheet.** The value booked for
  a corporation's unlocked tech-tree research rises 4.5x, so R&D-heavy firms
  carry their research investment at a weight closer to what it actually
  cost, lifting reported assets, book value, and the tangible-book floor under
  share price.
- **Build in any sector.** Founding a new sector is no longer restricted to a
  corporation's primary and secondary industries. Off-type sectors are
  buildable at the existing margin penalty; primary and secondary types still
  show their bonus badge first in the build picker.
- **Corporate tax goes consolidated.** Corporate tax was assessed per sector
  on that sector's positive income in isolation, so a diversified
  corporation's losses offset nothing, and one live company owed eight times
  its consolidated revenue in tax. Tax is now assessed on a consolidated
  basis: sector losses net against sector profits before anything is taxed.

## Interface and fixes

- **Acquisition search explains why a company is not listed.** A search that
  matched nothing, or an ineligible target, rendered a silent empty box. It
  now states plainly that only player-run private companies can be
  acquisition targets.
- **The favorability boost card flags charisma scaling.** The NPP "Boost
  favorability" card always advertised a flat +3, but the applied gain has
  always scaled with the acting character's charisma. The card now says so.
- **Commodity page no longer crashes on stale state-country data.** A single
  stale or removed country mapping in the server's state-to-country data could
  crash the whole commodity market page. The page now validates the mapping
  and falls back to the active country instead of erroring out.
- **The relocate button explains its cooldown instead of going dead.** After
  relocating once, the button went hard-disabled for the full 72-turn
  cooldown with the only explanation living in a tooltip that never appears
  on touch devices. It now stays tappable, shows the remaining wait, and
  opens a short dialog explaining it. The pre-relocation confirmation dialog
  also now warns about the cooldown up front.
- **The bill fiscal line reads in the same direction as the bill.** A
  provision's fiscal summary listed the proposed cost before the current one,
  the reverse of the Current Law to Proposed cards directly above it, and
  called the result "Net change" without saying net change of what. Because
  the provision card measures the treasury delta while the bill rollup
  measures the change in cost, the same bill could show a red minus on one and
  a green plus on the other. The pair now reads current to proposed, and both
  figures say what they measure.
- **Votes stop dragging your ideology to the centre.** A bill provision that
  took no position on an axis was stored with a literal 0, and the vote-time
  policy shift read that 0 as a real centre target. Every vote on a bill that
  only moved the economic axis nudged the voter's social position toward
  Moderate, and the other way round, so a character's stated ideology eroded
  through ordinary voting. Provisions now leave an unaddressed axis out
  entirely, and the shift treats 0 as no stance, which also stops the drift on
  bills already stored under the old rule.

## Unions: dues, approval and contested shops

### Membership is a headcount again

`membershipPressure` is retired. It was one number doing four jobs, and the
number players actually saw, "Membership 17.0", was none of them. A union's
members are now counted from the workers in the sectors it represents, weighted
by how unionized each of those sectors is.

### Dues and services

The union head sets annual dues per member. Dues fund the treasury and lower
approval. Four service programmes spend the treasury and raise it: a strike
fund, a health and welfare fund, legal representation, and training and
apprenticeships. Service costs are priced as a share of a member's annual wage
rather than a cash figure, so the same programme means the same thing in Lagos
and Detroit and survives an era change.

If the treasury cannot cover the bill in a turn, the services lapse for that
turn rather than overdrawing the account, and lapsed programmes stop paying
approval. Promises the union is not keeping do not buy goodwill.

### Approval decides whether you grow

Approval anchors the unionization drift target in every sector a union
represents, and it is signed about the midpoint. A union that charges heavily
and funds nothing watches its own shops drift back toward unorganized. The old
organizing term could only push density up, because recruiting was the only
lever and there was no way to run a union badly.

### Sectors are held, and can be taken

A sector now records which union represents it. A union head runs a targeted
drive at one specific sector from that sector's own page. On an unheld sector,
crossing the recognition threshold wins representation. On a sector held by a
rival, it is a raid, and the attacker needs a clear approval edge to win: a
well-run incumbent is hard to dislodge, and a failed raid still costs the
attacker. A drive spikes that sector's unionization, and the normal per-turn
trend walks it back toward the anchor, so a drive is a push rather than a
purchase.

Players can found rival unions, which is what gives raiding a target in an
industry that already has a union. The database guard that allowed only one
union per industry is now scoped to seeded unions, so the original protection
against duplicate seeding survives while founded rivals are free to pile in.

### Removed

The recruitment drive is gone. It existed only to raise the retired organizing
score, and organizing a specific sector replaces it with something targeted and
contestable.

## Tech tree costs stop counting yen as dollars

Reported as ticket #1118: a corporation was quoted 53,142,918 to unlock a
single tech node while its whole sector estate grossed about 17.9M. The
reporter guessed the cause himself, saying the Japanese sectors were what
broke it, and he was right.

A node's cash price is a quarter of the corporation's daily gross revenue.
That gross was a bare sum of every sector's revenue, but sector revenue is
stored in the sector's own host currency. The reporter held a Kansai retail
sector booking 268,000,000 yen, worth well under a million dollars at the
period rate, sitting next to about fifteen million dollars of American
sectors. The sum came out near 283,000,000, roughly eighteen times the real
figure, and the quarter charged against it inherited the whole error.

Each sector is now converted to the anchor at its own rate before anything is
added, and only the total is converted back into the corporation's currency.
The quoted price and the charged price run through one helper, so they cannot
drift apart. A corporation trading entirely in its home currency sees no
change at all.

The price itself also comes down. A node cost a quarter of daily gross, which
worked out at close to a full day of profit once the inflated figure stopped
hiding how steep that was. It is now fifteen percent. For the reporting
corporation a node goes from about 70,800,000 under the old broken sum, to
3,900,000 with the currency fix alone, to 2,300,000 at the new rate.
