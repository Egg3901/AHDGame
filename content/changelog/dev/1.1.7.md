## What was broken

A chartered bank keeps its cash in `bankCharter.cashReserves`, separate from its
holding company's `liquidCapital`. The owner can move surplus cash up to the
holding company through the bank's "upstream" action.

The cap on that move was the bank's reserves above its reserve requirement
(`deposits x reserveRatio`). That is the wrong ceiling. Reserves sitting above
the reserve requirement are still mostly depositor money, not the owner's. A
bank with a large deposit base therefore reported a large "surplus" the owner
could withdraw, even when the bank owed far more to depositors than it held in
cash. Borrowed cash (interbank, central-bank margin line, discount window)
inflated reserves the same way while being a liability the bank still had to
repay.

## What changed

Upstreaming is now capped at the lower of two figures:

- the reserves above the reserve requirement (unchanged), and
- the bank's book equity: cash plus outstanding loans, minus deposits and minus
  borrowed cash (interbank, central-bank margin, discount window).

Book equity is what is genuinely the owner's after depositors and lenders are
made whole. A bank whose deposits and borrowings exceed its cash-plus-loans has
zero equity and can upstream nothing, regardless of how far its reserves sit
above the requirement. The same ceiling is re-checked atomically inside the
write, so a stale read cannot slip a payout past it, and the bank page now shows
the corrected upstream capacity.

## Deposit base tied to capital

The NPC household deposits a bank attracts were sized purely from its financial
sector capacity, with no reference to the bank's capital. A bank with a large
financial sector but little or no capital could therefore anchor a deposit base
worth billions that it had nothing to stand behind.

The deposit ceiling is now the lower of the capacity figure and a leverage
multiple of book equity. A well-capitalized bank (whether its capital is posted
contributions or retained earnings) is unaffected; a bank with little or negative
equity has its ceiling pulled down toward zero, and the normal per-turn deposit
outflow then unwinds the excess gradually rather than all at once.
