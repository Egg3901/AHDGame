Version 1.11 brings the 1991 world its own institutions, successor economies and election rules, alongside clearer pages and a richer record of each season. It also includes the accounting, reliability and turn performance work accumulated since 1.10. Missing regional lean caches are repaired; historical regional calibration and observations under production load remain tracked separately.

## What landed in this release

222 changes shipped.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Bounded Russian Duma NPC nominees**

  A constitutional draft can dissolve the Grand Assembly or explicitly continue its deputies until the original term ends. Ending a continued mandate requires a separate ordinary parliamentary vote.

  In a 1991 world, Japan's 512-seat House of Representatives is decided by single non-transferable vote. Each candidate can win at most one seat, and seats go in vote order.

  Bulgaria's founding Grand Assembly counts separate constituency and party-list ballots. Unresolved constituencies hold real runoffs before the chamber takes office.
- **Bulgarian Grand Assembly list-seat replacements**
  Vacant list seats pass to the next eligible original nominee for the remainder of the term.

### Government and legislation

- **Negotiate federation financial shares**
  Federation mandate sponsors can propose separate financial asset and debt shares. Population-based allocations remain the default.
- **Revise rejected federation mandates**
  Rejected or withdrawn federation settlements can return for a fresh vote. Participant consent and rejection reasons remain visible.
- **Planned economies can legislate economic reform**
  East Germany, the Soviet Union and other planned economies can now pass an economy bill that moves them to reform socialism, a market economy, or back to central planning.
- **Russian bills follow their active constitution**
- **Federation consent waits for an enacted mandate**
- **NPC deputies follow the active Russian legislature**
- **Russian deputies can use their active legislature**
- **Pay sovereign bond principal from treasury cash**
  Governments now pay matured sovereign principal from treasury cash as well as retiring the debt. Repayment records use the same currency valuation as treasury accounting.

### War and diplomacy

- **Add conservation rules for negotiated federation settlements**
  Prepare negotiated succession with explicit consent, population-based financial defaults and conserved territorial totals. Runtime dissolution remains gated until country creation and contract servicing are connected.
- **Blocs can declare war together**
  A Bloc can vote to start a war against a player nation. Every player nation in the Bloc must consent, NPP-governed members join automatically, and each other eligible player nation then votes on its own declaration in its legislature.
- **War resolution reliability and combat balance**
  Keep war outcomes recoverable while reducing extreme results in evenly matched battles.
- **Ukraine's 1953 Warsaw Pact tribute uses Soviet-ruble GDP**
  In 1953 worlds, Ukraine's yearly tribute to the Warsaw Pact fund is worked out from its GDP on the same Soviet-ruble basis as Russia, Belarus and the Baltic republics.
- **War control shown to two decimals**
  Each side's share of control in a war is shown with two decimals, and the two sides always add up to 100.00%.
- **Crisis choices check approval before charging the treasury**
  A crisis choice rejected for low approval costs nothing, and cash checks cover crisis, settlement and peace payments.

### Economy and corporations

- **Apply ratified federation settlements and service legacy debt**
  Unanimously approved federation settlements now apply during the turn. Dissolved issuers retain a settlement administration funded by successor contributions.
- **Big currency trades cost more, and trading moves rates less**
  Your own currency trades now pay a size fee that grows with how much you convert, and fees depend on how busy each currency is. Trading volume now nudges exchange rates far less, and one trader alone can barely move a currency.
- **Corporation page and CEO Office redesign**
  The corporation page and the CEO Office are now laid out as tables. The CEO Office is one screen: the budget is edited inside the income statement, and every sector's output, pricing and wages are set from its row.
- **Clarified inflation timing**
  The inflation rule now describes its turn-by-turn change limit in the game's 48-turn year, with a deterministic calibration check across five years.
- **Smooth historical capacity price progression**
  Historical capacity price levels now progress between calibration years without abrupt era-boundary jumps.
- **Connect climate exposure to disaster risk and treasury costs**
  Weather disasters now respond gradually to emissions affected by environment spending and funded climate programmes. Local resilience limits extra losses, while geophysical hazards retain their baseline timing. Bounded treasury costs are charged once even if a turn is retried.
- **Continuous historical monetary calibration**
  Historical monetary reference rates now graduate between era anchors instead of jumping when an era boundary is crossed.
- **Federation firm protection scales with large settlements**
- **Retain full positive-demand market coverage**
- **Retained private headquarters keep successor compensation rights**
- **Successors service private facility compensation**
- **Count NPC fund subscriptions once in accounting**
  NPC fund subscriptions have one cash debit in reconciliation while investment income and fund receipts stay visible.
- **Countries export only what they have spare**
  Trade between countries could send more of a good abroad than a country had left over after its own buyers, which emptied its home market on paper and raised its prices. Exports now stay within each country's surplus.
- **Faster index fund bond purchases**
  Index funds now settle each turn's bond purchases together instead of one purchase at a time. Funds buy the same bonds at the same prices.
- **Faster market-cap snapshot**
  The end-of-turn market-cap snapshot no longer scans every corporation's full history to find its previous credit rating.
- **Mixed plants follow the product that sells, and energy markets can clear**
  Plants that make more than one product set their run by what buyers pay for each one. Energy, oil, iron and natural gas markets now show the demand buyers have. Room to build and Unclaimed share match what plants can sell.
- **NPP capacity build history follows successful cash writes**
  Failed NPP cash writes no longer leave phantom capacity spending in financial history. Successful builds retain their existing costs and accounting.
- **Optional explicit plant operating costs**
  Fresh worlds can opt into a cost model with positive operating overhead and capacity-based payroll. It remains off by default pending paired-world simulation and cost-plus qualification; current worlds keep the legacy model unless enabled.
- **NPP sector founding cash now reconciles with accounting**
  Accounting now records actual NPP sector founding expenses, including entry fees and starter builds, while preserving existing cash and capacity rules.
- **Preserve exact corporate escrow cash receipts**
- **Record bond pool cash movements in the ledger**
  Bond market liquidity adjustments and issuer payments now leave matching cash records, improving accounting checks across currencies.
- **Bound experimental entry into uncovered markets**
- **Central banks review policy every turn**
  An inactive bank record attached to East Germany asked for a liquidity injection every turn and was refused, and each refusal stopped most central banks from reviewing policy that turn. Each committee now acts only through its own bank, and a refused operation skips only that bank.
- **Private corporations can't move into a state-run economy**
  A private corporation can no longer move its headquarters into a country whose economy is still fully state-run. Before, a CEO who relocated could take their corporation along, even though founding one there was refused.
- **Record landed NPP bond cash returns**
  NPP investment accounts now record bond coupon and principal receipts in shadow accounting. Existing payments stay unchanged, and rejected writes do not create receipts.
- **Accounting includes central bank bond operations**
  Cash checks now record the bond market cash that central bank purchases and sales create or withdraw.
- **Accounting includes fines, group relief and nationalization compensation**
  Cash checks now record the treasury side of regulatory fines, corporate group loss relief and sector nationalization compensation.
- **Accounting includes new corporations' starting funds**
  Cash checks now record the starting treasury granted to newly spawned corporations.
- **Bank console as tables**
  The corporation Bank tab uses the same plain sections, figures and tables as the rest of the corporation page.
- **Keep bank loans with their charter epoch**
  Rechartering starts a new loan book. Existing loans retain their originating charter and continue through estate recovery, with recoveries credited to deposit insurance. This isolates loan servicing across charter epochs; it does not resolve broader bank viability questions.
- **Reconcile construction in progress from build orders**
  The turn processor is the sole writer of stored construction in progress and derives it from remaining build orders, clamped at zero. Build, delivery, cancellation, transfer, and NPP order handling update queue state; valuation and detail views use the queue they already loaded.
- **Bond market accounting includes secondary trades and refunds**
  Cash checks now include the bond pool side of purchases, sales and returned payments.
- **Clearer corporation page header**
  The corporation header separates the name, the share price and the key figures, and the section tabs read as two distinct levels.
- **Corporation and sector screens drop dash placeholders**
  Empty values on corporation and sector screens read n/a, none or 0, and dashes in their sentences became plain punctuation.
- **Corporation Finance tabs as tables**
  Financials, Credit and Bonds, Charts and Snapshot use the same table layout as the rest of the corporation page.
- **Corporation Operations tabs as tables**
  Sectors, Commodities, Tech, Contracts and Defence use the same table layout as the rest of the corporation page.
- **Corporation Ownership tabs as tables**
  The Shares, Deals and Structure tabs use the same table layout as the rest of the corporation page, and figures across the page are set in Geist Mono.
- **Defaulted bonds no longer count as corporate income**
  Corporations holding bonds in default were credited with coupon income that was never paid, and taxed on it. The tax came out of real cash, so a corp could show a profit while its capital fell every turn.
- **Faster bond turn processing**
  The bond step of each turn reads less data and makes far fewer database trips, with the same results.
- **Faster supply contract matching and settlement**
  The turn steps that match NPP supply contracts and settle all supply contracts now do the same work far faster. Matches and deliveries are unchanged.
- **Faster turns from leaner economy reads**
  Several turn steps now read less from the database: NPP supply contracts, the economic vital signs report, the bond market pools, and NPP stock trades. Nothing about how the economy behaves changes.
- **Faster union strike decisions during turns**
  NPP-led unions deciding whether to escalate a dispute now share the turn's country and state readings instead of looking them up again for every dispute.
- **Fund accounting includes foreign political investors**
  Cash checks now record fund purchases and sales by non-player politicians whose home currency differs from the fund's.
- **Fund trade recovery**
  Interrupted fund purchases and sales resume their accepted settlement once.
- **Large savings withdrawals explain the central bank's limit**
  A savings withdrawal larger than the central bank can pay out now says how much it can pay right now, instead of failing with an internal error.
- **Listing petitions cost the same value in every currency**
  An index listing petition charges the same value whatever currency the corporation holds its cash in.
- **Nationalization pays for shares the company held**
  When a whole corporation is nationalized, its shareholders are paid for the shares it held in other companies.
- **New corporations start with the same value in every country**
  A new corporation's starting treasury is converted at its currency's current exchange rate.
- **Older businesses count their bonuses once in running costs**
  Businesses set up before mid-August had their policy, tax and location bonuses counted twice in running costs, and a few showed a 100% margin. Each bonus now counts once. Margins fall for many Western businesses and rise for state firms in the Eastern bloc.
- **Plants sectors show an ordinary net margin**
  The margin that counts unsold output is now profit over revenue, so it reads like any other net margin instead of running to thousands of percent.
- **Queued fund payouts retain their original settlement during recovery**
  Queued fund redemptions resume their original payout after an interrupted turn. Recovery keeps the accepted amount and completes the payment records once.
- **Central-bank opening rates in 1991**
  Five central banks open with January 1991 policy benchmarks instead of modern defaults.
- **Expand market link encodes the sector type**
  The expand market dialog builds its state economy link from an encoded sector type.
- **Original-currency GDP in 1991 successor countries**
  Country GDP comparisons use the opening conversion factor for their seeded local currency.
- **Safe handling of unrecognized sector types**
  Unrecognized saved sector types no longer crash strategy resolution during turns.
- **Soviet and Nigerian opening GDP units**
  Nigerian regional output uses its national source total and both countries normalize their stored native currency consistently.

### Interface and platform

- **Clearer political metrics header and readable labels**
  The political metrics page keeps its registry look, category cards and colour, with a clearer header and labels that are easier to read. The overall condition now leads the page as a large figure, and the small labels across the page are larger.

  Your profile and other players' profiles have a calmer, clearer layout. - **Shared Poll rules**
  Poll action costs share a portable rules module across quote, debit and display.
- **Keep large federation relocations responsive**
  Federation settlements preserve resident choices and wallets with batched database operations as the number of affected characters grows.
- **Aged-world turn benchmark script**
  A developer script that restores an aged sandbox world and times real turns against it, so turn performance changes are measured on a mature world.
- **Anti-abuse checks no longer delay turns**
  Fraud and alt-account checks run right after each turn finishes instead of during it.
- **Faster index fund rebalance sales**
  Index fund rebalance sales read each market pool once and record their ledger entries together, so the daily rebalance turn takes less time.
- **Faster slate carry-forward during turns**
  The turn step that carries party slate boards into newly opened primaries now loads everything it needs in a few batched reads instead of several per race.
- **Faster union bargaining for computer-controlled unions**
  Computer-led unions reuse economic readings across the campaigns they open in a turn.
- **Faster vote counting each turn**
  Vote accumulation reads each election's incumbency history and country settings once per turn instead of once per race.
- **Vote totals saved together each turn**
  Each turn's vote counts for every open race are saved in one batch instead of one race at a time.
- **Wiki chronicle of Iteration 1**
  The wiki has an Iteration 1 page that follows the 1953 world from its opening in August to 1979, covering the American presidents, the war for Germany, the Soviet and British turns, corporations and markets, and the standings at turn 1,302.
- **Faster front page load and working site analytics**
  The front page globe waits for the page to finish loading before it spins, slows itself on weaker devices, and logged-out visitors no longer trigger account requests that can only fail.
- **Faster line of credit servicing each turn**
  Line of credit interest and payments settle with fewer database reads per borrower, with identical results.
- **Faster NPP action turns**
  The every-fourth-turn NPP action step now builds party organisation in each country at the same time and processes NPP share sales in each currency at the same time. Who builds, who sells, and the results are unchanged.
- **Faster supply contract settlement each turn**
  The corporation turn settles supply contracts and saves sector results faster, with identical results.
- **Lighter, faster 1991 front page**
  The 1991 front page drops the special report strip and the headline crawl, and the globe spins on far less work per frame.
- **Long simulation reports respect requested sample limits**
- **No failing requests on the front page for signed-out visitors**
  The navbar no longer asks for character status before anyone has signed in, and leader cards no longer request portraits that do not exist.
- **Plainer headings and fewer colors**
  Headings and section labels use one plain style, and color is kept for actions, gains and losses, and real warnings.
- **Position editor links encode their parts**
  The admin position editor builds its state link and preset request from encoded values.
- **Shorter turns from overlapping small steps**
  Several small turn steps that never touch the same records now run side by side, and a few steps read or write their records in one go instead of one at a time. Turn results are unchanged.
- **Wiki office history survives world resets**
  Generated wiki office pages keep every finished world's presidents, cabinet members and congressional leaders instead of starting over at each reset, and office dates follow the founding-phase calendar.
- **World resets keep cross-game data**
  A reset no longer drops moderation reports, login history, wiki politician pages, event templates, or site analytics, and a full reset keeps banned and staff accounts.

#### What changed

### Big currency trades cost more, and trading moves rates less

#### What changed

- Market and limit trades you make yourself pay a size fee on top of the base fee. It rises toward 20% on very large conversions and counts everything you converted in the last 24 turns, so splitting a big trade into small ones costs the same.
- The whole fee is scaled by how busy the two currencies are: half price in a busy market, up to one and a half times in a quiet one. The total never passes 30%.
- Conversions the game makes for you (dividends, coupons, purchase shortfalls) pay only the base fee, as before.
- Fees go to central banks the same way the base fee does.
- Net buying and selling now moves an exchange rate far less. It takes about ₳1B of net volume to reach half of the old push, and the push counts for a quarter of what it did. About five traders need to be behind a flow for it to work at full strength; one trader alone gets a fifth.
- Central-bank intervention keeps its full strength, so a bank can lean against a large flow.
- The trade window shows the actual fee for your amount and explains the size and market fee. The forex and investing guides show the current fees.

### Keep bank loans with their charter epoch

Rechartering starts with an empty loan book for the new epoch. Existing loans retain their originating charter and continue through estate recovery after resolution, with recoveries credited to deposit insurance. Failed estates must resolve before rechartering. Archived loan recovery is bounded to its charter window. An idempotent migration assigns epoch metadata to legacy loans while preserving reads during backfill. This isolates loan servicing across charter epochs; it does not establish broader bank viability.

### Reconcile construction in progress from build orders

The turn processor is the sole writer of stored construction in progress and recalculates it from remaining build orders, clamped at zero. Build, delivery, cancellation, transfer and NPP order handling update queue state only. Valuation and detail consumers use the queue already loaded for their query. A concurrent queue change can leave the stored snapshot briefly behind until the next turn reconciliation.

### Optional explicit plant operating costs

The fresh-world setting `explicitPlantCostsEnabled` selects positive operating overhead and payroll based on active capacity. The model bills inputs, labour and overhead directly rather than solving a negative residual to preserve a target margin. Recipe efficiency scales purchased input once. The deterministic scenario report covers a normalized basket, input-price shocks, efficiency and recession cases, but does not qualify the median seeded plant outcome with host wages, technology, utilization, policies or financing. The setting remains off by default pending paired-world simulation and cost-plus qualification; existing worlds retain the legacy model unless explicitly enabled.
