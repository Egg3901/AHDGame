## Fixed

- Brazil's 1953 monetary baseline carried an inflation target taken from the
  Vargas-era CPI, so the central bank was chasing 10% inflation instead of
  defending the currency. The real left its stable plateau and ran to a peak of
  26.19 per anchor before the target was corrected.
- A matured bond is not a choice. When one comes due the engine converts the
  face value out of the local currency on the spot, so every Brazilian bond that
  matured while the rate was wrong was cashed out at a price the bug set.
- Those maturities are now repaired: 22 forced conversions across 18 holders get
  the difference between what they were paid and what the corrected rate would
  have paid, roughly 91.6 million anchor in total.
- Bonds still held are not touched. They were never sold, and the rate is
  already walking back toward its target on its own. Bonds a holder chose to
  sell are not touched either.
- Every payment is credited in the holder's own settlement currency and posts a
  visible receipt on the source maturity, so the money is traceable rather than
  appearing out of nowhere.
