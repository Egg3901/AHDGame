## Added

Credit for response in the National Mood channel. Economic metrics move over
many turns and an election does not wait for them, so voters give partial
credit to a government that is visibly acting. National bills enacted in the
last 24 turns whose policy effects push a suffering component in the helpful
direction now reduce the economic penalty by up to 40%.

Credit is scored per component. A bill that cuts unemployment relieves the
unemployment penalty and nothing else, so a stack of jobs bills does not buy
off an inflation problem.

Three rules keep it honest:

- The bill must have carried a real budget cost. Free gestures earn nothing.
- The first bill on a component earns full weight, the next half, the next a
  quarter. Passing the same relief five times is worth barely more than
  passing it once.
- If the component's metric has kept getting worse over the eight turns since
  the response landed, that component's credit is cancelled. Spending without
  results is not a defence.

Inflation cannot earn credit, because no law in the game declares a direct
effect on the inflation rate. That is a limit of the data, not a judgement
about fiscal policy.

The National Mood panel shows the relief when it applies, with the size of the
reduction and the bills that earned it.

## Changed

Term fatigue is now priced in one place. The National Mood channel already
scaled the penalty side for a party seeking a third or later consecutive term;
the older "time for a change" drag inside the incumbency persuasion driver has
been removed. That drag only moved the small persuadable slice of the vote and
was too weak to notice, and keeping both meant the same effect was charged
twice at very different strengths.

Fatigue applies to what is left of the penalty after credit for response, so
acting on a downturn is worth more the longer your party has held the office.
The elections wiki has been updated to match.
