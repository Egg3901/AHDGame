Cut from development.

## Fixed

- An index fund that held shares in a company which later dissolved kept
  that position forever. Divestment was only ever a sale, and there is
  nobody to sell a dead company's shares to, so the attempt did nothing and
  the holding stayed. Funds now write such a position off at zero, which is
  what it is worth.

- Because those positions were still counted at their last traded price, a
  fund's quoted price included backing it did not have, and the gap widened
  as more companies went under. Quoted prices now track only assets the fund
  actually holds.

## Changed

- A write-off appears in the fund's transaction history as its own entry,
  naming how many positions went and what they were carried at. It reads as
  a price drop with no sale behind it, so it is recorded rather than left to
  be inferred from the number moving.

- A position the fund cannot sell because the market has no bid is left
  alone. That company still exists and may trade again, so its shares are
  not written off. The turn report counts these separately.
