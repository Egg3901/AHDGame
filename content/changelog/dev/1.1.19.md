## What changed

The production adjustment slider publishes two curves: an output curve, shown on
the sector panel as the effect on units (down to -10% at -25), and a smaller
revenue curve (down to -5%). Under the plants economy a sector's revenue is
derived from what it physically made, so only one of those curves can gate
production or the slider gets counted twice on the top line.

It was gating on the wrong one. Tonnage moved on the revenue curve, so a plant
set to -22% cut output by 4.4% while the panel promised 8.8%. The output curve
was then applied a second time, but only at the point where goods left the
sector: the world supply ledger and the clearing book. The plant therefore built
the full tonnage, offered about 9% less of it, and the difference was physically
unsellable. It was never on the book, so no price move could clear it, and the
owner paid production and holding costs on goods that could not be sold. With a
large market share there was no way out of it at all.

Reported in ticket-1072 by a state-owned CEO who set the adjustment to -22% and
saw the unit count refuse to move while unsold stock and units waiting on inputs
kept climbing.

Now the output curve gates production directly, the revenue curve steps aside
under plants, and the supply ledger and clearing offer take the produced figure
as-is. Produced equals offered. Move the slider to -22% and output falls 8.8%,
which is what the panel said it would do, with the input draw falling alongside
it as before.

Supply agreement capacity checks are unchanged in effect. They size a contract
against nameplate capacity, which has not been through production yet, so they
still apply the output curve themselves.

Sectors sitting at 0 are unaffected: both curves are 1.0 there, so nothing moves
for anyone who never touched the slider.
