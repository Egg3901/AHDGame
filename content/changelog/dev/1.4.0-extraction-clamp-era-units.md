  The per-state deposit ceiling is supposed to ration how much a mining or
  drilling sector can pull out of the ground each turn. On era worlds the
  rationing check measured desired output in the wrong units, so the ceiling

  and shortage relief can never push a sector past what its deposits can
  physically yield.

Cut from development.

## Fixed

- The extraction capacity check now measures a sector's desired output with
  the same expression the world supply ledger uses, including the era price
  basis and the per-resource output scale. On era worlds the old figure was
  70 to 174 times smaller than what the ledger books, so the per-state
  deposit ceiling almost never bound and sectors could book revenue for ore
  their state's deposits cannot yield. Modern worlds are byte-identical when
  `extractionOutputScaleEnabled` is off; when it is on, the configured output
  scale applies by design.
- Scarcity relief is now capped at the deposit ceiling. Relief exists so a
  global shortage stops taxing exactly the extraction the economy needs, but
  it could previously lift the cap entirely, letting a sector in a shortage
  resource produce past its state's physical capacity. Full relief still
  applies to any sector operating within its ceiling.

## Guarded

- With this follow-up, the ramp protects both first exposure and later large
  corrections. A one-turn utilization drop greater than 0.25 restarts the
  240-turn ramp from the new exposure, while the existing floor remains in
  place.
- New tests pin the era-to-modern correction factor, the modern-world no-op
  with the output-scale flag off, full relief within the ceiling, the clamp
  winning past it, and the corrected unit weighting.
