  The engine required 270 electoral votes to win the presidency outright, which

  (electoral votes, House delegations, Senate votes) now derive from the
  college and rosters actually on the ballot.

Cut from development. Part of the presidential rework, evidence-and-correctness
epic. On the modern roster every derived threshold reproduces the old constants
exactly (538 electors still need 270; 50 delegations still need 26; 100
senators still need 51), so modern worlds are unchanged.

## Fixed

- Outright presidential victory now requires a majority of the college the
  election actually apportioned, computed from the same census-aware
  apportionment that allocates the electors. On 1950s worlds that is 266 of
  531, not 270.
- The House contingent ballot's victory threshold is a majority of the
  delegations on the ballot (25 of 48 in the 1950s), and the Senate's
  vice-presidential threshold is a majority of the senators voting (49 of
  96), instead of the modern 26 and 51.
- Resolution messaging, wire embeds, and notifications state the actual
  threshold for the world instead of a hardcoded 270.
- The live-race contingent-risk banner and the election panel derive the
  college size and majority from the race's own per-state electoral votes
  instead of assuming 538 and 270.

## Guarded

- A golden-gate test pins that the modern roster reproduces the old constants
  exactly, and era tests pin 266 of 531, 25 of 48, and 49 of 96, including
  the previously wrong case of a 267-elector winner being denied outright
  victory.
- The contingent deadlock test now models a genuine deadlock (an even
  delegation split) rather than a majority that only the modern constant
  refused to seat.
