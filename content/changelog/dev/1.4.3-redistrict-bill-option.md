## Fixed

- The State Redistricting Authority Act, State District Compactness Act and
  State Electoral Fairness Act are proposable in state legislatures again. They
  had stopped being offered when the older United States law book was retired in
  favour of the new one, which has no equivalent for them. Every state was left
  sitting on the default bipartisan commission, and no bill on the list could
  move it to a legislature-drawn map or to an independent commission.
- Enacted redistricting settings are no longer cleared as stale when a world is
  reseeded.
- Executive orders no longer run off the end of a policy's option list. Orders
  assumed every policy offered the same seven choices, so on a shorter list an
  order could land on a setting that does not exist, and the policy page and the
  mechanic behind it would then disagree about what was in force. The order
  screen now offers only the steps a policy actually has, and the same applies
  to the current-law comparison shown while drafting a bill.
