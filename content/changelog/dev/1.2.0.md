## Added

- Reform Movement: a Prague-Spring-style crisis for the USSR and its Warsaw
  Pact satellites (Poland, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Bulgaria, Hungary,
  Romania), available from 1960. Suppressing it militarily restores control
  but costs standing abroad; tolerating it risks the same unrest spreading to
  a neighboring satellite; genuine reform wins over reformers but can provoke
  a Hardliner Backlash crisis of its own.
- Four 1960s American protest crises: Civil Rights Marches, Campus Unrest,
  Urban Unrest, and Anti-War Protests. Each has real trade-offs, federal
  intervention, real legislation introduced to Congress, or a crackdown that
  moves different voter groups in opposite directions and risks the unrest
  spreading further.

- Warehousing: a new per-sector choice between selling everything at market
  and building inventory. With stockpiling on, unsold storable goods go into
  a pile instead of being lost. The pile spoils a little each turn, costs
  money to hold, and sells automatically when the market clears your fresh
  output in full. A glut becomes an asset you realize later, not lost money.
  Services cannot be stored.
- Commodity pages now show where each market is out of balance: which states
  are paying a shortage premium, which are sitting on goods they cannot sell
  locally, and, for mined resources, which states have untapped deposit room.

## Changed

- Autonomous corporations now pick where to build with local market awareness.
  New plants go to the state that is actually short of the goods they will
  make, mining and drilling only goes where the ground has room for it, and a
  company's home state is a preference rather than an automatic winner. This
  spreads supply toward demand instead of piling it up in a few states.
- Plants that chronically cannot sell now wind down instead of standing
  forever. Autonomous corporations stop replacing worn-out capacity in
  oversupplied states, and after a long stretch of unsold output they close a
  misplaced plant entirely and rebuild where the demand is. Growth decisions
  also now read the plant's own state market rather than the whole country.
- Your sector page warns you when a plant is stranded. If a plant has sold
  less than half its output for six turns in a row, a banner explains why the
  money is not coming in and lays out the options: shrink it, mothball it,
  sell it, or abandon it. Player plants are never touched automatically.
- Interstate shipping now adapts to what each state actually hauls. The fleet
  used to be split 70/30 between bulk goods and special cargo everywhere; now
  each state's split follows its real shipping mix from the previous turn, so
  a state hauling mostly electronics no longer wastes most of its trucks on
  an empty bulk lane.
- Electricity and natural gas prices now pool nationally. Both ride grids and
  pipelines rather than trucks and rail, so a state's price reflects the
  national network instead of a local accident of where plants happen to
  stand. Extreme same-turn spreads between neighboring states go away.

## Defence procurement rebuilt on obligations

### Obligations, not intentions

Awarding a contract encumbers its full cost against the defence appropriation
at the moment it is signed. Total live obligations can never exceed the
uncommitted appropriation, the check is a guarded database update rather than
a read-then-write, and an order the budget cannot cover is refused with the
uncommitted figure and the maximum affordable lot count in the response.

Delivery draws the contract's own commitment down rather than spending afresh.
Cancelling, declining, or completing an order releases whatever it still
holds, on both the country's books and the contract's, so budget is never
stranded behind an order that no longer exists.

Contracts awarded before this shipped carry no reservation. They keep
delivering out of uncommitted budget rather than stalling forever on a
commitment they were never given, and they cannot reach money another contract
has committed.

### Lots cost something to build

Delivery now bills the supplier the commodity inputs and overhead behind the
lots it ships, priced through the same realisation function the revenue side
uses, so a shortage squeezes both legs instead of only the cost side. The
supplier is credited price minus cost in one write, so payment is margin and a
contract can no longer inflate a balance one-for-one with the appropriation it
drains.

### Nothing is discarded silently

Every rounding and affordability boundary carries the work and records why:
part of a lot built, appropriation short this turn, more built than the order
still needs, supplier no longer eligible, no output. Both order books show
lots ordered, built and waiting, delivered, paid, and what the buyer still has
committed.

### One fill-eligibility resolver

The award picker, the award route, the CEO's accept and the delivery sweep now
call one function. Awardable means deliverable. State-owned suppliers and
suppliers banking in a currency other than the dollar are first-class
throughout, with regression coverage for the ticket cluster where the four
paths disagreed.

### Minister-set price and grade

A minister may set the price per lot inside a band whose floor is production
cost plus a margin and whose ceiling is the GDP anchor, and may order a grade
from cheap mass to premium. Grade scales both ends of the band and lands as the
delivered materiel's tier, so doctrine is a real choice. Both are validated
server-side; leaving them blank reproduces the previous behaviour exactly.

### Production lines

A defence plant runs four production lines and the contractor decides how many
work each order. Throughput scales with the allocation, the price per lot does
not, and the total across a plant's orders cannot exceed its lines. The default
allocation reproduces the previous even split exactly, so no live contract
changes rate.

### Self-dealing is disclosed

An award to a corporation the awarding minister owns or holds a material stake
in is marked on the contract, stated on the public wire, and charged against
the minister's standing, scaled by how much of the national contracting tranche
the order takes. The one-third per-supplier concentration cap is unchanged.

### Under the hood

- Money movements claim an idempotency key before any transfer, so a re-run
  turn cannot pay a contract twice. There are no transactions available here,
  so claiming first is what makes the retry safe.
- Recruiting and upgrading now spend from uncommitted appropriation rather than
  the raw balance, so a purchase cannot eat money a contract has committed.
- The procurement freeze kill switch is unchanged and still available.

## Banking rebuilt on a single balance sheet

Private banking stays switched off in the live world and this release does not
change that. What it changes is the reason it was switched off: balance-sheet
quantities that no cash stood behind, and screens that disagreed about numbers
players are held to.

### One balance sheet

`src/lib/banking/balanceSheet.ts` now owns book equity, regulatory capital,
required reserves, the deposit ceiling, the run line and the confidence
denominators. The cash module, the supervisor, the solvency pass, the console
API and the console UI read it instead of recomputing. Two surfaces disagreeing
on a required number is now structurally impossible, because there is only one
implementation.

The two deposit kinds are separated everywhere and named as such. Household
deposits are cash: the engine moves real money out of the central bank's pool to
create them. Player savings are a pointer: the balance never leaves the
character. Reserve requirements, lendable headroom, the discount-window cap and
equity now count only the cash-backed half.

### One way to move money

Deposit flows, the deposit-book waterfall, capital injections and upstreams go
through a shared primitive that claims an idempotency key before any balance
moves, refuses a set of legs that does not net to zero, guards every debit in
its own write, and records anything that starts and does not finish so an
operator can find and repair it.

### One way a deposit book ends

Failure, supervisory revocation, admin unwind and a charter switch away from
deposit taking all run the same waterfall. Revocation used to set a status and
stop, leaving depositors pointed at a dead bank. The charter switch and the
admin unwind used to credit the household book back to the money supply while
leaving the matching cash in the vault, so the same money existed twice and the
shareholder was then paid out of it. Priority is depositors, then other
creditors, then the owner, capped at book equity in the write itself.

Player deposit haircuts are gone. The old resolution destroyed money the bank
never held and then had the insurance fund pay for balances that had not moved.
Deposit insurance now stands behind the household book, which is the part made
of cash.

### Central bank savings interest has a payer

Interest on savings held at the central bank was credited with nothing debited
anywhere. It is currency creation, so it is booked as currency creation. The
player's credit is unchanged; the money supply now knows about it.

### Caps that explain themselves

The bank console shows every cap a player can hit with its formula and the
numbers currently in that formula, generated by the same module that enforces
it. The wiki guide gains the equity half of the deposit ceiling, the run line,
the prop leverage caps and the new failure treatment.

### Conservation is a test now

A simulation test runs a bank through charter, deposits, lending, interest,
upstream, failure and resolution against a real in-memory store and asserts
after every step that total money is unchanged except for explicitly minted or
burned amounts. Borrowed money never counts as capital.

## The country page is a directory now

The country lander answered "what is the debt to GDP ratio" before it answered
"where do I go". Reading order is now identity and vital signs, then a grouped
directory into everything the country contains, then the detail sections that
were already there. Nothing was cut: the descriptor, the national ideology band,
regime stability and the sovereign debt panels all moved below the directory
rather than out of the page. Staff and admin surfaces stay under the settings
gear and are not in the directory.

### Composition is a tested pure function

`buildCountryDirectory` takes the country config, the era preset and the batched
counts and returns the groups. It lives outside the client component because the
ordering and the gating ARE the design, and both are now covered without
mounting a page that fires half a dozen fetches. The tests assert group order,
per row gating, figure formatting, that every sub surface a player can reach has
an entry, and that no label or destination repeats.

Four groups, in reading order: Politics, Government, Economy, Nation. A group
with no rows is dropped rather than rendered empty.

### Rows carry a live figure and degrade one at a time

The counts route now also serves national GDP, the budget balance as a share of
GDP, union count, referendum counts and a Cold War DEFCON. Every field is
nullable and a missing one renders a plain chevron, so a country whose budget
document has not been created yet shows nothing rather than asserting a balanced
budget on a zero economy. GDP comes off `federalBudget.gdp`, which is in
absolute currency units, divided back to the millions `formatGDP` expects; the
balance is `surplus / gdp`, both from the same document so the units cancel.

Tones are used sparingly. A deficit is a normal state, so it only warns past
three percent of GDP. DEFCON reads downward, so 2 and below warn.

### Gates drop rows rather than linking to redirects

SCOTUS stays US only. The Command Economy dashboard appears only for a flag on
planned economy. Referendums appear only once a country has run one. The Cold
War row is served only for a principal (US, RU) in a world with
`conflictsEnabled`, resolved server side against the same switch
`/world/conflicts` itself uses, so the figure's presence IS the gate and
switching the subsystem off removes the row.

Two surfaces were considered and left out. Demographics has no country scoped
page to link to. The Commanding General page is role gated to one player per
command and 404s when conflicts are off, so it stays reachable from Conflicts
and the cabinet office rather than from a hub every player lands on.

### Phone first

The directory is one column on a phone, two on a tablet and one per group on a
wide screen, and every row holds a 44px tap target at every width. The hero
image was shortened so the directory starts above the fold on a phone.

## Defence procurement follow-ups

### One money primitive, not two

Defence contract settlement moves onto `applyMoneyMove`, the same primitive banking
uses, and the bespoke `defenceMoneyClaims` collection is deleted. A delivery is now
one keyed, net-zero move: the appropriation is debited, the supplier's margin is
credited, and the commodity inputs consumed building the lots are burned. Both
`$expr` guards survive as the debit leg's own filter, so a legacy contract still
cannot spend appropriation another contract has committed, and the primitive adds
its no-overdraft `$gte` on top.

Two consequences worth naming:

- **Recording now precedes payment**, reversing the old order. That order existed
  because a bare guarded `$inc` could half-land and `advanceContract` clamps, so a
  rollback would have been a silent no-op. The primitive refuses a move having
  touched nothing, so a failed payment leaves exactly one thing to unwind, and
  `reverseContractDelivery` undoes it exactly. The three-step refund, reclaim and
  re-bank ladder is gone.
- **A half-applied move lands in the shared repair queue** instead of being
  swallowed by a `catch`. The commitment drawdown stays outside the move, because
  discharging a reservation is a memo entry rather than money entering or leaving
  the world, and it is ordered to fail in the safe direction: the cash moves first,
  so a failure leaves a country under-spending rather than able to commit the same
  money twice.

### Contract costs track the commodity market

`lotProductionCost` has always accepted a price-ratio map and was always called
without one, so every contract was costed at the recipe's nominal input share: the
cost floor and the minister's price band did not move when steel did. Live world
ratios are now wired into the delivery sweep, the award route, the supplier picker
and the CEO order book, read once per sweep and once per request. An absent or
half-seeded price book falls back to the nominal share, so a world without a price
book behaves exactly as before.

This makes a contract able to go underwater after it is signed, which is modelled
rather than clamped away. A supplier delivering into a loss funds it from liquid
capital, and one that cannot fund the loss stalls with a reason on the contract
instead of delivering into a debit that would strand the move half-applied. The
band's ceiling is deliberately still the GDP anchor: costs squeeze the band from
below, they never widen it from above, so a commodity spike is not licence to pay a
supplier more than the economy says a lot is worth.

### One appropriation read per country per turn

The delivery sweep read the defence appropriation once per contract. It now reads
once per country and carries a running projection as it spends. Safe because the
read only ever informed the PLAN: every debit is still guarded inside its own
write, so the authoritative question is asked at the moment of payment. The
projection is what stops a country with ten contracts planning all ten against the
same opening balance and pushing nine refusals into the repair queue.

### The clawback rides the primitive too

The over-award remediation was the last defence money movement still hand-rolled.
It debited every supplier's liquid capital in one loop and credited every
appropriation in a second, with nothing joining the two: a throw between them
destroyed the recovered money outright, and re-running the heal debited the
supplier again because nothing recorded that the first run had paid.

It is now one keyed, net-zero move per contract, keyed on the heal run so a retry
of the same run replays and a genuinely new over-award found later still recovers.
A move that cannot settle stops the heal and sits in the shared repair queue with
the legs that landed, rather than being reported as a success. The contract stamp
replays with the money under the same run id, so a retry cannot double the recorded
lots. `apply` refuses outright when it is handed no run id, because unkeyed money
movement inside a retryable heal is the exact failure this primitive exists to stop.

### The failure waterfall pays every creditor, in order

A bank failure used to settle exactly two claims: household depositors, and
then the owner. Everything else was extinguished on the way past. Interbank
loans were flipped straight to `defaulted` with no attempt to recover anything,
the central bank margin claim was cleared by an empty branch with a comment in
it, and the discount window was not mentioned in the failure path at all, so a
bank could draw emergency liquidity, fail, and have the draw quietly disappear.

The estate now pays down a real priority order. Secured central bank facilities
rank first, because a lender of last resort that can lose money on a failure
has to price failure risk into the one facility whose whole purpose is to be
available when a bank is already in trouble. Both facilities minted their
principal on the draw, so repaying them destroys the same money rather than
moving it. Household depositors come next, out of what cash is left, still
backstopped by the insurance fund and the treasury behind it. Interbank lenders
come third and recover pro rata across their outstanding principal, since that
cash really did leave another bank's vault. The owner comes last and is still
capped at book equity.

A claim the estate cannot cover is now written off explicitly instead of
outliving the bank it was against. The result reports what each tier recovered
and what it ate.

### Loans outlive the bank that made them

Every wind-up path claimed a failed bank's loans were left in place and kept
amortizing. None of them did. The turn loop services charters whose status is
`active`, so the moment a charter failed or was revoked its entire loan book
stopped being touched by anything: the borrower stopped paying, the asset sat
at full value on a dead charter forever, and the value reached nobody.

Those loans are serviced again, and where the payment lands depends on where
the estate is. A payment that arrives before the deposit book has been resolved
goes into the estate, which simply makes the waterfall bigger, which is what
the waterfall is for. A payment that arrives after resolution closed goes to
the currency's deposit insurance fund, because the insurer is what stood in for
the depositors and there is nobody else left to pay. Both routes run through
the same per-loan idempotency key as a normal servicing pass, so a retried turn
cannot charge a borrower twice.

### The turn loop moves money the same way everything else does

The remaining loan servicing, deposit interest, insurance premium, interbank
interest and NPC book legs were still hand-written pairs of updates. Several of
them were not pairs at all: interbank interest debited the borrower behind a
sufficiency guard and then credited the lender unconditionally, so a borrower
whose balance had moved produced a lender credit with no debit behind it, which
is money created by a race in the subsystem whose entire problem is money that
appears from nowhere. Loan servicing debited the borrower and left the bank's
side as an in-memory total flushed at the end of the pass, so a crash in
between destroyed the payment outright.

They all go through the shared movement primitive now: the key is claimed
before anything moves, both legs are checked to net to zero, and a half-applied
move records which half. Deposit interest keeps its bulk write, because its
counterparty is every saver at the bank with a different amount each, but it
claims the key first and settles the bank side behind a guard, so interest the
vault cannot fund is no longer paid.

The banking pass also stopped writing the cash balance from memory at the end
of the turn. Each leg applies its own guarded increment, so writing a
recomputed figure over the top could only resurrect money a failed leg never
sent. The legacy `reserves` mirror that duplicated the same balance is gone
with it, including on the bank console, where the headline and the surplus line
beneath it could previously be two different numbers on the same card.

### The money-move ledger is indexed

`bankMoneyMoves` is both the idempotency ledger and the repair queue, and the
repair queue is read by status, kind and age. That was a full collection scan
on a collection that grows by several rows per bank per turn forever, which is
a repair tool that gets slower every turn the world runs. It has indexes now,
in the seed module for a fresh world and in a migration for one already
running.

## Banning unions starts a general strike

A `union_law` provision with `banAction: "ban"` always had a real mechanical
effect: it sets `unionsBanned`, suspends every union and shuts the strike
state machine off. What it never had was a consequence. Enactment now fires a
wildcat general strike crisis in the enacting country: steel, car plants,
docks and freight all stop, with a smaller economy-wide financial shock for
everyone downstream of the freight that stopped.

The trigger fires on the transition, not the state: re-banning an already
banned country, banning a country with no unions, or passing a second ban
while the strike is on all refuse cleanly. The strike runs 24 turns unless the
executive ends it sooner.

### Four ways out, all real

Deploy the army (ends it now, costs the President favorability and every
labour archetype's approval of them), open talks (a per-attempt chance keyed
to union approval, a treasury concession per round, retryable because the
option loops back to its own node), ride it out (remaining effects halve, the
approval bleed does not), or repeal the ban through the same
`applyUnionLawProvision` primitive the legislature uses, restoring every
suspended union intact.

### Action hooks can now answer back

`runCrisisOptionAction` returns a `CrisisActionResult` whose `nextNodeId`
overrides the option's static target. Negotiation is the first user: whether
talks settled is known only inside the handler, and one static target cannot
carry both answers. Every pre-existing action keeps its behaviour.

Coverage runs through the same wire desk as the Vietnam arc: global and
national posts on the walkout and on whichever ending it gets.

## Release audit: banking settlement hardening

A pre-promotion audit of the banking rework closed the remaining paths where
cash still moved outside the money-movement primitive, or where a crash at
the wrong moment could create or destroy money:

- Guarded debits now always apply before credits inside a money move, so a
  move that fails its overdraw guard can only leave money undelivered, never
  money created.
- The NPC deposit flow and the solvency-turn deposit flight only book their
  aggregates when the underlying move actually applied; the flight itself now
  travels through the primitive instead of a raw pool credit plus an absolute
  vault write.
- The NPC bulk loan book settles its cash before the book is stamped, so a
  crashed pass replays its moves instead of skipping them.
- Central-bank margin and discount-window interest each settle as one guarded
  move per bank, replacing an unguarded per-bank debit whose matching
  central-bank credit was flushed once at the end of the pass.
- A replayed deposit-book return re-runs claim settlement and aggregate
  clearing, so a resolution that crashed halfway can no longer leave a live
  deposit book on a dead charter or pay the household book twice.
- The dead-bank loan servicer's per-turn scan of corporations now has a
  charter-status index behind it.

## Release audit: defence delivery pricing

- Delivery now charges the supplier a build cost graded to the delivered
  grade, matching the scale the contract's price band was struck in. Ungraded
  cost made every grade-0 contract underwater from its first delivery even at
  the band floor, and over-credited grade-3 deliveries by the scale the band
  charged but the build never paid.
- A failed arsenal deposit no longer draws lots back out of the arsenal
  before refunding. The deposit is atomic, so a failure means nothing landed;
  the old reclaim pulled the country's pre-existing stock of that component
  and refunded the contract on top.
- The order book shows "build cost unknown" instead of a zero build cost and
  a fabricated full margin when the certified plant no longer exists, and the
  break-even it shows is graded the same way delivery charges it.

## Release audit: crisis chains

- Chain successors and follow-up crises now start on the NEXT turn, so the
  turn loop announces them to the wire and inbox and applies their one-time
  effects. Spawned on the turn being processed, every rung after the first
  and every spread-unrest crisis arrived silently and its flat effects never
  fired.
- Chain rungs keep their authored durations. The 24-turn minimum flattened
  the Vietnam ladder's escalating 8-to-16-turn pacing into a uniform day per
  rung; the floor still applies to standalone crises so effects can taper.
- A war still above level 0 when the world leaves the family's era window
  winds down instead of respawning rungs forever.
- A follow-up crisis refuses to spawn into a country that already has an
  active copy of the same crisis, so two neighbours tolerating reform cannot
  stack identical crises on the same third country.

## Release audit: economy and UI polish

- Stockpile accrual is capped at the sector's actual unsold remainder, so the
  itemized per-commodity path can no longer store more units than went
  unsold.
- The commodity dislocation panel treats a missing or zero state price as a
  data gap instead of classifying the state as oversupplied.
- The sector stockpile toggle resyncs when the page refreshes, so two
  officers no longer see contradictory states.
- Bank cap inputs carry an explicit unit instead of the renderer guessing
  percent-vs-money from the label text.
- The defence order book and the state economy sector picker got small copy
  corrections.

## What landed in this release

72 changes shipped under 1.2.0.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Founder becomes first chair, swap inactive founders, distinct same-party election colors**
  A newly chartered party now starts with its founder as chair instead of headless, founders can swap out an inactive co-founder before the charter is signed, and single-party election graphs give each candidate a distinct color.
- **1.2.59: The economy is on the ballot**
  Presidential races now hold a referendum on the economy. A national misery reading built from unemployment, poverty, inflation, and real incomes shifts vote share toward or against the party holding the presidency, and a party seeking a third or later consecutive term wears a bad economy harder. The new National Mood panel on the race page shows the push, its parts, and the fatigue scaling before election day.
- **1.2.60: Credit for showing up**
  A government that answers a bad economy with real, costly legislation now keeps part of the National Mood penalty back. Bills enacted in the last 24 turns that push unemployment, poverty, or real incomes the right way forgive up to 40% of the penalty, with three rules that stop it being farmed. The old party-tenure fatigue drag inside the incumbency driver is gone; term fatigue now lives entirely in the National Mood channel.
- **1.2.3: Central bank chairs are appointed again**
  The rate-setting committee was quietly refilling the chair with its own technocrat every time the seat came up, which pre-empted the appointment process and left every nomination in the world unread. Chairs now go to the people the executive puts forward.
- **1.2.3: East Germany's budget page stops speaking American**
  The DDR national budget page had no copy of its own, so it fell back to the United States sheet and titled itself "Federal Budget: United States Federal Finances" over East German figures. It now reads as the Staatshaushaltsplan, with its own revenue, spending, and debt lines. A region's officials list is also scoped properly, so a parliament no longer comes back empty.
- **1.2.5: A technocrat chair steps aside when you nominate someone**
  A stand-in central bank chair used to hold the seat for a full four years, with nominations locked out for almost all of it. Countries can now put a name forward at any time, and the stand-in gives way as soon as one is on the table.
- **1.2.10: The lean map learns to show its work**
  Lean maps coloured every state by rounding its lean to the nearest whole point on a ten point ruler, so a country whose states all sit within a fraction of a point rendered as one flat centrist colour. Fills are now a continuous scale fitted to the actual spread, a legend states the scale, and clicking a state opens the demographic groups behind its lean.
- **1.2.11: State organization counts in the general too**
  The State Organization screens said the investment was for the presidential primary. It also counts in the general election. The copy now says so, and two campaign operations branches got period-correct names.
- **1.2.26: cabinet orders and estates now show their contribution on the metric board**
  The standing effect of your tier settings, ministerial orders and sited estates always moved the target a political metric drifts toward, but no screen ever showed it and the target printed on the Active modifiers panel left it out. Build ten field offices and the panel looked identical. It is now its own line, the target includes it, and the panel warns you when the channel has hit its ceiling and further building on that metric adds nothing.
- **1.2.27: every cabinet channel now has its own ceiling, and metrics move four times faster**
  Building an estate could be worth literally nothing. Every cabinet effect in the country shared one ceiling on each political metric, and on live worlds the standing tier settings had already filled it, so the next estate you built added zero. Orders, tier settings, the military, estates, energy and infrastructure now each carry their own ceiling, so a full channel can no longer silence the others. Political metrics also drift toward their target four times faster, so the effect is visible in a session rather than a month.
- **1.2.6: A no-confidence motion needs the whole chamber, not just the room**
  A vote of no confidence used to pass on a simple majority of the seats that actually voted, so a minority bloc could bring down a government while the governing benches stayed silent. A motion now has to reach the chamber's majority threshold to carry.
- **Campaign Presence replaces State Organization, and reputation stops deciding races on its own**
  State Organization is now Campaign Presence, paid for out of the campaign treasury instead of your personal actions, with no level cap and an escalating price. The map shows every candidate in the race, not just your own build. Reputation matters slightly less in the vote, support and attack actions cost more, and campaigns can now appoint up to three managers.
- **Every era gets a real political map**
  The historical electorates for 1953, 1979, 1991, 1999, and 2007 have been recalibrated from the ground up. States now differ from each other the way they really did, the economic and social axes finally measure different things, and the fifties world gains a road-to-1960 arc that starts pulling the country toward the Kennedy-Nixon dead heat.
- **Grid collapse and other standing crises can finally end**
  A grid collapse, a recession, mass protests, a coup attempt, an inflation spike and a currency crisis all read a national number to decide whether to fire, and then spent every turn pushing that same number the wrong way. Once one started it could never stop being justified, so it came straight back every time its cooldown expired. Crises now have to see a real recovery before they are allowed to fire again.
- **Laws and referendums learn to speak in real demographics**
  Legislation effects, policy approvals, and referendum ground games now work on real demographic buckets instead of the retired voter archetypes. On the way through we found that four out of five seeded legislation demographic effects had been aimed at groups that never existed and so had never done anything; the live ones are converted exactly, the dead ones are removed.
- **State tax bills can actually be written**
  Governors and state legislators could open the Tax category and find nothing to select. The state tax rate laws existed in the game's law book but had never been written into the running world, so the picker had no options to offer. They are now added to live worlds, and a state can legislate its own income, sales, corporate, and property tax rates.
- **Unions can send political contributions to organizers**
  Union heads can send a share of remaining per-turn budget to organizers as campaign funds, split by influence, at the cost of up to 5 approval.
- **Utility and electrification access stops reading 0 or 100**
  Every region in the world showed Universal Utility and Communications Access either flat at the bottom or flat at the top, with nothing in between, so the number told you nothing about whether your grid was any good. Two channels were pushing it to the extremes at a scale far larger than intended. Both are corrected, and an energy estate can now raise its region's grid instead of only ever damaging it.
- **Voter archetypes retire from every screen**
  The old voter archetype system (soccer moms, young renters, and friends) is deprecated and on its way out of the game entirely. Every screen that still showed archetype rosters now shows the real demographic buckets the engine actually votes with, and a bug that silently discarded all archetype effects in Ukraine is fixed on the way out.
- **1.2.48: The metric board names which cabinet channel is moving a number**
  A single "Cabinet, orders and estates" line could not tell you which of six channels was actually moving a metric, so a negative number looked like it came from a cabinet action nobody had taken.
- **1.2.50: Poll segment shares compare like with like**
  A segment's vote share was measured against the whole electorate while the race-wide figure was not, so the two numbers on the poll panel were never comparable and the difference between them meant nothing.
- **1.2.52: UK regions collect council tax again**
  Every UK region was collecting £0 of revenue while still being charged for the programmes it had enacted, so all twelve sat in permanent deficit and forced austerity quietly downgraded real enactments turn after turn. Regional revenue now comes from the region's own economy and the Westminster grant pool instead of a legislation lookup that no longer resolves.
- **Crises page polish and a historical brake on Vietnam**
  Crisis cards now round their effect numbers, name the regions they hit instead of showing raw codes, and carry the country flag with clickable region links. Separately, the Vietnam escalation ladder can no longer outrun history: each rung is now floored to its earliest real-world year, so the war climbs toward air campaign and ground troops on roughly the 1955 to 1975 arc rather than in a couple of game years.
- **Crises page: a Debt Watch tab, conflicts in the global feed, and real crisis art**
  The world crises page now keeps the sovereign debt watch on its own tab instead of a panel wedged above the list, shows live conflicts alongside global crises in one feed, and swaps out the crisis images that showed the wrong thing or failed to load at all.
- **1.2.52: Soviet republics stop billing council tax**
  A Russian republic's budget page listed Council Tax, Business Rates and a Westminster grant — all reading zero — beside a healthy positive balance, because the page had no revenue view for RU and fell through to the British one. Republics now show the union grant that actually funds them.

### Government and legislation

- **Governance Style**
  Liberal democracies now show a Governance Style national spirit that tracks political direction separately from democratic health.
- **The defence readiness setting now moves readiness**
  Setting a department to Elevated raises the readiness every formation trains toward instead of only raising the bill, and the four defence seats whose readiness tier wrote to no metric at all now carry a real trade-off.
- **East German government and budget corrections**
  East Germany's budget and government screens now agree with the systems that drive them. The borrowing limit has more room for early deficit spending, party bills rotate instead of repeating forever, and union services explain where approval is actually heading.
- **Energy plants no longer drag down grid reliability**
  Power stations were pushing their own region's grid reliability down no matter what they were built from, which showed up on the metric board as a cabinet penalty on utilities that no cabinet action could explain. A well built, diverse fleet now raises reliability the way it was meant to.
- **Shareholder votes protected from share restructuring**
  Open shareholder votes now retain the voting-power denominator from proposal time, and share splits or issuances cannot change that denominator mid-vote.

### War and diplomacy

- **1.2.53: The German Question**
  A standing four-power contest over West Germany's constitutional settlement, fought across four weighted institutions rather than on a single meter. Two superpowers, two Germanies and every character in the world can push it, and it runs over in-game years rather than an afternoon. Admin-started, behind settlementCrisisEnabled, and off by default.
- **1.2.1: Vietnam front dates and map**
  yearOfTurn now routes through calendarTurn. Vietnam static host geometry includes both halves, NVN/SVN capitals anchor the axis, and the geo build clips Natural Earth 50m instead of 110m.
- **1.2.10: defence procurement gets a speed limit instead of a freeze**
  A defence contract could settle a country's entire quarterly procurement budget in a single turn, and because a lot costs almost nothing to build next to what it sells for, nearly all of that money landed in one supplier's cash balance. The appropriation now has a per-turn payout limit, so an order fills over several turns rather than emptying the budget in one. Nothing is lost: across a full contracting window a buyer can still spend everything it is allowed to commit.
- **1.2.14: defence contracts buy materiel instead of printing cash**
  A defence lot sold to the state for hundreds of millions but cost about a thousand to build, so almost the entire contract was profit and the appropriation ended up as a number in one company's bank account rather than as equipment. Building a lot now consumes a realistic share of what it sells for, leaving the supplier a normal arms-industry profit. Arsenals also fill somewhat faster than before. Contracts already signed keep their agreed price.
- **Overseas plants of a domestic defence supplier can take its contracts**
  A UK defence minister could only award plants sitting in the UK, even when the supplier was a UK corporation with larger works abroad. The award list now follows the corporation, not the plant's host country, and the CEO order book names the contracted plant so extra production lines are not read as extra plants.

### Economy and corporations

- **1.2.54: The market numbers that never agreed**
  Eight open tickets across six reporters turned out to be one accounting mismatch: plants paid full cost on everything they made while booking revenue only on what sold, and the market-share pool grew whether or not anyone built into it. Plants now cut production toward what buyers actually take, building consumes market share the way founding always did, and the three market figures a sector screen shows are named apart instead of contradicting each other.
- **1.2.2: Refinancing a defaulted bond is priced on your books**
  Rolling defaulted debt into a replacement bond is no longer priced off the credit floor the default itself set. The rating now reads your actual balance sheet, so the fix for a default stops being more expensive than the default.
- **1.2.3: Being short of cash is not the same as being broke**
  A bond default now asks whether your company can cover its debt, not just whether your cash went below zero for a turn. Building with borrowed money leaves you cash-poor by design, and that alone no longer starts the default process.
- **Inflation reads price changes, not price levels**
  Consumer prices were being driven by how far commodity prices sat above their 1953 starting values, which only ever grows. That put a permanent 4 to 8 point floor under inflation in every country and kept central banks holding rates far above where they belonged.
- **Mines stop being judged on ore their state does not have**
  Extraction sectors were offering every raw material to the market, including the ones their state holds no reserves of. Those phantom loads never sold, and the unsold share was what the sector page used to call the market oversupplied. Mines are now measured only on what they can actually dig.
- **1.2.12: East German steel finally has somewhere to go**
  East Germany was seeded with heavy industry and almost nobody to buy from it. Its steel demand ran at a sixth of Czechoslovakia's on a larger population, and the CoCom embargo means the surplus cannot be exported west. New worlds now seed the DDR with the vehicle plants and building programme its steel was always meant to feed.
- **1.2.16: Electricity and gas ship, and freight capacity is a price**
  Energy and natural gas were classed as never shipped, so a state could only consume what it generated while their price was pooled across the country. They are now a third freight class, grid, carried by wire and pipe at a transmission loss. The per-state freight gate also stops being a hard stop: hauls run past nominal capacity at a surcharge, and the buyer's price ceiling is what ends the flow. Sectors now separate output nobody wanted from output that could not get there.
- **1.2.17: Brazilian bond maturities forced out of BRL get paid back**
  Brazil's 1953 baseline aimed monetary policy at 10% inflation, so the real itself ran away and every bond that matured was cashed out at a rate the bug set. Those forced conversions are now a registered repair, and the holders get the difference.
- **1.2.22: supply agreements reserve volume, they no longer blackhole surplus**
  A supply agreement now reserves its contracted volume for the buyer and lets the supplier keep selling everything above it on the open market. It used to be able to cap a supplier's total sales at the contracted amount, so a small exclusive deal could strand the rest of a much larger output at a few percent fill no matter the price. Agreements are additive again.
- **1.2.23: freight-limited sectors stop claiming sales they actually made**
  A sector selling all of its output could still read that most of it "went unsold" because a thin freight network could not carry it out of the state. That was a display contradiction, not a loss. The freight signal now describes the export constraint honestly, and unsold attributions count only units that actually went unsold.
- **Carved-out state enterprises now appear in the plan**
  Splitting a sector type into its own National Corporation under a command economy used to produce an enterprise the plan could not see: no entry in the enterprise table, no plan target, and no director seat to claim. The split now hands the new enterprise its place in the plan, and East Germany's eight missing enterprises have been repaired in place.
- **East Germany's state enterprises get their numbers straightened out**
  East Germany's state-owned enterprises were numbered under three different id layouts at once, and eight sector types had no enterprise of their own at all. The seeder now recognises an enterprise it already created under an older numbering and moves the sector onto the current one, instead of quietly creating a second copy of the same factory beside it.
- **Sector profit comes from the books, not from a percentage**
  A sector page now reports the profit the turn actually booked, and shows subsidies, tariffs, tech and state conditions as a money line you can open up, instead of burying them in an unnamed residual.
- **Sector revenue totals add up, and savings APY explains itself**
  The Total line under the Sectors table summed nameplate revenue while every row above it showed realized revenue, so the column did not add up to its own total. Savings APY is half the real rate, not half the prime rate, but both the banking hub and the wiki said otherwise.
- **The sector money panel adds up again**
  Revenue minus the cost lines on a sector page now equals the profit printed underneath, because an operating credit is shown as a credit instead of being rounded away to zero.
- **1.2.41: East Germany builds instead of stockpiling cars**
  The last fix gave East Germany the heavy industry its steel was meant to feed, but it over-built the car works, and the DDR cannot sell cars: every Warsaw Pact neighbour already makes its own and the West is embargoed. New worlds now seed a smaller auto plant and give the building programme the appetite to actually buy the crews it was sized for.
- **1.2.45: One credit rating instead of two**
  The bonds panel could show a different letter grade and a different score from the header, the health card and the peer comparison, for the same corporation at the same moment.
- **1.2.47: The capacity pill says what it is**
  The small pill showing capacity under construction read as part of the freight badge beside it, and its only explanation appeared on hover.
- **1.2.49: You can actually accept a central bank chair appointment**
  The nominee was told an appointment was waiting and given nothing to click, so the offer sat there until it lapsed and the old chair stayed in the seat.
- **1.2.51: A region's freight capacity now lifts its roads**
  Manufacturing was penalised for poor logistics in states where the freight sector was clearly over-supplied, because a state's road/transport reading moved only on government infrastructure spending and ignored the logistics sector entirely. Building freight now improves the metric the penalty reads.
- **1.2.52: Market share is your slice of the real market**
  A sector's Market Position pie could read "60% unowned" while every unit it made was selling, and you could not tell why building more would not grow your share. Market share now means what it should: your revenue over the total revenue of everyone actually producing in that market.
- **Private subsidiaries open for the parent CEO**
  Formalizing a subsidiary no longer crashes its corporation page for the parent CEO. The overview treated you as an outsider because a spin-off is run by someone else, then died while formatting a missing coupon rate.
- **Sector policy bonuses no longer show as losses**
  Opening "What is in policy, tax and support" on a sector page no longer paints a bonus as a red loss and a penalty as a green gain. Each modifier's money now follows the same sign as its percentage points.
- **1.2.53: Soviet grant allocations lapse instead of vanishing**
  The union handed its republics ₽44B a year that the national budget recorded as ₽0 spending, and nearly half of it evaporated unspent at the end of every turn. The centre now books the transfer it makes — but only what a republic actually draws down, because a plan allocation lapses rather than disburses.
- **1.2.58: Markets stop reporting themselves full**
  Every state sector claimed 100% market control, including the ones with no corporation in them at all, and exporting countries were told there was no room left for a new plant in any market they sell into. Both were arithmetic faults rather than real market conditions. Sectors now show the leading company's share, or say Empty when nobody is there, and a country that exports can once again see the demand abroad that nobody is serving.
- **Command economy capacity guidance**
  State-enterprise directors now see the real cost of investing into excess capacity, and National Corporation revenue uses actual sales.
- **Command-economy corporate values use the right currency**
  Eastern-bloc revenue, profit, cash, debt, and market values now convert from local currency correctly, and private corporations can no longer enter those state-controlled markets through a direct sector action.
- **Foreign sector rankings corrected**
  The global Sectors page now compares foreign operations in a common currency and shows the country where each sector actually operates.
- **Supply deals, Soviet confidence votes, and tax-rate clarity**
  Signed supply agreements now reserve their buyer-backed volume before the anonymous market and their links open the proposal form, Soviet deputies can use the USSR's no-confidence mechanism, and tax statutes distinguish a live phased-in rate from its enacted target.
- **1.2.59: Cap tables wear the company colour, build dialog takes a typed number**
  Shareholder pies drew from a fixed twelve-hue rainbow that said nothing about whose company it was, and rendered every holder, which on a real cap table means up to 251 slivers. Slices are now shades of the corporation's own brand colour, the tail past the top ten collapses into one slice, and every new corp is stamped with a random brand colour at creation. The capacity build dialog now accepts a typed number, carries plus and minus ten buttons, and multiplies any step by ten on shift and a hundred on ctrl.
- **Mothballing a plant no longer pays better than running it**
  A state-owned holding's operating profit is now built on what it actually sold, so a plant in cold storage reports the upkeep it burns instead of the profit it would have made if it had run.

### Interface and platform

- **Wiki: jargon tooltips, page reports, and task-first guides**
  Wiki pages now explain jargon on first mention, show when a page was last updated, take issue reports, and link out to design docs on core systems. New-player guides are click-path checklists with optional screenshot slots.
- **The Fundraise card quotes what it actually pays, and starting nationality is per character**
  The Fundraise action card advertised a yield converted at the live exchange rate while the money credited converts at the frozen campaign rate, so an East German politician was promised M1.8M and banked closer to M1.0M. Starting Nationality read an account-level field set from a player's first ever character, so a second character showed the wrong flag.
- **1.2.44: Wiki polish**
  A Guides section on the wiki home page, clickable category and guide badges, and jargon tooltips that only fire on real game terms instead of everyday words.

### Other changes

- **1.2.35: Test suites stop leaking stubbed globals into each other**
  A stubbed global fetch could survive from one test file into the next, so unrelated pull requests failed at random on a test that passed on its own.
- **Changelog badge and area values are checked where they are authored**
  The changelog vocabulary is defined once, the generator writes it into every new entry, and a bad value now fails at commit time instead of turning the build red for everyone else.
- **Changelog entries stop colliding between parallel branches**
  Every branch picked the next free version number the moment it was cut, so concurrent pull requests all wrote the same file and every one of them had to be merged by hand. Entries now carry the topic in the filename, so two branches write two files and the merge is clean.
