## Added

- Reform Movement: a Prague-Spring-style crisis for the USSR and its Warsaw
  Pact satellites (Poland, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Bulgaria, Hungary,
  Romania), available from 1960. Suppressing it militarily restores control
  but costs standing abroad; tolerating it risks the same unrest spreading to
  a neighboring satellite; genuine reform wins over reformers but can provoke
  a Hardliner Backlash crisis of its own.
- Four 1960s American protest crises: Civil Rights Marches, Campus Unrest,
  Urban Unrest, and Anti-War Protests. Each has real trade-offs, federal
  intervention, real legislation introduced to Congress, or a crackdown that
  moves different voter groups in opposite directions and risks the unrest
  spreading further.

- Warehousing: a new per-sector choice between selling everything at market
  and building inventory. With stockpiling on, unsold storable goods go into
  a pile instead of being lost. The pile spoils a little each turn, costs
  money to hold, and sells automatically when the market clears your fresh
  output in full. A glut becomes an asset you realize later, not lost money.
  Services cannot be stored.
- Commodity pages now show where each market is out of balance: which states
  are paying a shortage premium, which are sitting on goods they cannot sell
  locally, and, for mined resources, which states have untapped deposit room.

## Changed

- Autonomous corporations now pick where to build with local market awareness.
  New plants go to the state that is actually short of the goods they will
  make, mining and drilling only goes where the ground has room for it, and a
  company's home state is a preference rather than an automatic winner. This
  spreads supply toward demand instead of piling it up in a few states.
- Plants that chronically cannot sell now wind down instead of standing
  forever. Autonomous corporations stop replacing worn-out capacity in
  oversupplied states, and after a long stretch of unsold output they close a
  misplaced plant entirely and rebuild where the demand is. Growth decisions
  also now read the plant's own state market rather than the whole country.
- Your sector page warns you when a plant is stranded. If a plant has sold
  less than half its output for six turns in a row, a banner explains why the
  money is not coming in and lays out the options: shrink it, mothball it,
  sell it, or abandon it. Player plants are never touched automatically.
- Interstate shipping now adapts to what each state actually hauls. The fleet
  used to be split 70/30 between bulk goods and special cargo everywhere; now
  each state's split follows its real shipping mix from the previous turn, so
  a state hauling mostly electronics no longer wastes most of its trucks on
  an empty bulk lane.
- Electricity and natural gas prices now pool nationally. Both ride grids and
  pipelines rather than trucks and rail, so a state's price reflects the
  national network instead of a local accident of where plants happen to
  stand. Extreme same-turn spreads between neighboring states go away.

## Defence procurement rebuilt on obligations

### Obligations, not intentions

Awarding a contract encumbers its full cost against the defence appropriation
at the moment it is signed. Total live obligations can never exceed the
uncommitted appropriation, the check is a guarded database update rather than
a read-then-write, and an order the budget cannot cover is refused with the
uncommitted figure and the maximum affordable lot count in the response.

Delivery draws the contract's own commitment down rather than spending afresh.
Cancelling, declining, or completing an order releases whatever it still
holds, on both the country's books and the contract's, so budget is never
stranded behind an order that no longer exists.

Contracts awarded before this shipped carry no reservation. They keep
delivering out of uncommitted budget rather than stalling forever on a
commitment they were never given, and they cannot reach money another contract
has committed.

### Lots cost something to build

Delivery now bills the supplier the commodity inputs and overhead behind the
lots it ships, priced through the same realisation function the revenue side
uses, so a shortage squeezes both legs instead of only the cost side. The
supplier is credited price minus cost in one write, so payment is margin and a
contract can no longer inflate a balance one-for-one with the appropriation it
drains.

### Nothing is discarded silently

Every rounding and affordability boundary carries the work and records why:
part of a lot built, appropriation short this turn, more built than the order
still needs, supplier no longer eligible, no output. Both order books show
lots ordered, built and waiting, delivered, paid, and what the buyer still has
committed.

### One fill-eligibility resolver

The award picker, the award route, the CEO's accept and the delivery sweep now
call one function. Awardable means deliverable. State-owned suppliers and
suppliers banking in a currency other than the dollar are first-class
throughout, with regression coverage for the ticket cluster where the four
paths disagreed.

### Minister-set price and grade

A minister may set the price per lot inside a band whose floor is production
cost plus a margin and whose ceiling is the GDP anchor, and may order a grade
from cheap mass to premium. Grade scales both ends of the band and lands as the
delivered materiel's tier, so doctrine is a real choice. Both are validated
server-side; leaving them blank reproduces the previous behaviour exactly.

### Production lines

A defence plant runs four production lines and the contractor decides how many
work each order. Throughput scales with the allocation, the price per lot does
not, and the total across a plant's orders cannot exceed its lines. The default
allocation reproduces the previous even split exactly, so no live contract
changes rate.

### Self-dealing is disclosed

An award to a corporation the awarding minister owns or holds a material stake
in is marked on the contract, stated on the public wire, and charged against
the minister's standing, scaled by how much of the national contracting tranche
the order takes. The one-third per-supplier concentration cap is unchanged.

### Under the hood

- Money movements claim an idempotency key before any transfer, so a re-run
  turn cannot pay a contract twice. There are no transactions available here,
  so claiming first is what makes the retry safe.
- Recruiting and upgrading now spend from uncommitted appropriation rather than
  the raw balance, so a purchase cannot eat money a contract has committed.
- The procurement freeze kill switch is unchanged and still available.

## Banking rebuilt on a single balance sheet

Private banking stays switched off in the live world and this release does not
change that. What it changes is the reason it was switched off: balance-sheet
quantities that no cash stood behind, and screens that disagreed about numbers
players are held to.

### One balance sheet

`src/lib/banking/balanceSheet.ts` now owns book equity, regulatory capital,
required reserves, the deposit ceiling, the run line and the confidence
denominators. The cash module, the supervisor, the solvency pass, the console
API and the console UI read it instead of recomputing. Two surfaces disagreeing
on a required number is now structurally impossible, because there is only one
implementation.

The two deposit kinds are separated everywhere and named as such. Household
deposits are cash: the engine moves real money out of the central bank's pool to
create them. Player savings are a pointer: the balance never leaves the
character. Reserve requirements, lendable headroom, the discount-window cap and
equity now count only the cash-backed half.

### One way to move money

Deposit flows, the deposit-book waterfall, capital injections and upstreams go
through a shared primitive that claims an idempotency key before any balance
moves, refuses a set of legs that does not net to zero, guards every debit in
its own write, and records anything that starts and does not finish so an
operator can find and repair it.

### One way a deposit book ends

Failure, supervisory revocation, admin unwind and a charter switch away from
deposit taking all run the same waterfall. Revocation used to set a status and
stop, leaving depositors pointed at a dead bank. The charter switch and the
admin unwind used to credit the household book back to the money supply while
leaving the matching cash in the vault, so the same money existed twice and the
shareholder was then paid out of it. Priority is depositors, then other
creditors, then the owner, capped at book equity in the write itself.

Player deposit haircuts are gone. The old resolution destroyed money the bank
never held and then had the insurance fund pay for balances that had not moved.
Deposit insurance now stands behind the household book, which is the part made
of cash.

### Central bank savings interest has a payer

Interest on savings held at the central bank was credited with nothing debited
anywhere. It is currency creation, so it is booked as currency creation. The
player's credit is unchanged; the money supply now knows about it.

### Caps that explain themselves

The bank console shows every cap a player can hit with its formula and the
numbers currently in that formula, generated by the same module that enforces
it. The wiki guide gains the equity half of the deposit ceiling, the run line,
the prop leverage caps and the new failure treatment.

### Conservation is a test now

A simulation test runs a bank through charter, deposits, lending, interest,
upstream, failure and resolution against a real in-memory store and asserts
after every step that total money is unchanged except for explicitly minted or
burned amounts. Borrowed money never counts as capital.

## The country page is a directory now

The country lander answered "what is the debt to GDP ratio" before it answered
"where do I go". Reading order is now identity and vital signs, then a grouped
directory into everything the country contains, then the detail sections that
were already there. Nothing was cut: the descriptor, the national ideology band,
regime stability and the sovereign debt panels all moved below the directory
rather than out of the page. Staff and admin surfaces stay under the settings
gear and are not in the directory.

### Composition is a tested pure function

`buildCountryDirectory` takes the country config, the era preset and the batched
counts and returns the groups. It lives outside the client component because the
ordering and the gating ARE the design, and both are now covered without
mounting a page that fires half a dozen fetches. The tests assert group order,
per row gating, figure formatting, that every sub surface a player can reach has
an entry, and that no label or destination repeats.

Four groups, in reading order: Politics, Government, Economy, Nation. A group
with no rows is dropped rather than rendered empty.

### Rows carry a live figure and degrade one at a time

The counts route now also serves national GDP, the budget balance as a share of
GDP, union count, referendum counts and a Cold War DEFCON. Every field is
nullable and a missing one renders a plain chevron, so a country whose budget
document has not been created yet shows nothing rather than asserting a balanced
budget on a zero economy. GDP comes off `federalBudget.gdp`, which is in
absolute currency units, divided back to the millions `formatGDP` expects; the
balance is `surplus / gdp`, both from the same document so the units cancel.

Tones are used sparingly. A deficit is a normal state, so it only warns past
three percent of GDP. DEFCON reads downward, so 2 and below warn.

### Gates drop rows rather than linking to redirects

SCOTUS stays US only. The Command Economy dashboard appears only for a flag on
planned economy. Referendums appear only once a country has run one. The Cold
War row is served only for a principal (US, RU) in a world with
`conflictsEnabled`, resolved server side against the same switch
`/world/conflicts` itself uses, so the figure's presence IS the gate and
switching the subsystem off removes the row.

Two surfaces were considered and left out. Demographics has no country scoped
page to link to. The Commanding General page is role gated to one player per
command and 404s when conflicts are off, so it stays reachable from Conflicts
and the cabinet office rather than from a hub every player lands on.

### Phone first

The directory is one column on a phone, two on a tablet and one per group on a
wide screen, and every row holds a 44px tap target at every width. The hero
image was shortened so the directory starts above the fold on a phone.

## Defence procurement follow-ups

### One money primitive, not two

Defence contract settlement moves onto `applyMoneyMove`, the same primitive banking
uses, and the bespoke `defenceMoneyClaims` collection is deleted. A delivery is now
one keyed, net-zero move: the appropriation is debited, the supplier's margin is
credited, and the commodity inputs consumed building the lots are burned. Both
`$expr` guards survive as the debit leg's own filter, so a legacy contract still
cannot spend appropriation another contract has committed, and the primitive adds
its no-overdraft `$gte` on top.

Two consequences worth naming:

- **Recording now precedes payment**, reversing the old order. That order existed
  because a bare guarded `$inc` could half-land and `advanceContract` clamps, so a
  rollback would have been a silent no-op. The primitive refuses a move having
  touched nothing, so a failed payment leaves exactly one thing to unwind, and
  `reverseContractDelivery` undoes it exactly. The three-step refund, reclaim and
  re-bank ladder is gone.
- **A half-applied move lands in the shared repair queue** instead of being
  swallowed by a `catch`. The commitment drawdown stays outside the move, because
  discharging a reservation is a memo entry rather than money entering or leaving
  the world, and it is ordered to fail in the safe direction: the cash moves first,
  so a failure leaves a country under-spending rather than able to commit the same
  money twice.

### Contract costs track the commodity market

`lotProductionCost` has always accepted a price-ratio map and was always called
without one, so every contract was costed at the recipe's nominal input share: the
cost floor and the minister's price band did not move when steel did. Live world
ratios are now wired into the delivery sweep, the award route, the supplier picker
and the CEO order book, read once per sweep and once per request. An absent or
half-seeded price book falls back to the nominal share, so a world without a price
book behaves exactly as before.

This makes a contract able to go underwater after it is signed, which is modelled
rather than clamped away. A supplier delivering into a loss funds it from liquid
capital, and one that cannot fund the loss stalls with a reason on the contract
instead of delivering into a debit that would strand the move half-applied. The
band's ceiling is deliberately still the GDP anchor: costs squeeze the band from
below, they never widen it from above, so a commodity spike is not licence to pay a
supplier more than the economy says a lot is worth.

### One appropriation read per country per turn

The delivery sweep read the defence appropriation once per contract. It now reads
once per country and carries a running projection as it spends. Safe because the
read only ever informed the PLAN: every debit is still guarded inside its own
write, so the authoritative question is asked at the moment of payment. The
projection is what stops a country with ten contracts planning all ten against the
same opening balance and pushing nine refusals into the repair queue.

### The clawback rides the primitive too

The over-award remediation was the last defence money movement still hand-rolled.
It debited every supplier's liquid capital in one loop and credited every
appropriation in a second, with nothing joining the two: a throw between them
destroyed the recovered money outright, and re-running the heal debited the
supplier again because nothing recorded that the first run had paid.

It is now one keyed, net-zero move per contract, keyed on the heal run so a retry
of the same run replays and a genuinely new over-award found later still recovers.
A move that cannot settle stops the heal and sits in the shared repair queue with
the legs that landed, rather than being reported as a success. The contract stamp
replays with the money under the same run id, so a retry cannot double the recorded
lots. `apply` refuses outright when it is handed no run id, because unkeyed money
movement inside a retryable heal is the exact failure this primitive exists to stop.
