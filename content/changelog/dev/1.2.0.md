## Added

- Reform Movement: a Prague-Spring-style crisis for the USSR and its Warsaw
  Pact satellites (Poland, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Bulgaria, Hungary,
  Romania), available from 1960. Suppressing it militarily restores control
  but costs standing abroad; tolerating it risks the same unrest spreading to
  a neighboring satellite; genuine reform wins over reformers but can provoke
  a Hardliner Backlash crisis of its own.
- Four 1960s American protest crises: Civil Rights Marches, Campus Unrest,
  Urban Unrest, and Anti-War Protests. Each has real trade-offs, federal
  intervention, real legislation introduced to Congress, or a crackdown that
  moves different voter groups in opposite directions and risks the unrest
  spreading further.

## Changed

- Autonomous corporations now pick where to build with local market awareness.
  New plants go to the state that is actually short of the goods they will
  make, mining and drilling only goes where the ground has room for it, and a
  company's home state is a preference rather than an automatic winner. This
  spreads supply toward demand instead of piling it up in a few states.
- Plants that chronically cannot sell now wind down instead of standing
  forever. Autonomous corporations stop replacing worn-out capacity in
  oversupplied states, and after a long stretch of unsold output they close a
  misplaced plant entirely and rebuild where the demand is. Growth decisions
  also now read the plant's own state market rather than the whole country.
- Your sector page warns you when a plant is stranded. If a plant has sold
  less than half its output for six turns in a row, a banner explains why the
  money is not coming in and lays out the options: shrink it, mothball it,
  sell it, or abandon it. Player plants are never touched automatically.
- Electricity and natural gas prices now pool nationally. Both ride grids and
  pipelines rather than trucks and rail, so a state's price reflects the
  national network instead of a local accident of where plants happen to
  stand. Extreme same-turn spreads between neighboring states go away.
