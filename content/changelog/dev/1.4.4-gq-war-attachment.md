Cut from development.

## Changed

- A war declared **by or against** East or West Germany, against a member of the
  opposing bloc, now freezes the German Question and attaches it to that war.
  Whoever wins the war takes the settlement outright: East Germany's side wins to
  reunification under the East, the opposing side wins to West Germany keeping its
  independence. Previously the two systems never spoke, so the United States could
  declare war on East Germany and the board would keep ticking plays and drift,
  free to resolve on the index while the blocs fought over the very question being
  played.
- The escalation ladder is not required on this road. Force the Issue and Declare
  remain the four powers' own route to war; a declaration ratified by a country's
  chambers is now a second one, and whichever arrives first freezes the board.
- A war the question attaches to is renamed The War for Germany and gains both
  Germanies as host entities, so the Conflicts board and the war's own record say
  what is being fought over.
- A country pulled into a war only by its alliance's collective-defence charter
  does not trigger this. An East Germany that entered a war over Poland under the
  Warsaw Pact leaves the question in play; an East Germany somebody declared war
  on does not.
- If the Germany that anchored the attachment later leaves that war on a separate
  peace, the question comes back onto the board and the war gets its own name and
  hosts back, rather than being decided years later by a war it is no longer part
  of.

## Fixed

- The German Question page no longer reports "no settlement crisis is open" while
  the question is frozen. It now names the war that is deciding it and links to
  that war's record.
