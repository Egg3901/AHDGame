Cut from development.

## Fixed

- A formation's casualties are now scaled by its share of the whole side's
  strength at that front, not by its share of its own country's contingent. The
  casualty line carries a concentration term, `0.6 + share`, which is meant to
  make a force's biggest formations absorb the worst of an engagement. It was
  written for a battle with one army on each side, and the denominator was that
  army's total. When a side is a coalition the engine works out each nation's
  losses separately, so `share` renormalised inside every contingent: a lone
  allied division read a share of 1.0 and took the full concentration penalty as
  though it were an entire army fighting alone, while a division in a
  twelve-strong contingent read 1/12 and took almost none.

  The size of it: the same four divisions fighting under one flag lost 2,192 men
  and lost 2,817 under two, for no reason a player could see or act on. Across
  400 seeded engagements a twelve-division army split into four contingents bled
  36.6% harder than the identical army fighting as one. In a coalition of one
  large and three small contingents, the single-division ally was losing 2.36
  times as many men per division as its large partner, of the same type, in the
  same role, on the same ground.

  This was the sharpest of the reasons the smaller members of a coalition took
  casualties out of all proportion to what they contributed. It is gone: split
  and unsplit armies now bleed identically, and the per-division rate across a
  coalition sits within a percent or two whatever size each contingent is.

  Battles with one nation on each side are unchanged, man for man. There the
  side total and the contingent total are the same number, and the code keeps
  taking the old path.

- `scripts/sim/coalitionCasualtyShare.ts` measures what a battle COSTS, which
  the front-capacity work never did. That calibration asked whether the size of
  an army orders the result, a question about who wins, so a term that moved only
  casualties was invisible to it. The new harness reports exchange ratio, the
  per-division cost of each contingent in a coalition, and whether one army
  fighting as several bleeds the same as it does fighting as one.
