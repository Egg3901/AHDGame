## Fixed

A country that absorbs another now ends with exactly one primary National
Corporation. The absorbed state's is folded into the survivor's — its sectors,
its bonds, its shareholdings and its cash move across, and the empty shell is
dissolved.

This was one fault wearing three faces, all reported together. The State
Enterprises panel listed two primaries. Merging a split-off back put it inside
the wrong one, because every menu that resolves "the primary" reads a single
record and there were two to choose from, with the absorbed country's winning on
order alone. And the list of sectors available to split came up empty, because it
was built from whatever that wrong corporation happened to hold.

The damage underneath was the part nobody could see. Ten of the seventeen state
enterprises a planned economy needs had never been created, so their industry
fell through to that same wrong corporation — leaving the state's entire coupon
bill on one enterprise with no income at all, and every producing sector on
another that earned for nobody. Neither could pay anything into the treasury.

State enterprises now also follow the state's own money. A National Corporation
belongs to no region, and both the passes that move a company to a new country
and re-price its books onto that country's currency work region by region, so
neither had ever reached one: the unified state's enterprises went on quoting the
currency of the country that had dissolved. They are re-denominated at the same
rate every other pot of money crosses at. Where no rate exists the balance is
left alone rather than converted at par, because a wrong number on a company that
still exists can be corrected later, and a company nationalised abroad keeps its
own home country — owning a firm is not the same as housing it.
