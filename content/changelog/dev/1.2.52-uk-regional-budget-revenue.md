Cut from development.

## Fixed

- UK regional budgets showed £0 revenue against real spending. On the
  political-legislation preset the legacy `countryScope: "uk"` legislation types
  are deliberately unseeded and pruned, but the seeder kept writing one state
  policy per legacy UK regional type — 18 types across 12 regions, all pointing
  at legislation that no longer exists. The regional budget phase resolved
  council tax and business rates through exactly that lookup and got a silent
  zero, while the cost half of the same phase priced through the current law
  catalogue and kept working. Revenue read the database, spending read the
  catalogue, and only one of the two had been migrated.
- Because revenue was zero and costs were not, London ran a £59.0M/yr deficit
  for 91 consecutive turns and Scotland £55.6M for 98. Forced austerity fires
  after one turn over budget, so it had been downgrading genuinely enacted
  regional programmes one tier per turn for most of the world's life. London's
  health prevention, education choice and public housing had been driven to
  their lowest tier this way.
- A region's budget page reported £0.0M of spending while the ledger charged it
  £59.0M. The page priced enactments only through the legacy per-capita cost
  field, so current-catalogue laws contributed nothing to its breakdown. The
  page and the turn engine now price through the same function and agree.

## Changed

- Council tax and business rates are statutory shares of the region's own GDP
  (1.6% and 1.0%, calibrated on post-war UK local-authority accounts), scaled by
  the region's drifted property and commercial value indices so that letting
  services decay still erodes the tax base and costs the region revenue next
  turn. The Westminster grant is a population-proportional share of the national
  state-grants pool. This is the same shape Russia, Germany, China and Japan
  already use — the UK path had simply never been moved onto it.
- The old per-capita value bases are gone from the revenue formula. They were
  calibrated for the modern era with no era awareness at all: on the 1953 start
  they would have billed London £18.8B of council tax against a £17.5B _national_
  GDP. Anchoring to live regional GDP is era-proof by construction.
- Westminster now books the grant it pays. With no funding law left to record it,
  the transfer was credited to regions but never recorded as national spending —
  £250M/yr of expenditure that existed on one side of the ledger only.
