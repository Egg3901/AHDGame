Cut from development. Raised on ticket #1134.

## Fixed

- The defence appropriation had rules for how much it could commit and no rule
  at all for how fast it could pay. A single high-output plant could therefore
  deliver a whole twelve-turn procurement allowance in one turn, and on the
  live world one contract moved over four billion into a supplier in a single
  tick. The appropriation now pays out at most three turns' worth of
  procurement income per turn, and any one supplier may take a share of that,
  so a large order fills over several turns instead of all at once.
- Whatever a plant builds while it waits is banked, not destroyed, and the
  order book now says why: "the appropriation is limited in how much it can pay
  out in a single turn", rather than leaving a delivery number sitting still
  with no explanation.

## Not changed

- The per-turn limit cannot cost a buyer lots. A contracting window is twelve
  turns, and the per-turn allowance adds up over those twelve turns to three
  times what the same buyer is permitted to put under contract in the first
  place. It limits the burst, never the total.
- Ministers who were already running their suppliers at a lot a turn see no
  change at all. The limit only bites on a payout several times larger than
  anything a normal procurement programme moves.
- A country whose lot price is larger than its whole turn allowance still ships
  one lot a turn rather than deadlocking, and that floor is granted once per
  country per turn, so it cannot be stacked across many contracts.
- The emergency procurement freeze stays in place as a switch. It is untouched
  by this change and remains available if procurement ever needs stopping
  again.
