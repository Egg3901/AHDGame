Cut from development.

## Fixed

- The Commodities tab reported a corporation's output by dividing each
  sector's revenue by a base price. That was the right answer before plants,
  when revenue was the only proxy for what a sector made. Since then a sector
  measures what it physically produced, and the world market, the clearing
  book and the supply-agreement capacity check all read that measurement. The
  tab did not, so it was the last surface still quoting the proxy, and it
  disagreed with every other number a CEO could see. It now reports the
  measured production, split across a sector's outputs the same way the market
  splits it.
- Output from sectors abroad was inflated by the exchange rate. A sector books
  its revenue in the currency of the country it operates in, and the tab
  divided that figure straight into a base price without converting it first.
  A French sector was reported at several hundred times its real output, which
  was enough to put a corporation's smallest product at the top of its own
  commodities list.
- An extraction sector operating in a state with no deposits of a resource
  showed output for it. Every other surface already refuses to credit a
  sector with a resource the ground does not hold.
- A mothballed plant reported full output and full input purchases. A cold
  plant makes nothing and buys nothing.
- The share of output written off under an export embargo was counted as
  reaching the market.
- Input consumption ignored how hard a plant was actually running. A plant at
  60 percent of nameplate was billed for 100 percent of its inputs.
- Media output was reported at ten times what reaches the market. Media sells a
  fraction of its nameplate onto the advertising book in every economy, which
  the market has always applied and the tab never did.
- A command economy's media was listed as producing advertising. A planned
  economy has no advertising market: its media output is state information, and
  it is derated differently again. Both the commodity and the quantity were
  wrong on every bloc media corporation.
- Output delivered to a state arsenal was counted as reaching the market, and
  once a contract ended the deduction was applied forever instead of not at
  all. The diversion is not cleared when a contract closes, so it has to be
  read with its age checked, which is what the market itself does.
- Sectors in countries whose currency is not player traded were reported at
  between 13 and 27 times their real size. Those currencies have no exchange
  rate to look up, so the tab treated their local revenue as though it were
  already converted.

Extraction is the one exception, and deliberately so. Its output depends on a
per turn rationing pass over every field in a state and the live contract book,
which a page cannot re run without becoming a second source of truth that
drifts from the first. Extraction is reported the same way the sector pages
report it, which is the comparison this ticket was about.

## Changed

- A corporation's charted commodity history is derived the same way as its
  live Commodities tab, so the chart and the tab can no longer disagree. Both
  now run off one shared derivation rather than two copies of the formula.
  History recorded before this change was written on the old proxy and is left
  as it was, so a series that spans the change will step where the two meet.
