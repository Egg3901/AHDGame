## Added

- **Nuclear assessments.** Strategic coverage on a country now reads out as a
  graded assessment. Below a working threshold you learn nothing. Past it you
  learn whether a weapons programme exists at all, which is worth a great deal
  when the alternative is guessing. Past that you get an estimated warhead count
  and a sense of how developed the programme is. At the top you get the real
  numbers.
- **Estimates that do not wash out.** A fogged figure is stable for as long as
  your coverage is, so refreshing the page will never average the error away.
  Two estimates about the same country are fogged independently, so you cannot
  recover the truth by comparing them.
- **Undeclared programmes can be found, and set back.** An undeclared programme
  shows as a suspicion first and a stage only once your coverage is deep enough.
  A successful covert action against one costs it the stage it was working on
  and one it had finished. Done by an outside power the setback is quiet, and
  the programme simply loses ground for reasons nobody in the capital can see.
- **When the patron finds it, everyone hears.** East Germany is building under
  Moscow's nose. If Moscow is the one who finds the programme and acts, the
  result is the same public raid, rebuke and tension spike the programme has
  always risked, rather than a quiet act of sabotage.
- **Being traced back now moves the world.** An intelligence operation in the
  strategic domain that is attributed to whoever ran it raises world tension.

## Changed

- The covert programme's own discovery roll is unchanged. A foreign service
  finding the programme is a second, independent route to the same crackdown,
  not a replacement for the programme's own carelessness.
