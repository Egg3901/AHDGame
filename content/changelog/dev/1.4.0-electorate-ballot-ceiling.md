Cut from development. Part of the presidential rework, evidence-and-correctness
epic.

## Fixed

- Each state's per-turn vote pool is capped at its electorate (voting-eligible
  population, falling back to total population where cohort data is not yet
  seeded), with the per-turn slice rescaled proportionally. Previously the
  granular electorate substrate could aggregate turnout over overlapping
  demographic dimensions into a pool far larger than the people who exist:
  the audited 1956 general certified 333% of the eligible electorate, and
  Kansas alone recorded 196% of its living residents voting.
- Because the count pacing conserves whatever pool it is given, the cap also
  makes total recorded ballots invariant to how long the counting window
  runs, closing the artifact where totals grew 2.6x between cycles as the
  window and engine internals changed.

## Guarded

- Vote shares are invariant to the pool basis, so standings, margins, state
  winners, and every resolution outcome are byte-identical; only the reported
  ballot magnitudes change. A regression test pins that an inflated pool
  produces identical distribution inputs to a pool exactly at the ceiling.
