1.16 collects everything merged to development after 1.15.0. The headline is NPP engine 6: non-player politicians get their own version line (6.0.0 to 6.17.0), their own changelog and roadmap at [/changelog/npp](/changelog/npp), and a new autonomy tier, v6, that switches every engine 6 change on. Engine 6 changes how well NPPs pursue their existing activities, never what they may do or what money and action points they receive. Alongside it: corporation autopilot, company revenue moving onto real market prices in even steps through turn 200, very large share sales and issues filling over several turns, a staged correction to the commodity market so prices stop climbing on a shortage that new capacity could never close, and supply, banking and interface fixes.

Coverage: base v1.15.0 source 1d92a71f06, source cd7a7f0703. Every feature and fix merge in that range carries a note; promotion back-merges and the 1.15 post edits are not listed.

## NPP engine 6

All engine 6 behavior runs only in worlds at autonomy **v6**. New singleplayer and simulation worlds default to v6 from this release (6.14.1, and the desktop client in AHDClient#137). Existing worlds keep their level, and the live multiplayer world now runs V6, so nothing below changes NPP decisions there after its V6 activation. Two cost changes run at every level with identical outcomes: batched ministerial writes (6.14.3) and the CEO track-record bookkeeping (6.17.0, read by no decision below v6). The 6.17.0 dead-NPP CEO fix applies from v5.

Owner constraints carried through every version: NPPs get smarter, not stronger. No version raises NPP action points, income, starting money or election weight. The NPP election modifiers and income rates are unchanged; once worldsim measures how much stronger v6 makes NPPs (Stage 5, not yet run), the income NPPs receive without acting is to be stepped down to hold strength level with engine 5.

## What landed in this release

109 changes are covered, including the 15 October follow-up.

### Elections and campaigns

- **NPP engine 6.1: parties run their strongest NPPs for the senior races**
  At autonomy v6, a party filling open primaries with NPPs places its strongest available NPPs in the most senior offices, and its nationwide executive slot goes to its strongest NPP.
- **NPP engine 6.2: NPP candidates rally their own race**
  At autonomy v6, a running NPP's first campaign action each cycle becomes a rally in its race, at the same cost and with the same Support effect as a player rally, instead of a general influence gain.
- **NPP engine 6.3: presidential primary challengers in countries without players**
  At autonomy v6, presidential primaries in countries with no players can draw a same-party NPP challenger, picked from the party's five strongest available NPPs nationwide.
- **NPP engine 6.4: trailing NPPs leave late primaries**
  At autonomy v6, a clearly trailing non-incumbent NPP withdraws from an NPP-only primary in its last 12 turns, without endorsing, and no new NPP challenger enters in that window.
- **NPP engine 6.9: ambitious NPP incumbents run for higher office**
  At autonomy v6, a single-seat NPP incumbent with ambition 70+ and strength 100+ files for an open, more senior race in its home region instead of defending its seat.
- **NPP engine 6.12: weak parties run their best NPPs where they can win**
  At autonomy v6, a party whose regional organization is under 75% of the leading party's places its strongest NPPs in its most local races instead of its most senior ones.
- **Load slate details on demand**
  Slate cards now load candidate assignments when opened, reducing detail requests for parties with many races while keeping each race's current controls available.
- **Faster US House seat projections**
  US House seat projections now load district data once across eligible races instead of race by race, with the same projected results.

### Government and legislation

- **NPP engine 6.10: sponsor vote counts include the government whip**
  At autonomy v6, the NPP sponsor's pre-filing vote count applies the 6.7 government soft whip to the governing party's members on goal bills, matching how those bills are voted.
- **NPP engine 6.5: NPP sponsors count the votes first**
  At autonomy v6, an NPP sponsor projects the lower-chamber vote with the no-whip voting forces and sets aside a bill that is short, trying up to three bills before filing nothing.
- **NPP engine 6.7: NPP governments whip their own goal bills**
  At autonomy v6, an NPP-led government puts a soft whip on its own members for the NPP bills it sponsored in a standing-goal domain, never where a whip already exists or a player leads the party.
- **NPP engine 6.8: NPP business stakes shape their votes**
  At autonomy v6, an NPP that runs or holds at least 1% of a company leans for subsidies and sector tariffs in that company's sectors and against ending them or nationalising its company, capped below a hard whip.
- **NPP engine 6.11: the opposition picks its battles**
  At autonomy v6, the opposition's bloc vote against government bills applies only while the government side holds under 55% of the lower chamber; against a commanding government opposition NPPs vote on the merits.
- **NPP engine 6.14: voters mark down NPP votes their holdings decided**
  At autonomy v6, an NPP whose own business interest decided its vote loses 1 favorability, once per voting pass, floored at zero.
- **NPP heads judge ministers on their goals**
  At autonomy v6, an NPP head of government judges a minister on the progress of the standing goals in their brief, keeping one who is turning a domain around and replacing one whose goal failed on their watch.
- **Enacted laws and Cabinet initiatives now move the V2 metrics they target**
  Metrics without a live source no longer sit at their opening value; funded laws and active Cabinet initiatives now move them toward their settlement each refresh.
- **NPP engine 6.13.1: NPP business-interest votes are visible**
  At autonomy v6, when an NPP's own companies pull its vote, its profile's vote forecast names the business interest.

- **NPP engine 6: NPPs trade on market opportunity**
  At autonomy v6, an NPP's chance to buy stock or bonds, or to trim a share position, scales with how good the opportunity is, normalized so the expected number of trades is unchanged.

  At autonomy v6, an NPP central bank chair sets a reserve-backed intervention band after a 15%+ currency fall over 24 turns and removes its own band once the move settles inside 5%.

- **Corporation budgets use your actual daily revenue**

- **Protect NPP investments and focus subsidiary industries**

  Mixed-output mines keep selling usable coal when a byproduct cannot be sold, and own-first supply can use another mine when the nearest haul is limited.
- **Staged commodity market correction**

## Detailed release notes

### New players: closed states, drafts and retries

- `src/lib/auth/intakeStatus.ts` computes whether registration and character creation are open (maintenance mode, `registrationEnabled`, expected end). The register, login and `/create-character` pages, `/api/auth/signup-methods` and `POST /api/auth/character` all read it, so a page never offers a form its route refuses. Gates are unchanged: partial maintenance still blocks creation, and admins bypass it.
- `/create-character` is a server page. It sends only a real no-session visitor to login and shows a closed panel with the countdown instead of the form. The form saves a per-account draft on the device (and immediately on submit), restores it with the party re-applied once options load, and shows Try again for a failed session or data load. Only a definite 401 goes to login.
- New accounts with no past character see "your account is saved" copy on `/maintenance`.
- The community Discord invite moved from an expired code to the permanent `DmF8zJJuqN`.

### New players: intake measurement

- Anonymous PostHog counts (`intake_anonymous: true`: random per-event id, no person profile, geoip off, enumerated tokens only): `landing_viewed`, `landing_cta_clicked`, `signup_viewed`, `signup_started`, `intake_blocked`, `create_character_*_failed`, and server-side `account_registered` (all four sign-in methods) and `character_registered`.
- `__ahd_src` (httpOnly, 30 days, shared with the wiki subdomain) holds the referring site's coarse domain, utm tokens and a landing area, and is copied to `users.acquisition` at signup. `users.lastSeenAt` is stamped once per UTC day from `/api/auth/me`.
- The consented `account_created` now carries `auth_provider` and fires for Google, Discord and Apple signups through a 15-minute provider marker. Consented tutorial events: plan chosen, step viewed, chapter completed, finished, closed.
- `GET /api/admin/intake/summary` and the admin Traffic tab intake panel cover every non-staff account: character within 1h/24h, back after signup day and after 7 days, by provider and source. The privacy policy describes the counts and both cookies.
- The cookie banner on small screens uses compact type, full-width buttons, `max-h-[40vh]` and an in-flow spacer. At 390px its height went from 268px to 188px.

### New players: campaign-first onboarding

#### Why

On the 1991 world, 29 of 61 characters created after the founding races were still at 0 political influence days later, against 16 of 109 launch characters. Political influence only rises through Campaign. Per-turn processing only decays it, and offices feed national influence and AP. Launch players were pushed to campaign by the founding scramble; later joiners were not.

#### What changed

- `campaign-action` is in the core chapter for every plan and counts only `actionType: "campaign"`.
- `first-vote` is `optional`: shown as a later step and excluded from `completedCount`, `total` and `allComplete`. The completion reward (unchanged, 20% of starting funds) is therefore claimable by joiners in their first session.
- `onboarding.steps.<id>` is stamped on first completion for every derived step (first write wins, via `after()` on profile views for characters under 30 days old). campaign-action uses the earliest Campaign in `actionLogs`. An anonymous `onboarding_step_done` count is sent per first stamp.
- The admin intake summary adds founding vs mid-world cohorts: Campaign within 24h (from `actionLogs`, so pre-release cohorts are included), zero influence after 72h, and per-step counts. Targets for mid-world joiners: Campaign within 24h at 70% or more, no influence growth after 72h at 25% or less.
- New characters start at `NEW_CHARACTER_STARTING_INFLUENCE` (10, the NPP seed) via `max(gameConfig.startingPoliticalInfluence, 10)`, with the existing 10% referral bonus on top. `startingPoliticalInfluence` is stored on the character. Because joiners no longer start at 0, the cohort metric is "no influence growth after 72h" (current influence not above the stored start, 0 when absent), which equals the old "still at zero" measure for pre-1.16 characters and keeps the 45% baseline comparable. The panel also counts characters that started at 10.
- The first-days panel (`src/lib/onboarding/firstDays.ts`, `FirstDaysPanel`) shows for 72 hours or until all three goals are met: Campaign three times from the panel, join a party, and enter a home race still taking candidates (entry closes at `primaryEndTime`, the same rule as the entry route). Skippers get a link to the core tour.

### Choose own supply first across states

Company supply settings now offer an optional own-first cross-state toggle. It stays off for existing companies. Own local plants keep first claim, and shipping still needs a reachable domestic route and freight capacity. The supply panel shows recorded own hauls and freight per unit in anchor currency so you can judge whether hauling outweighs your internal discount. Reservations use the previous turn's deliveries.

### Clear retries for interrupted corporation responses

The corporation page now offers a clear retry when its data response is empty or unreadable, while keeping previously loaded details visible during refreshes.

### Corporation autopilot

#### What changed

- The corporation Overview has an Autopilot switch for the CEO. You stay CEO and keep every manual action.
- Each turn autopilot sets marketing, logistics and R&D budgets and the dividend, grows plants that sell, pauses plants in a glut, and opens new plants when it has cash to spare. Budgets and dividends you change by hand are reset on the next turn while it is on.
- Autopilot never borrows, picks research, retools, sells or closes plants, or buys rivals. When one of those is worth doing, it lists it on the Overview with a button to the right tab, and sends an inbox message for urgent ones (nationalization risk, debt larger than profit, losing sectors, plants that cannot sell).
- Corporations on autopilot are not penalized for an inactive CEO.
- A CEO whose corporation is on autopilot earns at most the standard CEO national influence (0.15 per turn). The 0.3 tier for the 10 largest listed corporations requires running it yourself.
- Appointing an NPP caretaker switches autopilot off.

### Corporation Overview: Simple and Pro views

#### What changed

- The Overview tab has a Simple / Pro switch, also in Settings under Appearance. The choice is saved as `users.corpOverviewMode` through `PATCH /api/settings/corp-overview-mode`, returned by `/api/auth/me` and `/api/client-nav`, and cached in local storage for signed-out viewers. The account value wins over a stale device value. Pro is the default, so nobody's page changes until they pick Simple.
- Simple shows a Needs attention list, an At a glance list and the five largest sectors. The attention rules live in `overviewModel.ts` as a pure function with unit tests: negative cash, cash runway under 48 turns at the current retained rate (red under 12), a net loss, defaulted bonds, bonds maturing within 48 turns, sectors suspended by embargo, losing money or in the low fill band (mothballed sectors excluded), a leverage grade below B, and open shareholder votes.
- Pro removes the four stat tiles, which repeated figures already in the list below. Share price, revenue, net income and cash are now headline rows. Net income and short cash get a tone rule. Margins get a diverging bar, sector revenue a bar scaled to the largest sector, and quality, fill and float a gauge. Losing sector rows get a red left edge. Table heads and totals have a tint, and the leverage rating is coloured by grade.
- Every corporation-page section heading now has a primary accent tick. `KVRow` gains `lead`, `accent` and `mark` props, and the inline marks live in `dense/InlineViz.tsx`.

### NPP engine 6.0: its own version line, changelog and roadmap

#### What changed

- **NPP engine changelog.** NPP behavior now has its own version line, starting at engine 6.0.0, with a changelog and the overhaul roadmap at [/changelog/npp](/changelog/npp). The main feed links to it. Earlier autonomy tiers are recorded as engine 0.x to 5.x.
- **Autonomy tier v6.** A new tier above v5 that switches on engine 6 behavior. It allows exactly the activities and resources of v5. No running world changes level: production stays on v4, fresh worlds still default to v4.
- **Staggered action cycle at v6.** NPPs take an action cycle once every four turns. Below v6 all of them act on the same turn; at v6 each NPP has a fixed lane and a quarter act on each turn. Each NPP still acts once every four turns with the same action points. Share and bond buying follow the lane; share selling, party organisation building and company founding stay on the shared fourth turn.
- **Engine version on turn logs.** Each turn log records the NPP engine version it ran.
- **Wiki.** The NPP autonomy page now covers v5 and v6.

#### Why

On live turn 168 the shared action cycle took 27.6 seconds (18.0 s share buying, 4.7 s bond buying) on one turn in four and about 5 ms on the other three.

#### Scope

Behavior changes only in worlds set to autonomy v6. Worlds at v5 and below are unchanged, apart from one extra settings read on turns between action cycles.

### NPP engine 6.1: parties run their strongest NPPs for the senior races

- At v6, a party's nationwide executive primary slot goes to its strongest available NPP rather than the lowest id.
- The NPP election context now loads political influence and favorability.
- NPP engine version 6.1.0, recorded in the [NPP changelog](/changelog/npp).

Worlds below autonomy v6 are unchanged. Production runs v4.

### NPP engine 6.10: sponsor vote counts include the government whip

#### What changed

- For governing-party members on a goal bill, the count uses the soft whip in place of the party-line lean, exactly as 6.7 voting does.
- Government directives now report whether the head of government is an NPP.
- NPP engine version 6.10.0, recorded in the [NPP changelog](/changelog/npp).

#### Scope

Worlds below autonomy v6 are unchanged. Production runs v4.

### NPP engine 6.2: NPP candidates rally their own race

#### What changed

- At autonomy v6, when an NPP is an active candidate in a race where players can hold rallies, the first campaign action of its four-turn cycle is a rally in that race.
- The rally costs the campaign action's existing action points and funds and replaces that action's political influence gain.
- It moves the candidate's Support exactly as a player rally in that race family does: the full value is 10 times the race-family scale, 60% applied at once and 40% over four turns. Support decays toward 50 as before.
- An NPP rallies at most once per cycle (players can rally once a turn), and never twice on the same turn.
- The NPP action signals now carry the NPP's own race: the candidate row, the race type, whether it is rally-eligible, its Support, and its last rally turn. These come from the same two queries as before, with wider projections.
- Turn results report `rallies`.
- NPP engine version 6.2.0, recorded in the [NPP changelog](/changelog/npp).

#### Scope

Worlds below autonomy v6 are unchanged. Production runs v4.

### NPP engine 6.3: presidential primary challengers in countries without players

#### What changed

- At autonomy v6, the NPP primary challenger pass also covers presidential primaries in countries that are not enabled for players. Below v6, and in every country with players, presidential primaries stay excluded as before.
- A presidential challenger is drawn from the party's NPPs across the whole country instead of the primary's state, limited to the five strongest available (political influence plus favorability), then weighted by office-seeking as in other primaries.
- The challenge probability (15% base times office-seeking) and the per-country limit of two challengers per pass are unchanged.
- NPP engine version 6.3.0, recorded in the [NPP changelog](/changelog/npp).

#### Scope

Worlds below autonomy v6 are unchanged. Production runs v4.

### NPP engine 6.4: trailing NPPs leave late primaries

### NPP engine 6: NPPs trade on market opportunity

#### What changed

- At autonomy v6, the stock and bond buy sweeps score the market before an NPP decides, instead of rolling a flat chance first.
  - Stock: the best fundamental-to-price ratio on offer over the NPP's own value floor, squared.
  - Bond: the best current yield over the 1% yield floor, square-rooted.
  - The base chance (stock 0.075, bond 0.05, times risk tolerance) becomes base times a multiplier proportional to that score, capped at 2.
- The stock sell sweep responds to overvaluation more sharply: the engine 5 price ratio multiplier is replaced by the cubed ratio of price to fundamentals, capped at the engine 5 ceiling of 2.5.
- Volume is held: the multipliers are scaled across everyone deciding in the sweep so the expected number of purchases (or sales) equals the engine 5 expectation on the same candidates. A cheap market draws more of the buying and a dear one less; the total does not grow.
- NPPs with nothing worth buying hold their cash, as before. Candidate choice, trade size, float reserves and the CEO stake exclusion are unchanged.

- Household and government purchases are bounded at 1.25x last turn's supply instead of 1.5x, so building capacity actually narrows the gap.

- Surplus extractable stock above 96 turns of cover spoils faster, clearing the timber glut.

### Economy-wide demand matches each country's size

### Companies buy inputs for what they make, not for what prices did

- A company's demand for its inputs was measured from its revenue, so when prices rose, every buyer looked like it needed more steel, energy, chemicals and services. That extra demand pushed prices up further, which raised demand again, and most markets stayed short however much was built.
- Input demand now follows the volume each company actually produces, with the price premium it earned taken out. The change phases in over 24 turns once it is switched on, so producers have time to adjust.
- Expect shortages to ease and prices to fall across most markets as it phases in. A few markets that grew to meet the inflated demand, such as electronics, software and iron, may move into surplus.

### New entertainment companies where a country has none

- New computer-run companies were founded only by the world's wealthiest investors, so countries with poorer investors never got a new firm in an empty market. Russia had no entertainment business at all while entertainment sold at more than three times its base price.
- On founding turns, a country with a severe entertainment shortage and no entertainment business now gets one new entertainment company, founded by its wealthiest eligible investor. It then grows into the country's open markets like any other company. Command economies are excluded.
- Fixed computer-run companies not recognising entertainment markets as separate from general media when deciding where to expand.

- A player company handed to a caretaker that later showed no CEO now gets a caretaker again on the next turn. The owner keeps the right to reclaim it.

### NPP operators take over plants that corporations abandon or dissolve

- When a corporation abandons a sector, dissolves, or liquidates sectors in a bond restructure, an NPP operator in the same industry takes over about 75% of each plant's built capacity. The rest closes and that share of the market reopens to builders.
- The operator is an NPP already running that industry in the same region, then one elsewhere in the same country, then a new NPP corporation.
- Unfinished construction is refunded and cancelled as before. Payouts to the leaving corporation and its shareholders are unchanged.
- Extraction concessions and defence contracts move with the plants that hold them.
- Recent large exits in the current world are being restored the same way, sized from the markets' remaining free demand.

### NPP-run plants shrink to the market they serve

- A plant run by an NPP that has produced under 60% of its capacity for 24 turns, because demand will not take more, now gradually retires the idle capacity. It keeps room to grow back by half if demand returns.
- This cuts the payroll and upkeep those companies were paying on unused plants. Player-run plants are not affected.
- Applies in worlds on NPP autonomy V6.

### Rare earth shortages now draw new mines

When a raw resource runs short, AI-run mining companies retool existing mines and open new ones on idle deposits. That choice compared deposits by raw unit count, and a unit of rare earth is worth far more than a unit of iron or gas, so rare earth deposits always ranked last and never got a new or retooled mine even while rare earth sold well above its normal price. Deposits are now compared by value, so a rare earth shortage gets its share of new and retooled mines.

### Revenue follows output that actually sells

- Producers were paid for their whole output even when the delivery network found no buyer for part of it. A new phase-in moves revenue onto the share of output that was actually placed with a buyer, evenly over 24 turns.
- It does nothing until an operator sets its start turn, so it can be sized against live data first.

### Output stuck behind freight shortages keeps earning

- When a producer's goods are wanted but the local freight network cannot carry them, that output now keeps its revenue. The phase-in only stops paying for output that found no buyer at all.

### A revoked bank charter no longer blocks fund sponsorship

- A company whose bank charter was revoked can now charter a sponsored fund, including a Global one, like any financial corporation that never held a charter. Before, the revoked charter still counted against it and blocked every fund.

### Commodity prices come down faster once a shortage starts to ease

- A commodity whose shortage is shrinking used to shed its scarcity premium more slowly than one whose shortage was not changing at all, so recovering markets stayed expensive for many extra turns.
- Recovering markets now unwind their premium by 4% of the excess each turn, halving it in about 17 turns. Markets where the shortage is getting worse still rise as before, and unchanged markets keep their current slow decay.

### Faster construction where output is short

- While the switch is on, a plant expansion in a sector whose output is more than 25% short finishes in half its remaining build time, once per order.
- Orders already under construction speed up too. Capacity already delivered and money already spent are unchanged; the rest of the order simply arrives sooner.
