Cut from development.

## Fixed

- The war log names each belligerent's own dead. A battle result recorded a
  single country per side, chosen as whoever declared the offensive, beside a
  casualty figure summed across every ally that fought in it. A two nation
  attack therefore printed one nation and both nations' losses, which read as
  the declaring country having taken casualties it never took. The live report
  for the joint attack on the United States credited East Germany with 10,939
  Russian dead on top of its own 5,360.

- A nation that joins an ally's offensive now sees that battle in its own war
  room. Reports were looked up by who declared the attack and who was named as
  the target, and an ally that joins a merged offensive is neither, so a country
  could lose ten thousand men in a battle its war room never listed at all.

- The war room reads a joined offensive from the joining nation's side. Because
  an ally matched neither the declarer nor the target, every perspective the row
  is built from fell through to the defending branch: an ally was shown losing a
  battle its own side had won, fighting its own coalition leader, with the
  enemy's casualties printed as its own.

- A front's cumulative record counts each nation's dead against that nation.
  The per country totals were folded from the same single country per side, so
  a front's record credited an entire coalition's losses to whichever ally had
  led the attack.

- Commanders see their own order of battle in an allied engagement. Per battle
  rosters were built one per side and labelled with the declaring nation, so a
  commander whose country joined someone else's offensive was shown no roster of
  the battle their own troops had just fought in.

- A proxy force is charged only its own casualties. Where a real nation defends
  a front alongside a generated faction, the faction's strength was reduced by
  the whole defending side's losses, so it was ground down by its ally's
  casualties as well as its own.

## Changed

- Battle results now record a per nation breakdown alongside the side totals,
  and every reader goes through one accessor that falls back to the old single
  country shape for reports written before this. Existing reports keep reading
  correctly: they describe two sided battles, where the one country named does
  own the side's whole loss.

- A migration reconstructs the breakdown for coalition reports already on file,
  recovering each formation's owner from the unit records. It reports and skips
  any report it cannot attribute exactly rather than publishing a guess.
