Cut from development.

## The display margin is now the engine margin

The corporation detail query rebuilt each sector's margin from the CEO-set base
plus a modifier stack. Under the plants economy the engine does the opposite:
it derives the margin from the physical P&L (labor, upkeep, inputs) and stores
it on the sector — and the sector-turn code explicitly names this query as the
intended reader of that stored figure. The query never read it.

Measured on production (corp 484, turn 143): every sector's displayed margin
ran 20-55 points above the engine's, two agriculture sectors the engine booked
at -5% and -7% displayed at +48% and +46%, projected income read $32.4K per
turn against $12.7K booked, and the balance sheet capitalized the phantom
profits into $13.3M of sector NPV against the $5.9M capital book the share
price uses. That is why a corporation could show a fat income statement, a
16M balance sheet, and a collapsing share price at the same time: the market
was priced on the stored margins all along.

The P&L, income statement, and balance sheet now use the stored
`effectiveProfitMargin`. The modifier stack still renders as the advisory
breakdown, and remains the fallback for sectors that have never processed a
turn. Below plants the stored value is last turn's stack, so nothing moves.

Balance sheets of plants-world corporations will drop, some substantially.
Nothing was taken: the difference never existed. The share price never paid
for it.

## Credit collateral is priced on real margins

`sectorDailyProfitAnchor` — the shared basis for bond issuance capacity, the
credit rating equity leg, default restructuring, sector sale valuation — priced
every sector at the flat CEO-set base margin (typically 35%). A corporation
could borrow against loss-making sectors as if they earned 35%, and a genuinely
high-margin sector was under-credited on the same rule. The nationalization
path had already been switched to the stored margin with the same reasoning;
this brings the remaining callers in line.

Audited the corrected 2x-equity rule against every bond-issuing corporation on
production before shipping: one corporation exceeds it (excess ~₳49.5M);
every other issuer, including corp 484, remains within capacity, so no
existing position is forced by this change. Default auto-resolution still
prefers refinance over sector sales for corps inside their limits.
