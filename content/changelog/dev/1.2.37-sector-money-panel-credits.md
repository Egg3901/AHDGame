## What changed

- Sector pages showed cost lines that could not account for the profit below them. A California newsroom read $322K of revenue, $70.6K of wages, $0 on every other cost line, and $285K of operating profit. Revenue minus the visible costs came to $251K.
- The cause was presentation, not the engine. A sector's operating bill can land below its wage bill when subsidies, policy support and its calibrated overhead anchor hand money back. The panel treated every line as a subtraction, so it clamped the input bill and the running-costs line to zero and left the wage bill standing alone.
- The running-costs line is now signed. When the money coming back outweighs the overheads it reads as a credit, with a `+`, in the profit colour, the same way the corporation page's margin drilldown has always read it. "Things you bought" shows the real input bill instead of a clamped $0.
- Cost per unit produced no longer charges the full wage bill against a credit that had already offset part of it, so a sector's per-unit cost is no longer close to double what it should be.

## Why

The help text under the profit line promised "revenue minus every cost line above". That is a promise about arithmetic a player can check on screen, and it was not true on any sector whose operating bill came in under its wages. Hiding the credit made the wage bill look like the whole story and made the profit look unexplained.
