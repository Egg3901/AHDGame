  absorbed the difference. Payouts now use the current price, and a queue the
  fund cannot clear is split in proportion to units instead of first come,
  first served.

Cut from development.

## Fixed

- A queued redemption was paid at the fund's price on the turn it was
  requested, however many turns the payout ran for. A large redemption against
  a falling fund therefore drew value that was no longer there, and the loss
  landed entirely on the holders who stayed. Payouts are now struck at the
  fund's price on the turn the money actually moves, so a redeemer carries the
  market like every other holder.

- Units awaiting redemption were treated as a fixed cash debt owed by the fund
  rather than as units. As the fund's assets fell, that debt did not, so the
  remaining holders' share was written down twice and could reach zero while
  the fund still held assets. Those units now count as units, and every holder
  moves together.

## Changed

- When a fund cannot pay its whole queue at once, the available money is now
  divided in proportion to units held. Previously the queue was paid in order
  until the fund ran dry, so one large holder could take everything and leave
  the rest with nothing. A larger holding still receives more, it simply no
  longer receives it before anyone else receives anything.

- The price shown at the moment you request a redemption is now an estimate
  rather than a promise. It is recorded on the request for reference, and the
  amount you receive is settled at the price in force when each payment is
  made.
