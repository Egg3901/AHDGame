## Private banking

Rolling PR #4155, flag `privateBankingEnabled` (default off), kill switches
`bankPropTradingEnabled` and `bankContagionEnabled`.

- **Phase 0: flags and schema** (9ae55b46c). Bank charter, loan, deposit
  insurance fund, interbank loan and prop position types; the `savingsHolder`
  per-currency pointer on characters (savings stay a single balance so all
  existing readers are untouched); `bankCharter` on corporations; the three
  gameConfig flags and the flag helper module.
- **Phase 1: chartering.** A corp owning a financial sector charters a bank on
  objective criteria (no approval seat): era/FX-anchored posted capital (10x
  founding cost in modern USD reference, deflated by the era unit scale and
  converted through the GDP anchor rate), a matching-currency treasury debit
  in one atomic document update, and revocation by the central-bank chair of
  the charter currency or an admin (CEOs cannot self-revoke; capital refunds
  only when no deposits remain). Banking separation is a per-country reusable
  law, not a global flag: a `bankingLaws` doc per country (separated vs
  universal), era defaults when unlegislated (historical worlds separate,
  modern worlds universal), command economies get no private chartering at
  all. The bill catalog entry that flips it lands with the legislation phase.
- **Phase 2: deposits.** Players move their per-currency savings between the
  central bank and any deposit-taking private bank as a pure holder-pointer
  flip (the balance number never moves, so every existing savings reader stays
  correct). CEOs set deposit and lending offsets against prime inside a
  Regulation Q style corridor (era-aware defaults, per-country overrides on
  the banking law doc; out-of-corridor values are rejected, not clamped).
  Investment banks take no deposits. NPC household deposit capture is pure
  math over the central bank's external broad money pool: share scales with
  the deposit premium over the CB savings APY, capped per bank and in total,
  scaled proportionally under competition (movement wires up in bankingTurn).
  Bank blacklists cover characters, corporations, and index funds (a fund
  entry blocks every constituent). All rate/corridor/share constants are
  provisional pending balance review.
- **Phase 3: lending.** The central bank gains its third lever: a per-currency
  reserve requirement (era defaults 20% historical / 10% modern, settable by
  the CB chair or admin) that caps each bank's lendable headroom. Player
  characters and corporations apply for loans and are auto-approved on
  objective criteria (headroom, borrower caps, blacklist, matching currency);
  characters pay a spread over the posted lending rate. Origination is
  compensating-write safe on the transactionless Mongo (loan doc first, then
  book and credit increments, with rollback on partial failure). NPC regional
  loan-book math (volume falls and expected defaults rise with the lending
  rate) is pure and feeds the confidence model later. Constants provisional.
- **Phase 4: bankingTurn.** New turn phase immediately after savingsInterestTurn.
  Bank-held player deposits stop earning minted central-bank interest (a
  surgical holder check in savingsInterestTurn) and instead earn the bank's
  deposit rate paid from the bank corp's real cash, pro rata under shortfall
  and never below zero. NPC household deposits migrate between the CB's
  external broad money pool and each bank at a bounded per-turn flow. Player
  loans amortize each turn with arrears and default writeoffs after eight
  shortfall turns. The NPC bulk book resizes toward the rate-dependent target;
  its interest is real cash paid by the NPC economy out of external broad
  money (defaults destroy loan asset only, no cash). Per-bank idempotency
  stamps make every money movement retry-safe on the transactionless Mongo.
- **Phase 5: solvency, runs, contagion.** New bankSolvencyTurn phase after
  recomputeSharePrices. Each bank gets a 0..1 confidence score (reserve cover,
  capital cover, asset quality, panic penalty) published as a green/amber/red
  badge. Flight and failure key on the PRIOR turn's published band, so the
  badge always warns one full turn before it bites: amber leaks 10% of NPC
  deposits back to external broad money per turn, red leaks 30%, and a bank
  that stayed red with capital under half its required liquidity fails
  (frozen and archived to bankCharterHistory; depositor resolution arrives
  with deposit insurance). A failure panics every other same-currency bank
  for four turns behind the contagion kill switch; panic decays by one per
  turn. Revocations and re-charters archive history too. Constants
  provisional.
- **Phase 6: deposit insurance.** Premium-funded national fund per currency:
  every deposit-taking bank pays a per-turn premium on insured deposits,
  risk-weighted so thin reserves cost more. The insured cap is 5M in USD-1953
  reference terms, era/FX anchored like charter capital. On failure the failed
  bank's remaining cash and posted capital form the recovery pool; insured
  balances are kept whole, excess above the cap is paid pro rata from the
  remaining pool with the rest taken as a haircut, NPC household deposits
  return in full, and every kept balance is re-backed from recovery, then the
  fund, then a Treasury backstop booked to the federal budget. All depositor
  pointers flip back to the central bank. Constants provisional.
- **Phase 8: NPC banks.** Every non-command country seeds two NPP-run retail
  banks (generic era-appropriate names) through the real chartering path with
  a seed-time flag bypass that never mutates gameConfig. A small
  npcBankPolicyTurn before bankingTurn holds their rate offsets at corridor
  midpoints, so deposit and loan competition, interbank counterparties, and a
  contagion population exist from turn one.
- **Phase 10 (partial): banking separation legislation and admin unwind.**
  The Banking Separation Act is a reusable legislation type for every seeded
  legislature: two options, separated charters or universal banking, enacting
  writes the per-country banking law that the charter gate reads. Copy is
  era-neutral and LARP-safe. Plus an admin-only unwind command that recovers a
  stuck bank even with the feature flag off: flips depositor pointers home,
  returns NPC deposits, refunds capital, archives the charter; player loans
  deliberately keep amortizing. Wiki pages land with the UI phase.
- **Phase 7: prop trading, interbank market, CB margin line.** Investment and
  universal banks run a proprietary book across equities, index units, bonds
  and forex, marked to market every solvency turn against fresh prices.
  Leverage is capped at 3x an equity base of liquid plus posted capital plus
  book mark minus funding debt; forex carries an additional per-currency cap.
  A breach forces proportional liquidation at current marks and dents
  confidence. Funding: retail banks lend up to half their non-reserved
  headroom on an interbank market (defaults hit the lender), and a CB margin
  line advances up to half the book's mark value at prime plus a spread using
  the line-of-credit creation/destruction model. Insolvent investment banks
  fail through the same freeze-and-archive path. Behind the prop-trading kill
  switch. Constants provisional.
- **Phase 9: UI.** The /banking hub joins the World nav Economy group and
  replaces the nation-details central bank link: every central bank, every
  operating private bank with rates and warning badges, your savings with a
  per-currency holder selector, and a loan application form. Corporations get
  a Bank console tab (charter, rate sliders bounded by the corridor,
  blacklist editor, loan book, confidence badge). Central bank pages gain
  Reserve Requirement and Insurance Fund tabs. All banking API GETs ship with
  no-store cache headers.
- **Closeout: capacity allocation, prop console, wiki.** A chartered
  financial sector's capacity does one job: the CEO's branch share (default
  50%, slider 10..90%) sets the deposit ceiling (1.2M per allocated capacity
  unit, roughly 15x charter capital for a typical starter sector), while
  commodity financial_services production scales by the remainder, so
  chartering never double-earns one capacity investment. NPC inflows stop at
  the ceiling, player pointer moves respect it, grandfathered balances are
  never confiscated. The bank console gains the allocation slider, the prop
  book panel, and interbank lending with the CB margin line. A code-backed
  banking wiki guide joins the economy section.

## Sector profitability and market-spread rebalance

- **Dominance toll scales with market density** (suggestion #30). Under plants
  the build-cost multiplier is the entire dominance charge, `sectorTurn` gates
  the margin penalty and the regulatory burden off, so it now also scales by
  how contested the cell actually is. `dominanceDensityFactor` ramps from 0.35
  with no rivals to 1.0 at 4+ rival corps, applied to the toll's excess over
  1.0, so a sub-threshold sector is untouched at any density and the price stays
  continuous in both share and rival count. Rival count is distinct
  CORPORATIONS in the (state, sectorType) cell, excluding the builder's own.
  The quote path reuses `siblingsSectors` (already cell-scoped, no extra read);
  the command resolves the same figure via `fetchSectorCompetitorCount`, so the
  dialog price and the charged price cannot diverge. Unknown density prices at
  the FULL toll, so a failed read can never silently discount expansion. The
  legacy growth-cost line is deliberately untouched: it is vestigial under
  plants and moving it would shift flip-turn numbers the flip identity forbids.

- **Index-fund inclusion pays the issuer** (suggestion #62). The fund system
  was one-way: `fundCron` absorbed float, `fundCrossRebalancing` moved holdings,
  `dividendPassThrough` paid unit holders, and the corp being held got nothing,
  which is why the Global Top 50 sat at two or three real constituents. New
  `indexOwnership.ts` derives fund ownership from `fundId` shareholders and
  grants two issuer benefits at INDEX_INCLUSION_THRESHOLD (10%): a one-notch
  credit upgrade and a share-price premium ramping to +8% at 35% ownership.
  Both are the mirror image of the existing insider-concentration penalties and
  reuse their shapes. The credit notch NETS against the concentration
  downgrade instead of stacking, and a live default penalty still overrides
  everything. Private corps are exempt (no float to hold). Threaded into the
  turn credit pass, `recomputeSharePrices`, and the `/bonds` route so the
  displayed rating matches the rating the turn writes.

- **Personal trade history with realized P&L** (suggestion #38). Every share
  movement was already persisted in `shareTradeHistory`, but the only surface
  was the per-corporation PUBLIC tape at `/api/corporations/[id]/shares/history`
  with no per-holder view and no cost basis anywhere (`avgCostPerShare` describes
  the position you still hold, which says nothing about what you made on the
  ones you sold). New pure `tradeLedger.ts` walks one holder's rows for one
  corporation through a FIFO lot tracker. Splits are handled explicitly: a
  `stock_split` / `reverse_split` row moves no shares but rescales open lots, so
  it is matched on `structureChange` and applied to the lot book before the
  buy/sell branch, without it every basis computed across a split is wrong by
  the split ratio. A sale with no matching open lot reports UNKNOWN rather than
  booking the full proceeds as profit. New authenticated
  `/api/portfolio/trade-history` (own character, or a corp you are CEO of;
  chronological query because FIFO is order-dependent, plus a second read for
  the split rows the ownership filter cannot see), a Trade History section on
  the portfolio, and five `shareTradeHistory` indexes since the collection is
  append-only and unbounded.

- **Generation feeds grid reliability** (suggestion #90). `powerGridReliability`
  moved on infrastructure SPENDING and lagged renewables and nothing else, so an
  Energy Secretary could build generation every turn for a game decade and watch
  the number sit still, while `getGridReliabilityMarginModifier`
  (`corporations.ts:1001`) taxed every corporation in the state for the resulting
  low reading. New `gridEnergyAdequacyTerm` reads the region's energy + utilities
  revenue share off the `sectorRevenueTax` provider the node already had access
  to (no new plumbing), and adds up to +0.8 / −0.5 to the target around a 4%
  reference share, with diminishing returns above adequacy. Returns exactly 0
  with no sector data, so a region the provider cannot see keeps its old
  spending-only target.
- **National corps can set production methods** (suggestion #91). The national
  CEO panel never exposed the strategy control, so a state-owned sector ran
  forever on its seeded method while every private corp could retool. The
  command needed no change at all, `setSectorStrategy` only checks `requireCeo`
  and has no state-owned exclusion, so this is `strategyId` /
  `transitionFromStrategyId` on `NatViewSector` plus a selector on
  `SectorOpsRow`, locked while a retool is in flight to match the command's own
  mid-transition rejection.

## Industrial relations

## Bargaining campaigns and collective agreements

- Added explicit `BargainingCampaign`, `BargainingOffer`,
  `BargainingMandate`, and `CollectiveAgreement` domain records. A campaign is
  scoped to one union, one employer corporation, and a snapshot of that
  employer's matching locals.
- Offers carry a wage floor, agreement duration, and no-strike duration. Wage
  terms use the existing labor-cost bounds. Agreement duration is limited to
  24 through 192 turns, and labor peace cannot outlive its agreement.
- Negotiations require alternating offers, preserve a bounded offer history,
  and enter dispute when rejected or when their turn-backed deadline passes.
  Accepting your own offer is rejected by the domain layer.
- Campaign mandate and leverage are snapshotted from worker-weighted coverage,
  live worker-expectation grievance, national labor-market tightness,
  collective-bargaining law, and strike-fund runway. Clients cannot submit
  bargaining power or increase it by inflating an opening claim.
- Settlement writes a collective agreement and raises every covered local to
  the negotiated wage floor. The corporation turn keeps that floor in force,
  blocks organic and union-called strikes during the negotiated peace term,
  and expires the agreement by game turn.
- Added a deployable index migration with a partial unique index that permits
  only one unended campaign per union and employer. Read indexes cover union,
  employer, local, status, and expiry access paths.

## Disputes, escalation, and mediation

- Disputes now escalate one turn at a time from an overtime ban to a selective
  strike and then an industry strike. Each step requires a stronger member
  mandate. Strike escalation spends the existing strike fund and uses the
  existing local strike state machine.
- An overtime ban reduces output by 4 percent without inventing a second
  profit or GDP modifier. Selective and industry strikes use the established
  25 percent strike revenue throttle and margin effect.
- Government mediation becomes available after two dispute turns when the
  collective-bargaining law score permits it. The server derives a
  leverage-weighted package from both parties' latest offers. It expires after
  four turns and becomes binding only when both parties accept.
- The campaign turn expires mediation packages, bargaining deadlines, and
  collective agreements from game-turn state rather than wall-clock time.

## Dispute feedback loop

- Both parties can keep tabling offers during a dispute. Industrial action is
  pressure to move the package, so a dispute where nobody can table one leaves
  the escalation ladder with nothing to act on.
- Autonomous unions and CEOs answer industrial action. Their settlement wage
  now includes an action-pressure term that scales with escalation level and
  how long the action has been held, so a strike moves an NPP employer instead
  of being ignored.
- Campaign mandates are recomputed from live conditions every turn instead of
  being frozen at opening. Organizing during a dispute, losing members, and
  newly enacted collective-bargaining law all move the campaign already on the
  table.
- Grievance and escalation-forced worker expectations now read the same state
  cost-of-living index the strike model uses. A cost-of-living-blind figure
  read grievance as zero in expensive states and made a called strike resolve
  itself as a concession the employer never made in cheap ones.
- An unresolved dispute lapses after sixteen turns, ends its industrial
  action, and restores shop-floor expectations to their pre-campaign values.
  The same pair can bargain again after an eight-turn cooling-off period.
- Holding an overtime ban costs the strike fund upkeep every turn. The action
  ends when the fund cannot pay for it.
- Escalation honours the union-level strike cooldown that the retired
  direct-strike route enforced.
- A settled agreement sets a wage floor for its term instead of rewriting the
  employer's wage level, so pay returns to the employer's own level when the
  agreement expires rather than ratcheting up permanently.

## Player surfaces

- The union dashboard now has an employer-scoped Bargaining tab. Leaders can
  see local count and organization, make a package offer, answer a CEO
  counteroffer, accept, counter, or withdraw, and review the full campaign
  history.
- The CEO Office now has an Industrial Relations panel. CEOs can inspect the
  union's mandate and leverage, accept an incoming offer, make a counteroffer,
  or reject negotiations into dispute. Active agreement wage floors and labor
  peace are visible with their exact expiry turns.
- Retired the player-facing union-wide strike and standalone wage-demand
  endpoints. Player industrial action now starts from an employer-scoped
  dispute; the internal strike service remains available to system callers.
- Escalation preview and execution share one authoritative target and cost
  plan. The dashboard names affected locals, shows the exact treasury charge
  and new strike count, and requires confirmation before funds are spent.
- Local readiness, candidate names, and active domestic bills are shown
  directly. Legislative stances now validate that the bill is active and
  belongs to the union's country.

## Accounting and ROTDM contract

- Agreement wage floors enter the existing labor-cost calculation. That path
  already reaches sector profit, corporation income, the worker-weighted wage
  index, median income, and unemployment, so this feature does not add a second
  GDP or profit penalty.
- Strike protection and settlement resolve through the existing strike state
  machine and sentiment event path. Strike revenue and margin effects remain
  the only direct industrial-action hit, avoiding double counting.
- Bargaining reads the enacted union-law axis and national unemployment, which
  makes legislation and macro conditions inputs to union power rather than
  decorative context.
- A bounded political provider feeds unresolved disputes into worker security
  and civic life, with stronger effects for escalation and turn decay from the
  latest action. Settlements create a smaller leverage-scaled positive signal
  that expires after 32 turns. The provider does not touch GDP, employment,
  revenue, or profits.

## Autonomous parity and balance

- NPP union leaders and NPP CEOs now use the shared campaign, counteroffer,
  mediation, escalation, settlement, and agreement services. The legacy global
  demand, raw strike write, and direct wage-concession loop has been removed.
- NPP decisions are deterministic, replay-safe, monotonic, margin-aware, and
  limited to one campaign action per turn. Active agreements prevent reopening.
- Registered `nppUnionBehavior` as a declared simulation phase and excluded it
  from elections-only simulations alongside the frozen union economy.
- Added a repeatable industrial-relations balance simulation. Its calibrated
  scenarios verify the 35/50/65 support ladder, 4 percent overtime-ban output
  loss, 25 percent full-strike throttle, strike-fund costs, and power ordering
  across weak, viable, and strong unions.
- Extracted wage, unionization, strike, agreement-floor, and industrial-action
  processing into `sectorLabour.ts`, clearing the blocking architecture size
  gate while keeping one authoritative economic path.

## Verification

- Added pure domain tests for mandate scoring, term bounds, offer alternation,
  deadlines, dispute transition, settlement, agreement expiry, wage-floor
  selection, and no-strike enforcement.
- Added command tests for server-derived campaign opening and employer
  settlement, migration tests for every new index, and UI tests for both union
  and CEO bargaining workflows.
- Added escalation, mediation, political-provider, NPP policy and integration,
  simulation-profile, balance-scenario, and extracted sector-labour tests.

## Refinement arc: funds, central banks, subsidiaries (#4191-#4261)

An audit of `release/1.1` at `b77320190` found the three systems the release
leans on hardest (index funds, central banks, corporate subsidiaries) had
correctness holes underneath them, so the arc fixed the foundations first and
then built depth on top. Every PR targets `release/1.1`. Track prefixes: A is
funds, B is central banks and banking, C is corporations and subsidiaries.

### Waves 1 and 2: correctness

- **C0 ledger every money leg in corp deals** (#4191). Agreed acquisitions,
  hostile takeovers and spin-offs moved purchase prices, shareholder payouts
  and target cash with zero `financialTxLog` rows, so a money-conservation
  check reading the ledger saw an acquisition as cash appearing and
  disappearing and the shadow ledger had nothing to derive from.
  `payShareholders` now takes a ledger context and emits a
  `share_buyout_payout` per credited holder plus a government leg for the
  public-float slice. `hostileTakeover` collects legs and flushes them only
  once the merge commits, so a rollback never leaves logged money that was
  reversed. The spin-off fee was a single-sided debit that destroyed money; it
  is an incorporation fee and now credits the parent's country treasury.
  Index-fund rows are deliberately excluded: fund cash is not a ledger account
  yet, and a leg with no counter-account would read as a leak.
- **B0/B1 one rate authority and a liquidity lever that reaches banks**
  (#4192). The schema had said since the FOMC landed that a seated committee
  decides rate moves, and the NPP auto-rate path already skipped banks with a
  board, but `updatePrimeRate` never checked, so a chair could POST `/rate` and
  bypass the motion, the majority and the per-term change cap. It now returns
  409 when a board is seated; the prime rate card routes players to the
  committee room; an admin override still works but spends one of the term's
  moves. Separately, `liquidity_injection` only incremented the central bank's
  own `reserveBalance` with `moneySupplyDelta: 0` while the UI called it
  "lend more to banks". It now advances newly created money to the currency's
  chartered banks pro rata by deposits, booked as CB advance debt that repays
  through the existing margin path, falling back to the old reserve-pool
  behaviour when no bank can take it.
- **A0 one currency for every fund leg** (#4193). Fund cash, NAV, bond
  principal and both trade routes are denominated in ₳; equity
  mark-to-market was not. `convertLocalPriceToAnchor` converted into the
  FUND's anchor currency, which only coincides with ₳ at parity, so every
  non-USD fund mispriced itself and the same-currency short-circuit hid it for
  USD funds and for the tests. The helper now returns ₳ at all four call
  sites. Three more legs mixed the same two units: dividend pass-through
  credited a raw ₳ figure into the fund-currency wallet (a JPY fund underpaid
  its holders by the whole exchange rate), the tx log passed a ₳ amount
  labelled as fund currency and let `emitTx` divide by the rate a second time,
  and NPP fund wealth ran `units x quotedNav` through `toAnchor` when
  `quotedNav` is already ₳.
- **A1 admin capital injections are an attributable mint** (#4194).
  `inject-capital`, `inject-capital-all` and `deploy-cash-all` raise a fund's
  `cashAnchor` with no debit anywhere, which is intentional, but the only
  trace was a fund-scoped row so the ledger saw fund cash appear with no
  counterparty or actor. Each route now emits an `admin_transfer` leg naming
  the tool, the admin and the fund with `mint: true` in meta, so the creation
  is attributable and filterable out of player-economy aggregates. NPP
  investable-cash accrual is deliberately untouched: NPP wealth sits outside
  the ledger perimeter and needs an NPP account kind first.
- **A2 fund solvency surfaces stop claiming safety they do not have** (#4196).
  Auto-pause is off by design, but it was expressed as `ratio < 0`, false for
  every non-negative ratio, so the pause branch, its `pauseReason` and the
  gauge's error tone were unreachable while still reading as live safety.
  Disabled is now explicit via `autoPauseDisabled()` and a separate warn ratio
  drives the display band. Immediate redemption debited fund cash with no
  balance predicate, so two concurrent redeems could both pass the
  affordability check on the same cash; it now uses a `$gte`-guarded update and
  returns 409 on the race. Reactivating a fund `$set` its pause fields to
  `undefined`, which leaves them on the stored document, which is why the heal
  script had to `$unset` them by hand. Admin listings reported a seed-time
  `reserveUnits` figure forever; it is now derived from live reserve positions.
- **C1/C2 one ownership model, and tests on the commands that move money**
  (#4198). Control is voting power everywhere that matters, but the parent's
  own subsidiary list counted raw shares, so a dual-class corp could appear in
  a list where every offered action was refused, or sit outside a list the
  formalize button would act on. `wouldCreateOwnershipCycle` had exactly one
  call site, `formalizeSubsidiary`, which is the LAST step of becoming a
  parent, so a corp could buy its way to majority control of the corp that
  already controlled it and only be stopped once the loop was in the share
  graph; the check now runs at purchase on both the direct-buy and buy-order
  paths. The player wiki described automatic subsidiary status at >50% of
  shares with no mention of formalization; it now separates de facto control
  from managed subsidiary and states outright that profits do not consolidate.
  `capitalInjection`, `releaseSubsidiary` and `setParentDividendFloor` had no
  tests despite moving money and granting control, and now do.
- **B2 count the private-bank books and fund cash in M2** (#4199).
  `bankingTurn` debits `externalBroadMoney` on an NPC deposit capture and
  credits the bank's book; the aggregates counted the debit and not the credit,
  so measured M2 shrank as private banking grew. That reads as tightening that
  never happened, and the NPP policy engine keys QT decisions off annualized M2
  growth, so a successful banking sector could talk the central bank into
  tightening against an accounting artefact. A new `bankDeposits` component
  enters M2 alongside `externalBroadMoney` so moving a deposit is flat by
  construction, `creditOutstanding` picks up the banks' loan books, and
  `fundLiquid` is no longer pinned at 0 now that the currency SSOT fix makes
  the conversion well defined.
- **B3 margin interest is revenue, and unpaid margin interest bites** (#4202).
  A central-bank loan to a player-controlled borrower moved money two opposite
  ways depending on who borrowed: LOC interest credited the issuing bank's
  `reserveBalance`, CB margin interest was destroyed under a comment calling it
  a mirror of creation on draw. Draw creates PRINCIPAL; servicing interest is
  revenue, and it now credits the same balance, flushed once per currency.
  Worse, the shortfall was computed, counted, and never used: a bank that could
  not pay simply did not, forever, with no arrears and no effect on its
  capacity to draw more. Unpaid interest now accrues as `cbMarginArrears` and
  joins the principal the collateral cap is measured against.
- **A3 count index-fund holdings in the portfolio snapshot** (#4203).
  `portfolioSnapshot` measured stocks, bonds, cash and LOC debt; fund units
  were absent, so a player whose wealth was mostly in funds watched a flat
  portfolio line while their net worth moved. `fundValue` is optional and
  absent on every earlier snapshot, and consumers must read `undefined` as
  "not measured" rather than zero or a long-time holder appears to gain wealth
  on the turn this landed.
- **Fund tax question settled in the player wiki** (#4208). Fund dividends and
  redemption gains are not taxed at the individual level, and the decision
  follows from consistency: direct share dividends and share sales are not
  taxed per transaction either, and capital-gains tax is modelled as a macro
  budget line off GDP. Taxing only funds would make an index fund strictly
  worse than holding the same equities directly.

### Wave 3

- **B4 scrutiny rework: credibility with economic teeth** (#4207). Scrutiny
  already existed as `chairInfamy` but its only consequence was a personal tax
  on the chair's influence, so every lever the bank had was worth the same
  whether or not anyone believed it. It now scales the inflation-expectations
  channel, with three guards against a death spiral: the dampener is floored at
  `TRANSMISSION_FLOOR = 0.6` and touches that ONE channel (loan rates, cost of
  capital and bond pricing stay mechanical, so a discredited bank can still
  cause a recession, it just cannot talk inflation down cheaply);
  `MAX_SCRUTINY_GAIN_PER_TURN = 8` caps ruin while leaving improvements
  uncapped; and recovery is bought with resolve, not results, by holding the
  stance the corridor calls for for `RESOLVE_TURNS_REQUIRED = 3` consecutive
  turns for `RESOLVE_SCRUTINY_RELIEF = 6`. Recovery keys off the same
  `corridorVerdict` the UI shows, so what a player is told and what they are
  scored on cannot drift, and the chair card states the remaining turns
  outright. Two political edges: a vacancy no longer zeroes scrutiny (it decays
  at `CHAIR_CHANGE_SCRUTINY_RETAINED = 0.75`, so replacing the chair is not a
  credibility laundromat), and a government-controlled bank having its rate set
  by the finance seat adds `INTERFERENCE_SCRUTINY = 12`.
- **A6 index funds vote the shares they hold** (#4227). Fund stakes already
  counted in `totalVotingPower`, the denominator every pass threshold is
  measured against, but the vote path only accepted character and corporation
  voters, so funds DILUTED every corporate vote and decided none: the more of a
  company a fund absorbed, the closer that corporation drifted toward permanent
  deadlock. A fund has no opinion, so its ballot is principled: a unit holder
  controlling the fund follows their instruction, an undirected fund mirrors
  the majority actually cast, and with no director and no majority to mirror it
  abstains AND leaves the denominator, because measuring a threshold against
  shares nobody can cast is the deadlock itself. A tie counts as no majority
  rather than something to mirror, since letting a passive fund break ties
  would make it the kingmaker.
- **C3 merger review** (#4235). Deals above a share threshold are referred to
  the state instead of clearing automatically. No new legislation entry: it
  reuses each country's existing `{cc}.economy.competition.primary` as the
  antitrust dial rather than authoring a parallel statute, with thresholds
  `[null, 75, 60, 50, 40]` by option level, so a country that has never
  legislated competition policy reviews nothing. Authority is the era-gated
  cabinet seat from `cabinetMembers`, gated on marketization and ownership
  rather than a country list. Bands are +5 for remedies and +15 to block.
  Remedy is MEASURED, not procedural: a spin-off does not discharge it,
  because the spun-off corp is wholly parent-owned and the concentration has
  not changed. `loadIndustryBasis` was extracted so the concentration chart and
  the referral decision share ONE denominator.
- **C3 officeholder surface** (#4313). The review panel served two readers
  off one endpoint on the corporation Deals tab, so the only route to a national
  duty ran through some corporation's page, and a seated minister who ran no
  corporation could not reach their own queue at all. Split by READER, not by
  screen: `GET /api/merger-reviews` now returns the CEO's `involving` list only,
  and the national queue moves to `GET /api/merger-reviews/queue`, which
  re-resolves the seat from `cabinetMembers` on every request and answers
  `applies: false` to everyone else. The officeholder works the queue from a
  Merger Review tab on their own cabinet office page, next to their other
  duties, with the decided history alongside the pending referrals. The tab is
  drawn only when the server says the duty applies, so a country whose era has
  no such seat, or a command economy, gets no tab and no empty state implying a
  feature that does not exist there. Deep-linking `#competition` where it does
  not apply falls back to Overview rather than leaving a blank body. The card
  itself is shared (`MergerReviewCard`) so a deal reads identically to the firm
  and to the regulator. Decision rules, thresholds, bands and remedy logic are
  untouched: this is surfacing only. One deliberate widening, not a rule change:
  at No Enforcement the queue still opens while referrals opened under a
  stricter law are pending, so a repeal cannot strand a live referral with
  nobody able to decide it.

### Wave 4

- **B5 central bank independence** (#4250). Taking the bank costs what the bank
  is worth: dismissing a chair costs `DISMISSAL_SCRUTINY = 18` and revoking
  independence `REVOCATION_SCRUTINY = 22`. Dismissal applies no retention
  haircut, and a test sweeps retention 0..100 asserting dismissal is worse than
  an expired term everywhere, so there is no seam where firing the chair is the
  cheap option. Granting independence back refunds nothing.
- **A5 sponsored funds** (#4251). Players charter their own index fund for a
  ₳2.5m fee plus ₳10m seed capital. The mandate is scope and kind only, not a
  hand-picked basket. The sponsor earns an expense ratio of 0.1% to 2.0% off
  AUM and gets NO investor stake, so sponsoring is a fee business rather than a
  second way to hold the same shares. The fee suspends below 0.9 backing and
  during wind-up, so a sponsor cannot bill a fund that cannot pay its holders.
  Wind-up is a STATE rather than a delete: holders are paid at `cash / units`
  and seed capital comes out LAST.
- **C4 corporate groups** (#4253). A formalized parent and its subsidiaries get
  a group balance sheet and group loss relief, implemented as a REBATE rather
  than a recomputation of the tax pass, so the existing per-corp tax path stays
  the single authority and the effective rate is DERIVED for display. Four
  limits: same country only, losses capped at group profits, capped at tax
  actually collected, and formalized subsidiaries only. Found and fixed on the
  way: ownership cycles were split into singletons, silently dropping relief.

### Wave 5

- **C5 transfer pricing** (#4257). Intra-group CROSS-BORDER trades priced off
  market accrue exposure; crossing ₳5m triggers an arm's-length reassessment
  plus a 40% surcharge. Deterministic, not a random audit roll. Same-country
  transfers are ignored because C4 relief already nets them out. Premiums are
  recorded BEFORE the damages, solvency and rounding gates, so the exposure a
  player is shown is the exposure that accrued.
- **B7 bank supervision** (#4258). `MIN_CAPITAL_RATIO = 0.08` and
  `STRESS_CAPITAL_RATIO = 0.06` are two separate thresholds: an annual stress
  test applies `STRESS_LOSS_FRACTION = 0.15` and shrinks BOTH the book and
  capital, so a bank can pass on paper and fail under stress. A breach starts a
  `RECAP_GRACE_TURNS = 12` clock; missing the deadline REVOKES the charter in
  an orderly wind-down rather than FAILING it with a depositor haircut, so
  supervision is not a second route to a bank run. Recapitalization emits
  `corp_capital_injection`.
- **B8 discount window** (#4259). The reserve requirement already existed; this
  adds the lender of last resort. Prime plus 3pp, above `CB_MARGIN_SPREAD_PP`
  so it is never the cheap funding option, capped at 25% of deposits, retail
  and universal charters only. Stigma is the mechanic: drawing is visible and
  dents confidence, so the window is a real option that you do not want to
  take. Bug found and fixed: servicing selected on `cbMarginDebt > 0`, so a
  bank that borrowed only at the window paid no interest at all, forever.
- **C6 group synergies** (#4260). Group members converge toward the group's
  BEST operating figure at 5% per turn, capped at 60% of the gap. It is a
  floor, never an average, so acquiring a weak subsidiary never drags the
  parent down. `isSpinOff` and `spunOffFromCorpId` were dead fields flagged by
  an earlier audit and now do work: a spin-off carries an 85% ceiling for 96
  turns, so splitting a corp to farm synergies is not free. Added
  `spunOffAtTurn` to make that measurable.
- **B6 currency regimes** (#4261). `fxRegime` is float, band or peg, with a
  separate `capitalControls` switch, and the impossible trinity is enforced at
  `updatePrimeRate`: a peg or band plus open capital plus an independent rate
  move is refused, not silently allowed. A band counts as a commitment, not a
  soft preference. The feature's own test caught a modelling error before
  merge: float plus controls holds only ONE corner, which is wasteful rather
  than impossible, so `givenUp` became a list with a `wasteful` flag instead of
  a single verdict.

### A6 follow-up: the instruction surface

- **Fund direction is reachable** (#4266). The headline principle of A6, that a
  unit holder controlling half a fund directs its vote, was DEAD CODE:
  `resolveFundStewardship` took a `directions` map that nothing ever wrote and
  `loadFundDirector` had no caller, so every fund was mirror-or-abstain at
  runtime and buying units was never a route to corporate influence. Two
  independent reviewers flagged it. The write path now exists.
  `CorporationVoteFundDirection` is stored on the vote itself rather than in its
  own collection, because an instruction is only meaningful for one vote, so it
  needs no separate lifecycle, index or cleanup. `GET`/`POST`/`DELETE` on
  `/api/corporations/[id]/votes/[voteId]/fund-direction` list, set and withdraw,
  authorized against the SAME `loadFundDirector` check the resolver uses so the
  two cannot disagree about who controls a fund. Withdrawing returns the fund to
  mirror-or-abstain rather than flipping its vote. An instruction can decide a
  vote, so the route runs the same resolve-and-apply pass the cast-vote route
  does; without it a decisive direction would sit open until someone loaded the
  page. The vote card grows a "Funds you direct" block, hidden entirely for the
  majority of viewers who control none.
- **Fixed while wiring it:** the resolver honoured an instruction whenever a
  director merely EXISTED, without checking it was the same character who gave
  it, so an instruction transferred to whoever bought control afterwards. The
  stored instruction now carries `directorCharacterId` and must still match at
  resolve time. `FundInstruction` replaces the bare `"yes" | "no"` map value.
  A test covers it.
- **Deleted:** `fundsHolding`, the other unreferenced export, rather than
  finding it a use.
- The abstain-leaves-the-denominator half, which is what actually unstuck
  corporate votes, is untouched. Its tests still pass unchanged.

### A7 part 1: listing standards

- **Index inclusion is earned** (#4271). Eligibility was purely mechanical: be
  public, have a price, be in the right country and sector. A shell corporation
  with two shares outstanding qualified for the same index as a national
  champion, and the funds dutifully bought it, which matters because inclusion
  is a large automatic price-insensitive buyer. Three standards now apply:
  `MIN_FREE_FLOAT_RATIO = 0.15`, a size bar of `MIN_RELATIVE_SIZE = 0.05` of the
  candidate pool's MEDIAN cap, and a solvency screen. The size bar is RELATIVE
  on purpose: an absolute ₳ floor is meaningless in a modern world and
  impossible in a 1953 one, while a median-relative bar means the same thing in
  every era with no per-era constant to maintain. The median is computed over
  the WHOLE pool including failures, because computing it over survivors is
  circular. Standards are applied per index, to the corporations matching THAT
  mandate, so a small-country fund judges you against its own market.
- **Fails OPEN on unknown float.** Existing fixtures carry no `publicFloat` at
  all, and reading absent as zero would have emptied every index on the first
  rebalance. Absent is UNKNOWN and never fails; a malformed number is a
  different thing and does.
- **The grace period is wired, not just written.** The partial branch defined
  `LISTING_GRACE_TURNS = 3`, `shouldDropConstituent` and `describeFailure` and
  called none of them, which is the same dead-code shape as A6 and would have
  shipped the churn the module's own docblock says grace exists to prevent. New
  pure `applyListingRetention` plus `IndexFund.listingFailureStreaks` carry the
  count between rebalances. The asymmetry is deliberate: an INCUMBENT gets three
  consecutive failing observations before it is sold, an APPLICANT is held to
  the bar on the spot, and one passing turn clears the record entirely.
- **Delisting actually divests.** `findRemovedConstituentHoldings` cannot see a
  delisted corp, because it is still mechanically eligible, so without an
  explicit sell the position would have sat in the book forever, out of the
  target and never sold. The sell names the standard that was missed via
  `describeFailure`, so a holder can check the fund's judgement.
- Streaks are only carried for corporations still in the index's candidate pool.
  A corp that left the mandate (went private, relisted abroad) is not failing a
  standard, and a stale count would drop it on re-entry.
- **A7 part 2: the index committee and issuer lobbying** (#4277). Standards are
  a rule; the committee is the discretion on top of them. A corporation that
  misses a standard petitions to be admitted anyway, and the committee is the
  SAME era-gated cabinet seat that rules on merger review, resolved through the
  same `resolveMergerAuthority` rather than a parallel copy: two resolvers would
  eventually disagree about who holds a country's commercial discretion, and the
  point of the seat is that it is one person. A country whose era has no such
  seat has no committee and no waivers.
- **Solvency is not waivable**, enforced in `failuresAfterWaiver` at the screen
  rather than at the API, so no future caller can grant what the rule forbids.
  Free float and size are qualification bars and are arguable; a committee that
  can wave an insolvent corporation into an index makes fund holders pay for a
  political favour.
- **Lobbying is corporate cash with a political trace.**
  `index_listing_lobbying`, both legs ledgered: the corporation pays on filing
  and either the seated officeholder receives it personally or, for a vacant or
  NPP seat, the country treasury does. Never refunded on a refusal, because a
  contribution that came back when the answer was no is a free option rather
  than a political act.
- **The automatic decision is DETERMINISTIC**, since most seats in most worlds
  are NPP-held or vacant. `AUTO_GRANT_CONTRIBUTION_RATIO = 0.02` of market cap
  with a ₳250k floor, and `AUTO_GRANT_MIN_SHORTFALL_RATIO = 0.5`: money alone
  does not carry a shell into an index, and being nearly qualified does not get
  you in for free. `PETITION_DECISION_TURNS = 12`, `WAIVER_TURNS = 48`. A waiver
  is a fixed-term licence, so a corporation that never fixes the problem has to
  ask again in front of whoever holds the seat by then.
- The petitioner is re-measured at DECISION time, not snapshotted at filing: one
  that fixed its float should not get a waiver it no longer needs, and one that
  got worse should not be judged on how it looked twelve turns ago. A petition
  with nothing left to waive is refused with `no_longer_failing` rather than
  granted, so the record never shows a waiver doing work it never did.
  `ListingVerdict.worstShortfallRatio` is new: "marginal" has to be a number the
  screen computed, not one the committee guessed at.
- Due petitions settle BEFORE the rebalance screen runs, so a waiver granted
  this turn is honoured this turn. Index migration
  `2026-08-10-index-listing-petition-indexes` adds a PARTIAL uniqueness index on
  pending petitions (a plain unique index would refuse the second petition a
  corporation ever filed, not the second one it had open) plus the waiver, due
  and inbox read paths.
- Surface: `IndexCommitteePanel` on the corp Deals tab, mirroring
  `MergerReviewPanel`, serving the issuer and the officeholder off two
  endpoints and hidden entirely for viewers who are neither.
- **Deliberately NOT built:** discretionary EXCLUSION, the committee throwing a
  qualifying corporation out. The state forcing funds to sell a company that
  meets every published standard is a materially more dangerous power than
  admitting one that narrowly misses, and it should not arrive as a side effect
  of a listing feature.

### A8: union pension schemes

- **The funded second pillar** (#4285). The state pension already exists and is a
  BUDGET line, `{cc}.health.socialInsurance.primary`, pay-as-you-go out of tax.
  This does not touch it. A8 is the occupational pillar: a scheme attached to a
  union, holding real assets paid in by real employers.
- **No mint anywhere.** Every ₳ in a scheme was debited from an employer's
  `liquidCapital` and ledgered on both legs. Deliberately unlike the NPP
  investable-cash accrual, which `nppInvesting.ts` documents as an
  uninstrumented modelled mint outside the ledger perimeter; a pension scheme
  that minted its own assets would be a second one of those, in a system whose
  entire point is that the assets are real and can fall short.
- **`pension_scheme` is a real ledger account.** Added to `LedgerAccountKind`
  with subject and counterparty mapping, so the employer's contribution derives
  to a real counterparty instead of reading as a leak. Deliberately NOT added to
  `REAL_ACCOUNT_KINDS` yet, for the reason `state_party` is not: scheme assets
  will also move through fund subscriptions and benefit payments that are not
  instrumented, so stock-checking now would manufacture divergences.
- **The contribution is a bargained term**, not a statutory rate:
  `pensionContributionRate` on the offer, the mediation package and the
  resulting agreement, bounded 0 to 0.15, optional everywhere because an offer
  that says nothing about a pension is a well-formed offer and every offer made
  before this shipped says nothing. Mediation splits it leverage-weighted like
  the wage rather than dropping a term one side asked for.
- **Liabilities are what make the assets mean something.**
  `PENSION_ACCRUAL_RATE = 0.08` of the covered wage bill accrues every turn, set
  BELOW the 0.15 contribution ceiling on purpose so a scheme contributing at the
  maximum builds a surplus rather than running to stand still, and so the
  bargained rate is a real choice in both directions. Below
  `PENSION_DEFICIT_RATIO = 0.9` the employer is charged
  `PENSION_TOPUP_FRACTION = 0.05` of the SHORTFALL, not of the liability, so a
  nearly-funded scheme asks for nearly nothing and a deficit closes over
  game-years rather than bankrupting the employer in one turn.
- **An employer that cannot pay does not get the claim forgiven.** The
  contribution fails, the accrual happens anyway. That is the whole shape of an
  underfunded scheme.
- **Where the wage bill comes from, and the contract that changes.**
  `sector.laborCost` is written by the corporation turn and documented there as
  display-and-analytics telemetry "not read back into the economy". `pensionTurn`
  reads it back, which makes it load-bearing, so: an absent or unusable figure
  contributes NOTHING (the labour system can be off entirely, and inventing a
  wage bill would charge an employer for workers the economy is not modelling),
  and the same figure drives BOTH the contribution and the accrual so the two
  can never be measured against different populations. `pensionTurn` runs AFTER
  `corporationTurn`, which is what writes that figure.
- Order within the pass is charge, then top up, then accrue. Accruing first
  would ask an employer to top up a shortfall created by the same turn's accrual
  before that turn's contribution had been counted against it.
- Migration `2026-08-10-pension-scheme-indexes`: unique `unionId` (two scheme
  documents for one union would split its assets in half with no error
  anywhere), plus a SPARSE index on the agreement sweep, since the overwhelming
  majority of agreements never bargain a pension and should not sit in it.
- Surfaces: pension field on both bargaining panels (union and CEO office) so
  the term is proposable, counterable and visible to both sides, plus a scheme
  panel on the union dashboard that renders NOTHING when there is no scheme, so
  a union that never won a pension is not shown an empty fund.
- **Deliberately NOT built:** benefit payments to retirees, and investing scheme
  assets into index funds. Both need a retiree population and a new fund holder
  kind respectively; the scheme holds cash and a measured funding ratio until
  then, which is a complete story rather than a half-built one.

### Banking ledger legs (the review's other P1)

- **The three high-frequency money paths are instrumented** (#4286). The review
  found `originateLoan`, deposit-interest bulk credits, prop trades, insurance
  payouts and CB advances emitting zero `emitTx` entries, against a convention
  followed in 42 lib files. On standalone Mongo the log IS the journal (see
  `ahd-standalone-mongo-no-txns`), so the compensating-write strategy the
  banking code itself implements had nothing to compensate against.
- **Origination is a MINT, not a transfer.** `bank_loan_origination` names
  `system` as counterparty on purpose, so the row derives a mint contra rather
  than a debit on the bank. Bank lending CREATES deposit money: the bank's own
  `liquidCapital` is untouched at origination and only `bankCharter.totalLoans`
  moves. Naming the bank as counterparty would book a cash outflow that never
  happened and read as the bank losing the money it just lent. A test asserts
  the counterparty is `system` with no id, and another asserts a failed
  origination emits nothing.
- **Repayment is ledgered at the chokepoint.** `bank_loan_repayment` is emitted
  inside `debitBorrower`, which every servicing path already goes through (full
  payment, partial/arrears, and the final debit before a write-off), rather than
  at the three call sites. That is what stops the next servicing branch from
  being added without a leg.
- **Deposit interest is real cash leaving the bank** for a depositor's savings,
  and the only record was the balance itself. Now `bank_deposit_interest`, one
  leg per credited depositor with the bank as counterparty, emitted through
  `emitTxBulk` with thresholds loaded ONCE before the loop per that function's
  contract, since this runs for every depositor of every bank every turn. The
  depositor's `name` joins the existing projection rather than adding a lookup
  per depositor per bank per turn.
- **Still uninstrumented, deliberately:** prop trades (asset swaps at marks
  inside the bank's own book, no external counterparty), insurance payouts (the
  rare catastrophic path, and the one that most needs its own pass rather than
  a hurried leg), and CB advances. Named here so the next reader knows the gap
  is narrowed rather than closed.

### The "flaky test" was a real bug

- **`isNameFromCountryPool` rejected multi-token surnames** (#4291). The
  order-dependent suite failures recorded all release reproduced by looping the
  full suite: three clean runs, then `npp/healNames.test.ts` failed on the
  fourth. Pure-logic test, no shared state, and NPP name generation is unseeded
  `Math.random()`, so it was never order dependence: a real defect that
  surfaced when the dice landed on it.
- The Italian pool stores compound surnames ("De Luca", "De Santis"). The
  predicate split on whitespace and asked whether each token was a surname,
  found neither "De" nor "Luca", and declared the name foreign. A probe over
  4,000 generated names per pool put it at **~3% of Italian names**; every
  other pool was clean.
- **Product impact, not just the suite.** `planNppNameHeal` uses this predicate
  to decide who needs renaming, so those NPPs were permanently flagged and
  renamed on every heal run, to another name that could be compound again.
- Fixed by matching every contiguous run of the family tokens, so a stored
  surname of any length matches. Re-probed: **zero mismatches across 64,000
  generated names.** New tests cover the compound surnames directly, assert a
  foreign given name attached to a real compound surname is still rejected (the
  fix must not degrade into "any token matches something"), and assert the
  property the heal depends on across nine pools without relying on luck the
  way the randomised test did.
- **Also observed:** happy-dom emits `DOMException [AbortError]` at window
  teardown, 68-333 times per full run, from component tests whose `useEffect`
  fetches are still in flight when the window is torn down. Noise in every run
  so far rather than a cause of failure, but a real unmounted-fetch leak.
  `useAbortableEffectFetch` (#4305) generalises the pattern `useBondHistory`
  already used, and the components added in this arc adopt it.
- **The 200-component migration was the wrong call, and measuring killed it**
  (#4309). "205 components fetch on mount" counts the wrong thing. A response
  landing after unmount is noise; the defect that reaches a user is a STALE
  response overwriting a fresh one, which needs a dep the user can change while
  a request is in flight. Filtering for that: 267 components refetch on some
  dep, 71 build the URL from one, 5 are driven by local state a control can
  flip, and 2 of those lack the `cancelled` guard that already prevents the
  overwrite without an abort. **Two files, both admin.** `CodeQualityTab`
  (environment selector) and `PositionMapView` (era/country pickers) are fixed;
  the sweep is dropped rather than deferred.

### One definition of an agreement in force

- **Six copies of the same rule, five written out inline** (#4293). The

### The rest of the banking ledger legs

- **Prop trades, insurance payouts and CB advances** (#4302). The three paths
  named as deliberately skipped when origination, repayment and deposit
  interest were instrumented. The gap is now closed rather than narrowed.
- **A prop trade is a RECLASS, not a loss.** Cash moves between the bank's own
  liquid capital and its own trading book, so only the cash side is a money
  movement, and `bank_prop_trade_buy` / `bank_prop_trade_sell` share ONE
  `prop_book` mint/sink reason. That is the same shape `corp_capacity_build`
  already uses: the contra side is an asset account the shadow ledger does not
  carry, and pairing both directions on one reason lets the reconciler net a
  purchase against its own sale per currency instead of reporting two unrelated
  single-sided flows. The standing book value then shows up as a named
  `sink:prop_book` bucket rather than sinking into `unattributed`.
- **A haircut is money that stops existing**, so it derives a sink with a
  `system` counterparty rather than naming a recipient who does not exist. The
  KEPT balances are deliberately not logged: they do not move, the resolution
  only changes what backs them. What the insurance fund and the taxpayer paid to
  keep them whole is logged, separately, as two government legs.
- **CB advances** mirror the discount window: a `government` counterparty is not
  derivable from a tx row, so the leg resolves to a mint contra, which is
  exactly right for money the central bank has just created.
- A test asserts the two prop directions share a reason, that they do NOT share
  one with capacity capex, and that the genuinely one-sided rows (origination
  mints, haircuts sink) are not given a pairing reason they have no counterpart
  for.
- **Interbank and CB margin principal legs** (final pass). `lendInterbank`,
  `repayInterbank`, `drawCbMargin` and `repayCbMargin` moved or created cash
  with no `emitTx` at all: the last unledgered money paths in the arc. Now
  ledgered as `bank_interbank_lend`/`bank_interbank_repay` (a real two-sided
  corp-to-corp transfer) and `bank_cb_margin_draw`/`bank_cb_margin_repay`
  (LOC-style creation and destruction sharing one `cb_margin` mint/sink
  reason). The margin draw and repayment also mirror the discount window's
  `netMoneyCreatedLifetime` bookkeeping on the central bank, which they had
  skipped. `repayInterbank` gains a compensating refund of the borrower if the
  lender credit fails, instead of destroying the repayment on a half-completed
  two-leg transfer.

### One definition of what a bank charter may do

- **Six copies of the deposit-taking rule** (#4310), in `deposits.ts`, `rates.ts`,
  `interbank.ts`, `lending.ts`, `bankingTurn.ts` and `bankSolvencyTurn.ts`, five
  byte-identical and one differing only in its type guard. They agreed, so this
  removes the chance for the seventh to disagree rather than fixing a live bug.
- The predicate decides who may hold player deposits, who originates loans, who
  lends on the interbank market, who pays deposit interest, and who is exposed
  to a run. A copy that dropped the `status === "active"` half would let a
  revoked bank keep taking deposits on one path while every other path treated
  it as closed.
- `isLendingCharter` is kept as an alias rather than folded away, so call sites
  still read as the question they are asking. A test pins them in lockstep, so
  a future divergence has to be a deliberate edit to one file.

### Pensions that actually pay out, and invest (A8 phase 2, #4314)

- **Phase 1 built a scheme that could only ever accumulate.** Employers paid in,
  workers accrued claims, a funding ratio was computed, and absolutely nothing
  turned on it because no benefit was ever paid. This is the half where the
  promise comes due and the ratio starts to mean something.
- **There is nobody addressable to pay, and that is a finding rather than a
  shortcut.** Union membership is modelled in aggregate (a sector's unionization
  rate, a union's membership pressure); there is no member document and no link
  from a character to a union. And `retireCharacter` DELETES the character
  outright, snapshotting it into `retiredCharacters` and removing the wallet a
  benefit could be credited to. So benefits are a flow against the modelled
  covered workforce, ledgered out of the perimeter against
  `counterpartyType: "system"`, exactly like every other payment to households.
  The alternative was inventing a pensioner collection nothing else in the game
  knows about.
- **A share of accrued claims comes into payment each turn and the in-payment
  stock drains gradually.** A pensioner draws for years, not once, so a scheme
  that stops receiving contributions still owes benefits for a long time. That
  lag is what makes underfunding a slow visible problem instead of a one-turn
  event.
- **Benefits are cut PRO RATA when the cash is not there.** This is the entire
  point. A scheme that could pay a benefit it had no assets for would make every
  contribution, every top-up and every funding ratio in phase 1 decorative. The
  cut is proportional because a scheme paying some members in full and others
  nothing would be choosing between them, and nothing in the model gives it a
  basis to choose. The unpaid part is neither forgiven nor carried as arrears:
  the claim stays on the books, so the scheme still reads as underfunded and the
  employer still gets asked next turn.
- **Paying discharges liability by exactly what was paid.** Without that leg the
  funding ratio would never improve however much a scheme paid out, and the
  measure would be meaningless in the other direction.
- **The riskiest change in this PR is that `assetsAnchor` stopped being the
  assets.** It is now the CASH leg; total assets are cash plus the marked value
  of the scheme's index-fund units. Every ratio, band and top-up read goes
  through `pensionSchemeAssetsAnchor`, because a cash-only reading would treat
  every unit a scheme bought as money it had lost and bill its employer for the
  difference. Tests pin the ratio as unchanged across a subscription and pin the
  cash-only reading as wrong.
- **Marked to market, not held at cost.** A scheme whose fund fell is genuinely
  worse funded and its employer is genuinely asked for more. That is the point
  of holding assets rather than a number. The mark is refreshed at both ends of
  the pension turn: before contributions so top-ups price off today's value, and
  after investing so the union surface never shows cash that has already left.
- **Schemes subscribe through the existing path.** `creditFundPosition`, the
  `$inc` on the fund's `unitSupply` and `cashAnchor`, an `indexFundTransactions`
  row. `pension_scheme` is a new HOLDER KIND, not a new fund mechanic, with the
  same unique partial index every other kind already has (migration
  `2026-08-11-pension-scheme-fund-positions`). Whole units at quoted NAV, so the
  fund receives exactly what the units are worth and its backing ratio does not
  move.
- **The liquidity buffer is eight turns of benefits, plus a flat 10% of cash.**
  Fund units are not a payment instrument for a scheme (there is no scheme
  redemption path), so anything invested is unavailable to pensioners. Investing
  to the last ₳ and then cutting pensions would be the scheme's own fault rather
  than a consequence of underfunding, which is the only thing the cut is
  supposed to mean. The flat floor exists because a young scheme has no
  pensioners yet, so the benefit buffer alone would let it go fully illiquid the
  turn before its first one appears. A test walks the full buffer forward with
  no further contributions and asserts no turn cuts.
- **Schemes buy broad indexes only, home country first, global as fallback.**
  Sector funds are deliberately ineligible: concentrating a union's pensions in
  the sector its members work in is the failure mode occupational schemes are
  regulated against, and a scheme whose assets collapse the same turn its
  employer does is not a mechanic worth having.
- **Fails closed.** With index funds off, the investment pass returns having
  done nothing and schemes hold cash. Contributions, accruals, benefits and
  funding ratios all still work. Investment is an option a scheme has, never a
  dependency it carries.
- Both money-moving writes are guarded (`assetsAnchor: { $gte: amount }`), so a
  scheme's cash moving under either pass skips the operation rather than driving
  the balance negative. A pension scheme with negative assets would be a mint
  wearing a disguise.
- The benefit and investment passes run even when no live agreement carries a
  contribution. A scheme with liabilities and no agreement is the ordinary end
  state of a workplace that closed, and it still owes the pensions.

## Ruling-party factions

The Stage-3 faction split existed but had never had teeth: `fireFactionSplit`
scored EVERY official's divergence at zero, so the split fell back to input
order and took an arbitrary 15% of the ruling party's bench, then gave the
breakaway party the ruling party's own axis positions verbatim. The "Reformist
Faction of the CPSU" was ideologically indistinguishable from the CPSU it had
just left. That is a rename, not a split.

- **`factionDivergence.ts`**: two pure kernels, both DB-free. `scoreDivergence`
  ranks an official by Manhattan distance from the party line on the same
  (economic, social) axes the election engine uses for policy distance, so a
  player reading their own position against their party's can predict it. A
  missing position scores 0 rather than maximally divergent, otherwise every
  imported official with no recorded stance gets purged first.
- **Caucus cohesion**: a faction leaves AS a faction. `applyCaucusCohesion`
  blends each member's score toward their caucus mean at 0.6, so a moderate in a
  militant caucus goes with it and a lone hardliner can still be divergent
  enough to walk. This is the mechanic players already reached for on their own:
  the live 1953 world's only non-US/UK caucuses are the CPSU's "Soviet Honor
  Guard" and "New Leninist Course".
- **`factionCentreOfGravity`**: the breakaway party is positioned at its
  defectors' mean, so it stands somewhere its parent does not.
- The ruling-party chair and the sitting leader stay excluded, which matters
  more now that divergence is real: a reformist General Secretary would
  otherwise score high enough to defect from their own party.
- Clamping happens on input as well as output, so one out-of-range score cannot
  drag a whole caucus's mean out of band.

Unchanged for any country without `factionDefectionName`, and identical to the
old ranking in a world with no caucuses.

## B4 market effects (#4312)

The market half of B4, deliberately deferred out of #4207 because it ships
independently and carries none of the spiral risk that made the transmission
dampener contentious. Same input, `CentralBank.chairInfamy` (0-100), no new stat
and no new schema field. Pure math in `centralBank/marketEffects.ts`, wiring at
three call sites.

Scrutiny had exactly one economic consequence, and it was the one channel that
could feed back on itself. That left the strange result that a bank nobody
believed still borrowed, defended and funded itself at the same terms as one
everybody believed. Credibility is priced by markets first and by economists
second, so these three are the effects that should have existed before the
expectations dampener did.

- **Sovereign spread** (`SOVEREIGN_CREDIBILITY_SPREAD_MAX_PP = 1.5`). Added to
  the coupon at issuance, on both the scheduled quarterly path and the admin
  reconcile. Sized against the existing yield curve rather than real-world
  crisis spreads: the 5yr term premium is 0.75pp, so total loss of credibility
  costs about twice what the longest maturity costs. Big enough to be a budget
  line on a large debt stock, small enough that it cannot drive a country into
  sovereign default on its own, which would have been an inflation-adjacent
  spiral by a second route. `getSovereignCouponRate` takes the spread as a third
  argument defaulting to 0, so seeds and any caller that does not know the
  issuing bank price exactly as before.
- **FX target adherence** (`FX_ADHERENCE_FLOOR = 0.7`). Scales the synthetic
  volume an intervention injects into the shared volume-pressure term, NOT the
  reserves it spends. A discredited bank pays the same and buys less of the
  target, which is the correct shape for a market that doubts it will hold the
  line. Higher than `TRANSMISSION_FLOOR` (0.6) on purpose: an intervention is a
  real outlay, not a statement, so less of it is lost to disbelief. The failure
  test that charges `INTERVENTION_FAILURE_INFAMY` is judged against the
  UNDAMPENED rate, so the dampener can never manufacture the breach it is then
  blamed for. That would have closed a scrutiny-feeds-scrutiny loop, which is
  the same spiral in a different currency.
- **Deposit flight** (`DEPOSIT_RETENTION_FLOOR = 0.8`). Multiplies the NPC
  household deposit share domestic banks capture in `bankingTurn`, so the rest
  stays outside the domestic banking system as foreign currency. The stickiest
  of the three, because accounts, wages and prices are all denominated at home
  and because the private-banking system is young. Nothing is destroyed, so the
  money-supply books still balance. Gated by `privateBankingEnabled` for free:
  with the flag off `processBankingTurn` returns before this runs, so the effect
  no-ops rather than guessing at a system that is not there.

Three invariants hold across all of them, and are asserted directly rather than
inferred:

- **No inflation feedback.** None of these returns into the inflation term. The
  expectations dampener stays the only credibility-to-inflation channel.
- **Bounded and floored.** A maximally discredited bank keeps a finite spread, a
  partly effective intervention and a banking system that still takes deposits.
  Ruin is expensive, not terminal.
- **Exactly neutral at full credibility.** At scrutiny 0 every multiplier is
  exactly 1 and every delta exactly 0, so a clean bank is byte-identical to
  before and no existing balance moves.

Non-finite or out-of-range scrutiny resolves to NO scrutiny in every function,
matching `credibilityFromScrutiny`. A corrupt value must not price a punitive
spread against a country that did nothing.

## Live error triage (#4319)

Four unresolved GlitchTip issues cleared at the source, chosen because each was
a real defect rather than a reporting artefact.

- **Achievement recent-holders 500 (AHD-1FN/1FO).** 1212 production award docs
  in `characterAchievements` are character-scoped and carry no `userId`, so they
  predate per-user grants. The route called `award.userId.toString()`
  unconditionally, so any achievement whose five most recent awards included one

  `handleRouteError` as an unhandled fault. It is a bad request: `optimizeImage`

  player can act on and nothing is captured.
- **401 reported as a client error (AHD portfolio).** `PortfolioClient` captured
  every load rejection, so a signed-out or expired session on `/portfolio` filed
  `401 /api/character/me` and `401 /api/character/portfolio` as errors. The two
  highest-count client issues. 4xx is now excluded; `fetchJson` still reports
  network and 5xx faults with its own context, and the user-facing error state

- **Alt-detection pool cap raised, 300 to 800.** Every hourly run was
  truncating, which is not noise: accounts past the cap were never scored
  against each other, so the ring detector was running blind on part of the
  graph. The whole registered base is well under 800, so the pool now fits with

## Rank-and-file organizing and union strength

The union page had no loop for anyone who was not the president. Every control
was gated on `!union.ownerId` or `isLeader`, so a member looking at a led union
saw a read-only page. Leadership elections were also unreachable in practice:
the threshold read `membershipPressure >= 25`, but the only action that raised
pressure on an unowned union was the organize drive, and the only other source
was NPC drift.

- **`Union.strength`, uncapped, new field.** Distinct from `membershipPressure`,
  which stays exactly what it was: 0-100 shop-floor coverage feeding
  `unionizationDriftTarget` and the dues trickle. Strength is a pool any number
  of players can pile into. `unionStrength()` reads a missing field as 0, so
  every pre-existing union document is valid without a migration.
- **Organize is open to everyone, on any union.** `organizeUnion` no longer
  refuses when `ownerId != null`, and no longer refuses a player who leads
  another union. Country match and the union ban still apply. Every write is an
  `$inc` with no read-modify-write on a shared field, so hundreds of players
  can press the button against one union in one turn without contending. The
  old exact-value CAS on `membershipPressure` would have serialised them.
- **Cost moved from cash to action points.** `ORGANIZE_ACTION_COST = 5`,
  debited with the standard conditional `{ actions: { $gte: cost } }` update and
  refunded if the union disappears mid-write. `ORGANIZE_PERSONAL_COST` is gone.
  `UnionOrganizer.totalSpent` is kept and zeroed on new rows so the historical
  record of cash-era drives survives.
- **Votes are weighted by banked strength.** `tallyUnionLeaderVotes` takes a
  weight map and returns the winning strength total rather than a headcount;
  `loadUnionVoteWeights` builds it from `UnionOrganizer.strength`. An organizer
  with no banked strength cannot vote. The election threshold moved to
  `LEADERSHIP_ELECTION_MIN_STRENGTH = 100`, ten drives from nothing, and
  `isUnionLeadershipElectionOpen` now reads strength.
- **Decay, 0.5% a turn, both halves.** `UNION_STRENGTH_DECAY_PER_TURN = 0.005`
  applied in `processUnionsTurn` as a `$mul` over unions and over
  `unionOrganizers` in one pass each. Unlike dues and pressure decay it runs for
  every union, led or not, because strength is built by the rank and file rather
  than by a president. Suspended unions are excluded, same freeze rule as dues.
  Half-life is 139 turns.
- `/api/character/me` now returns `actions`, for the affordability hint.

Residual: the organizer decay is an unindexed sweep over `unionOrganizers`. It
is one `updateMany` per turn and the collection is small today, but it grows
with (players x unions organized) and will want an index if organizing catches
on.

  unconditionally and `setUnionWageDemand` had no caller, so `demandedWageLevel`
  could never be non-null in a live game. That left three surfaces permanently
  dark: the per-local wage-gap column, `unionWageDemand` in `sectorDetail`, and
  the CEO warning in `WageLevelPanel`. The route is a working leader-only action
  again, with a Set/Withdraw control on the dashboard. Every reader was traced
  first and all five are display-only, so nothing gained a mechanical effect.
  `WageLevelPanel` copy claimed a claim "keeps strike pressure elevated", which
  was never true, and now says what it actually does.
- **Union-wide strike preview deleted, not revived.** The detail route computed
  and shipped `strikePreview` and `lastCalledStrikeTurn` that the page never
  read. Unions no longer call strikes directly, so those were not numbers any
  player could act on. `buildBargainingEscalationPlan` already emits the same
  figures campaign-scoped and is now the only place they appear.
- **Escalation preview and the escalation gate now share code.** The preview did
  not account for `UNION_STRIKE_CALL_COOLDOWN_TURNS` while the command did, so a
  leader could be shown an escalation as available and then refused on click.
  `buildBargainingEscalationPlan` takes the union, returns `blockedCode`, and
  `commands/bargaining.ts` maps that to its statuses instead of duplicating the
  checks. They can no longer diverge.
- **Overtime-ban upkeep is visible before, during and after.** The charge is
  shown in the escalation preview, in the confirm, and while the ban is held
  with a runway in turns. A ban cancelled for lack of funds or a dispute that
  lapses now notifies the leader with the employer, the upkeep and the turn.
  New notification types `overtime_ban_defunded` and
  `bargaining_dispute_lapsed`.
- **Full bargaining mandate shown.** `laborTightness`, `lawSupport` and
  `strikeFundRunway` fed leverage but were never rendered. The union sees all
  five inputs. The employer sees four: not `support` (the internal ballot) and
  not `strikeFundRunway`, because an employer that could read the strike fund
  exactly would know how many turns to sit out any dispute, which removes the
  bluff that makes a dispute a decision. The employer type is narrowed to those
  four so the surface cannot silently start rendering the hidden two.
- **Pension scheme benefits rendered.** The panel declared 7 fields and rendered
  4 of the 13 the API returns. Benefit cuts now surface as a banner rather than
  a grid cell, and assets split into cash, invested and total, since the funding
  ratio is built on total and total was previously unshowable.
- **Suspended unions say so on their own page.** `Union.suspended` was omitted
  from the detail DTO, so every action button rendered enabled and the player got
  a bare 403. Computed with the same OR the leaderboard uses, because a union
  seeded under an existing ban carries no flag of its own.
- **Union busting leaves a union-side record.** `notifyUnionOfBustingAttempt`
  writes one batched notification to the president and every organizer with
  banked strength. Attribution is to the corporation, not the CEO, matching
  `corp_sector_attacked`: hostile economic action in this game is already
  attributed, and hiding the actor would have made union busting the only
  unattributed hostile act in the economy. Backfires are reported too, worded as
  a win, because an organizer who cannot tell a 15-point surge from an employer's
  own goal cannot read their own board.
- **Labour political term persisted.** `labourResiduals` on the region doc,
  sibling to `cabinetResiduals`, same shape and same bulk write, skipped
  entirely when the channel is silent. A strike wave that moves the national
  board is now traceable to industrial action.

## Employer pension charges: a 24x overcharge, and a visible expense line

`sectorTurn.ts` persists `sector.laborCost` on a DAILY basis (per-turn cost times
`TURNS_PER_DAY`, which is 24), documented in place as "display/analytics only,
never read back into the economy". `pensionTurn.ts` read it back into the economy
and charged against it once per turn, so every employer under a collective
agreement paid 24 times the rate they bargained and accrued 24 times the workers'
claim.

`coveredWageBillPerTurn()` restates it. Contribution and accrual both scale, so
funding ratios are unaffected and only the cash charge and the liability stock
change size. This is a unit error, not a balance tune.

**Live-data residual:** existing pension schemes carry 24x-inflated
`totalContributionsAnchor` and `totalTopUpsAnchor` stocks. Backfilling those is a
data question and has not been done.

The charge is also now visible. `employerPensionCostForTurn()` recomputes what a
corp's active agreements charge, split into bargained contribution and deficit
top-up, rendered as two rows under Operating Expenses and folded into
`operatingCosts` so EBIT reflects them, with a matching segment in the
revenue-allocation bar.

On the uncapped top-up: literally uncapped, but it cannot drain a CEO. The debit
is an all-or-nothing `$gte` guard so liquid capital cannot go negative, the
top-up closes 5% of the shortfall per turn against an 8% accrual so it converges,
and a shrinking corp with a legacy liability is already skipped when there is no
covered wage bill. Left alone deliberately.

## Members ratify settlements

Organizing v2 gave any player a way to bank per-character `strength` and made
that strength the ballot weight in the leadership election. Acceptance of a
settlement was the last leader-only step in the loop: the president alone bound
every organizer to a wage floor and a no-strike term.

The union `accept` action no longer settles. It opens a weighted member ballot
embedded on the campaign as `BargainingCampaign.ratification`, and settlement
happens only when that ballot ratifies. Ballots live in
`bargainingRatificationBallots`, one per organizer per offer revision, cast
through `POST /api/unions/[id]/bargaining/[campaignId]/ratify`.

The four calls, and why:

- **Rejection returns nothing.** The campaign stays exactly where it stood, with
  the employer's offer still on the table. No new status is invented, so there is
  no new state to be stuck in: the deadline still runs a negotiation into
  dispute, and `BARGAINING_DISPUTE_MAX_TURNS` still lapses the dispute, which is
  the exit the `lapsed` docblock requires. A membership that rejects everything
  ends the campaign on the existing clock rather than freezing it. The same offer
  cannot be re-tabled to the same electorate; a counteroffer bumps the revision
  and clears the block, so the way out of a rejection is to move the package.
- **The vote closes on a turn deadline, or early when the result cannot change.**
  `RATIFICATION_VOTE_TURNS` is 3, measured in turns like every other clock here.
  A majority of ALL snapshot strength either way closes it immediately; otherwise
  the deadline decides, where a tie and total silence both ratify. Rejecting on
  apathy would let an inactive membership veto every settlement with nobody
  having decided anything, and would hand the campaign to the lapse clock by
  default. `closeDueRatificationVotes` sweeps in `processLabourRelationsTurn`
  before the lapse pass, so a settlement ratified on the same turn a dispute runs
  out of road is a settlement, not a lapse.
- **The employer sees it.** The corporation bargaining payload carries the
  ratification status, the offer revision and the closing turn, and nothing
  about the tally. The CEO is waiting on an answer to its own offer, and a CEO
  reading silence as refusal escalates into a vote that was about to ratify. How
  the ballot is going is a union-internal fact, the same line the mandate payload
  already draws around `support` and `strikeFundRunway`.
- **Weights are snapshotted at open.** `loadUnionVoteWeights` is reused
  unchanged, and its output is frozen into `ratification.weights` with a
  `totalStrength` denominator. Strength decays `UNION_STRENGTH_DECAY_PER_TURN`
  every turn and new drives add to it, so a live recompute would silently reprice
  ballots already cast and could flip a closed tally between one organizer voting
  and the next. Same reasoning that snapshots `sectorIds` at opening. Organizers
  who join mid-vote hold no ballot until the next one.

Edge cases: a withdrawn or lapsed campaign voids its open ballot in the same
update; a vacant presidency changes nothing, because it is the members' vote and
the turn sweep closes it; an employer corporation that no longer exists voids the
ballot and lapses the campaign, since there is no counterparty an agreement could
bind. A union with no organizer holding strength settles directly on the
president's acceptance, which is the one case where a ballot could never be
answered. NPP-led unions still settle through `persistBargainingSettlement` in
the turn pass: they have no player membership to ask.

UI: organizers see the offer, their own weight, the running weighted tally, the
unvoted strength and a ballot; the president sees the same card plus why the
current offer cannot be put to the members. New indexes ship in
`2026-08-11-ratification-ballot-indexes`.

## CEO votes you can take back, and a union that says who runs it

PR #4396. Five reports from one session, all in the same family: a vote or a
seat that the UI described wrongly, or gave the player no way to act on.

- **The sitting CEO is exempt from the candidate residency rules.**
  `POST /api/corporations/[id]/ceo/vote` required the candidate's `homeState` to
  equal `headquartersState` and their `countryId` to equal the corp's, and the
  shareholder list applied the same gate before it drew a Vote button. Neither
  had an incumbent exemption, so a CEO who moved house, or whose corp relocated
  its HQ, became unvotable in the corporation they run: shareholders could not
  re-affirm them, the incumbent could not defend the seat with their own
  supershares, and any challenger who did still live in the HQ state ran
  unopposed. Client and server now share the rule.
- **Votes can be withdrawn.** POST was an upsert with no counterpart, so a
  shareholder could switch their vote but never abstain. Founding casts a
  self-vote on the founder's behalf (`corporationCeoVotes` insert in the found
  route), which meant every founder had a vote on the books they had never
  chosen to cast and could not remove. `DELETE /api/corporations/[id]/ceo/vote`
  withdraws both identities the caller can vote with, their own and any
  corporation they run, then re-runs the tally. The shareholder row's Voted pill
  is now the undo, and the tally panel carries a Withdraw button so a vote for a
  candidate on another page of the list is still reachable.
- **A pending offer to the person already holding the seat is cleared, not
  set.** Voting for the incumbent set `pendingCeoCharacterId` to them and then
  skipped the notification as redundant, leaving a permanent "you have been
  offered the CEO position" banner on the corp page with nothing behind it to
  accept. `reconcilePendingCeo` now unsets the field when the leader is the
  sitting CEO or when the tally empties out, and offers the seat only to a
  genuine challenger. Ties go to the incumbent rather than to map insertion
  order.
- **The bank console stopped blaming the CEO check for the freeze.**
  `canMutate` is `isCeo && privateBankingEnabled`, and the charter form printed
  "Only the CEO can issue a charter" for either half being false. With private
  banking frozen, every CEO in the game was told they were not the CEO. The two
  reasons are now distinct strings.
- **Union leadership is visible.** The leadership block only rendered for a
  union with no owner, so a led union showed no president, no explanation for
  the missing vote, and no roster. `GET /api/unions/[id]/leader/vote` now
  returns the organizer roster with each member's banked strength, drive count
  and share of the vote weight, plus the seated leader. The page names the
  president, says the seat reopens on resignation or retirement, and lists the
  roster under both branches. The organizer documents are read directly rather
  than reusing `loadUnionVoteWeights`, which drops the legacy pre-v2 organizers
  who carry no `strength` field and who still belong on a membership list.

Not changed: there is still no recall or challenge vote against a sitting union
president. `isUnionLeadershipElectionOpen` requires `ownerId == null` by design,
and adding a challenge path is a balance decision, not a bug fix.

## Office generation outside the US vocabulary (ticket #974)

Two office vocabularies exist: the flat `gameConfig.officeActionBonus` map, and
the per-country `COUNTRY_CONFIGS[*].officeTypes[*].actionBonus` registry. The
turn engine only ever read the first, and that map was only ever extended to
US / UK / DE / JP / CN / CA keys. Every office key that lives solely in the
registry (`volkskammerDeputy`, `landAssembly`, `supremeSovietDeputy`,
`sejmDeputy`, `dail`, `deputy`, `senator`, `member`, and the rest) resolved to
a 0 action bonus. The same gap existed in the hardcoded `OFFICE_NI_BONUS` table
in `actionRefresh`, so those seats also drew no per-turn national influence.
454 player-held seats across 22 countries were affected on prod, including all
34 DD Volkskammer deputies.

New `src/lib/actions/officeBonusRegistry.ts` makes the country registry the
fallback for both bonuses. An explicit `gameConfig` entry still wins, including
an explicit zero, so admin overrides are untouched; an office the config has
never heard of now resolves against its country's registry entry instead of
returning 0. NI tiers keep their tuned values for the offices that had them and
fall into generic tiers otherwise (2.5 national executive, 1.0 any other seat).
`resolveOfficeActionBonus` takes an optional `countryId` so shared keys
(`deputy`, `senator`, `governor`) resolve per country rather than by scan.

Wired through `actionRefresh`, the turn dashboard route (which read the raw map
directly), `client-status`, `getActionBreakdown`, and the profile and character
pages, so the displayed number and the granted number stay identical.

Backfill: not run. Affected characters were shorted 1-4 actions and 1.0-2.5
national influence per turn for the lifetime of their seat. A backfill would
need the per-character seat tenure in turns, which is recoverable from
`electedOfficials` term start and the turn counter. Recommend crediting actions
only (capped at the energy cap) and leaving national influence alone, since NI
feeds election modelling and a retroactive lump would distort standings.

## The bank console stops speaking Mongo

PR #4404. Reported from live play: the blacklist editor took raw ObjectId strings, and the
console as a whole did not say what state the bank was in.

**Ids out of the UI.** `BlacklistEditor` was three textareas, one id per line,
and the saved list rendered back as 24-character hex. A player has no way to
obtain a corporation or character ObjectId through the game, so the feature was
unusable by anyone who was not reading API responses. The loan book printed
`borrowerId.slice(0, 8)`, and the interbank lend form asked for a "Borrower
corporation id" the same way. All three now work in names: a debounced search
picker (`PartySearch`) backed by `/api/characters/search` and a new
`/api/corporations/search`, entries as removable chips linking to the character
or corporation page, and index funds chosen from a named catalog instead of by
slug. `/api/banking/corporation/[id]` resolves every borrower, counterparty and
blacklist entry to `{id, name, sequentialId}` in one pair of bulk lookups.

The existing `buyer-search` and `country/[code]/corp-search` endpoints both
filter their results (player-run private corps; one country's non-state corps).
A bank refusing business can refuse anyone, so neither fits, hence the general
`/api/corporations/search`.

**The blacklist is private.** The console payload shipped `blacklist` to any
authenticated viewer, so who a bank refuses was readable by anyone who called
the endpoint. It is now `null` unless the viewer is the CEO, an admin, or the
chair of the chartering currency's central bank. Not a change of intent, just
the gate the UI already implied.

**A cap on the list.** `setBlacklist` deduped and validated but never bounded,
so a caller could store an arbitrarily long array inline on the corporation
document, walked on every refusal check. `BLACKLIST_MAX_ENTRIES` is 50 per list,
counted after dedupe.

To be clear about what was not wrong: the endpoint validated. Zod parsed the
body, `setBlacklist` checked ObjectId shape and existence, and `requireCeo`
gated the write. This was a usability and exposure problem, not an injection or
authorization one.

**Console layout.** Nine editor panels stacked flat under a six-cell stat strip,
every one equally prominent, with no read on whether the bank was healthy. The
chartered console now leads with a health card (what the confidence band means
in words, capital standing against the minimum and the stress scenario, reserves
against the requirement, and loans in arrears) and splits the editors across
Overview, Lending, Funding, Trading and Admin sub-tabs. Trading only appears for
charters that can hold a prop book, and says so plainly when the world has
trading frozen.

## Multi-seat chambers were decided by ballot slots, not votes (ticket #1048, PR #4407)

Both vote-distribution engines split each demographic group's turn pool
straight across CANDIDATES by relative appeal weight. The swing-flow engine
then summed a party's candidates into `partyAppealVotes` for the swing step and
handed the total back at Step 4, and `allocateSeats` pools the same candidate
votes by party for largest remainder. That chain is only sound when every party
fields one candidate, which is the FPTP assumption written into the 7.3.2
notes. In a multi-seat chamber a party fielding two candidates of comparable
appeal took two candidate-shares of every group's pool, so its pooled total and
therefore its seat count scaled with how many candidates it put on the ballot
rather than with its support. Fielding a co-partisan was worth roughly a third
of a delegation: the exact inverse of the real vote-splitting the FPTP spoiler
pass models.

Measured on the 1953 founding House, comparing each state's multi-seat result
against the single-seat races the same electorate voted the same turn:

| State   | Slates       | House result     | Same electorate, single-seat races |
| ------- | ------------ | ---------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| PA (30) | DEM 2, FLP 1 | DEM 62.0%, 19-11 | FLP 53-59%                         |
| CA (30) | FLP 2, DEM 1 | FLP 72.9%, 22-8  | DEM 38-45%                         |
| LA (8)  | DEM 2, FLP 1 | DEM 85.0%, 7-1   | FLP 42%                            |

New `src/lib/electionEngine/slateAllocation.ts` splits a group's pool by SLATE
first, where a slate's weight is the MEAN of its candidates' appeal weights,
then divides a slate's take among its own candidates by their within-slate
weight share. Slate size cancels; slate quality still counts, so a weak
co-partisan drags the mean and a strong one lifts it. Independents are never
pooled, matching `eligibilityGroupKey` in `turn/election/seatAllocation.ts` so
the eligibility gate and the vote split agree on what a party is.

Both engines call the one helper so the two lanes cannot drift again. The
change is byte-identical for every one-candidate-per-party race and every
primary: a slate of size 1 has mean equal to its own weight, the slate weights
sum to the old group total, and the within-slate share is 1, so the arithmetic
reduces term for term. A primary's candidates all share one party, so the
single slate takes the whole pool and the within-slate split reproduces the old
per-candidate split. The identity is pinned by an oracle test against the
legacy expression. Only multi-candidate-per-party races move, which is exactly
the broken set, and the cross-engine 10pt diff harness still passes.

Static re-derivation of the three states above, using final vote totals as a
proxy for appeal weight (approximate, since votes are the post-swing output):
PA becomes FLP 55.0% and 17-13, against 53-59% observed in the state's
single-seat races; CA becomes DEM 42.7% and 13-17, against 38-45% observed. LA
moves from FLP 15.0% to 26.1% and 2 seats, so slate size was the dominant but
not the only factor suppressing FLP there.

Not backfilled. Existing worlds already seated their chambers from the old math
and the fix applies to future races only. Re-running the founding allocation would
rewrite a seated House mid-cycle with bills, whips and leadership ballots
already in flight. The live US House stands at DEM 220 / FLP 215 against a
seeded DEM 213 / FLP 221 / IND 1; the reporting player's party won its
statewide races and lost the chamber to the artifact.

## Bank console party picker threw on select (#4404 follow-up)

`BankConsoleTab`'s search dropdown called `setResults([])` in its `onClick`, but
the component's state had been refactored to a single stamped
`answered: { term, results }` and no `setResults` setter exists. Selecting any
search result threw a ReferenceError, so the blacklist and borrower pickers were
unusable. Now clears via `setAnswered({ term: "", results: [] })`.

The dropdown already collapsed on its own (`setQuery("")` drops `query` below
the 2-character render gate and `visible` derives from `answered.term === term`),
so the throw was the only behavioural effect.

This was also the repo's only outstanding `tsc --noEmit` error. Type errors are
not build-blocking, so it shipped rather than failing the build.

## Interstate haul is now real freight demand (ticket #1039, PR #63)
