Cut from development.

## Added

- National approval now responds to a war while it is being fought. Three
  things feed it: how the front is moving, whether treaty allies are
  contributing, and how long your country has been at war. Nothing appears
  while your nation is at peace.
- How the front is moving is judged against what the war was expected to do,
  not simply against who holds ground. A defence that gives up nothing is a
  political success, an invasion that stalls is a political failure, and a
  government that puts down a rebellion is credited for winning rather than
  penalised for fighting at home. On a proxy front, where neither side is
  expected to gain, any movement counts for whoever made it.
- An ally pulled into a war by a mutual defence treaty is judged on what it
  fields against what its co belligerents field. Matching them is neutral,
  sending nothing costs approval, and carrying more than your share earns it.
  Nobody is penalised in the first few turns, before their forces could
  plausibly have arrived.
- The public backs a fresh war and tires of a long one. That support turns to
  cost after the first year and keeps deepening, measured from the turn your
  own country entered rather than the turn the war began, so joining a war
  already in progress does not bill you for the years before you arrived.

## Changed

- Everything a war contributes arrives and departs a couple of points per turn
  instead of landing at once, including when a war ends. A war that was already
  running when this shipped eases in from nothing rather than applying its whole
  accumulated weight in a single turn.
- Your approval page now lists the reasons behind the stored rating, including
  the ones that were previously invisible: an active national address and any
  international statement about your country. Those were folded into the number
  but never shown.
- National address bumps, international statements and the war block now share
  one ceiling on how much they can lift approval between them, rather than each
  getting its own. A country running a national address alongside a favourable
  international statement will see slightly less combined benefit than before.
- The headline figure on the approval page is now the same stored number that
  the history chart plots, the Executive page shows and elections read. It was
  previously recalculated from national metric averages alone, so it could
  differ from the chart directly beneath it and did not account for a national
  address, an international statement or a war.
