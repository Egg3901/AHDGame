## Bond reliance uses net bond income

The bond-reliance machinery exists for corporations that live off coupons
(#941): their coupon slice is discounted 0.75x for interest-rate risk and a
graduated penalty caps how richly coupon-derived earnings are valued.

It measured that reliance as gross coupons received over NET normalized
earnings. For a leveraged operator the net figure is already shrunken by its
own interest expense, so the more a corporation borrowed, the more of its
earnings the formula classified as bond income. Observed on production (corp
484): 371K/year of coupons against 763K/year of interest PAID — a net bond
COST of 392K/year — yet the formula clamped its entire operating earnings to
zero, applied the 0.75x discount to what remained, and halved that with the
maximum reliance penalty. A farming and retail operator was priced as a pure
bond fund because it was in debt.

The bond slice is now `max(0, coupons − issuer interest)`. A net debtor has
zero bond reliance and keeps its operating earnings whole; a pure coupon fund
pays exactly the same discount and penalty as before. Corporations that hold
bonds while carrying debt are penalized only on the net coupon slice.

Leveraged operators with real earnings re-rate upward on their next pricing
pass. Nothing changes for corps without issued bonds.
