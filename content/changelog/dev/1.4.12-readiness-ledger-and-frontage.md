  A study of the German front found that a formation was written back at the

Cut from development.

## Fixed

- A formation now ends a battle with the readiness it has left, rather than
  with the amount the battle took out of it. The two were being confused when
  the result was written back, so a fresh division that should have come
  through a fight in good order was recorded as almost spent, and every army
  in the game sat at the bottom of the readiness scale no matter how it was
  posted. It also inverted the ledger a second time: because a protected
  formation loses less, it was recorded as more exhausted than the infantry
  beside it, which is the very thing the last release set out to end.

- Resting an army no longer shrinks its front line. Frontage was charged
  against how hard a formation could fight, and readiness is part of that, so
  an army that recovered found it had less room than when it was worn out.
  What a formation costs in frontage is now a question of how much ground it
  takes up. Everything else about it, its equipment, veterancy, training and
  its general, still tells.

- Supply is no longer decided by a label. Depot throughput was granted for
  every formation a commander marked as rear area, without limit and without
  checking whether it was standing in the line at the time, so an army could
  buy itself perfect supply by relabelling and lose nothing for it. Supply now
  reads the formations actually held behind the line, and counts them only up
  to the size of the line they are feeding.

## Changed

- A front holds considerably more in contact than it did. The width a front
  offers was set against a measure that has since changed twice over, and
  under it no army in the game fitted, not even a small one. Numbers now tell:
  a great power committing its army fights like one, and an alliance that puts
  two armies in the field is meaningfully stronger than either of them alone.
  Before this a superpower's forty four formations performed worse on their
  own than a client state's eleven.

- Because an army recovers between battles again, the pace you fight at
  matters. Pressing every turn keeps a force exhausted; giving it a few turns
  to gather itself brings it back to the readiness its posture is meant to
  hold.

- Formations recover twice as quickly, eight points a turn rather than four.
  A battle costs a worn formation far more than a fresh one, so at the old
  rate an army spent down to nothing could not climb back out between
  engagements at any pace it was realistically fought at. A force at rock
  bottom now returns to full alert in twelve turns rather than twenty three.
  The war room quotes the new rate.

- Formations already in the field have had their readiness restored. The
  numbers recorded against them were never readiness in the first place, so
  there was nothing to preserve. Every army caught by this was repaired in the
  same pass, so no side gains on another.
