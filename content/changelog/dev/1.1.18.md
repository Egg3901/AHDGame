## What changed

The corporation masthead and the Overview tab reported income per turn as
"realized income minus dividend distribution". That was wrong twice over. The
turn engine books realized income as after-tax operating profit with the
dividend payout already taken out, so subtracting a dividend again double
counted it. Worse, the figure being subtracted came from the projected income
statement, which is rebuilt from live sector state and can run several times
ahead of what the engine actually booked. Netting a projection-sized dividend
off a realized-sized income flipped the sign: a corporation earning money read
as losing money, while the Financials page, which used the realized figure
directly, correctly showed a profit. Reported in ticket-1098.

The Financials headline had the mirror problem. It added bond coupon income and
dividends received on top of the realized figure, which has folded both in
since the earlier bond-income fix, so it overstated net income by the whole
non-operating leg.

All three surfaces now read one shared income basis. The headline is
pre-dividend net income, the masthead and Overview show what is retained after
the payout, and the dividends used are the ones the engine actually paid, which
are now carried through from the turn snapshot instead of being re-estimated.

## Why market cap can fall during an expansion

Nothing changed in the valuation model, but the driver was invisible. A share
price is built from tangible book (cash, sector value, builds in progress and
tech assets, minus issued bond debt) plus capitalised earnings power. When an
expansion is financed with a corporate bond, the debt lands on the book the
turn it is issued, while the new capacity only lifts sector value as it starts
to earn. If the debt outruns the assets, the tangible book leg falls to zero
and the share price is carried by earnings power alone, so market cap can halve
through a build-out that is going well on revenue and profit. The market-cap
tile now spells this out, so the number is explained rather than mysterious.
