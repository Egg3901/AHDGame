Cut from development.

## Fixed

- A House race now resizes when a census reapportions its state. The census
  rewrote each state's district count and redrew its map, but the election
  already under way kept the seat count it opened with, and the next cycle
  inherited that stale number from the last one. The two then disagreed about
  how big the delegation was: seats were awarded across the new map while the
  page counted them against the old total. A state reapportioned from nine
  seats to eight showed "projected 7 of 9 seats" with a ninth block that could
  never fill, and seated eight members against a nine seat header.
- The House seats its full membership again. Because every reapportioned state
  resolved against a stale total, the chamber came up short by the number of
  seats the census had moved.
- Live races are corrected on the next turn rather than at the next election,
  so a race already on the ballot when the census fires is fixed in place.
- The next cycle is sized from the census too. A House race that resolved right
  after a reapportionment used to hand its old seat count straight to its
  successor, so the correction was undone the moment the seat was contested
  again.
- A territory admitted to statehood partway through a game now sizes its House
  race from its own apportionment instead of falling back to a single seat, and
  a later census can resize it like any other state.
- District maps are redrawn for every reapportioned state, so a state's map can
  no longer disagree with the number of seats it holds.

## Changed

- Seat projections in states with drawn districts now apply the same minimum
  vote share as every other chamber. This path had kept an older rule that
  readmitted parties below the threshold whenever fewer of them cleared it than
  there were seats to fill. Districted states almost always have more seats than
  parties, so the threshold almost never applied and a party well under the
  floor could still collect a seat through proportional rounding, while the same
  party was awarded nothing in states without drawn districts. A candidate
  taking 89 percent of a state could be projected to lose a seat to a party on
  7 percent.
- Parties below the minimum share no longer receive seats in districted states.
  Where a small party previously held one or two seats on rounding alone, those
  seats now go to the parties that cleared the floor. The rule that stops a
  leading party from sweeping every district is unchanged.
