## Cold War Tension

The world now shares one tension reading, 0 to 100, banded from DETENTE up to
BRINK. Nuclear tests, escalation on the war ladder, and active crises push it
up; it relaxes each turn toward a floor set by how armed and embroiled the
world actually is, so a spike fades but a hot world never reads calm.

Tension is not a scoreboard. It tightens DEFCON, raises defence procurement
demand across the world, and burns detente goodwill. The Conflicts page has
been reframed around it: one headline gauge, a recent-developments feed, and a
strip of the world's nuclear powers, with the boards below decluttered.

## The nuclear programme

Defence chiefs of the United States, the Soviet Union, and the United Kingdom
can now open a nuclear weapons programme, gated behind the Nuclear Delivery
doctrine. The device ladder runs from the first fission bomb through boosted
weapons to the hydrogen bomb and MIRVs; every device advance requires a
nuclear test, and a test is a world event with a tension spike attached.

Delivery is its own investment: bombers, IRBMs, ICBMs, and missile submarines
each harden the deterrent. Warhead production is ordered per turn, paid for
out of the defence appropriation, and a stockpile without a delivery leg
deters nobody.

## What landed in this release

27 changes shipped under 1.3.0.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Impeachment now counts the whole chamber, and NPP blocs vote on the merits**
  Removing a president or governor now needs a majority or two-thirds of ALL chamber seats instead of a majority of whoever showed up, and NPP blocs vote on ideology rather than party label alone.
- **Slate assignments stop vanishing without explanation**
  An NPP a party chair assigned through the Slate tab could quietly disappear on the next turn, as though the assignment had never saved. Those rows now stay on the slate and say why they could not be filed, and a chair's pick now takes the party slot from a candidate the game picked on its own.
- **Turn processing keeps money and political actions consistent**
  Economic, election and corporation actions now keep one consistent result when player requests overlap turn processing, while several automatic systems use the correct currency, migration, wage and election data.

### Government and legislation

- **1.3.0: The German Question stops turning on the bank balance**
  A country carrying national debt was refused every German Question play that cost money, because the treasury reading is a signed cash position and a debt makes it negative. Nations can now spend into debt here, as they already do everywhere else. Delegation plays can also be bought with political capital instead of money, and delegations bank capital faster.
- **Contracting economies no longer report growth**
  Seventeen countries recorded a flat 2.5% annual growth figure regardless of what their economy was actually doing, and several of them were shrinking. Their central banks were setting policy against that fiction.
- **Budget and metric figures read from one source**
  The Economy page, the Budget page and the public API each derived the same figures their own way, so the deficit, national averages and inflation could disagree between pages. Each now reads one traced source.
- **Cabinet office records are no longer public**
  A cabinet office page now opens only for the minister who holds it, their country's head of government or head of state, and admins. Everyone else, foreign governments included, gets a notice instead of the department's figures.
- **Region bills keep the law they replaced**
  A state or region bill now records the law in force when it was proposed, so its detail page keeps showing what actually changed after it passes instead of looking as though it re-passed the law already on the books.

### War and diplomacy

- **1.3.0: Crises become campaigns**
  Global response crises now remember earlier commitments, move through a persistent campaign, test each country's real capacity, conceal covert action, and leave human and strategic consequences behind.
- **1.3.0: The bomb in the basement**
  East Germany can now pursue a nuclear weapons programme in secret, under the Soviets' nose. It is long, slow, and expensive; funding it harder moves it faster and burns brighter; and a Soviet crackdown sets it back years. The breakout test, if it ever comes, is a world event.
- **1.3.0: The world answers**
  International crises are now shared political moments. Governments receive different choices according to their role, spend real money when they commit aid or forces, and collectively determine whether a confrontation escalates, settles, produces sanctions, or opens a relief effort.
- **1.3.0: Defence contracts you cannot tear up for free**
  Cancelling a defence contract now costs the minister who does it. A supplier whose live order is torn up is paid the margin it was promised, the lots stay charged to the quarter's tranche, and the cancellation goes on the public wire with the minister's name on it.
- **1.3.0: The German Question names its delegations**
  The four delegation blocks on the German Question board listed a tier, a multiplier, and whether the seat had moved, but never said which offices carry the seat or who holds them. Each block now names both eligible offices in the country's own words and the player in each, or marks it vacant.
- **1.3.0: The open floor stops being first come, first served**
  One character could spend an afternoon's action points on a single button and take the whole public tier's influence for the turn, leaving everyone else's contribution scaled away to nothing. Each character now gets one use of each personal play per turn, and the floor's ceiling grows with how many people turn out instead of sitting at a level six players could fill on their own.
- **NATO and the Warsaw Pact defend their members**
  The two Cold War alliances now honour the collective-defence charters they already carried. An attack on a member takes every player-led member of that alliance into the war at once, with no vote, and an ally brought in that way cannot make a separate peace while the country it came to defend is still fighting.
- **1.3.0: Arsenals with a history**
  New worlds now open with the nuclear age already underway: historical programmes and stockpiles for the founding powers are seeded by era, and the wiki documents both the nuclear programme and the cold war tension gauge.
- **Bar war declarations between alliance members**
  Two members of the same bloc can no longer declare war on each other. NATO states cannot attack NATO states, and Warsaw Pact states cannot attack Warsaw Pact states, unless one of them withdraws from the alliance first.
- **German Question shows when a category is maxed for the turn**
  Each institution card on the German Question board now shows how much of the open floor's per-turn limit has been used, and marks the category MAXED once the public's net push has reached the ceiling and further personal plays can add nothing until the next tick.

### Economy and corporations

- **1.3.0: Nobody works in two places at once**
  Corporate sectors can no longer employ more people than live in the state. Labour demand now uses productive plant capacity, higher wages compete for scarce workers, and persistent vacancies gradually grow and attract the local workforce. Capacity a sector cannot staff no longer produces.
- **Freight capacity now sells where it is based**
  Freight supply is haulage capacity based in one state, but it was sold into a national market where unrelated logistics sectors could claim its demand. Freight now clears against its own state, so local shortages and gluts reach the corporations that can act on them.
- **Economic figures agree across every page**
  GDP, national growth, company revenue and stock market totals were each derived separately by the pages that showed them, so the same figure could differ depending on where you looked. Every one now reads from a single traced source.
- **1.3.2: The corporation commodities tab reports real production**
  The Commodities tab derived a corporation's output from sector revenue, a proxy the rest of the game stopped using when plants shipped. It disagreed with the sector pages and with what a corporation could actually contract, by one to two orders of magnitude in both directions. It now reports the same production the world market books.
- **Buff non-US catch-up growth through public capital and planned development**
  Productive public budgets and capable planned economies now support sustained catch-up growth without reducing US growth.
- **Supply agreements are fair and explain their settlements**
  Scarce private supply is now shared proportionally instead of depending on contract row order. Damages are measured against achievable output, every settlement explains its cash movement, and the corporation pages distinguish plant activity, market output, corporate net flow, and historical reporting changes.
- **The freight warning now names the state that actually ran out of trucks**
  A sector's freight warning was computed from world totals, so every state got the same number and almost every physical sector wore the light whatever its local network was doing. It now counts only the output an origin state's own freight capacity refused to haul.

### Interface and platform

- **Keep ads and search focused on reviewed content**
  Google ads now stay on reviewed editorial pages. Player news permalinks and unedited wiki starter pages are no longer submitted to search engines.
- **Wiki search covers every wiki surface**
  The wiki search box now finds parties, seats, offices, elections, category indexes and learning paths, not only written articles, and it puts the closest title match at the top instead of whatever the database happened to return first.
