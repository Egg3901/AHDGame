  plants were taxed gross while its farming losses were invisible to the
  base. Tax now nets losses against profits across the whole corporation,
  with per-state and per-country attribution scaled proportionally.

Cut from development.

Under the old nexus rule every jurisdiction taxed the activity inside it,
regardless of what the rest of the corporation was doing. That is a real
taxation model, but combined with sector-level margin dispersion it produced
absurd bills: a corporation could run a consolidated loss every turn and
still be one of its country's largest taxpayers.

Corporate tax is now assessed on a consolidated basis: sector losses net
against sector profits before anything is taxed. A corporation with no
losing sectors pays exactly what it paid before; a corporation at a
consolidated loss pays nothing; in between, each profitable sector's
taxable income scales down pro-rata, so the state and country revenue
attribution keeps the same shape at the reduced level. SOEs, pass-through
structures and bond-coupon taxation are unchanged.
