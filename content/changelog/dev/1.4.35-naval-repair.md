Cut from development.

## Fixed

- Ships and aircraft now recover condition. Combat took condition away and nothing
  ever gave it back, so a formation reduced to nothing stayed that way permanently:
  it fought at no strength, closed no trade lane, and no order a commander could
  give would change it. On the live world this had already happened to several
  fleets, one of them a nation's entire navy.

- A formation too damaged to fight now withdraws to home water to mend, instead of
  being sent back to the front every turn. This matters more than it sounds: supply
  at a distant front is usually too low for repairs to happen at all, so a wreck
  ordered back to the fighting stayed a wreck for ever.

## Changed

- Repair is faster where a formation is properly based. A ship resting in port
  recovers several times what one holding station recovers, and repair scales with
  supply, so a fleet operating far from home mends slowly and one sitting in
  unsupplied water does not mend at all. An Airlift wing raising a station's supply
  now speeds up every repair there.

- Materiel from the national arsenal repairs a formation past what free repair can
  reach, wherever it is stationed. Free repair alone tops out short of full unless
  a formation is resting in a home port, so getting the last stretch back on a fleet
  that is staying at sea means spending naval or air lots on it. Repairs draw from
  the store before equipment top-ups do, because returning a wrecked hull to service
  is worth far more than adding a little kit to one that is already fighting.

- Badly damaged ships are now much worse at blockading, not merely proportionally
  worse. Blockade has always weakened as a hull took damage; below half condition it
  now falls away sharply, because closing a trade route is patient work that a ship
  held together by damage-control parties cannot keep up.

- The war room now warns when ships at a front are too damaged to blockade
  effectively, says roughly how much pressure that is costing, and names both ways
  out: send them to a home port, or award a defence contract to a shipyard so the
  arsenal can repair them where they are.

- Formations do not mend on a turn they fought. You repair between engagements, not
  during one, so breaking off contact is a real decision rather than a free one.

- How far free repair goes depends on where you are. A home port restores a
  formation completely, an allied port most of the way, and holding station well
  short of full. Getting the last stretch back means either rotating home or, once
  it arrives, spending materiel from the national arsenal.

- The naval and air command page now states what each formation is recovering and
  why. It names the rate, the limit it is heading for, and when a formation is not
  mending it says what is stopping it, whether that is supply too low for a yard to
  work or a limit that only a home port can lift.
