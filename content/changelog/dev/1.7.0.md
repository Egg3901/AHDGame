Local worlds now start on Windows in seconds and keep every turn when you quit. The presidential primary has one screen and new ways to fight a rival in a state, turns read far less data, and every country's starting army is written by hand in every era.

## What landed in this release

34 changes shipped.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Getting to a state, and the levers listed once**
  Travelling to a state was a working mechanic with no button anywhere, so canvassing outside your home state was gated behind an instruction you had no way to follow. The campaign manager now shows where you are and lets you move, right above the canvassing it unlocks. The operations levers, which appeared three times on one page, appear once.
- **One more way to fight a rival in a state**
  State operations gains vote suppression, which takes a slice off a rival's count in one state and shows on the board as well as on the night. Building it meant teaching the primary board to read turnout modifiers at all, so party GOTV and canvassing now show up on it too.
- **State operations: one hub, and something to do about a rival**
  A presidential primary came down to camping in one state and hoping your positions beat theirs. Camping, the home-state surge, Campaign Presence and canvassing now sit together in a State operations section on the campaign manager, and the field of rivals sits beside them: open a rival's row and you can open a local attack that drags their favourability down in a state you name.
- **The presidential primary reads in one place**
  The primary screen now carries the state board, the carve-up of whichever state you pick, a calendar you can click into, and the two campaign actions that used to live only on the per-party page. Both screens read the same assembly, so their figures cannot disagree, and a state that has already voted shows what happened rather than what was forecast.
- **Coalition weakness means a group you are losing**
  The card ranked demographic groups by how much of your own support they make up, so it led with small groups you were winning and buried large ones you were being beaten in. It now ranks by your share of each group against the rest of the field, which is the question the card was always meant to answer.
- **The home-state surge moves votes**
  Surging your home state charged the fee, spent the actions, marked itself used, and changed nothing. The boost it recorded was never read by the primary, so the contest ran as though you had not bought it. It now lifts you where you paid for it to, on the wave and in the projection alike.
- **Auto-reelection entry stops querying per eligible character**
  The auto-reelection turn phase issued roughly three awaited database round trips for every eligible character in every open race, plus an insert each. A nationwide election cycle put that into the hundreds of serial round trips inside the turn budget. It now resolves everything up front.
- **Bundestag second votes stop being a copy of the first votes**
  The ticket-split layer was keyed on a retired demographic vocabulary, its rate table named parties that exist in no seed, and it was never reached anyway, so every Land's second-vote total came back identical to its first. Split-ticket voting now works, with era-appropriate rates.
- **Election spawning split out of its 4,000-line module**
  Every country's electoral calendar lived in one file alongside the shared engine, so a single-country change meant a diff against the whole thing. The engine, the cross-country helpers and each country's calendar now live apart.
- **Endorsement withdrawal phases stop issuing one query per endorsement**
  The two endorsement-withdrawal turn phases resolved their election, candidate and office holder one lookup at a time inside the loop, so a busy election cycle cost hundreds of serial round trips on the turn hot path. Reads are now batched up front.
- **Maine and Nebraska stop voting three and four times**
  Every presidential electoral unit drew its electorate from its parent state, so once the district split activated Maine's three units and Nebraska's four each polled the whole state. Both states contributed several times over to the national popular vote.
- **Projected seats and resolved seats now run the same apportionment code**
  The seats a player was shown before an election and the seats the race actually resolved to were computed by two separate implementations of the same rule. They had already drifted twice. There is now one.

### Government and legislation

- **A country's surplus is now the same number everywhere it is used**
  The stored figure for a nation's surplus and its debt principal could drift away from what the underlying revenue, spending and treasury actually said. That drifted figure was deciding whether a treasury transfer breached the debt ceiling and how much sovereign debt to issue each quarter. Both now read the real number, and the stored copies are corrected at the end of every turn.
- **Atomic defence appropriation settlement**
  Defence appropriations and upkeep overdrafts now settle atomically, preventing concurrent turn passes from charging a treasury twice.
- **Dead policy-reactions phase removed, state policies stamped with their country**
  Policy reactions were written on every enactment and decayed by a turn phase that nothing ever read; the live collection was empty. The module, phase and type are gone. State policy rows now carry the enacting country.
- **Editing a starting army no longer re-prices every army already in the field**
  What a country's forces cost to run is measured against the army it started with. That comparison was recalculated from the current rulebook every time it was asked, so changing any starting army quietly changed the running costs of every army in a world already underway. A world now remembers its own starting figures.

### War and diplomacy

- **Armies no longer perfectly pave the roads they bombed**
  A war fought across your territory now wrecks its roads, and the damage reaches the economy through transport efficiency and productivity. Until now a war could kill more than a million men and leave the ground it was fought over in perfect condition, because nothing in the economy knew a war was happening at all.
- **Every country's starting army is now authored, in every era**
  The United States, Britain, Germany, Japan, Ireland and China had no written order of battle, so the seeder invented three to five random units per branch for them. That left Ireland outgunning America in 1953 and gave France the only aircraft carrier in the game. All six are now written out by hand across all seven eras, along with the Soviet rosters for 1999, 2007 and 2023.
- **What the War for Germany taught us about how battles work**
  Four fixes from the post-mortem on the war that killed 1.24 million men. An ally's supply train no longer stops your bullets. A front no longer runs to the pole before the other bloc is allowed to field an army. An army that has lost everything can rearm again even without a defence industry. And formations held in depth are neither punished nor rewarded for being out of the line.
- **"Bring both sides to the table" now actually opens mediation**
  During a scripted nationwide strike there is usually no player or NPP wage dispute in the struck industry, and the crisis option that brokers one required a pre-existing dispute to mediate. The most diplomatic presidential path silently did nothing while still paying out its approval effect.
- **Covert nuclear programmes no longer survive a world reset**
  The covert nuclear programme collection was never classified in the seed manifest, and the reset sweep is manifest-driven, so every prior campaign's programme stayed on disk. A fresh world could open with a country already part-way to breakout.
- **Crisis GDP rate shocks now survive the metric engine**
  Crisis GDP growth effects now enter the sector growth signal consumed by the metric engine. The engine no longer overwrites those rate shocks while the separate one-time physical GDP loss remains unchanged.

### Economy and corporations

- **Clear stale latent shortage diagnostics**
  Commodity price rows now remove prior-turn truncation diagnostics when a commodity becomes uncapped, and remove only the unavailable multiple when supply reaches zero.
- **Corporation financials maths pulled out where it can be tested**
  The three calculations behind the corporation detail page's most persistent bugs (realized against nameplate revenue, currency restatement and brand-loyalty disclosure) were buried inside a 1,750-line function and could only be exercised by loading the whole page.
- **Idle upkeep can no longer be priced at a running unit's full cost**
  A sector whose first plants turn was input-starved stamped an upkeep cost basis of 1.0 and held it for life, billing every idle unit at the full running-unit cost. The held and live bases are now capped at the default sector cost share.
- **Idle-capacity upkeep pulled out of the sector turn where it can be tested**
  The rule that charges a plants sector for capacity it bought and is not using lived inside an 1,800-line function and could only be exercised by running a whole sector tick. It now has its own module and its own tests.
- **NPP bond purchases use the financial ledger subject model**
  Autonomous NPP bond purchases now record their own financial transaction and derive balanced shadow-ledger entries, preserving NPP investment-account semantics alongside player bond settlements.

### Interface and platform

- **NPP autonomy v5 and a difficulty policy that changes decisions, not entitlements**
  Adds the v5 autonomy tier, where a government keeps a bounded set of standing goals across agenda recomputes and is graded on them, and splits local-world difficulty into a resource half and a decision half so a funding advantage is never presented as skill.
- **Turns decode 60% less data and make far fewer database calls**
  The hourly turn reads 60% less data from the database and issues far fewer queries, so turns finish sooner on the server and much sooner in singleplayer. New guards keep future phases from undoing it.
- **Local worlds no longer freeze on their first start on Windows**
  On Windows a new local world could sit at "still building the world" for minutes and then give up, while the same build started in seconds on Linux. The game server was started on the launcher's own input and output handles, and on Windows that left it accepting connections without ever answering. The server now runs on its own captured output, loads only the pages it is asked for, stops its database cleanly when you quit, and the launcher says which step it is on and why a step failed.
- **Modal dialogs announce themselves and close on Escape**
  Nine hand-rolled overlays carried no dialog role, no modal flag and no Escape handler, so a screen reader did not know a dialog had opened and keyboard users could not dismiss one. The shared Modal also announced itself as an unnamed "dialog".
- **Thirteen more routes show a layout skeleton instead of a blank page**
  Segments with an error boundary but no loading state rendered nothing at all while the server worked. Officials, notifications, news, actions, world, bond and forex now show a skeleton matching the layout that is about to arrive.

### Other changes

- **Changelog entries are cut per release, not per pull request**
  313 per-change entries reaching 1.4.63 are folded into the ten releases that actually happened, ending at 1.6.0. A pull request now writes a versionless note and only the release script mints a version, and merging a version bump to main tags it and opens the GitHub release.
- **Public copy reads the playable country list and the version from the game**
  The FAQ, the about page, the search snippet and the landing promo pill each kept their own hand-typed answer to which countries are open and which version is live, and all four had gone stale in different directions. They now resolve both from the running world, and a test fails the build if any of them starts hardcoding again.
