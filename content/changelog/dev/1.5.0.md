Money used to appear and vanish at the edges of the financial system. This release closes those edges. Loans advance under one rulebook with a full audit trail, every bank flow settles once and only once, bonds are underwritten by a pool that can run out, and index funds price redemptions when they are paid rather than when they are asked for.

## What landed in this release

32 changes shipped under 1.5.0.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Building Org now costs money as well as Political Strength**
  Organizing a state was free apart from Political Strength, so a party's treasury and its ground game had almost nothing to do with each other. Build Org now bills the treasury of whichever tier pays the PS, at a price that scales with your country's currency and with the pressure ladder, so grinding one state costs money as fast as it costs Strength. A short treasury slows organizing rather than stopping it: a click the treasury can only part cover still lands, for proportionally less Org, and a click it cannot cover at all is refused for free.
- **Batched campaign-strength support, an endorsement prompt on Actions, and the map leads the presidential race page**
  Supporting a presidential campaign was one click for one contribution, so backing a candidate meaningfully meant sitting on the button. The Support dialog now offers x1, x5 and Max, priced at exactly what the same number of single clicks would cost. The Actions page gained a card that prompts players with a live presidential race at home and no endorsement yet to pick a candidate, and issues the endorsement in place. The electoral map now opens the presidential race page instead of sitting below three cards that analyse it.
- **Public election results carry seat splits and candidate standing**
  The public API's opt-in election results now return a projected seat split for multi-seat races, plus current favorability and political influence for every candidate. The single-election endpoint reports the same two figures, which it had always listed as fields but only ever returned as null.
- **The registration drive actually registers people now**
  Every party funding a voter registration drive has been paying into a lever that could not move. The drive drew only on a state's pool of unregistered and independent voters, and that pool has been empty everywhere for hundreds of turns because nothing ever put anyone back into it. Drives now reach the rolls of parties carrying more registration than their organisation supports, half of all lapsing registration returns to the pool instead of passing straight to a rival, and two states that were quietly frozen out of the whole system are back in it.
- **A region that changes country is re-sized and re-scored for it**
  Two things a region carried across a border were never recalculated for its new country. Its seat count in the national chamber stayed frozen at whatever its very first election said, so the German Laender showed one number on the elections page and another on the map. And its political metrics kept the structural baseline that had been measured against the law book it left, so the acceded Laender were credited with two law books at once and their scores climbed toward the ceiling. Both are now recalculated when the border moves.
- **Organizing a big state now costs more than a small one**
  Building Org cost the same in New York as in Alaska, even though a point of Org in New York carries about sixty times the weight at election time. The price now follows the size of the state you are organizing, up to twice the national average in the largest and as little as half in the smallest. Since most parties concentrate on the states that decide elections, this raises the typical organizing bill rather than simply moving it around, and building out into smaller states is correspondingly cheaper.
- **NPP management list shows the race and links to profiles**
  The NPP Management tab labelled every candidate with a bare "Running", so party leadership could not tell who was standing for what without opening each politician one at a time. The tag now names the race, and clicking a name opens that politician's profile.

### Government and legislation

- **States and regions get the full political registry, and their own code of law**
  The political registry that runs a country was built from each region's own board, but no region page ever showed it properly. Regions now have their own Metrics tab carrying the whole thing: nine category scores computed from that region's values, a drill-down to any single metric, the laws and standing conditions moving it, the underlying statistics behind it, and a side by side comparison against up to three neighbours. Regions also start keeping a trend record, so the movement figures fill in as the campaign runs. The state Laws and Policy page has been rebuilt to read like the national one, with numbered titles, enactment stamps, costs and a Record view of the region's own legislative history.
- **The intelligence services now answer to the legislature for their money**
  The intelligence console shipped with no way to pay for anything, so no operation could ever be run. Every country now carries an intelligence appropriation law that starts at Unfunded, and a service spends only what its legislature votes it. The console gains the controls to fund a network, order an operation and set a counter intelligence posture.
- **Germany's budget: grants that were never booked, and tax bases that outgrew the economy**
  Germany's sixteen states were each being paid a federal transfer that the national budget recorded as nothing at all, and their own budgets had stopped updating entirely when the two Germanies became one. On the income side the tax bases had been growing faster than the economy they are measured against, with nothing able to stop them: taxable income had reached more than the whole country produced in a year, and a quarter of the resulting revenue was being quietly thrown away by the ceiling meant to catch punitive tax rates. Both sides are now accounted for.
- **NPP companies keep the money they earn**
  A company run by a caretaker board lost its entire operating income every turn it placed a build. The caretaker wrote the cash balance as a whole number rather than as a change, and that write landed after the turn had already paid the income in, so it overwrote the payment with the balance from before it. A build costing a few hundred was enough to erase millions. Caretaker spending is now recorded as a change to the balance, so income and spending both stand.

### War and diplomacy

- **Reunified Germany gets its mines back**
  A country that absorbs another kept its new regions' factories and quietly lost their mines. Reunification left the western Laender holding the Ruhr coal, the Saar iron and the Niedersachsen gas fields with nothing to dig them up: no state enterprise, no market to found one in, and no way for any player to notice beyond the sector list coming back empty. The mines are rebuilt, the cause is fixed, and a reunified country is now named and listed as the country it became.

### Economy and corporations

- **Banking rebuild, stage one: every charter's loans advance, one rulebook, full audit trail**
  An investment bank's loans now advance every turn like everyone else's. The rules that decide what a charter may do, which switches are on, and every balance-sheet number now have exactly one definition each, and every banking action leaves a uniform audit record with per-turn health counters behind it. No balance constant changed.
- **Banking rebuild, stage three: savings accounts, charter lifecycle and recovery**
  Player savings become real accounts with real backing: when you point your savings at a bank the cash moves into its vault and the bank owes you, when the bank pays interest it stays as your backing, and when a bank fails or hands back its charter your balance goes back to the central bank intact, paid out before anyone else. Every bank now has a visible lifecycle stage, and anything a crash leaves half-done is finished automatically at the start of the next turn.
- **Banking rebuild, stage two: every bank flow settles exactly once**
  Capital injections and payouts, the discount window and margin line, loan origination, approval, servicing and default, household funding, bank resolution and the interbank market now run through one rules boundary and one settlement journal. A crash anywhere leaves a visible, repairable record and never a loan without cash behind it or a payment charged twice. Banks now fund the loans they write from their own vault.
- **Bond index funds**
  Twelve new index funds hold bonds instead of shares: a government bond fund for each of the eight fund countries, and global investment grade sovereign, emerging markets sovereign, investment grade corporate, and high yield corporate funds. They buy from the bond market pool at its ask, earn coupons into their NAV, and sell to the pool at its bid to pay redemptions. Any fund short of cash for a queued redemption now sells bonds after shares.
- **Fund redemptions are priced when they are paid, and share out fairly**
  A redemption used to lock the fund's price on the day it was requested and hold that price for as long as the payout took. If the fund fell in the meantime, the redeemer still drew the old price and everyone who stayed absorbed the difference. Payouts now use the current price, and a queue the fund cannot clear is split in proportion to units instead of first come, first served.
- **Index funds drop holdings in companies that no longer exist**
  A fund could only leave a position by selling it, and a dissolved company has no buyer. Those positions stayed on the books at whatever they were last worth, and the fund's price counted them as backing. Funds now write them off instead.
- **Index funds now make deeper equity markets**
  Index funds can place cash-backed bids across their mandates, and market sells can execute directly against those bids. Profitable caretaker corporations can also keep paying dividends while they invest.
- **New bonds are underwritten, not conjured**
  Issuing a bond used to fund the issuer in full the instant the button was pressed, with every unit parked in a float nobody had paid for. The bond market pool now underwrites a new issue with its own cash at par, the issuer is funded for exactly the units it took, and the remainder places turn by turn as the pool finds cash. A sovereign auction the market will not absorb feeds the sovereign-default crisis classifier with its real fill; an autonomous central bank monetizes a bounded slice of the shortfall, a player-run one is told.
- **Public equity markets now have real liquidity**
  Public shares now trade against a finite market pool in each currency, using an executable bid, ask, and visible sell depth. Purchases replenish the pool, sales draw down real cash, public-float dividends refill it, and new share issues place only as far as the market can fund them.
- **The bond market has a counterparty**
  Every bond trade against the public float used to mint or burn money. Buying destroyed the buyer's cash, selling created the proceeds from nothing, and coupons on unsold units vanished. Each currency now has a bond market pool that holds the float and real cash: purchases pay into it, sales are paid out of it and refused when it cannot cover them, and issuers pay coupons and principal on pool-held units into it. Surplus countries also stop rolling over debt they no longer owe.
- **The bond market quotes a bid and an ask**
  Each currency's bond market pool now sizes itself to the economy, takes in savings when it is short and returns cash when it hoards, and prices every bond around the rate-derived fair value with a dealer spread. Sells settle at the bid, buys at the ask, and both slide down when the pool is short of cash or when the sovereign-default demand model has little appetite for the issuer. The bond page shows the quote and how many units the market can buy right now.
- **Corporate tax booked once on the corp ledger**
  A corporation's transaction log counted its corporate tax twice. The turn credited the company with its income after tax had already been taken out, then billed the same tax again as a separate line. Any company whose tax bill came to more than the income it kept therefore read as loss making on a turn it had genuinely made money. The credit is now recorded before tax, so the two lines settle to the cash the company actually keeps.
- **Fund constituent names survive world resets correctly**
  Index funds no longer carry holdings, unit supply, or reserve state from a previous world into the next one, and existing orphaned holdings are removed.
- **Line of credit takes savings through the account**
  A line-of-credit payment that runs past your wallet takes the rest from savings. Once a currency's savings accounts are the book of record, that overflow is now a real withdrawal: the cash behind your balance moves, your balance falls, and the payment is made from money that actually exists.
- **Placing unsold bonds no longer empties the pool before an auction**
  The bond market pool holds a quarter of maturing sovereign face as a working balance for the next auction. Per-turn placement of earlier unsold units was spending that reserve, so a country could arrive at its quarterly auction to an empty pool. Placement now spends only cash above half the pool's target.
- **Savings migration reads float dust as zero**
  The savings account migration refused to run if any player's savings balance held float residue left over from hundreds of interest credits, values like minus five hundred-millionths of a cent. Those are read as zero now. A balance a player could actually be owed still stops the migration.

### Interface and platform

- **Supporter tiers get a bigger API allowance**
  Personal API keys were capped at the same 60 requests per minute for everyone. Keys owned by an active Supporter+ account now get 1.5x that allowance and Supporter++ keys 3x, applied automatically to every key on the account for as long as the pledge is active.
- **The turn stops reading things it never looks at**
  A turn was pulling hundreds of thousands of documents out of the database and throwing most of them away. The ledger was loaded 61,398 rows at a time to compute a sum the database could have done itself, every corporation carried a block of policy positions no turn phase reads, and each turn wrote a megabyte of market diagnostics nothing ever read back. Decoding those documents, not running the simulation, was the bulk of the work. The reads are narrower now and the numbers they produce are unchanged.

### Other changes

- **Monetary governance state machine**
  Central bank meetings, rate votes and rate changes now run through one shared set of rules, so skipped meetings, stuck sessions and dead boards behave the same everywhere. The committee panel tells you what you can do, why not, and when the next deadline lands.
- **Supporter status reads the same to you as it does to everyone else**
  Your own profile kept showing your supporter badge, border and highlight colour after the pledge lapsed, while every other player already saw none of it. The two pages disagreed about the same person. Both now follow the same rule. Supporters who subscribe through Lakeside rather than Patreon are also described as such instead of being labelled Patreon patrons.
