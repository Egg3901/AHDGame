The longest arc the game has had. It opens with the presidential rework and closes with a reunified Germany, and in between the world learns to fight: declared wars, fronts with real supply and frontage, coalition casualties, and peace terms that can convert a system of government. Countries with no player at the wheel stopped being scenery, and the read API let the outside world see all of it.

## What landed in this release

146 changes shipped under 1.4.0.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Appoint campaign managers from your campaign page**
  You can appoint other players to help run your campaign, and the backend has supported up to three managers for a while, but there was no way to actually do it from the game. Your campaign page now has a Campaign Managers panel: search for a player, add them, remove them, up to three. Managers can take campaign actions alongside you.
- **Presidential nominations get a convention, working endorsements, and fairer suspensions**
  Three changes to how a presidential nominee is chosen, all switched on only for elections that start in the next cycle so nothing changes for a race in progress. A nomination with no clear delegate winner now goes to an actual multi-ballot convention instead of quietly picking a plurality. Endorsements are wired to lend organization and credibility. A campaign that drops out and endorses now passes along a share of its support based on how aligned the two candidates are, rather than a flat quarter.
- **Presidential races freeze their rules when they open**
  Each presidential election is now stamped with a ruleset version the moment it spawns and runs under those rules for its whole cycle, so code deployed mid-race can no longer change how a live presidency is decided. Races that opened before this change keep the rules they opened under. This is the foundation that lets future balance changes take effect from the next election cycle instead of landing mid-campaign.
- **Primaries spread out, and momentum builds**
  Presidential primaries now spread their voting waves across the whole primary window instead of firing every wave in the final stretch, so each result lands with turns to spare before the next. Beating expectations in a wave now builds momentum a candidate can carry forward. The new calendar applies only to races that start after this ships; races already running keep the schedule they opened under.
- **Regional parties stand across the whole country**
  Plaid Cymru, the SNP and the Northern Irish parties are no longer locked to their home nation. They can stand anywhere in the UK, starting from almost no organisation outside the places they already hold.
- **Running mates can campaign for the ticket**
  In a presidential general election, the running mate can now take the stump for the ticket. They can visit a state to lend the ticket a presence there and canvass voters for it, all inside a shared daily action pool. It spends the running mate's own actions and funds, not the campaign treasury, so a ticket gains from an active running mate and loses nothing when theirs sits idle.
- **See why a candidate is ahead or behind**
  Presidential races now carry a Factor Ledger: a plain breakdown of a candidate's projected votes into named factors, so you can see what is actually driving the race. It reads straight off the vote math the engine already ran, so it changes no result. It only explains one.
- **Vote counts can no longer exceed the number of voters**
  The 1956 presidential general certified 378.8 million ballots in a nation of 204.9 million people, because the engine's turnout pool could resolve to several times the eligible electorate. Each state's vote pool is now capped at its voting-eligible population, so recorded ballot totals stay physically possible. Vote shares, margins, and winners are unaffected: the pool basis scales every candidate equally.
- **Winning the presidency now takes a majority of the actual Electoral College**
  The engine required 270 electoral votes to win the presidency outright, which is the majority of the modern 538-elector college. Era worlds have a smaller college: 531 electors in the 1950s, where the true majority is 266. A candidate winning 266 to 269 electors held a real majority but would have been wrongly sent to a House contingent ballot. All three victory thresholds (electoral votes, House delegations, Senate votes) now derive from the college and rosters actually on the ballot.
- **Your campaign page now has a war room**
  The campaign page now opens with a manager briefing: your path to victory, how many turns your treasury lasts, where your coalition is weakest, how far your operations are built out, and what each next upgrade buys. It only reads numbers your campaign already produced, so nothing about the race changes. The briefing is yours alone: rivals viewing your campaign never see it.
- **Reunification carries the East across intact**
  Winning the German Question used to dissolve the winners. Every East German seat, every party organisation and every player's office was deleted on the way into unified Germany, and the surviving state was left with no ruling party at all. The East now arrives with its parties, its chamber and its government intact, and Berlin becomes one city again.
- **Bill votes show and cap the shift to your positions**
  The Aye and Nay buttons now tell you exactly how each vote would move your economic and social positions, the shift is based on the bill's average position and capped at one 0.25 step per bill, and changing your vote is measured from where you stood before that bill so flipping cannot stack.
- **Election results get a results-desk display, and primaries count real ballots**
  The region Elections tab and the election detail page now read like a results desk: a verdict headline, the count with shares and seats, a seat block for multi-seat chambers, and a wire strip across the region's whole ballot. Under it, down-ballot primaries accumulate real ballots each turn from each party's registered voters, general elections only count registered voters, and a race can no longer certify more ballots than it has voters.
- **Public API adds fiscal, regional, forex, and referendum data**
  Public API clients can now read the country directory, regional summaries, national budgets, currency markets, and referendum campaigns and results. Existing game and economy responses also include more calendar and macro data.
- **Leadership elections start when you press start**
  The admin button that opens a 24 turn Congress leadership election did nothing except repost the previous winner to the feed. It now opens a real race. The President Pro Tempore also becomes a majority party office, and a Pro Tempore, Majority Leader or Majority Whip who leaves the majority party is vacated on the spot with a fresh election opened for the seat.
- **Party whips reach the votes that remove people from office**
  A motion to vacate the Speaker and an impeachment were the two votes a party could not whip, which are the two votes where discipline decides everything. Both now appear on the Leadership tab of the Whip Room for the national party, a caucus, and, for a governor's trial, the state party. Blocs also take a side on a motion to vacate when its window closes, so a motion can now carry at all.
- **A party's organisation is its own again**
  In reunified Germany a party's Org page showed a different party's number, and building Org for one party moved the figure displayed for another. The regional party list and the party page look their records up by different keys, and reunification had left those two keys pointing at different parties.
- **A primary keeps the calendar it opened on**
  Safety guard so a presidential primary already in progress can never switch its voting calendar partway through, even if the race is moved onto a different ruleset. A primary that opened on the compact calendar finishes on it; only a primary that has not started yet uses the spread-out one.
- **House delegations resize after a census**
  A decennial census reapportioned the House and redrew the district maps, but the races already on the ballot kept their old seat count. States that gained or lost a seat showed a projection that could never fill, and their delegations resolved to the wrong size. Seat projections in districted states now apply the same vote threshold as the rest of the election engine.
- **Presidential ruleset v3 (rework foundation)**
  Internal groundwork for the presidential rework. Adds the full set of tuning switches for the reworked primary, nomination, and campaign systems behind the existing per-election ruleset seam, all set to their current values so nothing changes yet. Future rework pieces flip individual switches on, and only for elections that start after they ship, never mid-race.
- **Redistricting is reachable again**
  US states now start with legislature-drawn congressional maps, the historically correct default for the era and the only authority setting that lets a governing trifecta redraw. Before this every state was silently locked to a bipartisan commission that can never redraw, so nobody could use the redistricting editor at all.
- **Restore the state redistricting authority bill**
  States can legislate who draws their congressional map again. The three redistricting laws had dropped out of the bill picker, leaving every state stuck on commission-drawn maps with no way to change it. Executive orders on shorter policy ladders no longer overshoot the end of the list.
- **The public API can tell a primary from a general**
  Elections read through the public API now report which phase they are in, when that phase closes, and who is actually still standing. Before this, every live race read back as "active" whether it was in its primary or its general, and the ballot listed everyone who had ever entered.
- **International organization votes now count the roll, not the turnout**
  A bloc resolution used to pass on a single yes against no opposition, while the panel above it advertised a majority of the full membership. Both ends now agree: only members holding a ballot count, silence is a nay, and calling a bloc into a war needs every one of them.
- **System conversions finally hold the election they promise**
  A regime that collapsed into a new system of government flipped its type and scheduled a fresh election that never arrived. The marker recording it had no reader, so a one party state that fell kept the same faces in the same seats indefinitely. Conversions now dissolve the legislature and go to the country, and a dissolution imposed from outside no longer drags every future election off its calendar.
- **Public elections API can return per-candidate results in one call**
  The public elections list gave you the ballot but no vote numbers, so a bot that wanted detailed standings for a whole country had to make one /elections/[id] call per seat. Passing results=true now returns per-candidate vote standings for every race in a single response.
- **Wiki pages brought back in line with current mechanics**
  A read of every authored wiki page against the engine found seventy-odd places where the guide described a mechanic that no longer works that way: primaries decided on a score instead of ballots, a 270-vote Electoral College in a 531-elector era, three peace terms where there are four, a Federal Reserve run by a lobbying draw, and a turn-order page with no battles in it. Every one of those is corrected, and the mechanics that had shipped with no wiki coverage at all now have it.
- **Bill timelines read in your own timezone, and two page crashes are gone**
  Congress bill timelines and the campaign suspension notice were printing timestamps in UTC instead of your local time. The corporation Overview card and the executive page could also crash outright on incomplete data.
- **Fewer phantom server errors on money moves and appointments**
  A handful of actions could fail with "Internal server error" for reasons that were never real faults. Party and caucus treasury sends, fund subscriptions and redemptions, and corporate currency conversions now retry automatically when the database hands them a stale session instead of failing outright. Losing an appointment race for a cabinet seat now says so plainly, founding two companies at the same moment no longer collides on the ticker symbol, and election tallies carried over from older worlds no longer break when a presidential race re-initializes them.
- **Long-serving legislators no longer erased at the ballot box**
  Time in office charged a fixed number of points against a representative's influence and favorability, so an ordinary member who had served five terms was left with no influence at all and under half their favorability. It now takes a small share of those standings instead, and can never take all of them.
- **New players can vote in founding caucus elections**
  During the founding phase a brand-new player could vote for their party chair but was turned away from their caucus chair election, even though both races run in the same accelerated window.
- **Bloc admissions no longer stall on members who never vote**
  An application to join a bloc could not pass, however many members backed it. Admissions need every voting member to agree, and the roll had quietly grown to include members run by the game rather than by players. Those governments decide once every six turns and take one diplomatic action when they do, so a vote had to beat a tariff or an embargo for the slot and usually lost. One member never getting round to it was enough to sink the application. Casting a ballot no longer costs a government its action for the cycle, the members who cannot answer a deadline are off the ballots where silence blocks, and the panel now shows the same threshold the game applies.

### Government and legislation

- **The Chancellor's Budget now changes the law**
  The UK annual Budget now bundles real tax rates and statutory programme levels into its Commons vote. It shares one fiscal ledger with ordinary legislation, includes a forecast before tabling, and gives the Prime Minister temporary authority when the Chancellorship is vacant.
- **Acting secretaries can be appointed, and no longer last forever**
  Presidents can now install an acting secretary from the cabinet page, which until now had no control anywhere in the game. The appointment fills a vacant seat only, lasts 24 turns, and is limited to one per office per term.
- **Acting secretaries keep the lights on, not the keys**
  An acting appointment existed to stop a department going dark while the Senate got round to a confirmation vote, but it handed over the whole office. An acting secretary now runs the department day to day and leaves the decisions that outlast the appointment to whoever the Senate confirms. Each one on the books costs half a point of national approval, and that cost is now visible on the rating instead of silently applied.
- **Every country now runs an intelligence service**
  Governments can stand up an intelligence service under a new cabinet seat, build networks inside other countries, and spend those networks on collection and covert action. Networks take turns to build and are what a compromise costs you. What you learn goes stale on its own, so a service that stops collecting slowly goes blind. Getting caught is graded: a clean miss, a rolled up cell, an operation the target notices, or one the whole world can put your name to.
- **Reunification now delivers the state the winner fought for**
  Winning the German Question carried the border and little else. The victor was dissolved into the state it had just defeated, so it came out under the loser's name, currency and system of government; the chamber seated the defeated side's parties at seventy percent of a state where they were outlawed, the winning cabinet was deleted while the defeated one governed on, the victorious leader lost their mandate, and the unified treasury came out with a smaller borrowing ceiling than the half it started from. A settlement imposed by force can also now name which party takes power, instead of handing the monopoly to whoever was already strongest.
- **The government gets its own bank rate back**
  On a central bank the government controls, the bank's own autonomous chair was still setting the policy rate every few turns, and because both share one cooldown the Chancellor's window shut the moment it opened. The rate card sat permanently on cooldown and no minister could ever move the rate. The bill that would have settled who holds the rate could not be proposed at all: the provision had no place in the request schema, so every such bill was rejected before it was read. Separately, a crisis response that promised to end the crisis sooner now actually does.
- **Governance Style Counts Both Chambers**
  Democratic health now reads chamber margins, continuity in government, and whether the presidency reinforces or divides legislative control.
- **The defence seat can post generals to a war again**
  The Commands tab in the defence office never received the list of live conflicts, so its posting dropdown offered no wars to post a general to and the badge on an already posted general printed the raw theatre id instead of the war's name. Every country was affected.
- **Econ-only governments can complete policy**
  Econ-only governments now form against the correct historical legislature, staff core ministries, and move national bills through a complete lifecycle.
- **The union pages now tell you how to lift a union ban**
  When unions are banned, the union pages said the ban could be repealed by legislation but never said how, and the repeal lever lives inside an industry bill where nobody looks. The banned-state banner now spells out the exact path and links straight to Propose Legislation.
- **War entry reads a country's real approval**
  A treaty bloc's war-entry bill could pass a legislature and still not put the country in the war. The check on public support read a country with no stored approval snapshot as having zero approval rather than as not yet measured, and only playable countries and current belligerents are ever snapshotted. Every other country was refused entry whatever its public actually thought. The check now measures approval when nothing is stored.
- **Regional bills show their current law again**
  A bill filed in a state or region showed no current law, no cost comparison and no metric effects, because the region had never been given a starting position on the laws it is allowed to pass. Every region now holds one for every law it can legislate, so the comparison panel fills in.
- **Vacant FOMC seats now tell the President to act**
  When governor terms lapse the board falls vacant by design and waits for a presidential nomination, but nothing told the President that, so the Fed committee could sit dead for months with every motion failing 1-0-6. The executive now gets an in-app notice the moment seats fall vacant (plus a throttled reminder while they stay empty), the wire carries the story, and the committee page shows an understaffed-board banner explaining why motions cannot pass.
- **A confirmed secretary now vacates their old seat**
  Confirming a sitting cabinet member to a new position used to jam: the cabinet allows one seat per character, so the confirmation could never be applied and the nomination sat unresolved turn after turn. A confirmed nominee who already holds a cabinet seat now leaves it as they take the new one, the way a real reshuffle works.
- **Law country roster and its tables are one declaration**
  Two fixes landing days apart disagreed about whether a reunified Germany belongs in the law catalogue's country roster, and the build stopped compiling. Germany is priced as a cost anchor and keeps no law book of its own, so the empty rows added for it are gone. The roster and the tables that must cover it are now a single declaration, so the two cannot drift apart again.

### War and diplomacy

- **Autonomous foreign policy actions**
  Econ-only governments can turn alignment, bilateral opinion, trade exposure, and war conditions into real diplomatic and trade actions behind a staged rollout control.
- **Autonomous war operations**
  Autonomous governments that ratify alliance war entry now deploy real forces, conduct ordinary offensives, and seek peace when military or political pressure becomes unsustainable.
- **Wars now move national approval**
  A nation at war carried no political cost or credit for it. Approval now answers to the war itself: how the front is moving against what the public expects, whether an ally dragged in by a treaty actually sent anyone, and how long the country has been asked to carry the fighting. All three appear only while a war is live, arrive and fade gradually rather than in one jump, and are shown as a single entry beside your approval rating so the number always has a reason attached.
- **An even front for the War for Germany**
  A war the German Question attaches to now opens on an even front instead of handing one side the whole theatre, the front map draws every country the war is fought over, and reunification leaves Germany in the Warsaw Pact alone rather than in both alliances at once.
- **Every page of a country at war says so**
  A country fighting a war now carries a red strip under the navigation on all of its pages, naming the war and linking to it, so you cannot read a nation's politics or its economy without being told there is a war on.
- **German Question settled by a declared war**
  A war declared by or against either Germany now freezes the German Question and decides it outright, instead of the board carrying on as though nothing had happened.
- **A coalition can be talked apart, not only beaten**
  Every peace offer used to mean the same thing: let me out. There was no way to ask an enemy to leave, so an alliance could only be broken by force, and a country dragged into a war by treaty had no exit at all until the ally it came for settled. An offer now says who leaves, either party. Buying a withdrawal that would end the war outright needs the front already well in your favour, unless it is a white peace.
- **Active wars now hold up world tension**
  The world tension index now counts active wars, including the Vietnam ladder and direct fighting between nuclear-armed powers. War declarations spike the index immediately, and continuing conflicts enforce a standing floor.
- **Allies can join an offensive without declaring their own**
  Defence already worked this way. An ally with troops at a front defends it automatically, because sending them there was the decision. Attacking demanded a fresh order from every ally on the same turn, so a joint push took two nations acting in the same hour. A Theater Commander can now set a standing order instead.
- **Combat rebalance, frontage and the readiness ledger**
  Three defects found by a study of the German front. Casualties tracked your own force size rather than the enemy's, so the winning coalition was losing eleven men for every one it killed. Readiness punished armour and safe roles, then sat pinned at its floor for whole wars. Breaking off was supposed to be exceptional and happened in nine battles out of ten.
- **Peace can now take more than money**
  A settlement used to move an indemnity and nothing else, and winning a war outright handed the victor a truce and no say at all. A peace deal now carries one term of three: a reparation, a forced change of government, or a bar on re-arming. A war won to the end of the track no longer ends on the spot either. It waits while the victor names what they are taking, and every settlement is reported to World News.
- **Foreign policy rollout and ledger**
  Admins can activate autonomous foreign policy one capability family at a time and inspect its operational results from a bounded decision ledger.
- **Readiness, frontage and the supply tail**
  A study of the German front found that a formation was written back at the size of its own exhaustion rather than what was left of it, that a front charged an army for how good and how rested it was rather than how much room it took, and that supply was decided by a label. Together these meant the Soviet Army fought worse alone than East Germany did.
- **War approval is visible, itemised, and no longer forgets the last war**
  A nation forty two turns into the War for Germany had no war anywhere in its approval breakdown, and neither did either country it was fighting. The war block now shows one line per term, so a war going well no longer reads as a penalty, and war exhaustion carries across a peace instead of resetting the moment a treaty is signed.
- **Combat odds are a real chance of winning**
  The percentage beside an offensive was a measure of relative strength wearing a probability's label. A projected 16 percent won about one battle in ten thousand, and a projected 84 percent won essentially every time, so the stronger side always took the ground and players reported the odds as broken or the enemy as impossibly lucky. Both readings were right about the number. It is now the chance it says it is.
- **Front supply: air arm, depth, Logistics commands and home soil**
  A large coalition front read CUT OFF no matter what its commander did, because every wing and missile brigade parked at the theater was billed like a division in the line and a Logistics command was worth a flat 20 against a deficit of over a thousand. Air, naval and rocket formations now draw a quarter of their upkeep from the land front, formations held in depth draw 60 percent, a Logistics command delivers a share of the force it actually covers, and the side fighting on its own soil hauls 25 percent more.
- **Historical conflicts on the conflicts hub**
  A war dropped off the conflicts hub the moment it ended, and nothing else linked to the record it left behind. Ended wars are now listed under Historical Conflicts with their outcome, and a resolved war keeps its fog of war for 480 turns before the full record opens to everyone.
- **Admin switches for NPP offensives**
  Two admin switches decide whether countries run by the NPP engine fight offensively: one lets them declare offensives of their own, the other lets them follow an ally into one at a front where they already have troops. Both start off, and defence is unaffected either way.
- **Damaged ships and aircraft can be repaired**
  Nothing in the game had ever restored a hull. Combat only ever took condition away, so a formation shot to pieces once was combat ineffective for the rest of the game, contributed nothing to a blockade, and could never come back. Fleets now mend, quickly in a home port and slowly on station, and a formation too damaged to fight pulls back to home water on its own.
- **Full national conversion when the German Question carries**
  A challenger victory now converts everything: the survivor assumes the absorbed state's treasury, debt and sovereign bonds, its national law book, its armed forces, its command economy and its organisation seats, all currency-converted, and dissolved countries stop being processed anywhere.
- **Intelligence can now read a nuclear programme**
  Strategic coverage now buys a real reading of another country's nuclear posture, graded by how much of it you have. A little tells you only whether a programme exists. More gives you a warhead estimate that carries real error and will not sharpen no matter how often you look. Only sustained coverage confirms a figure or shows how far an undeclared programme has actually got. Covert action can now set one of those undeclared programmes back a stage, and when the power it was being hidden from is the one that finds it, the raid is public.
- **Intelligence can now read an army**
  Military coverage now reads out as an assessment of another country's forces, graded the same way the nuclear one is. A little tells you whether they are fighting and in how many places. More gives you an estimate of their strength and readiness that carries real error. Only sustained coverage gives you the real figures and the supply position at each front, which is the part worth having before you plan anything.
- **Intelligence can now read an economy, and leak a company**
  Economic coverage now reads out as a picture of another country's corporate sector, graded the same way the nuclear and military ones are. And a covert operation can now leak a company's books: for a while afterwards, every trader reads its real financials instead of the quarterly estimate everyone outside the boardroom normally sees.
- **One state, one National Corporation**
  A reunified Germany came out of the merge with two primary National Corporations, and the one every menu quietly picked belonged to the country that had just stopped existing. Merging a split-off folded it into that dead shell, the split list came up empty, and the state's whole coupon bill sat on the enterprise with no income while the enterprise holding all the industry earned for nobody. The surviving state's corporations also kept the absorbed country's currency on their books.
- **Settling the German Question at the negotiating table**
  A war fought over Germany could only be settled by fighting it to the end. Two founding belligerents can now end their war between themselves, and East Germany can put reunification itself on the table while the fighting is still going on. The record also stopped naming the wrong country as the one that walked away from a separate peace.
- **Auto-crisis spawner no longer trips over its own index**
  Every turn, the crisis spawner tried to record new crises and failed a database uniqueness check that was meant to stop the same historical event from spawning twice. Crises without a linked historical event were being stamped with an empty marker instead of no marker, and the second empty marker collided. The marker is now written only when a real event id exists, and the uniqueness rule ignores empty values.
- **Crisis responses say why they were refused**
  Answering an international crisis could fail with nothing but "Internal server error", whatever the actual reason was. Every refusal on that screen now comes back with the real reason and the right status, and the Actions card closes the options your nation cannot support before you click them, listing what it would take, exactly as the crisis page already did.
- **A front fought over twice in one turn now counts both battles**
  When both sides declared an offensive at the same conflict on the same turn, the second engagement was resolved against the front and the armies as they stood before the first one was fought. Its writes then replaced the first battle's rather than building on them, so the ground moved once and the dead from the opening engagement came back.
- **A general who moves abroad leaves their old army behind**
  Relocating to another country now takes a commissioned general out of the old country's chain of command, instead of leaving them holding its commands, its postings and its divisions.
- **Commanding generals can see and edit their commands again**
  A commanding general can now read their own command's structure and units without the defence office, the conflict record says who is posted at a front, and a commander who has left the country no longer freezes a command.
- **Coalition battle reports name every belligerent's own casualties**
  A battle fought by allies recorded one country per side, so an offensive's whole butcher's bill was filed under whichever nation happened to declare it. A joint East German and Russian attack on the United States reported 16,299 East German dead when East Germany had lost 5,360 and Russia 10,939. Every nation that fights now appears under its own flag, with its own losses.
- **Naval formations no longer fight land battles at full strength**
  A carrier strike group, two attack submarines and four other ships were defending a German forest at full effectiveness, in a battle no hull could reach. Fronts now know whether they touch the sea, and each ship contributes according to what it can actually do to a land engagement.
- **Autonomous governments plan foreign policy**
  Econ-only governments now evaluate alliances, rivalries, trade ties, wars, and organization business before recording one explainable foreign-policy intent.
- **Foreign policy balance harness**
  Seeded balance runs can compare disabled, shadow, and staged active autonomous foreign policy under the same world preset and autonomy level.
- **The conflict record says each thing once**
  A conflict page stated its casualty count four times, its engagement count four times and its belligerent roll three times, and a war fought somewhere the map cannot draw spent a full screen on an empty box saying so. The page now states each fact once, in the place that owns it, and the panel that takes orders sits at the top of the rail instead of behind three reference panels.
- **War economy pressure reaches Vietnam and consumer sectors**
  A hot superpower war now pushes Vietnam up its available escalation ladder, while civil defense emergencies create a visible shift from consumer trade into construction and war production.
- **Allies who ratified a bloc war now take their turn on offense**
  Countries that voted to enter an alliance war alongside a player sat on the roster but never joined an offensive, because their decision to conduct the war always ranked below routine votes and tariffs. A committed belligerent now ranks war conduct above routine diplomacy, so allied armies show up among the attackers.
- **Bloc influence plays refuse an amount too small to move anything**
  Committing a bloc influence play with a tiny amount debited the fund, queued a play that landed zero points, and refunded it a turn later, so the button looked like it did nothing. The panel now refuses a spend below one grid step up front and tells you the minimum it takes to move the nation.
- **Commands of different types can share a region**
  Pairing a Logistics command with the Regional command already holding a region is the recommended way to sustain a fight overseas, but the builder warned you off it and then displayed the region as UNASSIGNED once you did it anyway. Only two commands of the same type were ever meant to count as a conflict. The warning and the coverage readout now agree with that, and the wiki no longer says a region takes exactly one command.
- **Remove the deep-push control drag**
  An advance no longer slows to half pace once the winning side holds three quarters of the contested country. Supply already punishes a deep push, and the two together made the closing stretch of a war crawl.
- **Show command posture trade-offs and type bonuses in the builder**
  The twelve command postures now show their stated trade-offs and the three command types their bonuses: live in the Create Command dialog, on the command detail panel, and on the commanding general's own command page.
- **Command logistics warning follows theatre spread**
  Building a Command that covered South Asia, East Asia and Southeast Asia raised a logistics warning, even though all three sit in the Indo-Pacific and any two of them raised nothing. The warning counted regions rather than reading the map. It now fires only when a Command's regions span more than one macro theatre.
- **Conflict record names the country that actually left**
  A separate peace where one country asks its enemy to withdraw was written up backwards on the public war record, naming the country still fighting as the one that walked away. The record now reads the settlement off the party the deal actually removes.
- **Influence commit field accepts the M/B shorthand it prints**
  The bloc influence commit form quoted its prices and minimum spend as "4.0M" but only accepted plain units, and the number box refused the letter M outright, so a player typing what they just read bought nothing and the button stayed dead. The field now takes the same K/M/B/T shorthand it prints, along with comma-grouped amounts, through the shared parser every other money input uses.
- **A reunified country is called by its own name**
  Germany reunified and half the site went on calling it East Germany. The country pages knew the new name, but the navbar, the nation switcher, the Select a Nation cards, the maps and the banking hub all read the name the country was compiled with, so which name a player saw depended on which page they happened to be looking at.
- **Active crises stay visible to the players they affect**
  A crisis carrying a decision only the head of government can take was hidden from everyone else on the Actions page, and hidden from the leader too the moment they answered it, even though it went on draining growth, employment, confidence and approval for the rest of its run. Recessions were the clearest case: on the live world only four of thirty two running crises were visible to an ordinary player. They are all visible now, as effects only cards.
- **Dissolved countries stop applying to blocs, and renamed countries render their live name**
  A country absorbed into another kept a foreign policy. West Germany applied to NATO two turns after reunification dissolved it, because the guard that skips players read blank for a state that no longer existed and let it through. A dissolved country is now dropped from alignment entirely: it does not drift toward a bloc, ask to join one, or get walked out of one. Separately, bloc rosters now name countries the way the rest of the game does, so a state renamed by a reunification reads under its current name and the Soviet Union is no longer listed as Russia.

### Economy and corporations

- **Advertising purchases reach their sellers**
  Corporate advertising purchases now reach the media and entertainment companies that fill them. Buyers pay only for advertising delivered, while unfilled budget remains in their treasury and unfunded settings cannot place advertising demand.
- **Domestic freight follows regional routes**
  Domestic shipments in 22 countries had no regional route data, so freight treated every haul as a six-hop ocean import. Those countries now have real neighboring-region routes, including appropriate mainland ferry links for islands, so domestic shipping prices reflect the distance traveled.
- **Extraction deposits actually cap extraction on era worlds**
  The per-state deposit ceiling is supposed to ration how much a mining or drilling sector can pull out of the ground each turn. On era worlds the rationing check measured desired output in the wrong units, so the ceiling effectively never applied, and a global shortage could lift the cap entirely. Extraction is now rationed in the same units the world supply ledger books, and shortage relief can never push a sector past what its deposits can physically yield.
- **Local freight demand now reaches local logistics sectors**
  Freight demand counted only the loads that moved. Willing haul requests a state's own freight network was too full to carry were left out, so the freight market never priced the capacity those states were short of. Refused requests now count as freight demand in the state that could not haul them.
- **NPC miners open idle shortage deposits**
  Extraction automation could redirect a mine that already existed, but it could not open a mine in a state where a seeded deposit sat completely idle. NPC corporations now open focused extraction sectors on idle deposits during genuine global shortages, while player corporations remain untouched.
- **Shortage premiums now attract new suppliers**
  AI-run corporations stopped diversifying after five sectors, even when essential commodities were trading at severe shortage premiums. Profitable corporations can now enter genuinely short markets beyond that ordinary cap, using normal corporate credit when retained cash is insufficient.
- **The Donbass has coal again, and China mines its iron**
  When the eastern-bloc countries became playable, their states were created with zero mineral deposits: the Donbass, Upper Silesia, and Ploiesti were all mining against empty ground. Every bloc region now has its historical 1953 deposits. Separately, China's state mining enterprise was running coal operations in all seven regions while sitting on some of the world's largest iron deposits during a world iron shortage; its four iron-rich regions now retool to iron mining.
- **Yugoslavia trades across the curtain**
  Yugoslavia broke with the Soviet bloc in 1948, five years before the era begins, and reoriented its trade toward the West while keeping a planned economy at home. The iron-curtain trade wall was grouping it with the countries that were embargoing it. Yugoslavia now trades with the open world; its domestic planned economy is unchanged.
- **Advertising sellers are paid once**
  Advertising sellers were booking a filled campaign as ordinary revenue and then receiving the marketing settlement for the same campaign on top, so the sale paid twice. The settlement now nets against that revenue, so the seller is paid once and the marketing budget stays a real cost that neither mints nor destroys money.
- **Freight settlement can be phased in gradually**
  Activating geographic freight settlement no longer has to be a single hard step. A configurable ramp fades the effect in over a window of turns, so the delivery cap on sales and the shipping bill arrive together and gradually, with the phase-in visible each turn instead of landing all at once.
- **The market ring shows your share again**
  A sector with seventeen NPP rivals drew seventeen near-identical slices, and your own share was one wedge in a rainbow. The autonomous corps now fold into a single muted arc behind the player-owned companies, and the list below puts players first with the NPP field collapsed into one row you can open.
- **The Federal Reserve is no longer run by the machine**
  When a Fed governor's or chair's term ran out, the game quietly installed an appointed technocrat and handed it the vote — so the committee ran itself and the same AI chair kept re-seating itself. Those seats now fall vacant and wait for the President to nominate a successor for the Senate to confirm.
- **The Senate can now vote on Federal Reserve nominations**
  A President's Fed chair and governor nominations went to the Senate for confirmation, but Senators had no way to actually vote on them. The committee page now lists pending nominations with For / Against / Abstain controls for Senators, so confirmation is decided by real votes.
- **Public API history and OpenAPI**
  Public integrations can chart economic, fiscal, debt, and trade history and generate clients from a complete OpenAPI 3.1 route contract.
- **The public API can read the world layer**
  Public integrations can now follow national metrics, international organizations, trade restrictions, and sovereign risk through six new v1 routes.
- **State-scoped freight supply agreements**
  Freight can be contracted again. A freight supply agreement names the state it is fulfilled from, and is reserved, delivered and settled against the supplier's and buyer's plants in that one state.
- **Era currencies use their authored rates in the world ledger**
  Six live currencies without a tradable foreign-exchange row were treated as equal to the internal anchor when the world ledger valued sector revenue. The ledger now uses each currency's authored era rate, keeping production and demand in Poland, Romania, Yugoslavia, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, and Hungary on the same basis as the corporation turn.
- **Market measurements now state what they count**
  Market demand, sourcing demand, and clearing estimates measure different stages of the commodity market. Their stored records now identify whether a figure is a calibrated world aggregate, state buyer intent, pooled world availability, or country-scoped clearing, so dashboards and audits can name the basis instead of presenting unlike totals as interchangeable.
- **Bond debt ceiling and insolvency test now measure the same assets**
  A corporation could be quoted a debt ceiling up to 75 times larger than the assets the insolvency test would credit it with, then be declared insolvent for borrowing a fraction of it. Both ends now read the same balance sheet, and the bonds a corporation holds finally count as the assets they are.
- **GDP growth no longer annualizes one turn of revenue noise by 48**
  The plants-mode cyclical signal multiplied a single turn's realized-revenue delta by 48, so ordinary settlement churn became annualized prints of several hundred percent, saturated the signal clamp, parked the output gap open, and produced the recurring sharp negative GDP prints the advisors diagnosed in the US. The signal now measures growth of a smoothed revenue level over a trailing year.
- **FOMC rate votes no longer pass before the committee sees them**
  A rate meeting could open and resolve inside the same turn, so a decision carried on the automatic ballots before the chair or any member had a chance to vote. Meetings now stay open for their voting window and resolve at the deadline, so the committee actually votes on the motion.
- **Market attack cost shown now matches what the attack charges**
  A rival's card on the state market board advertised one attack price, then the attack was rejected for insufficient capital at a much higher one. The card now quotes the same cost and capture the attack actually enforces.
- **Only the US Federal Reserve runs a rate committee again**
  Every independent central bank had been given a US-style rate committee of appointed technocrats, which then blocked the government and legislative rate-setting those countries actually use — you could not change the Bank of England's rate or legislate control of it. The committee is now the US Federal Reserve's alone; other central banks keep their own governor model.
- **A skipped accounting check no longer reads as a clean one**
  The stock-versus-flow reconciliation is skipped on any turn that starts without an opening balance snapshot. It used to report that as zero divergences and a green status, which is the same reading a turn gets when the check runs and finds nothing wrong. It now reports the count as unknown and the status as unverified, and the economic vital signs record which turns in the window produced no snapshot at all.
- **Bond issuance says what maturity costs**
  Issuing a bond quoted the coupon and nothing else, so the face value coming back out of cash in a single payment arrived as a surprise, sometimes as a default. A CEO now sees a standing notice of what is owed and when on every tab of their corporation page, and the issue card prices the principal alongside the interest.
- **Index funds are paid out when a holding goes bankrupt**
  When a company an index fund held was wound down through bond default, the fund was counted among its shareholders but received none of the liquidation payout, and the dead company stayed on the fund's books at its last traded price. Funds now collect their share of the wind-down in cash and drop the holding, the same way voluntary dissolution and nationalization already work.
- **A dead Fed board hands the rate back to the chair**
  When governor terms lapse the FOMC board falls below working strength and no rate motion can ever carry, so the committee deadlocks every decision. Meetings now pause on such a board and the chair sets the rate directly using the ordinary central-bank controls, exactly as a single-governor bank does, until confirmations restore a board that can vote again.
- **A sector split now tells you what happened**
  A successful split could leave no confirmation on screen at all, because the panel holding the result message disappeared at the moment the split worked.
- **AI mining companies open mines on rich deposits**
  Company-run mines are founded where the ore actually is and the commodity is short, instead of only where a nearby buyer already exists, so rich deposits no longer sit idle forever in an export-oriented state.
- **Company growth rate reads real revenue again**
  Every company was reporting a growth rate of 0.00%. The page was still averaging the old growth-target field, which the plant-based economy stopped using. It now shows the growth in the revenue the company actually booked.
- **Fed chairs can move the rate again after an autonomous chair**
  A bank run by an autonomous chair drifted onto a rate that was not a whole quarter point, which left the next human chair unable to submit any rate change at all.
- **Government fines convert into the collecting country's currency**
  A fine paid by a company based in another country was banked by the government as if the figure were already in its own money, so the state collected the wrong amount whenever the two currencies differed.
- **Index funds and AI parties are repaid when a bond default is cured**
  Curing a defaulted corporate bond with cash repaid people and companies but skipped index funds and AI-run parties, even though the issuer was charged for their units and the bond was closed out. Their money simply disappeared.
- **Operating budgets are now capped when they are charged, not just when they are set**
  The 150% overhead limit was only checked at the moment you saved a budget, so a corporation whose revenue later collapsed kept paying its old logistics and R&D bills in full against revenue it no longer earned. Both legs are now capped every turn on the same basis, and neither one builds strength for spending the corporation could not afford.
- **Sector splits no longer fail with an internal server error**
  Confirming a sector split returned an internal server error because the route fired four database reads at once down a single transaction, and they raced each other to open it.
- **Sector splits work again on standalone Mongo**
  Every attack-sector attempt returned an internal server error because the split had been moved inside a database transaction that production Mongo cannot start.
- **Supply-shortfall damages now tell the CEO who paid them**
  A corporation that under-produces against a signed supply agreement pays damages every turn. Those damages were charged silently, so a CEO saw healthy income and flat cash with nothing on screen to explain it. The paying owner now gets a notice each turn damages are assessed.
- **Plants P&L residual credit cap**
  A sector's calibrated cost residual can no longer be a credit larger than the bills it was solved against, so plants profit is bounded by revenue for every sector by construction.

### Interface and platform

- **Current iteration displays as 1.0**
  The live world, stored internally as Iteration 4, now shows to players as "1.0" wherever an iteration name is displayed: office and seat history, the iteration-history groupings, the legacy leaderboard, and season-recap titles. Past iterations (Alpha 1, Beta 1, Beta 2) are unchanged.
- **Admins can put a survey link across the top of the site**
  There was no way to point the whole playerbase at a feedback form without asking every player to go looking for it. An admin can now write a short message, attach a link, and switch it on. It sits under the navigation on every page, for everyone, until they switch it off again.
- **Game Health now warns before a turn phase runs out of time**
  Every phase of a turn has a hard four minute ceiling, and going past it fails the phase and abandons the whole turn. Nothing surfaced a phase creeping toward that ceiling, because a slow phase that still succeeds raises no warning anywhere. Game Health now reports one when a phase crosses half its budget.
- **Shard CI tests and switch vitest to the threads pool**
  The test suite now runs across four parallel CI jobs on worker threads instead of one job on forked processes, cutting the wait on every pull request without changing or removing a single test.

### Other changes

- **A frightened world now acts like one**
  Panic buying, bank runs, civil defense fever, and peace marches can now confront national executives while world tension remains high.
- **A tied Supreme Court no longer hands down a ruling**
  A docket case decided by an evenly split Court (for example 4-4) was being scored as a divergence from history and enacted as a ruling, changing policy and shifting the game's timeline off a decision the Court never actually reached. A tied Court now produces no decision: the case affirms the existing outcome and history is unchanged, matching how a real tied bench works.
- **Turns finish instead of being killed partway**
  The server capped its own working memory at 1 GB, and turn processing had grown past what that leaves. The memory watchdog was ending the process partway through a turn, which abandoned the turn and blocked the retry for twenty minutes. The cap is now 3 GB against a container that has five.
- **Turns no longer get stuck on "Processing...**
  Turns that appeared to take ten minutes or more were not slow, they were dead. A turn that lost its process partway through left the processing lock held with a frozen heartbeat, and nothing could pick it up again until the next half-hourly turn tick. The engine now hands that lock back when it knows it is going down, and sweeps for an abandoned one every five minutes.
- **World News stops posting the same headline twice**
  A slow turn could be taken over as if it had crashed while it was in fact still running, so some turn events ran twice and posted duplicate World News items — the same Supreme Court ruling or cabinet announcement back to back. News posts are now de-duplicated, so a double-run reports each event once.
- **Era content fires on the calendar year, and only the live deployment posts to Discord**
  Scheduled era content was reading the raw turn counter, so on a world with a founding phase it ran a full game year ahead of the date on the status bar. Discord webhook sends are now bound to the deployment that configured them.
- **Influence costs quoted in the fund's currency**
  Bloc influence costs on the international organisations page no longer fall back to a dollar sign for funds denominated in another currency, so a Warsaw Pact play now reads in Soviet rubles end to end.
- **Registration drift sources from over-registered parties**
  Voter registration was frozen: every state's Independent and Unregistered pool had run dry, and drift only ever drew from that pool, so a party that built organization never gained registered voters. Drift now takes the shortfall from parties holding registration above their own organization, so registration answers organized opposition again.
- **Enforce a portable rules core for game systems**
  System rules now belong in a `rules.ts` or `rules/` module that runs without a database, a clock, or a network, and the architecture audit blocks anything in that zone that reaches for one.
- **Preserve player corporation controllers in clone simulations**
  Clone simulations now honor the player rail flag by leaving existing human and player corporation controllers unchanged when requested, while the default clone path remains fully NPP-driven.
