Cut from development.

## Added

- A settlement carries one term, and you choose which. A white peace ends the war
  where it began: no victor is recorded, nothing changes hands, and everybody goes
  home. It is not the same as an indemnity of zero, which is a settlement somebody
  imposed and chose to take nothing from, and which still names a winner. An
  indemnity is still there and behaves as it always did. Regime change makes
  the other country adopt a different system of government, dissolves its
  legislature and sends it to the polls. Demilitarisation bars it from awarding
  new defence contracts for a set number of turns, while everything it has
  already ordered keeps arriving. Exactly one term travels with a deal: you are
  saying what the war was for.

- Winning a war outright now means something. Running your front to the end of
  the track stops the fighting and opens a window in which you name your terms,
  rather than ending the war on the spot with nothing to show for it but a
  truce. Nobody has to agree: a country with no ground left is in no position to
  refuse. Let the window lapse and the war ends with nothing taken.

- Only one country dictates. The nation that began the war on the winning side
  names the terms, and they fall on the nation that began it on the losing side.
  Allies who joined a war somebody else started get the truce and nothing more, so
  a coalition of five does not collect five settlements.

- A strip under the navigation tells the people who can act that something is
  waiting: terms offered to you, a war you have won and may still name terms for,
  or simply that talks are open in a war you are fighting. It sits under the
  wartime strip rather than replacing it, and only the head of government and the
  foreign minister see it.

- A war fought over a question that ends in a white peace hands the question back.
  The German Question is the case that exists today: win that war and you take the
  settlement outright, but draw it and the crisis comes off ice and resumes on the
  diplomatic track exactly where it stopped, with the same index, institutions and
  ladder position it had when the shooting started. Until now such a war could only
  be won or lost, and a draw would have left the question frozen against a finished
  war for good.

- Every war that settles is reported to World News, with the term that was taken.
  A white peace is reported too, because a war ending with nobody paying anything
  is still news.

## Changed

- A war being fought over is now distinct from a war already won. One that has
  run to the end of the track takes no more offensives, accepts no more peace
  offers, admits no new belligerents, and reads as winding down on the conflicts
  board. It still counts as a live war everywhere else: the wartime strip stays
  up, and no second war can be declared between the same two countries until it
  is settled.

- An indemnity is capped at twice the paying country's annual GDP. A reparation
  can be ruinous; it cannot be arbitrary.
