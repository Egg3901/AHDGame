## Changed

- The ground a victory takes no longer halves once the winner holds three
  quarters of the contested country. That drag sat on top of the supply
  penalties that already grow with distance from the starting line, so the
  last quarter of a war moved at a fraction of the pace of the first three
  even when the defender was compressed and short of everything. A decisive
  win now takes the same step at 80 percent control as it does at 20.

- The three-quarter mark itself still matters. A war still reads as winding
  down once the front has travelled that far from where it started, and a
  peace offer can still demand ground only past that point. Only the advance
  penalty is gone.

- The wiki pages on occupation and peace now describe the step as it is: a
  campaign from one pole to the other needs on the order of twenty decisive
  wins that go unanswered, and the last stretch is hard because of supply,
  not because the step shrinks.

Balance note: this changes a military outcome constant. The simulation report
is `scripts/sim/reports/control-drift-deep-push.md`, produced by
`scripts/sim/controlDrift2026-08-30.ts` against the live front. With the
halving in place, 1 campaign in 120 reached the pole within 400 offensives;
without it, 92 did. The halving keyed on the winner's share, so past the
three-quarter mark the attacker's wins were halved and the defender's were
not, which on a near-even front is a wall rather than a slowdown.
