## Changed

- Energy and natural gas are a third freight class, `grid`. They were typed as
  never shipped, so a state consumed only what it generated, while
  `COMMODITIES_NATIONAL_REGIONAL_PRICE_BLEND` pooled their price across the
  country: the price said national market and the physics said the state you
  built in. Measured on prod at t225, energy ran 128 states in local surplus
  against 100 short, state prices ran 1.17 to 4.34, and 27.8% of all energy
  produced went unsold.
- Grid legs cross state lines, spend no freight TEU, and book no freight
  demand, so power and gas never compete with steel or food for the haulage
  fleet. Distance is paid as 3% transmission loss per hop (`GRID_LOSS_PER_HOP`)
  plus a wheeling charge of 2% of the seller's ask per hop
  (`GRID_WHEELING_PER_HOP_FRACTION`). Six hops lose about 17% of the units
  dispatched, so the seller's drawdown and the buyer's fill are now two
  different numbers. Pricing the leg off the seller's ask rather than off the
  `freight` market keeps a trucking price spike out of the cost of electricity.
- Both commodities price on the same three-leg state, national, and world blend
  as everything else that physically moves. The national-regional blend was a
  stopgap for having no freight class: with no transport to arbitrage it, a
  pure local regional leg priced each state off whatever happened to be built
  there (t202: natural gas state prices 0.24 to 0.84 in one turn against a 0.95
  global ratio).
- The per-state class capacity gate is no longer a wall. Hauls run up to 1.5x
  nominal class capacity (`FREIGHT_CONGESTION_OVERFLOW`), units above nominal
  pay a 35% surcharge on the shipping leg (`FREIGHT_CONGESTION_SURCHARGE`), and
  the buyer's tolerance ceiling, the local price plus 35%, is what finally
  stops the flow. An overflow the buyer cannot afford is now counted as
  tolerance-bound rather than capacity-bound. The old wall is a large part of
  why 60.4% of world production sat in a state that did not need it while 28.7%
  of world demand went unmet.
- Sourcing summaries carry three new counters: `congestionUnits`,
  `congestionSurchargePaid`, and `gridLossUnits`.
- A sector's clearing offer is capped by what the freight network could
  actually place, and the commodity price document carries
  `statePlacedSupply`: the units of a state's own production that found a
  buyer, local fill plus everything the network carried out. A seller is no
  longer credited with output no network could deliver.
- Sectors report `deliveryLimitedFraction`, the share of offered output that
  was produced and could not be delivered. Selling percentage stops folding two
  opposite instructions into one number. Low sales with a low delivery-limited
  share means the market did not want the goods. Low sales with a high one
  means the goods had no route, and the answer is freight capacity out of that
  state or production sited closer to the demand.

## Not changed

- Shipping is still not billed to the buyer as a separate payment. Haul books
  demand on the `freight` commodity, and that remains how logistics
  corporations earn from it. Grid legs book none, by design.
- Imports still use the flat six-hop sea equivalent and consume no domestic
  capacity. A grid import pays six hops of wheeling and six hops of loss.
- The adaptive bulk and special capacity split is unchanged, and grid holds no
  share of the haulage fleet.
