Cut from development.

## Fixed

- Passing a Budget now enacts its tax and programme provisions through the
  ordinary UK legislation lifecycle. A later Act or later Budget controls the
  setting, so neither route silently overrides the other through a private
  ledger.
- The retired percentage-allocation channel no longer drives the NHS. NHS
  funding reads the enacted national spending ledger, including changes made
  by ordinary Acts.
- The era revenue cap now compresses tax-like receipts only. NHS receipts,
  public-enterprise income and other non-tax revenue are added after the cap.
- A failed bill insertion reopens the draft instead of leaving a tabled Budget
  with no Commons bill.

## Added

- The Chancellor can set legal tax targets and programme levels, inspect the
  projected revenue, spending, balance, category changes and tax phase-in, then
  table the package as one confidence vote.
- If no Chancellor is appointed, the Prime Minister may act until the vacancy
  is filled.
- The Treasury page now explains raw and smoothed debt-to-GDP, the primary
  balance, debt interest, law-based fiscal controls and the tax phase-in rule.
