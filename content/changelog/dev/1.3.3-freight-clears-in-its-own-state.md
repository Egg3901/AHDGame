Cut from development.

## Changed

- Freight clears against the seller's own state instead of a national book.
  A state's freight supply sets the nominal capacity and congestion threshold
  for haul leaving that state. Selling the same capacity into a country-wide
  book let a logistics sector claim demand its fleet could not serve.
- Freight legs realize their own state's blended price, so a corporation that
  relieves a local shortage receives the same regional signal the market map
  shows.
- Scoping is per output commodity, not per sector. A logistics sector sells
  freight and consulting services from the same plant, and only the freight is
  state-locked, so its consulting keeps clearing nationally.
- Sector pages and expansion suggestions use the same scope. A state with a
  freight shortage can show room even when its country has excess capacity,
  while consulting continues to use the reachable national market.
- New freight supply agreements are unavailable because corporation-wide
  agreements do not carry the state identity local freight settlement needs.
  Unaccepted legacy proposals are withdrawn, while live agreements receive the
  normal four-turn cancellation notice and keep national freight clearing until
  that notice ends. This preserves their existing terms without postponing the
  state-market correction forever.

## Guarded

- Tests cover a short state and a glutted state that average out nationally, a
  plant whose two outputs clear in two different books, missing state data, and
  the case where a state id and a country id collide.
- Every reachable commodity is compared with and without state-market context
  to keep its clearing result unchanged.
- Player and NPP contract paths both reject new corporation-wide freight
  agreements, and migration coverage verifies the legacy notice period.
