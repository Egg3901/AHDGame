Cut from development.

## Changed

- Freight clears against the seller's own state instead of a national book.
  One number was doing two incompatible jobs: a state's freight supply is read
  as the hard TEU ceiling on everything leaving that state, and was at the same
  time sold into a country-wide market and filled cheapest-first. Ohio's trucks
  cannot haul a crate out of New Jersey, but an Ohio seller could take the
  demand New Jersey's hauling created.
- The consequence was that no local signal reached the player who could act on
  it. On production at turn 361, New Jersey held thirty times the freight its
  own state consumed while Arizona was short, and both faced the same national
  average, so nobody built where it was needed and everybody was paid the same
  glutted rate.
- Freight legs also realize their own state's price now. The model already
  priced freight locally, from 63.72 in New Jersey to 91.70 in Arizona on the
  same turn. Clearing volume locally while realizing price nationally would
  have left the seller who relieved a shortage paid at the glutted rate.
- Scoping is per output commodity, not per sector. A logistics sector sells
  freight and consulting services from the same plant, and only the freight is
  state-locked, so its consulting keeps clearing nationally.

## Guarded

- Tests cover a short state and a glutted state that average out nationally, a
  plant whose two outputs clear in two different books, the fallback to the
  country book when a state has no entry, and the case where a state id and a
  country id collide (DE is both Delaware and Germany).
- One test asserts that clearing is unchanged for every commodity that is not
  state-scoped, so nothing outside freight moves.
