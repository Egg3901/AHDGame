## Balance and gameplay changes

1.10 brings together the changes released since 1.9. It changes how parties
expand, how votes become seats, how failing institutions affect the economy,
and how company earnings affect valuations. The 1991 changes cover population,
currencies, institutions and the government decisions that shape Europe.

## Election and government balance

- **UK Commons eligibility falls from 20% to 10%.** The party-pooled seat
  threshold is lower in ordinary and snap elections. Structural support now
  follows a party's current regional registration, removing the advantage
  inherited from its starting-era share.
- **US House eligibility follows delegation size.** The threshold remains 20%
  in states with one to four seats, gradually falls for larger delegations,
  and reaches 10% at nine seats. Projections and final results use the same rule.
- **Presidential campaigning has a smaller ceiling.** Under the active v3
  rules, campaign strength approaches a maximum 25% vote boost instead of
  100%. State lean is no longer applied a second time after votes are allocated.
  Incumbent approval blends national and state approval equally. During the
  closing period, 5% of new ballots for candidates outside the local top two
  move toward the nearest viable candidate. Ballots already cast keep their
  recorded totals; older v1 and v2 races retain their rules.
- **Institutional failure costs votes and growth.** The ruling-party penalty
  from low Democratic Health doubles, and a sitting President can face a
  combined penalty of up to 30% at complete failure. Below 60 health, the
  economy loses up to four percentage points of annual potential GDP growth.
  That loss reaches employment and tax bases, and election screens explain it.
- **Snap elections reset the term clock permanently.** Later parliaments
  follow the new schedule rather than reverting to the original calendar.
  Dissolution vacates outgoing MPs, and election retries preserve the correct
  winners, offices and presidential terms.
- **Past presidential results stay historical.** Previous and next links work
  again, and completed races show the result recorded at resolution rather
  than being recalculated against today's political map. Wiki history also
  labels snap elections and shows the correct game year.

## Parties grow from their existing base

Recruiting or relocating a politician now follows a party's growth frontier:
regions where it already has a member, politician or officeholder, and their
neighbours. Player membership follows the same regional presence rule, with an
exception for players who cannot be placed anywhere on the frontier. A party
with no presence anywhere can still start from any region. Recruitment and
relocation pickers explain which regions are reachable.

UK parties can reach Major status after qualifying in three regions. That is
three of the UK's twelve regions; other countries retain their one-third rule.

Party, state-party and caucus treasuries with two different seated officers can
pay a member **five times the base per-turn limit**. One person holding two
seats still counts as one officer, and each treasury earns its own increase.
Any two different officers can provide the two approvals a transaction needs.
Approved transfers pay once even when officers approve together or a request
is retried.

Anyone can contribute to a presidential candidate's campaign strength again,
and the control is usable on a phone.

## Corporate earnings and market balance

- **The revenue ramp starts at turn 1213 and targets +20%.** Operating revenue
  ramps to a **20% boost**, replacing the originally planned 15%. It begins
  at turn 1213 and reaches its target over 192 turns. Financial sectors keep
  their additional 10% revenue multiplier.
- **The valuation ramp starts forty turns after revenue.** It starts at turn
  1253 and lasts 192 turns. The
  existing targets remain **30% higher sector NPV** and **50% higher bank
  income NPV**, with the financial risk premium easing from 6% to 4%.
  These are gradual multipliers, so the full increase does not arrive at once.
- **More autonomous investors buy equities.** Computer-controlled investors
  participate more broadly, while cash, share inventory and fund reserves
  still constrain what they can buy.
- **An IPO lists the whole selected float immediately.** Every available
  share can be bought without waiting for a market-pool placement. The
  corporation receives proceeds as buyers pay. Old pending issuances no
  longer block new proposals.
- **Building respects national dominance.** Plant expansion now applies the
  same national market-dominance checks as the other investment paths.
- **Critical shortages can attract new suppliers.** Automated corporations
  can close essential supply gaps. Their investment decisions account for
  demand, capacity and available funding.

The stock market gains candlestick charts using recorded intraday prices,
comparison lines with aligned timestamps, and a mobile layout. Corporation
Financials and market views update financial labels and use consistent
historical currency values.

This release collects changes promoted to production after v1.9.0, through 11830cea6c, including the shipped 1991 readiness work. Balance changes are called out first; source notes retain their detailed rules below.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Legislatures and executives now match the era the world is set in**
  The United States opened its 1991 and 2019 worlds with no president at all, and the seat maps disagreed with the chambers they were filling. Every legislature a player can lead now starts at its real size, with the real people's parties in it, and a reset no longer leaves the previous era's parties behind.
- **Any two officers can approve a treasury transaction**
  The two approval slots on a party treasury transaction are no longer tied to particular seats. Any two different officers can sign, so a party with a Chair and a Vice-Chair but no Treasurer keeps real two-person approval instead of dropping to one signature. Sending and requesting now also show how much the member can still receive this turn, and refuse an amount that could never be paid.
- **Parties grow outward from where they already are**
  Recruiting a politician, moving one, and joining a party are now limited to regions a party already holds or borders.
- **Two officers raise the per-turn payout ceiling**
  A party, state party or caucus with two or more different officers seated can now pay a member five times as much per turn as one running on a single officer. The limit exists to slow down an officer paying themselves with nobody watching, so a second pair of eyes on the ledger earns a larger ceiling.
- **Election pages skip the 31KB stance maps; market page loads tabs on demand**
  Opening the elections page no longer pulls every candidate's full policy stance map out of the database just to draw name-and-avatar summary cards, and the stockmarket page no longer fetches auction data until you open the Auctions tab.
- **Presidential election history is viewable again**
  Past presidential races are reachable from the current race again, and each one now shows the result as it was decided rather than as today's map would score it.
- **Democratic failure now carries sharper consequences**
  Low Democratic Health now weighs more heavily on the ruling party and slows national economic growth until institutions recover.
- **Richer interactive US map**
  Explore political and economic map layers, inspect seated officeholders with portraits, and compare states in a sortable data view. Save your preferred display and explore population, representation, and chamber breakdowns without leaving the map.
- **1991 resets can start without political parties**
  The 1991 reset now offers a clean political start for player-created parties, while retaining the selected era's economy and institutions.
- **Anyone can fund a presidential campaign's strength again**
  Contributing campaign strength is open to every player, not only the candidate. The control had been folded in behind the campaign desk's manage-only section, so the nominee, their managers and their running mate were the only people who could still see it.
- **Treasury approvals pay out exactly once**
  A party treasury transaction can no longer pay out twice. Two officers approving at the same moment now settle who completes it before any money moves, and a transfer that has already gone through is never handed back as though it had failed. Piercing the Treasurer reserve without the Treasurer is recorded as an emergency override again, and the per turn limit is quoted in the party's own currency.
- **Reduce election vote accumulation reads**
  General-election sweeps share candidate, endorsement and country-level lookup data across races.
- **Correct 2027 political party rosters**
  Countries in the 2027 world now open with contemporary political parties instead of inheriting dissolved organizations from older historical presets.

- **Make UK election baselines and seat thresholds fairer**
  UK elections now use each party's current regional registration instead of its starting-era share, and the Commons party threshold is now 10%, removing unintended advantages and harsh zero-seat cliffs in crowded regional races.
- **Clarify election, banking, market, and game-date displays**
  Race entry now asks for confirmation. Election, banking, market, news, and notification screens show clearer labels, prices, and game-calendar dates.

  Politicians with a party stored as a database ID now show the party name and color in the roster and party filter instead of exposing that ID.
- **Make House representation fit state delegation size**
  US House party eligibility now scales with each state's number of seats, reducing all-or-nothing results in large delegations while keeping a higher bar in small states.

  UK regional executive offices follow enacted founding and abolition laws, with elections anchored to their founding settlement.
- **Campaign prices match the world's era**
- **Keep election holders and retries consistent**

  Existing worlds keep their campaign pricing when configuration is migrated or seed data is topped up. Migration requests now reject unknown selections and unsafe forced reruns before making changes.

## Detailed release notes

### Every country now declares what it is in each era, and the game agrees with it

#### Fixed

- East German parties are no longer created in worlds set after reunification.
- Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and East Germany no longer appear in eras after they
  ceased to exist.
- The landing page no longer advertises a country as playable when the world does
  not open it, and no longer lists countries the era does not contain.
- The admin reset picker now lists the countries a reset actually produces. It
  previously named seven for a world that contained sixteen.
- Sphere sponsorship works in the 2023 era, where previously no country could
  sponsor one at all.
- Country pages for a country the era does not contain now say so, instead of
  failing to load.

#### Changed

- Japan is open to players.
- Country access is set by the era declaration when a world is created, rather
  than carried over from whatever the previous world was left on. An operator who
  has hand-tuned a country's access should expect a reset to restore it to the
  declared value.

### Countries are now checked against the era they are in

#### Fixed

- Worlds set in 2019 no longer give Poland, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Belarus
  or Ukraine one-party constitutional policy. Those countries now start with the
  policy levers a modern state has.
- The Soviet Union, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and the Baltic
  republics no longer contribute policy to worlds set after they ceased to exist.
- Country readiness now answers for the era being asked about. It previously
  credited Russia with the Communist Party of the Soviet Union in a 2019 world,
  and reported Germany's region count against the reunified sixteen even in a
  1953 world that correctly seeds eleven.
- The seed report no longer asks for data from countries an era does not
  contain, so a correct reset no longer reports East Germany as missing.

#### Changed

### Trim repeated work out of the hourly turn

#### What changed

- `buildTradeAffinity` indexes active embargoes by directed `source|target` pair
  once, instead of running a `some`/`for` over the full list on every
  `affinityFor` / `capUnitsFor` call. Block and cap embargoes, and the
  `export` / `import` / `both` directions, stay in separate indexes so the
  predicate is unchanged.
- `allocateDeliveriesToBuyers` buckets supply agreements by scope once, instead
  of re-scanning (and re-deriving `scopeOf` for) the whole book inside a loop
  over every scope. Each scope's flow graph is independent, so the result is
  unchanged.
- The round-trip profiler caches the phase it last resolved from a turn trace
  id, and takes the phase name after the second colon instead of splitting and
  rejoining the string. It runs on every Mongo command, and the trace id changes
  once per phase, not once per command.
- Tests pin embargo direction handling and the smallest-cap-wins rule, and cover
  the profiler's trace-id cache across a phase change.

#### Why it matters

All three were cost proportional to list or command count where a lookup would
do. The clearing engine calls the affinity helpers once per
commodity/exporter/importer triple against an embargo list that runs to
thousands of documents in a mature world; the delivery flow ran
O(scopes × agreements) over a contract book of the same order; and the profiler
ran its string work once per command, tens of thousands of times a turn. A CPU
profile of a real turn measured `embargoMatches` at 2.1 s and
`allocateDeliveriesToBuyers` at 1.1 s of self time; after the changes neither
scan remains.

### Treasury approvals pay out exactly once

#### Fixed

- A treasury transaction can no longer be paid out twice. When two officers
  approved at nearly the same moment, each filled a different approval slot and
  each then saw a fully approved transaction, so both went on to move the
  money. Completing a transaction is now claimed by one approver before any
  funds move, and the other is told their approval was recorded and someone
  else is finishing it. A transaction that was cancelled or that expired while
  approvals were being collected now says so, instead of telling the approver
  to wait for someone who is not coming.
- A transfer that has already gone through is no longer reported as a failure.
  Writing the audit trail happens after the money moves, and a failure there
  used to unwind the approval and reopen the transaction, leaving it ready to
  spend the same funds a second time. The payment now stands and the audit
  failure is logged for staff instead.
- A failed payment can no longer leave the party treasury short. The live
  database applies the debit and the credit one after the other rather than as
  one indivisible step, and a failure in between put the money back. A failure
  in the putting back was itself unhandled, so the funds simply vanished from
  the treasury and the transaction reopened for someone to spend a second time.
  Those transactions now stop and are held for staff to reconcile.
- Piercing the Treasurer reserve target is recorded as an emergency override
  again. Since any two officers can sign, a Chair and a Vice-Chair could empty
  the reserve with nothing in the record to show it, because the old rule
  assumed the Treasurer had signed every completed transaction.
- An admin can once again send an amount above the per turn member limit. The
  limit was being applied before the admin exemption, so the exemption could
  not be reached by any amount large enough to need it.
- The per turn limit quoted when a send or request is refused now uses the
  party's own currency symbol instead of always showing dollars.
