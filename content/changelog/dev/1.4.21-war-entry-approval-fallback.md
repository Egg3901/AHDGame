  A treaty bloc's war-entry bill could pass a legislature and still not put the
  country in the war. The check on public support read a country with no stored
  approval snapshot as having zero approval rather than as not yet measured, and
  only playable countries and current belligerents are ever snapshotted. Every
  other country was refused entry whatever its public actually thought. The
  check now measures approval when nothing is stored.

## Fixed

- A war-entry bill sponsored by a non-playable government could pass both
  chambers and still leave the country out of the war. The approval check read a
  missing `governmentApprovals` document as zero approval, and that document
  only exists for playable countries and for countries already fighting, so the
  check could never pass for anyone else. It was a closed loop: a country had to
  already be at war to be measured, and had to be measured to go to war. Where
  nothing is stored the country's approval is now computed from its live
  political metrics, and a stored rating still wins wherever there is one
  because it carries the war block, address bumps, organisation statements and
  cabinet effects that the live figure leaves out.

- Six of NATO's eight open entry bills for the War for Germany were sitting in
  this state, including Turkey's, which had carried its chamber 263 to 164. All
  six score between 49 and 51 once measured, comfortably clear of the floor of
  45 the check applies. Nothing about the refusal was visible to players: the
  bill still signed, and the halt was recorded only in a server log line.

- A war-entry check that cannot complete, or that scores a country as anything
  other than a real number, now refuses entry instead of continuing. A malformed
  metric can carry a not-a-number value through the population-weighted average,
  and a plain "below the floor" comparison against one of those is false, so the
  country would have been waved into the war rather than held out of it.

- The approval page and the war-entry check both scope their region-metrics
  query to the country they are scoring. Region ids are not unique across
  countries, so the unscoped lookup could fold another country's metrics into
  these national averages. No country in the current world is affected; the two
  now match what the per-turn snapshot has always done.

## Changed

- The live approval recompute moved out of the country approval page loader into
  `recomputeNationalApproval`, so the page and the war-entry check score a
  country the same way instead of keeping separate copies. The page passes in
  the metrics it has already read, so it makes no extra queries, and the check
  no longer reads region metrics it has no use for.
