Cut from development.

## Added

- **A national intelligence service, one per country.** It is directed from a new
  cabinet seat: Director of Central Intelligence in the United States, Chairman of
  the Committee for State Security in the Soviet Union (Minister of State Security
  before 1954), Chief of the Secret Intelligence Service in Britain, Minister for
  State Security in East Germany, and a Minister of State Security across the
  Warsaw Pact. The seat carries a Collection Posture tier and two standing orders
  of its own.
- **Networks, built country by country.** Funding a network in a target country
  advances it a little each turn, and its level is how deep your access runs
  there. Funding harder moves faster and burns brighter, the same trade the covert
  nuclear programme already makes.
- **Coverage, which goes stale.** Collection operations raise what you know about
  a country in one domain: strategic weapons, military, or economic. That reading
  decays every turn on its own, so nobody buys sight once and coasts. Covert
  action needs both a network and current coverage, because you cannot act blind.
- **Getting caught is a ladder, not a coin flip.** Whether an operation worked and
  whether it was traced are rolled separately, so a successful operation can still
  be attributed. A compromise costs you the network, never the intelligence you
  already collected.
- **A counter-intelligence posture.** Set how hard your own service guards the
  country. Countries with no player government have theirs worked out every turn
  from the world around them, so nowhere is undefended just because nobody is
  playing it.

## Changed

- East Germany's Minister for State Security is now the Minister of Internal
  Affairs, which is what that seat always was. The State Security name now belongs
  to the separate service that actually held it.

## Notes

- Countries run by the game rather than by a player do not yet run operations of
  their own. That is an administrator switch and it starts turned off. They still
  defend either way.
