Cut from development.

## Changed

A peace between the two countries that STARTED a war now ends that war for
everyone on both sides, instead of requiring one side's roster to run out first.
Allies who joined a war keep fighting only for as long as the government they
came in behind is still in it. The country that leaves is the one recorded as
having lost, whichever side sent the offer: agreeing to withdraw and demanding
the other side withdraw are the same deal written from its two ends, and the war
now reads the same way round either way. A white peace still records no winner,
which puts any question the war was being fought over back on the board.

Asking the opposing founder to quit is treated as buying the war outright, so it
needs the front well in your favour first, exactly as emptying their side always
has. Peeling a country that merely joined off the other side is unchanged.

## Added

East Germany can now offer, or impose, German reunification as a settlement term
in its own right. It is available for as long as the German Question is riding
the war, whatever the front looks like, and it settles the question the moment it
is accepted rather than waiting for the war to be won. The other side has to
agree, and the deal has to be one they withdraw under: reunification cannot be
won by offering to leave the war yourself.

## Fixed

The war record named the sender of a separate peace as the country that left,
which read a "you withdraw" deal exactly backwards and printed a country still
fighting as the one that had walked away.

The negotiation panel told you a withdrawal would leave nobody on the other
side even when that side would still be full of allies, and it went on saying
the fighting would continue after a deal that would end it.

Settlement terms the game did not recognise were announced as whatever the last
kind happened to be, so a reunification would have been published to the war
wire and the public record as a defence procurement freeze.
