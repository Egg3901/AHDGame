## Added

- **Military assessments.** Coverage in the military domain now reads out as a
  graded picture of a country's forces, in the same four steps the nuclear
  assessment uses. Below the threshold you learn nothing. Past it you learn
  whether they are at war and on how many fronts. Past that you get estimated
  strength and readiness. At the top you get the real counts and the supply
  position at each front.
- **Supply readings are the real ones.** A front's supply is worked out the same
  way the war itself works it out, from the front's own position, so the figure
  you are shown is the figure a battle would actually be fought at.

## Notes

- This is sight, and only sight. An intelligence service reads an army from the
  outside; it does not get the view a country's own command staff has, and
  nothing here lets anyone give an order at a front they do not command.
- Covert action against an army is built but is not switched on. What it does to
  a front is a balance question, and the answer has to be measured before it can
  be trusted. There is no war running to measure it against at the moment, so it
  waits.
