## Changed

- The Build Org price now scales with the population of the state being
  organized. A point of Org is a share of its own state, so the same point buys
  far more at election time in a large state than a small one, and charging both
  the same underwrote exactly the states worth winning.
- The curve follows the square root of population, not population itself, and is
  capped at twice the national average and floored at half. The largest states
  therefore cost double the smallest four times over, rather than sixty times
  over, and no state is ever close to free to organize.
- The scale is set per country against that country's own average, so it holds
  wherever the map is lopsided and does nothing where it is even. In practice the
  cap and floor only bind in the United States; British and Soviet regions sit
  inside the band on their own.
- The scale averages out to no change across a country's regions, but parties do
  not spread their effort evenly across regions. Replaying a real week of play,
  about three quarters of American clicks land in states that now cost more, so
  expect organizing to get dearer overall for a party focused on the big prizes,
  and cheaper for one spreading into small states. That is the intent: the clicks
  that got more expensive are the ones buying the most valuable Org.
- The projection panel names the multiplier when it applies, so a chair can see
  why the same click costs more here than in the state next door. Both pool
  buttons and the bulk tool price it too.
- Parties run by the game pay the same scaled price.
