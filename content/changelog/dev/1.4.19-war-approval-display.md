Cut from development.

## Fixed

- The war line vanished whenever the block happened to land on zero. War
  exhaustion is a bonus for the first year a war is fought and a penalty after
  that, so it routinely cancels against war effort and the block genuinely reads
  zero for long stretches early on. Every one of those turns showed a country at
  war with nothing on its approval page to say so. Zero is a real reading and now
  renders as one, in a neutral line rather than the red a penalty gets.

- A country at war that is not yet playable was never given an approval snapshot
  at all, so its war block was scored nowhere and shown nowhere. Both such
  belligerents in the War for Germany were carrying a war rating nobody could
  see. The per turn snapshot now covers every country actually fighting, and
  releases them again once their war is over and their exhaustion has healed.
  Their approval page also stops disagreeing with itself: the effects listed on
  it are now the ones folded into the percentage above them.

- The approval popover on the executive page sorted effects into gains and losses
  and quietly dropped anything sitting at zero. It now lists those too, and every
  row explains itself on hover.

## Changed

- **The war block is now one line per term rather than a single combined
  number.** War effort, war exhaustion and alliance contribution are listed
  separately, each with its own explanation. A long war a country is winning used
  to collapse into a single negative number, because a tired public outweighed a
  successful front and the two cancelled out of sight. Those are opposite facts a
  government answers in opposite ways, and the breakdown now says which is which.

- **War exhaustion carries across a peace.** It was a function of the current
  war's clock, so it reset the instant a war ended: a government could sign a
  treaty and declare again the same turn with a clean slate and no cost that ever
  caught up with it. Exhaustion is now carried between wars and heals one point
  per in-game year of peace, the same pace it accrues at while fighting.

- Declaring a war from peace still rallies the public, but the rally is added to
  whatever exhaustion is being carried and capped, so a country that ended its
  last war deep in exhaustion opens the next one already paying for it. A country
  that never stops fighting gets no rally at all: going straight from one war
  into another, or ending one war while a second is still being fought, no longer
  buys a fresh wave of public support.

- Recovery is symmetric with the cost. A year of fighting takes a year of peace
  to forgive; a war carried to the floor takes twenty five in-game years. A
  country that has never fought is unaffected, and a first war behaves exactly as
  it did before.
