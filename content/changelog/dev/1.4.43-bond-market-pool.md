title: The bond market has a counterparty
summary: >-
  Every bond trade against the public float used to mint or burn money. Buying
  destroyed the buyer's cash, selling created the proceeds from nothing, and
  coupons on unsold units vanished. Each currency now has a bond market pool
  that holds the float and real cash: purchases pay into it, sales are paid out
  of it and refused when it cannot cover them, and issuers pay coupons and
  principal on pool-held units into it. Surplus countries also stop rolling
  over debt they no longer owe.

Cut from development. PR #1330.

## Added

- One bond market pool per currency, in `bondMarketPools`. A bond's
  `publicFloat` is now the number of units that pool holds; the pool document
  is the cash side of the same balance sheet, with lifetime counters for every
  flow so the audit can reconcile cash against flows without replaying the
  ledger. The migration seeds every traded currency at five percent of its
  latest broad money and treats existing float as opening inventory.
- Pool cash is a component of M2 in the money supply snapshot
  (`bondPoolCash`). It is institutional money, so it does not enter M1.
- A read-only audit at `scripts/audit/bond-market.ts`: per currency units
  outstanding, pool share, holdings by type, pool cash against target and
  against the sum of flows, coupon income the pool earns per turn, the unit
  invariant on every bond, and sovereign bonds outstanding against budget
  principal.

## Changed

- Buying bonds from the float credits the pool in the bond's currency. This
  covers players, corporations, index funds, autonomous NPPs, NPP corporate
  treasuries, and issuer buybacks.
- Selling bonds to the float debits the pool. A sale the pool cannot pay for
  is refused with the number of units it can take at the current price, and
  the response carries `marketDepthUnits`. Nothing is claimed or paid on a
  refusal.
- Coupons and maturity face value on pool-held units are paid into the pool
  by the issuer. Corporate issuers were already charged for those units; the
  money now lands somewhere.
- Quantitative easing buys from the pool, so the deposits it creates sit in
  the pool. Quantitative tightening sells back to the pool and is refused when
  the pool cannot pay.
- A dissolving corporation's bond holdings are sold to the pool at the
  current market price, for whatever the pool can pay, instead of being
  credited at face value from nowhere. The pool is also paid its share of a
  dissolved issuer's bond recovery, which was being burned.
- Sovereign rollover is capped at the budget's principal. A country whose
  debt is paid down lets maturing series retire instead of reissuing them; a
  surplus country with no debt issues nothing. France carried 4.2T FRF of
  bonds against a principal of zero this way.
