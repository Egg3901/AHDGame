## Fixed

- Regional chamber elections advertised a seat count frozen at a region's first
  ever cycle. Every later cycle copied the one before it and never looked at the
  region again, so a chamber resized mid-game kept electing to its old size. In
  Germany this put two different numbers on screen for the same Land: Sachsen's
  People's Chamber delegation read 151 on the elections page and 55 on the map,
  and the Land assemblies were worse, electing 24 deputies into a 161-seat
  chamber. A new cycle is now sized from the region itself, which is what the
  chamber's real size is counted from. A region that carries no seat figure at
  all still inherits the previous cycle's, since there is nothing better to use.

- A chamber election already under way when its chamber was resized was never
  corrected at all, and for these chambers the seat count on the race is the
  number the count actually fills. Sachsen's Land assembly race would have cut a
  161-member delegation back to 24 on the next count. Races in flight are now
  brought into line with their chamber the same way the American House and the
  Commons already were, so the correction lands on the current election instead
  of waiting for the next one.

- A region moving to a new country kept the structural baseline of its old one.
  That baseline is the part of a region's scores that its country's laws do not
  account for, measured once and then held fixed, so a region that changed hands
  was credited with its new country's laws and its old country's leftover at the
  same time. After reunification this pushed the acceded Laender's targets to the
  maximum on most of the board while the eastern Laender sat far below them, in
  the same country, under the same laws. The baseline is now dropped as the
  border moves and re-measured against the new country's law book. Because it is
  re-measured from where the region already stands, the handover itself moves
  nothing.

- Two regions merging into one had the same problem in miniature: the surviving
  region absorbed the other's regional programmes but kept a baseline measured
  before it had them, so those programmes counted twice. East Berlin fusing into
  Berlin is the case in the current world. The survivor's baseline is now
  re-measured on the fused region, alongside its tax base, which was already
  being re-derived for exactly this reason.

- A civil-liberties swing landing on a region whose baseline had not been
  measured yet wrote a partial one covering only the metrics it touched. That
  left the board looking measured when it was not, so the real measurement would
  never run and every other metric on it would sit on a figure recalculated from
  scratch each turn and never kept. The swing now moves the scores and leaves the
  baseline alone, and the proper measurement still picks the change up because it
  reads the scores as they then stand.
