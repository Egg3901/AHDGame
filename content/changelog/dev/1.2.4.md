## Fixed

- Cost-push inflation compared each commodity's current price against its 1953
  seed price. That gap is a price level, and it only widens as the world runs,
  so every country carried a permanent 4 to 8 point inflation floor that no
  policy could clear.
- Cost-push is now a rate of change: it reads the movement in commodity prices
  since the previous turn, so a stable price feeds nothing into inflation no
  matter how far it has risen since 1953.
- Central banks were reacting to that floor and holding policy rates far above
  the level the real economy justified. With the floor gone, rates settle where
  the rule intends.
