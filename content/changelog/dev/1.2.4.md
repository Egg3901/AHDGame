## Fixed

- The commodity cost-push term measured the gap between a country's prices and
  their 1953 base price. That is a price level, not inflation. Base prices never
  move, so once prices had climbed a few multiples above them the term became a
  large and permanently growing number that no interest rate could answer. On
  the live world it was adding between 4 and 8 points to every country's
  inflation every turn, while several of those countries' prices had in fact
  been flat for the past half year. Inflation now reads the change in prices
  over the last half year, so it falls back to target when prices settle.
- Central banks were raising rates to fight that number. With the floor gone,
  policy rates can return toward their era's normal level instead of sitting
  three to five points above it.
- A country in a genuine supply crisis still gets real inflation from it, capped
  so a single collapsing market cannot detonate the whole price index.
- Austria had no 1953 monetary settings, so it was quietly using late-1970s ones
  and targeting 4% inflation with a 5.5% neutral rate. It now uses 1953 Austrian
  figures.
