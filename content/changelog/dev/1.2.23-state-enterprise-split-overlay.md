Cut from development.

## Fixed

- Carving a sector type into a new National Corporation now registers that
  corporation as a planned enterprise. Before this, the split created a
  perfectly valid holding company that the command economy simply did not
  recognise: the enterprise table, the plan, and the director seat all select
  on enterprises that are under the plan, and the new corporation was not one.
  A finance minister could therefore remove a whole sector type from their own
  country's plan by reorganising it, with no warning and no way back through
  the interface.
- The new enterprise starts on plan. Its capacity is read from the plant its
  sectors already own and its output from what they actually produced, so the
  numbers do not jump on the first turn after the split, and its quota is set
  to what it is currently producing rather than to its nameplate. That last
  point matters for an enterprise that is short of inputs: setting the quota
  from nameplate would have scored it as failing a target it never had the
  materials to meet. The Gosplan chair sets the real quota from there.

## Changed

- East Germany's eight carved-out enterprises (media, manufacturing,
  healthcare, automobiles, technology, real estate, telecommunications and
  logistics) have been given their place in the plan on the live world, keeping
  their existing history, capital and leadership. Two of them had misspelled
  names, which are corrected. All seventeen East German sector types now have
  an enterprise that appears in the plan and offers a director seat.

## Not changed

- No enterprise changed hands, and no sector moved. Every director and chief
  executive currently in post stays in post.
- Market economies are unaffected: a National Corporation outside a command
  economy has no plan to join, and the split behaves exactly as before.
