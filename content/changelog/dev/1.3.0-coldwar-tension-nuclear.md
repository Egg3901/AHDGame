## Cold War Tension

The world now shares one tension reading, 0 to 100, banded from DETENTE up to
BRINK. Nuclear tests, escalation on the war ladder, and active crises push it
up; it relaxes each turn toward a floor set by how armed and embroiled the
world actually is, so a spike fades but a hot world never reads calm.

Tension is not a scoreboard. It tightens DEFCON, raises defence procurement
demand across the world, and burns detente goodwill. The Conflicts page has
been reframed around it: one headline gauge, a recent-developments feed, and a
strip of the world's nuclear powers, with the boards below decluttered.

## The nuclear programme

Defence chiefs of the United States, the Soviet Union, and the United Kingdom
can now open a nuclear weapons programme, gated behind the Nuclear Delivery
doctrine. The device ladder runs from the first fission bomb through boosted
weapons to the hydrogen bomb and MIRVs; every device advance requires a
nuclear test, and a test is a world event with a tension spike attached.

Delivery is its own investment: bombers, IRBMs, ICBMs, and missile submarines
each harden the deterrent. Warhead production is ordered per turn, paid for
out of the defence appropriation, and a stockpile without a delivery leg
deters nobody.
