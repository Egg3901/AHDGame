Cut from development.

## Fixed

- A bill filed in a state or regional legislature now shows what the law is
  today, what the proposal would cost against it, and which metrics it moves.
  Those three sat in one panel that only draws when there is a current law to
  compare against, so a region that had never been given a starting position on
  a law showed a bare provision instead: no comparison, and no metric effects.
  Infrastructure was where players hit it most often, because it carries the
  largest block of laws a region is allowed to pass, but the same gap ran
  through economy, education, health, order, environment and society.

- Every region now holds a starting position on every law it may legislate,
  fixed at "no programme of its own on top of the national law". That is what
  the engine has always read for a region that has never passed one, so nothing
  a region is measured on moves. It simply says so out loud, which is what the
  bill page needed in order to draw. Existing worlds are backfilled.

## Changed

- Proposing the level a region is already at is now refused, the way it always
  was nationally. Filing a bill to keep everything exactly as it is was never
  meant to be possible; without a starting position on record there was nothing
  to compare the proposal against, so it went through.

- A governor's executive order on one of these laws now moves the region from
  where it actually stands. Before, an order on a law the region had never
  passed started from the middle of the ladder and could hand a region a level
  of provision it had never legislated and was not paying for.
