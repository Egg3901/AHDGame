summary: >-
  A vote of no confidence used to pass on a simple majority of the seats that
  actually voted, so a minority bloc could bring down a government while the
  governing benches stayed silent. A motion now has to reach the chamber's
  majority threshold to carry.
tags: [politics, legislature]
---

## Fixed

- A vote of no confidence now carries only when the ayes reach the chamber's
  majority threshold. In a 625 seat Commons that is 313 seats, so a 215 aye to
  0 nay motion no longer removes a government that holds 364 seats.
- Seats that abstain or never vote now count in the government's favour, which
  is how a confidence vote is meant to work: the government survives unless the
  chamber actively withdraws its confidence.
- The survival notice tells you how many seats a motion needed, so a failed
  motion no longer looks like an unexplained result.

## Changed

- Party members who are not run by a player now vote their party line on a
  confidence motion by default. Government and coalition benches vote to keep
  the government, opposition benches vote against it, and members with no party
  abstain.
- Before this, those seats cast nothing at all unless a player spent a whip on
  them, so a government holding 364 of 625 seats could be brought down by an
  opposition that whipped its own benches and delivered 215.
- Whipping still works and still matters. The party line is only the starting
  position: a whip moves your benches off it, and a whip already issued is never
  overwritten.

This does not change cloture, which still uses three fifths of the votes cast,
and it does not change any other vote type.
