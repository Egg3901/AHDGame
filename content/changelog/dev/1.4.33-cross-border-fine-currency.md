Cut from development.

## Fixed

- Overdue divestiture fines were charged to the company in its own currency
  and then handed to the reviewing government as the same bare number, with no
  conversion. A company based abroad therefore paid the state too much or too
  little by the whole exchange-rate difference, while its own books were
  charged correctly.
- The same mistake applied to transfer-pricing assessments, which are by
  definition collected by a country other than the company's own, and to
  lobbying contributions that fall to the treasury when no officeholder
  receives them.
- All three now convert into the collecting country's own currency before the
  treasury is credited. The amount charged to the company is unchanged.
