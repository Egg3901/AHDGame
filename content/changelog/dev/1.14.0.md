1.14 collects everything merged to development after the v1.13.0 tag: the presidential map and primary rebuild, weekly contests, the sub-hour clock, supply exchange follow-ups, and a long run of economy, banking and turn-performance work. The 1.13.0 posts were extended after the tag to describe much of this work as it landed, so some entries here also appear in the 1.13 diary. This post is the per-change record for the interval from v1.13.0 to the commit named below.

Coverage: base v1.13.0 (f0f222f309), source e6307588d2. Merged feature and fix work in that range has a note; CI-only changes (build swap, Semgrep and federation mirror) and small dependency bumps are covered by the build entries rather than their own.

## What landed in this release

126 changes shipped.

### Elections and campaigns

- **Coalitions vote together in contingent elections, and a deadlocked House seats an acting president**
  When the House and Senate decide a presidential election, coalition members now back their partners' candidates. If no candidate wins a majority of state delegations, the vice president chosen by the Senate serves as acting president while the House keeps voting.
- **Election night for the presidency, one electoral map, and contingent elections explained**
  The last hour of a US presidential race now plays out as election night: the board clears, states report in poll-closing order, and calls land only when they cannot be reversed. The race page has one interactive electoral map with state and county detail, and a House-decided presidency is named and explained.
- **The House votes again every turn after a presidential deadlock**
  When the House deadlocks on a presidential election, it now ballots again every turn like the real House. The first candidate to hold a majority of state delegations becomes president, with the Senate's pick as vice president. Members can change their vote, and party and coalition chairs can whip.
- **Acting presidents are announced and labelled**
  When the House cannot choose a president, the acting president, the remaining candidates and every House member are told what happened, and the White House shows the office as Acting President.
- **Election results update every 30 minutes**
  General election results now move at half past the hour as well as on the hour. Campaign money, actions and every race deadline stay hourly.
- **Newsroom slant now moves local opinion**
  The stance your newsrooms publish slowly pulls voters in that state toward it, and the state page shows how fast.
- **County results in presidential primaries**
  Zoom into the presidential primary map to see a party's primary by county.
- **Field offices, and campaigns that matter in every race**
  Campaigns can open field offices from a county lean map to lift their turnout, Ground Game now moves votes outside the presidency, and UK and Japanese candidates can run campaigns.
- **Party NPP capacity scales with membership and country size**
  The flat 25 NPP limit on parties is gone. Capacity grows with active members, tapering as a party gets larger, up to a ceiling set by the size of the country.
- **Presidential map views, themes and phone layout**
  Colour the presidential map by margin, winner, vote share or momentum, resize its side panels, and use it in any theme and on a phone.
- **Presidential map: road to 270, state views and your campaign**
  A 270-to-win snake on the general election map, states that open in place, your campaign presence on the map with building from the state view, and cleaner, faster county maps.
- **Presidential primaries vary more from state to state**
  Presidential primary results now differ more across the country: ideology fit matters more, every state has its own swing, and candidates do better across their home region.
- **Primary night**
  Every presidential primary wave now has a primary night: eight hours of polls closing state by state and returns coming in live.
- **Replay a presidential race week by week**
  Finished presidential races can be replayed week by week on the map, by electoral vote or popular vote, down to the county.
- **Sandbox testers can found a party alone**
  In the sandbox, a tester can draft a party charter with one to three founders and the party goes live right away when nobody else needs to sign.
- **The presidential race is a full-screen map**
  On desktop, the presidential election page is now a full-screen national map with the race docked beside it. Zoom in close to see county results.
- **Fix missing regional statistics and targeted ad purchases**
  Regional statistics handle missing categories, and targeted ads accept valid fractional action balances while guarding insufficient actions.
- **Registration drives require local party presence**
  Stop registration drives from growing party registration in regions without a local player, active NPP or officeholder.
- **Show presidential campaign presence to international spectators**
  Non-US characters can inspect presidential candidates' campaign presence instead of seeing a blank tab.
- **Supreme Court nomination whips**
  Parties can now whip Senate votes for or against Supreme Court nominees. Cabinet and Supreme Court nominees appear together under Nominations.
- **Build reliability and primary architecture maintenance**
  Build jobs have more memory headroom, dependency metadata is refreshed, and primary running-mate logic has a clearer module boundary.
- **Correct campaign operation income previews**
  Campaign operations now show income in the campaign's currency and era prices. Branch labels distinguish the current effect from the next upgrade.
- **Higher party NPP recruitment ceilings**
  Parties can recruit up to six NPPs per region at 50% organization. Country ceilings also rise to six per region, about 300 in the US, while active-member requirements still apply.
- **Keep independent candidates in their race when joining a party**
  An independent candidate who joins a party stays in the race under their new party.
- **One map on the presidential primary page**
  The presidential primary page no longer shows a second map and repeated campaign panels under the main map.
- **Plainer labels for Political Strength, Organization and other short forms**
  The party treasury now explains what Political Strength is for and how it connects to building Organization in states, and a sweep of party, state, election and wiki pages spells out abbreviations that were left unexplained.
- **Prevent duplicate media entry and streamline whip messages**
  Media entry avoids duplicate setup, and party whip messages reach players through a batched delivery path.
- **Restore approval conditions for Metrics v2**
  National and regional approval now respond to Metrics v2 owner readings. Named effects return with updated units, and national breakdowns explain smoothing.
- **The Soviet Congress of People's Deputies now holds elections in 1991**
  The Soviet Congress of People's Deputies now holds elections in the 1991 era, so the Soviet Union can seat deputies, form a government and pass legislation.
- **Honeymoon for new leaders and a fairer empty cabinet penalty**
  The public expectations drag on national approval now builds up over a new leader's first turns, and the empty cabinet penalty scales with how many seats are empty.
- **The national approval page now shows how the headline adds up**
  The national approval figure is broken into the population-weighted state average and each national adjustment, so the numbers reconcile.

### Economy and corporations

- **Vehicles and entertainment fold into manufacturing and media**
  Automobile makers are now manufacturing corporations on the vehicles line, and entertainment houses are media corporations on the entertainment line, in every era. Their recipes, prices and tech carry over unchanged.
- **Clearer product, advertising and editorial panels**
  The product studio, coverage advertising, editorial stance and strategy switch panels now show real money figures, say why a button is disabled, and use the same compact tables as the rest of the corporation page.
- **Computer-run companies now develop products too**
  Media and manufacturing companies run by the computer start their own product developments, so product hits now lift the wider economy and not only player firms.
- **Corporation ownership insights and desktop settings**
  See current ownership and voting power at a glance, then follow shareholder stakes through recorded turns. Corporation settings use a wider, cleaner desktop layout with a compact mobile form.
- **Index funds rebalance across the day**
  Each index fund now rebalances on its own turn of the day instead of every fund rebalancing on the same turn.
- **Spin-offs keep baseline tech; large groups trade at a discount**
  A spin-off now starts with the same past-decade tech a new corporation gets, and a parent with more than three subsidiaries is valued lower.
- **Shortages pay producers more, and power plants need less machinery**
  Goods in deep shortage now sell for up to twice their base price instead of one and a half times, power plants draw far less on scarce vehicles and machinery, and the margin breakdown names state enterprise efficiency, expropriation risk and economic model fit.
- **Simpler fund pages and corporate fund investing**
  Fund pages put trading first and paginate holdings and subscribers. CEOs can invest corporation cash in funds, with holdings valued on the balance sheet.
- **Reduce repeated banking console reads**
  Reuse charter capital quotes within each banking console request while preserving fresh checks when creating a charter.
- **Banks settle their turn in parallel**
  Each bank's turn (deposits, interest, insurance and loan payments) now runs alongside other banks instead of one bank after another.
- **Closed companies leave a record of why**
  When a corporation disappears from the game, the reason is now kept, so closures, bankruptcies, takeovers and nationalizations can be told apart.
- **Computer-run companies keep cash to grow instead of paying every contract premium**
  Supply contract premiums owed by computer-run buyers are now paid only from half of their cash above the growth reserve, so retail and manufacturing firms can fund new capacity.
- **Corporate income and tax settle in shared batches**
  Profitable corporations that pay tax to the same countries now settle their turn income together in batches instead of one corporation at a time.
- **Corporate tax reaches the treasury in one write**
  Each corporate tax payment now updates a treasury's cash and its fiscal position together, instead of in two separate steps.
- **Government debt no longer grows when bonds are rolled over**
  When government bonds came due, the market exchanged most of them for new bonds and the treasury also borrowed the same amount again in advance. That counted the same debt twice and let national debt climb faster than the deficit. Governments now only borrow ahead for the part of a maturing bond that actually has to be paid in cash.
- **Governments roll over bonds they cannot repay**
  When a Treasury is too short on cash to repay a maturing bond, the bond market now rolls its holding into a new bond instead of leaving the old one unpaid.
- **Half of a plant's overhead is now a fixed cost**
  Plant overhead no longer falls to nothing when a plant runs well below capacity, so firms that sell or make little of what they built earn less.
- **Home-turf profits are lower and service businesses carry more payroll**
  Operating your own sector type in your home state earns a smaller margin bonus, and technology, finance, healthcare and real estate now spend far more of their revenue on wages.
- **Index fund market quotes refresh in fewer steps**
  Index funds now refresh their standing buy and sell quotes on the stock market each turn in a handful of steps per fund instead of one step per order.
- **Large government bond maturities now pay out**
  Some matured government bonds with very large face values were stuck unpaid because of a rounding check. They now settle and pay their holders.
- **New companies start up faster where markets are short**
  Computer-run executives in every country now pay the same real price to found a company, and they found more of them when many markets are short of goods.
- **Old profit peaks fade out of company valuations**
  When a sector earns much less than it once did, the value it adds to its company now fades toward what it earns today over about a year, instead of holding the old peak. Share prices follow current profit more closely.
- **Opening corporations now scale with each economy**
  In a new 1991 world, the starting AI-run corporations in every country are sized from that country's whole economy, not from its capital region.
- **Parent companies can see their subsidiaries' sector books**
  The CEO of a controlling parent company now sees revenue, profit and margin on its subsidiaries' sector pages, matching the subsidiary's corporation page.
- **Power plants get steadily more productive**
  Energy plants in the 1991 world now produce a little more from the same capacity each hour, easing the electricity shortage over the coming year.
- **Profitable corporations settle income and tax in one step**
  A profitable corporation's turn income and its tax payment now settle together, instead of as two separate payments.
- **Retooling plants keep earning while they switch products**
  A plant partway through a production change no longer reports a tiny share of its revenue while still paying for its full input bill.
- **Rival companies push harder into sectors one firm dominates**
  When a single company holds most of a sector, computer-run companies are more likely to be founded there and the rivals already in it expand faster.
- **Safer negotiate links on supply offers**
  The Negotiate link on a supply offer now always opens the chosen corporation's page.
- **Show corporation cash arrears in Financials**
  CEOs can see arrears paid last turn and arrears remaining in Financials, explaining why positive income may not increase available cash.
- **State enterprises expand faster the scarcer their products are**
  The size of a state-funded expansion now grows with how far above normal its products are priced, instead of a flat 5% order.
- **State enterprises now build new capacity when their market is short**
  Governments with healthy finances pay for modest expansion of their state-owned enterprises in commodities that are in shortage, instead of only replacing worn-out plant.
- **Supply agreement premiums now match what a business really buys**
  Premiums and breach damages on supply agreements were charged on a full day of volume every hour. They now settle on one hour of the buyer's real purchases.
- **Supply agreement proposals open beside the button**
  Proposing a supply agreement now opens the form directly beneath the button, so a long list of open offers cannot hide it further down the page.
- **Supply offers show the price per unit**
  Supply offers now show what one unit costs next to the premium, so "+20%" reads as 20% above the market price.
- **Taking and negotiating computer-run supply offers**
  Offers posted by computer-run corporations can be taken again, and the Negotiate button on the offers page now opens a counter-offer with the offer's terms already filled in.

  Each turn's treasury update and the large government bond repayments every twelve turns now run for several countries at once instead of one at a time.
- **Bring the wealth list back to The Market**
  The wealth leaderboard is a tab in The Market again, and old stock-market wealth links open it.

## Detailed release notes

### Coalitions vote together in contingent elections, and a deadlocked House seats an acting president

#### What changed

- Coalitions in contingent elections: House members and senators treat candidates from their coalition partners as allies, second only to their own party, so a coalition votes as a bloc.
- House deadlock: if no candidate reaches a majority of state delegations, nobody is elected president. The vice president chosen by the Senate serves as acting president, and the House keeps voting for about a day.
- The contingent election warning, the election night board and the results page explain who is acting president and how long the House vote stays open.

### Election night for the presidency, one electoral map, and contingent elections explained

#### What changed

- Election night: during the final hour of a US presidential race the page becomes a live broadcast. Every state starts grey, polls close in real-world order on an election-night clock, early counts stay fogged, and a state is projected only once its lead survives anything the last turn could add. Called electoral votes, the next poll closing, a feed of calls and the key races update as results come in, then the board settles on the resolved result.
- One electoral map replaces the battleground board and the second map further down the page. It stays still while you scroll; tap the lock to pan and zoom. Select a state for its leader and margin, each ticket's share and change since last turn, which way the race is moving, and county results.
- The tickets and campaign operations are one table: electoral votes, share, votes, funds, actions, levels, endorsements and a link to each campaign.
- Desktop rails sit at the screen edges so the race gets the full width; on phones the side panels open from a chip row.
- Contingent elections: when no ticket has an electoral majority, the warning appears once, beside the college bar, and shows how the House and Senate ballots would go with the Congress being elected now. Results for a House-decided presidency now name the president the House elected, not the popular-vote leader.

### The House votes again every turn after a presidential deadlock

#### What changed

- Repeated ballots: the House ballots again every turn while the vote is open. The moment a candidate holds a majority of state delegations, that candidate becomes president and the acting president becomes vice president. If the window ends with no majority, the acting president keeps serving.
- Votes can change: any sitting House member can back one of the three candidates, and change their vote at any time while the vote is open. The latest vote counts on the next ballot.
- Whips: a party chair can whip their party's House members for a candidate or a free vote, and a coalition chair can whip every member party without its own whip. NPP members follow their whip, otherwise they vote along coalition lines. Players are never forced, and the panel shows which members defied their whip.
- Candidates who leave: a candidate who withdraws, is deleted, or retires drops off the ballot. A lone remaining candidate still needs a majority of delegations.
- Live board: the election and results pages show the acting president, turns left, each candidate's delegations against the majority needed, the state by state grid, whips in force and the ballot history for each turn.
- Notices: House members, the candidates and the chairs are told when the vote opens, members who have not voted get a short reminder when the board moves, and everyone is told how it ended.

### Vehicles and entertainment fold into manufacturing and media

#### What changed

- Automobiles is no longer its own corporation type. Car and truck makers are manufacturing corporations running the vehicles line, with the same recipes, prices, plant sizes and tech tree as before.
- Entertainment is no longer its own corporation type. Studios, venues and labels are media corporations running the entertainment line, with the same economics as before.
- Founding, expanding, type switching, unions, strategic sectors, tariffs, subsidies and nationalization all offer the vehicles and entertainment lines by name.
- Existing corporations, sectors, markets and unions move to the new form automatically. Balances, plants, workers and shareholdings do not change.

### Acting presidents are announced and labelled

#### What changed

- A House deadlock now sends notifications to the acting president, the candidates still on the House ballot and every player in the House, and posts the outcome to the game events feed.
- The White House shows the office holder as Acting President until the House chooses.
- An acting presidency is recorded as an appointment and does not count toward presidential term limits.

### Clearer product, advertising and editorial panels

- Product studio: one table of spend levels with cost per turn, total, hit chance and revenue lift in money. Decisions show their cost. Past products show spend, extra revenue and net result.
- Coverage advertising: buyer picker with no empty dropdown, errors in plain sentences, and money per turn on every deal.
- Editorial stance: says what the stance does and shows the ends of each scale.
- Strategy switch: rates shown per 100 output, and the switch rules in plain words. Removed buttons that did nothing.

### Computer-run companies now develop products too

#### What changed

- Computer-run media and manufacturing companies now fund new products over three days, the same way a player's company does.
- A company only starts one when it can pay the full development bill and still keep its cash reserve. Companies behind on payments or in distress never start one.
- Roughly one in three eligible companies has a product in development at any time. Each company runs at most one per area.
- Manufacturers lean toward goods that are in short supply in their market.
- Decisions that come up during development are settled by the default response if nobody answers, and a hit lifts the company's matching sectors for three days.
- Player companies are unchanged.

### Corporation ownership insights and desktop settings

#### What changed

- Added a current ownership chart with separate economic stake and voting power figures.
- Added per-turn ownership trends, selectable holders and ranges, and a stake change tracker, including exited holders.
- Kept missing historical registers visible as gaps and retained the existing trade activity log.
- Organized corporation settings into profile and brand sections, with a wider desktop window and restrained theme styling.

### Election results update every 30 minutes

#### What changed

- Votes in a general election used to arrive once an hour, with the turn. Half of each hour's votes now arrive at half past the hour, and the rest arrive with the turn, so results update every 30 minutes.
- Every race still ends at the same time, on the hour, and finishes with the same number of votes as before. Campaign funds and campaign actions still arrive once an hour with the turn.
- Primaries, Irish ranked ballots, the presidential race and a few national ballot systems with their own counting rules still update once an hour for now.
- Vote counters that showed how many turns had been counted now count each turn once.

### Index funds rebalance across the day

#### What changed

- Every index fund used to rebalance its holdings on the same turn once a game day, and that turn ran close to a minute longer than the others. Each fund now rebalances on its own fixed turn of the day, so the work is spread evenly across the day's turns.
- Each fund still rebalances once a day. The market sees fund rebalancing trades spread through the day rather than arriving all at once.

### Newsroom slant now moves local opinion

#### What changed

- Local newsrooms that take an editorial stance now slowly pull the electorate in their state toward that position, on both the economic and social scales. The pull grows with the share of local coverage that takes a stance and with how far opinion currently sits from it. ### Honeymoon for new leaders and a fairer empty cabinet penalty

### Rate committees decide as soon as every vote is in

### Plants ramp up properly in deep shortages

## What changed

- When a plant sells everything it makes, it raises output toward the market's unmet demand. That demand figure used to stop at one and a half times supply, so in a deep shortage plants ramped toward a gap far smaller than the real one. It now counts the full shortage, the same figure prices already use.
- AI corporations only expand plants that run at 85% of capacity or more. In a critical shortage that rule no longer blocks expansion, because the low run rate comes from the old ramp limit rather than from weak demand.
- Expect supply of the scarcest goods (retail, real estate services, healthcare, advertising and similar) to climb over the next several game days, and their prices to ease as it does.

### Corporate margins brought down to a realistic level

## What changed

- Each sector's margin now includes a flat 8-point charge alongside its other modifiers (location, specialization, subsidies, tariffs, technology and the rest).
- The typical corporation had been earning a net margin above 20%, and almost no corporation lost money. The charge brings margins closer to real-world levels, so running a sector well matters and running one badly costs you.
- The charge is the same for every sector and every owner, so the ranking between corporations does not change. Your bonuses keep their full value.
