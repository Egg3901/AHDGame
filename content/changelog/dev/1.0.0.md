Maintainer record for the 1.0.0 cut. This supersedes the 0.4.2 planning bucket
(`content/changelog/dev/0.4.2.md`); the onboarding-legibility items that shipped
are folded in below. Range: everything merged to `development` from the 0.4.1
release (2026-07-11) through 2026-07-20 (#3407, ~150 PRs). Player-facing notes
live in `content/changelog/public/1.0.0.md`.

## Headline arcs

- **Live pre-iteration founding election cycle.** On era reset the engine runs a
  real founding election so every nation starts from an earned mandate instead
  of a seeded default (fixes the China-1994 style fairness gap). Behind
  `preIteration` (default-off) with a `--pre-iteration` CLI flag for offline
  seeding; live-validated on an isolated Mongo, which caught and fixed a
  cycle-1 regionalCouncil bug and a priors-seed skip (#3385, #3398). Also
  corrects the 2023 anchor.
- **Functional historical eras.** A new granular cell-electorate engine, a
  historically-faithful 1953 US Congress, and era-economy correctness make the
  1953 and 1991 worlds playable (details in their own sections below). Granular
  polls ship internationally, flag **ON** for new worlds, with legacy archetype
  panels retired on granular worlds (#3218, #3375, #3377). Era anchors now key
  on the current in-game year, so worlds graduate through eras (#3272).
- **Economy correctness.** Realized revenue is now the single source of truth
  across corp financials, the deflation spiral is closed, and the static-peg
  carry trade is dead (details below).

## Elections & campaigns

- **Primaries** unified onto the delegate vote engine; presidential-primary
  standings, projections, and page parity now agree (#3028, #3022, #3009). The
  chair party-influence boost was removed (#3019), then party-influence in
  primaries was uncapped and retuned 0.2 → 0.4 after the snapshot-vs-vote
  mismatch surfaced (#3317, #3321).
- **Incumbency & fatigue.** Approval-scaled presidential incumbency (#3089 /
  #3083), party-tenure voter-fatigue penalty (#3095; missing-import crash fix
  #3098), incumbency approval pivot raised 43 → 46 (#3324), incumbency-bonus fix
  (#3192).
- **Projection accuracy.** US House projected district-by-district rather than
  proportionally (#3110); vote-surge window anchored to general-election start
  with 3-tier weighting (#3156); vote pool capped at eligible population to kill
  the votes-greater-than-people bug (#3376).
- **Tickets & endorsements.** Running-mate selection for non-US presidential
  races (#3185); suspended endorsers are VP-eligible and count as endorsements
  (#3119); computed Support Campaign costs shown instead of formulas (#3065);
  true net Actions/Turn including rally-tour drain (#3118).
- **Maps.** Historical presidential/legislative maps colour by election-time
  party (#3047, #3069, #3215); county maps (#3225, #3383); USSR 1953 map
  (#3378); compact map-card layout and query fixes (#3229, #3235, #3284).
- **Sim.** Elections-only worldsim profile — scoped, dynamic, votes-over-time
  (#3381). Opposition-research notifications to targeted candidates (#3105).

## Historically-faithful elections — the real 1953 Congress

- **Down-ballot contestation (#3399).** New `generateChallengers` turn phase
  (before `nppBehavior`) files a challenger directly into any open single-seat
  primary (governor / special_governor / senate) that lacks the opposing major
  party's candidate. Governor is LAST in `RACE_PRIORITY`, so `nppBehavior`'s
  Phase-2 fill starved it and its short primary window closed empty — Governor
  came back `{0 candidates : 50 races}` in the elections-only sim. Files directly
  (bypasses priority-ordered Phase-2), reusing a free NPP in the
  `(party, home-state)` bucket else generating via `createNPP`; presence-gated
  (`canPartyFieldInState`), duplicate-guarded, bounded
  (`MAX_CHALLENGERS_PER_TURN = 400`), self-extinguishing; runs in both the live
  engine and the sim. Validated (1953-default, US): Governor 77/77 and Senate
  100/100 contested, 0 uncontested-but-contestable.
- **Real 1953 US legislature — full 83rd Congress (#3394).** 1950-census
  apportionment bundle in `states.ts` (`HOUSE_SEATS_1953` = 435 across 48
  states; `ELECTORAL_VOTES_1953` = 531, the real 1952/56 map: NY 45, CA/PA 32,
  IL 27; winner-take-all, no pre-1972 ME/NE split, no pre-1961 DC votes);
  `getHouseSeats/getElectoralVotes/getElectoralVoteUnits` gain a `1953-default`
  case (previously it silently used the 2020 apportionment, into which the real
  delegations could not fit). Full delegations in `historicalSeats.ts`:
  `US_HOUSE_1953` (213 D / 221 R / 1 I — Frazier Reams, OH), `US_SENATE_1953`
  (47 D / 48 R / 1 I — Wayne Morse, OR), `US_GOVERNOR_1953` (18 D / 30 R); VA
  corrected to 7 D / 3 R so the Solid South is 100 D / 6 R. Alaska/Hawaii are
  territories (absent from apportionment; `seedSeats` skips them until 1959).
  `states1953.houseDistricts` NY 45 → 43 fixes a stray 1940 value (House 437 →
  435); `backfillMissingSeats` capped at the chamber's real slot count (96
  Senate slots) so it cannot seat phantom third senators. Validated via the prod
  path: House 213/221/1, Senate 47/48/1, Gov 18/30, 0 AK/HI, 0 states with >2
  senators.
- **Decennial reapportionment, recalibrated (#3405).** `runCensus`
  (`src/lib/turn/census.ts`) already fires Week 1 of each year ending in 0 and
  recomputes House seats from live `state.population` via Huntington-Hill;
  confirmed it is driven by the cohort model's real per-state population change.
  `INTERNAL_MIGRATION_SENSITIVITY` 0.0002 → 0.00004 (5×): the net-migration
  target scaled with country (not receiving-state) population, so small
  attractive states ballooned (+8%/yr) and the 2020 census flipped seats after a
  single game-year. Now cross-state divergence is ~2.9 pts/yr, the 2020 census
  flips no seats after one year, and a decade still moves a realistic handful;
  214 demographics tests pass. Follow-up: scale the net target by receiving-state
  size to remove the small-state asymmetry.
- **Residual (#3402).** The SIM path (`runWorld` → `bootstrapGameWorld`) skips
  historical officeholder seeding (the NG-governor pre-seed always trips the
  `officialsCount===0` gate), so an elections-only sim starts from a backfilled
  legislature rather than the historical one. Prod reset seats it correctly. Root
  cause: `seedFromSeats` is not idempotent.

## Worlds, seeds & eras

- Granular cell-electorate engine for the 1953 and 1991 seeds, with era-economy
  correctness (#3263) and 1991 NPP-tier completeness — FR/IT/ES/SE/TR budgets,
  policies, org rosters, era starting-years (#3271).
- 1953 audit: BR taxes P0, party resolution, president seating, and
  `rulingPartyId` wiring for Eastern-bloc one-party states (#3371, #3372).
- Feature flags default the full feature set on and expose the demographics
  rework toggle (#3374).
- **Non-Aligned Movement (IntOrg).** Added as a sixth built-in organization,
  founded 1961 with an elected rotating chair on a 3-year (144-turn) term. A
  1953 game now watches it come into existence mid-run — it auto-founds empty
  on reaching 1961 and countries may apply from that point. The 1979 preset
  seats Yugoslavia and Nigeria, the only members among the countries the game
  models; the 1991/2019/2023 presets seat nobody, since none of the countries
  they contain were ever members. `BUILTIN_ORG_IDENTITY` is now keyed by the
  shared `BuiltInInternationalOrganizationId` union, so a future built-in that
  omits its identity fails to compile instead of silently falling through to
  the custom-org accent hash. No alignment mechanics yet — the movement's
  influence channel lands with the alignment slice.
- **Bloc alignment + the Cold War Ledger.** Every nation now carries a share per
  bloc pole plus an uncommitted remainder, always totalling 100, seeded from an
  authored table covering 199 entities — the ops 1953 seed-readiness roster,
  colonies and protectorates included, plus the secession-gated `CountryId`s.
  Which poles exist depends on the live year, not just the era: 1953 is
  West/East, the Non-Aligned Movement becomes a pole in 1961, and 1991 onward is
  Washington/Moscow/Beijing/Non-Aligned. The join gate scales with pole count
  (50/40/35) because a four-way split cannot open a fifty-point lead. Colonies
  inherit their metropole's alignment at 85% and drop to a 30% residual on
  independence, with provenance recorded per row (487 authored, 385 derived).
  New page `/world/cold-war-ledger` lists every nation's shares, lead, trend and
  band. Behind `intOrgAlignmentEnabled`, **default off** — but seeding runs
  regardless, the one gate-off write in the system, so enabling the gate reveals
  a populated map rather than blank rows.
- **Retired the mock alignment board.** `/world/conflicts/alignment` and
  `/world/conflicts/active-measures` rendered a client-side prototype: 16
  hardcoded nations, state in `localStorage`, its own control-percentage model,
  hardcoded fonts and colours. Two alignment systems producing different numbers
  for the same nations is worse than one, so both routes now redirect to the
  Ledger and `_coldwar/AlignBoard.tsx` + `alignment.ts` are deleted. The board's
  influence-lever interaction returns for real as influence plays on the per-org
  Influence tab; until then alignment is read-only and `trend` reads as a dash,
  because no turn has moved it yet.
- **Alignment drifts, and spheres now follow it.** A new `alignment` turn phase
  moves every nation's pole shares each turn, so the Ledger's Trend column reads
  real numbers instead of a dash. Drift is deliberately a tide, not a rudder:
  bounded to a few share points a turn, halved for nations inside the non-aligned
  band (the genuinely uncommitted are hard to move, which is what makes them
  contested rather than merely undecided), and zero past the locked gate. A world
  that reaches 1991 re-projects once — West becomes Washington, East becomes
  Moscow, Beijing starts at zero and has to be earned — guarded by the stored era
  key so it converts exactly once and clears the trend across the boundary rather
  than comparing two different vocabularies.
- **One mechanism behind bloc migration, not two.** Alignment is now the cause and
  sphere membership the effect: each turn a nation's pole shares are projected
  onto its sphere relationships (`share / 100`, so the sphere system's own
  thresholds read as "28% of this nation's sympathy"), and its `primarySphereId`
  is set from whichever patron it has actually committed to, using the same join
  gate the Ledger's bands use. A nation whose sympathies drift far enough changes
  bloc. The hand-tuned sponsor alignment deltas (`COURT` +0.04, `SUPPORT` +0.03,
  `RETAIN` +0.025, `LOSE` −0.08) are **retired** — they were a second mechanism
  editing the same number. Sponsor intents still create relationships, move
  `integration`, and drive treaty state; they simply no longer buy sympathy.
  `integration` stays sphere-owned, because economic entanglement legitimately
  lags political sympathy and that lag is worth keeping. Non-alignment maps to no
  patron at all, so a NAM-led nation has no primary sphere — honest, since the
  movement has none.
- **Alignment now covers every entity the sphere system sponsors.** The
  collection is keyed by `entityId` rather than `CountryId` and seeds all 41
  sphere-macro entities (Jordan, Afghanistan, Laos, Egypt, ...) alongside the
  playable roster — 53 rows in 1953, up from 21. Without this, retiring the
  sponsor deltas would have frozen alignment for the 37 sphere members that are
  not playable countries and left treaty activation unreachable for them.
- **Alignment decides membership, on share thresholds.** A nation may join a bloc
  once its **share in that bloc's own pole reaches 60**, and a member is on its
  way out once that share falls to **40 or below**; both must hold for
  `SUSTAIN_TURNS` (24 — half a game year). Measuring the share rather than the
  lead is what makes this simple: at 60 of a hundred-point distribution a nation
  already leads its pole, so the threshold subsumes any separate "does it lean our
  way" test, and the Non-Aligned Movement needs **no special case at all** — a NAM
  share of 60 means exactly what a Western share of 60 means.
- **41–59 is a deliberate deadband.** `eligible` and `wantsOut` are not
  complements. A nation in that band is neither joining nor leaving, which is what
  stops a member flapping in and out of its bloc on a couple of points of drift.
- **Non-player nations act on their own.** A nation that has held 60 in a pole for
  24 turns **asks** to join every founded org on that pole, and a member that has
  sat at or below 40 for 24 turns walks out. Both clocks are cleared the moment
  the share recovers, so each needs a sustained run rather than a cumulative
  tally: the accession clock is per-pole on the alignment row (`joinReadySince`),
  the departure clock per-membership (`wantsOutSinceTurn`).
- **An NPP request only asks.** It inserts a normal membership proposal with
  `domesticApproved: true` — an NPP government's legislature is abstracted and the
  sustained run IS its decision — which leaves the org's **unanimous member vote**
  as the only live gate. That is the part players actually play. A refused
  applicant is barred from re-applying for 24 turns; without that it would
  re-apply every single turn, since its share is still high and its clock still
  old.
- **Players are never moved for.** They choose their own alliances: no automatic
  request, no automatic departure. A player's country still accrues its clocks so
  the Influence tab can show the countdown, and `propose-join` still holds them to
  the same 60 bar — the automation is what's NPP-only, not the rule.
- **Alignment governs only the blocs you join by picking a side.** A new
  `alignmentAccession` flag on the channel separates NATO, the Warsaw Pact and the
  Non-Aligned Movement — which a nation joins and leaves purely on where its
  sympathies sit — from orgs whose membership answers to something else. The
  Commonwealth is a former-empire association and the EU has its own accession
  criteria: both still carry influence as channels, so a play through them still
  moves a nation's shares and belonging to them still pulls it toward their pole,
  but a nation at 60 Western now asks to join **NATO and nothing else**, and never
  walks out of the Commonwealth for ceasing to be Western. The flag is
  fail-closed: a channel that has not opted in is not alignment-governed.
- **Orgs without an alignment channel are untouched.** `standingFor` returns
  `null` for the UN, every custom org, and the EU before 1991 — and every caller
  reads null as "no condition", never as "ineligible". Getting that backwards
  would make the UN unjoinable. A pole that has not opened yet reports a share of
  0, which is why NAM membership means nothing before 1961.
- **A bloc's own founders can never be expelled.** Nigeria founds the Commonwealth
  while still a colony, carrying a colony's damped metropole alignment (a Western
  share of 24); NAM's founders hold a NAM share of 0 in 1953 because the pole does
  not exist yet. Founding members are skipped by the departure sweep entirely (no
  clock is even started), and the Influence tab marks them `founder` rather than
  showing a countdown it will never honour.
- **Departure reuses the withdrawal path.** `removeOrganizationMembership` writes
  the tombstone, so the founding-member self-heal cannot re-add a defector —
  skipping it would reintroduce the "DE keeps re-joining NATO" bug. Standing is
  judged on the POST-drift shares, so a nation that recovered this very turn is
  not shown the door on last turn's numbers.
- **Non-alignment is the remainder, not a pole.** NAM is gone from
  `AlignmentPoleId`, from every era's pole list, and from the channel table. A
  country's bar is now West | East | whatever neither bloc persuaded, and that
  leftover IS non-alignment once the movement exists — there is no longer a
  separate "NAM share" sitting alongside an "uncommitted" share, which were two
  names for the same idea. The Non-Aligned Movement never acts: it has no channel,
  makes no plays, and captures exactly what East and West fail to take.
  `standingFor` reads its accession from the remainder, so the same 60/40
  thresholds apply to it as to any bloc. The Ledger draws the remainder as a real
  segment rather than blank track and labels it _Non-aligned_ from 1961,
  _Uncommitted_ before. Authored seed rows needed no edit: with NAM out of the
  pole list its trailing column folds into the remainder automatically, which is
  precisely the collapse.
- **The Commonwealth carries no channel either.** Stacked behind NATO it gave the
  West 1.75 weight against the Warsaw Pact's 1.0 — a permanent structural
  advantage no play could answer, made worse once allies began stacking properly.
  The Cold War now has one channel per bloc at matched weight, and a test asserts
  that symmetry so it cannot quietly drift again. It remains a former-empire
  association with no alignment role at all.
- **The crisis desk.** Flashpoints put one nation in play for 12 turns. Three
  sources: **authored anchors** (a Hungarian rising, Suez, a divided Berlin, a
  missile standoff, Prague) pinned to era windows and fired once per world;
  **retargeting**, so an anchor whose nation is absent (Egypt and Cuba are not
  implemented) or already locked moves to the most contested nation instead of
  fizzling — which is why the copy names a _kind_ of crisis, not a country; and
  **emergent flashpoints** from world state, either a tug-of-war (two blocs each
  holding 25+ with no decisive leader) or a **defection crisis** when a member is
  halfway to walking out. That last one is the "defection crises" the alignment
  spec listed under slice C and left to this desk. At most three run at once, one
  per nation, and none open on a nation nothing could move.
- **A crisis lifts a ceiling; it grants nothing.** While one runs, its target's
  per-turn movement cap rises from 5 to 7.5, so influence spent there goes further
  than it could anywhere else — and a flashpoint nobody acts on changes nothing at
  all. No pool, no bidding, no held state, and nothing to farm: the crisis pays
  off strictly in proportion to what blocs actually commit.
- **Movement is much slower than it was.** `PER_NATION_TURN_CAP` drops from 15 to
  **5** share points a turn (7.5 in crisis). Swinging a nation from contested to
  committed now takes the better part of ten turns of sustained full spend rather
  than three — an alliance is won over months, not bought in an afternoon.
- **Opposing pushes now cancel BEFORE the ceiling, not after.** `computeDrift`
  nets each pole against the strongest rival push and caps what survives, rather
  than capping raw churn. Three consequences, all deliberate: allies on the same
  pole **stack** instead of cannibalising each other (two Western orgs at $2.5B
  apiece previously achieved exactly what one did); an evenly matched contest
  **barely moves the meter**, and a dead heat moves it not at all; and a rival's
  push **cancels** the one it answers rather than merely landing alongside it —
  the answering bloc gains nothing, it only denies.
- **The automatic counter-op is gone.** A play used to draw an unpaid rival
  answer worth half its points, so the acting bloc kept half of what it bought
  and the tax was paid to nobody. Worse, it fired unconditionally — a bloc with
  zero shares and no members in a country still denied half the spend for free,
  and passivity was quietly the best-funded strategy. The meter now moves on what
  organisations actually **do**. Rivals still cancel each other, because
  `computeDrift` nets opposing pulls before the cap — but only by spending.
  `COUNTER_OP_FRACTION`, `counterOpPull` and the `provokesCounterOps` channel flag
  are all deleted, along with the crisis special case that existed solely to
  suppress the reflex.
- **Contested targets resolve proportionally, with no special machinery.** Two orgs
  acting on the same nation in the same turn each add their own weight to the pull
  vector; `computeDrift` scales the whole vector when it exceeds the cap, so rivals
  dilute each other automatically. An earlier auction design — a fixed pool divided
  by spend share, with bids held until a crisis closed — was **removed**: it made an
  uncontested crisis a windfall (a $100M bid taking a whole 20-point pool), and with
  no NPP bidding behaviour, uncontested was the default rather than the exception.
- **Influence plays — the ledger becomes a lever.** Any member's foreign minister
  can spend their organisation's pooled fund to pull a nation toward that org's
  pole, through `POST /api/country/[code]/international-organizations/[orgId]/influence`
  and the new per-org **Influence** tab. Money buys share points on a square root
  (`$100M` = 1 point, `$900M` = 3, capped at 10), so the tenth point costs far
  more than the first and no single cheque ends the Cold War. The channel's own
  weight scales it. Plays are queued in a new
  `alignmentPlays` collection and resolved by the alignment turn phase into the
  SAME pull vector drift feeds — so a play shares the per-nation turn cap with
  drift, is halved against a nation inside the non-aligned band, and cannot move
  a locked nation at all. Resolved plays are stamped rather than deleted
  (`resolvedTurn`, `appliedPoints`) so a member can see what its money bought.
- **A locked target is refused before the debit.** `computeDrift` no-ops a nation
  at or past the locked gate, so a play against one would have taken the money
  and moved nothing. The command rejects it first, and the tab does not list
  locked nations at all rather than offering a trap.
- **Reconciled the alignment roster against the world entity manifest.** The
  roster was authored from the ops report before the three-tier epic landed, and
  the two had diverged in ten places. Five entities had invented administering
  powers — Trieste (Italy), Tangier (France) and Palestine (the UK) are
  international-zone or disputed-sovereignty entities with no single metropole in
  1953 and are now unparented, New Hebrides was an Anglo-French condominium the
  manifest primaries to the UK, and Nauru was a UN trust territory administered
  by Australia, not Britain. Five more gained map geometry the manifest had and
  the roster lacked (Gold Coast, French Algeria, British Guiana, Portuguese
  Angola and Mozambique). Where both sources carried geometry they agreed
  everywhere, so the two were complementary rather than contradictory. Three
  tests now hold them in agreement on tier, status, metropole and geometry, so
  manifest drift fails the build.
- **An organisation admits any entity the game models, not just playable
  countries.** `OrganizationMembership.countryId` is widened to `OrgMemberId`
  (`WorldEntityId`) — the field is widened, not renamed, so no migration and no
  live rewrite. Every one of the ~17 sites that then failed to typecheck was
  decided on its merits rather than cast away: the NPP nudge builders
  (`agency`, `directives`, `posture`) filter to `id in COUNTRY_CONFIGS` because
  a macro entity has no metrics to nudge; leadership sites filter to playable
  countries because a chair is an office a character holds; display sites fall
  back through `ROSTER_BY_KEY` via a new `entityLabel.ts` so a macro member
  renders with its name and flag instead of a bare id.
- **A vote belongs to a player-enabled country, not to membership.** `votingMembers`
  / `tributeMembers` / `isVotingMember` in one module partition the roll on a
  single predicate — `enabledForPlayers === true`, fail-closed, so an entity with
  no access row (every macro entity, by construction) needs no special case. This
  is what makes entity-wide membership survivable: adding thirty client states to
  NATO would deadlock every admission under unanimity if they were voters who
  could never cast a vote. **This changes live behaviour** — a non-enabled full
  country that votes today stops voting.
- **Six rules, not the three the plan named.** Admission unanimity, resolution
  passage (including FTA `parties`), leadership _elections_ — the plan missed
  that fourth one, and its quorum was counting silent members toward turnout they
  could never supply — plus the three write-side gates (`voteMembershipProposal`,
  the legislation vote route, the leadership-election vote route). Narrowing the
  resolvers alone would have accepted a ballot at the door and then discarded it,
  telling a player their vote counted when it did not; the refusal copy changed
  from "is not a member of" to "has no vote in", which is now the true statement.
- **What is narrowed is the ballot, never the binding.** A ratified FTA still
  binds every party, sanctions still land on every member, and effects still read
  the full roll — only who may vote is restricted. Country-history events stay on
  the modelled countries, since a macro entity has no page to write a timeline to.
- **NATO and the Warsaw Pact seat the alliances they actually had.** Both defs
  were limited to countries the game simulates as playable, so 1953 NATO was six
  members instead of fourteen. Entity-wide membership removed that limit and the
  defs had not caught up — `foundingMembers` was still `CountryId[]`, now
  `OrgMemberId[]`.
  - **NATO 1953 (14):** the twelve Washington Treaty founders of 4 April 1949
    plus Greece and Turkey, who acceded 18 February 1952. Canada, the
    Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, Norway, Denmark, Portugal and Iceland join
    as background entities. West Germany stays out until 1955.
  - **NATO 1979 (15)** adds West Germany; **1991 (16)** adds Spain (1982). The
    1979 entry also fixes a real gap: without it the preset fell through to the
    bare founding list and a 1979 world's NATO was **US, UK and West Germany
    alone** — the same bug the 1991 entry had been added to fix, which 1979
    silently inherited.
  - **Warsaw Pact 1953 (8)** seats Albania, a founding signatory of the 1955
    treaty; **1979 (7)** does not, since it stopped participating after the 1961
    Soviet-Albanian split and formally withdrew in September 1968.
  - **The UN's 1953 roster goes from 12 seats to 60.** The real UN of 1953 had
    60 members: the 51 founders of 1945 plus the nine admitted before the Cold
    War froze admissions — Afghanistan, Iceland, Sweden and Thailand in 1946,
    Pakistan and Yemen in 1947, Burma 1948, Israel 1949, Indonesia 1950. Nothing
    then joined until December 1955, which is why the roster reads oddly
    incomplete: Spain, Austria, Finland and Portugal are all 1955, Japan 1956,
    Nigeria 1960, both Germanies 1973, both Koreas 1991, Switzerland 2002.
  - **The founding-membership self-heal is batched, and had to be.** It runs on
    EVERY read of the world-org view and did one `findOne` per member per org —
    about a hundred sequential queries to discover, almost always, that there was
    nothing to do. Growing the UN from 12 seats to 60 made that five times worse:

    bargain with their clients, not a general property of membership: charged per
    organisation it would compound — a client of four would owe four times the
    rate — and the UN would have collected as much from the world as NATO does
    from its own bloc. Scoped by PRESET rather than live year, so a 1953 game
    keeps the arrangement as it runs forward instead of losing it after one game
    year. Outside that scope a non-voting member pays nothing at all: dues are
    voters-only, so the two rolls no longer partition into two paying halves —
    they only guarantee nobody is billed twice. The Influence tab marks such a
    member "no vote" rather than "no vote, pays tribute". Fixed rather than
    member-voted on purpose: letting the members who vote set what the members who
    cannot vote owe would be taxation without representation dressed as a mechanic.
    **Debit where a treasury exists, mint where it does not**: a non-enabled full
    country pays real money out of `federalBudget`, a macro entity has no treasury
    modelled at all so its tribute is created and reported as `minted` rather than
    passed off as revenue. The debit is floored — unlike dues, tribute never pushes
    a treasury negative; a payer that cannot cover it is skipped for the turn.
- **Dues now charge voting members only.** The turn phase splits the roll once and
  charges each half its own way, so nobody is billed twice and nobody escapes
  both. The access table is loaded once per turn and passed down rather than
  resolved per organisation, which would have fanned out to two round-trips per
  org per turn.
- **Live GDP for any entity, in one place.** `loadGdpUsdMillionsByEntity` reads
  playable countries from `states` and macro entities from Σ sector capacity ×
  `MACRO_TURNS_PER_YEAR` — the seed builder derives capacity as
  `annualGdp / 48` from normalised weights, so summing and multiplying back
  inverts it exactly, and reads whatever the macro kernel has since evolved it to.
  Both sources are live: a country that grows gets more expensive to buy and pays
  more tribute. An entity in neither source is **absent** from the map, never
  zero — zero would make an unpriceable nation free to influence and exempt from
  tribute.
- **Influence costs a share of the target's economy.** `pointsForSpend` takes the
  target's GDP and the square root is **gone**: `POINT_COST_GDP_SHARE = 0.01`, so
  1% of a nation's annual GDP buys one share point, linearly. The root existed to
  stop a rich nation buying the world in a turn, and the 5-point turn cap plus
  pull-netting now do that job twice over; linear buys legibility instead — 1% is
  a point, a nation moves at most 5 points a turn, so past 5% of their GDP the
  money is wasted unless a rival is pushing back. `PLAY_MAX_POINTS = 10` stays,
  bounding one play at 10% of GDP while still permitting the over-bidding netting
  makes rational. A target whose economy is not on record is **refused**
  (`unknown-target-economy`) rather than priced at nothing.
- **UNIT TRAP, handled once per call site.** `loadGdpUsdMillionsByEntity` returns
  USD _millions_; play spends and treasuries are absolute USD. Every call site
  converts through the existing `GDP_MILLIONS_TO_USD` and names the variable
  `targetGdpUsd`, and a test asserts the realistic case in the units the caller
  actually holds ($6bn arrives as `6_000`, and $60m must buy one point) — mixing
  them would misprice every play by a factor of a million with nothing failing
  loudly.
- **Every surface built from the member roll had to learn the difference.**
  Widening membership let entity ids reach code that assumed a playable country.
  Two places were quietly wrong: the vote rosters listed tribute members and
  macro entities under "no vote yet" for a ballot they can never cast (nine call
  sites, all passing the full roll), and the aid recipient picker offered
  macro-tier members, where an unguarded `COUNTRY_CONFIGS[recipient].name`
  threw rather than explaining itself. The summary now carries `hasVote` and
  `isCountry` per member — deliberately independent, since a client state cannot
  vote but does have a treasury to receive aid — and rosters, the FTA party list
  and the aid picker each filter on the right one. Aid to an entity with no
  treasury is now a plain 400.
- **Shares resolve to a tenth of a point, not a whole one.** Alignment shares
  were integers, and that silently destroyed every effect worth less than half a
  point — a share was re-rounded on every write, so a recurring 0.4 was not
  "slow", it was nothing at all, forever, no matter how many turns it ran. Any
  low-magnitude lever was inert by construction. `normalizeShares` now works to
  0.1, which is what lets a small influence play, a modest sanctions erosion or
  a fractional drift actually accumulate.
  - The arithmetic runs in **integer tenths** (0–1000) rather than on the decimal
    values, and divides by ten once at the end. That is not fussiness: 0.1 has no
    exact binary form, so summing decimal shares drifts and the exact-total
    invariant would fail on floating-point dust. Integers keep the apportionment
    and the total exact.
  - Anything _derived_ from shares — a lead, a trend — is snapped back onto the
    grid by `roundToTenth`, because the ledger prints those numbers directly with
    `String(n)` and a raw `33.4 - 33.1` renders as `0.30000000000000071`.
  - **No migration.** An existing integer share is already a valid tenth.
  - The sanctions erosion went back to **1** as a result. It had been raised to 2
    purely to clear the old rounding floor; with tenths, a contested nation's
    halved 0.5 registers and accumulates properly, so the value chosen on merit
    stands.
- **Aid buys loyalty.** An aid package already moved real money into a member's
  treasury; it now also pulls that member toward the paying bloc's pole, priced
  against its own economy exactly as a play is and resolved through the same
  vector. The two instruments are not interchangeable, and the difference was
  already in the code rather than being a new dial: **aid can only reach a
  member** and needs a member vote, while **a play reaches anyone** and any
  foreign minister can commit one alone. Aid is how a bloc holds the clients it
  has; a play is how it takes one off somebody else.
- **Sanctions cost the sanctioner.** Squeezing a nation now erodes the
  sanctioning bloc's own standing there, 1 share point a turn while the embargo is active — the historical pattern, and something a rival can bait an opponent
  into. Expressed as negative pull rather than a push toward a rival, because
  where the resentment lands is not the mechanic's to decide: the uncommitted
  pool absorbs it and a rival still has to act to collect. Deliberately smaller
  than `MEMBERSHIP_PULL`, so sanctioning your own member sours the tie without
  expelling it by accident.
- **The tab quotes the DELIVERED price, not the list price.** A nation inside the
  non-aligned band absorbs half of what is pushed at it, so a point costs twice
  as much there. The tab had been quoting the raw 1%-of-GDP figure, understating
  the true cost of the contested nations — the ones actually worth courting — by
  2x, and understating a full turn's movement by up to 4x once the counter-op was
  counted. Pay the quoted price now and you move exactly what it says.
- **The alignment screens call countries by their era's name.** Both the Cold
  War Ledger and the Influence tab read `COUNTRY_CONFIGS[id].name`, so a 1953
  world said "Germany" and "Russia" while every other screen said "West Germany"
  and "Soviet Union" — `ERA_COUNTRY_NAMES` and `getCountryDisplayName` had
  existed all along and these two never called them. Fixed once in the shared
  projection, so both screens change together. The Ledger additionally had to
  add `preset` to its `gameState` projection: without it the preset resolved to
  the modern default and the era override silently never applied, which no type
  error could have shown.
- **The Influence tab is a situation room, not a form.** It opened as a
  `<select>` of every non-locked nation plus an amount box: you had to already
  know who you wanted. It now leads with the world balance — the standing split
  between the poles, weighted by economy or by nation count, toggleable, because
  a bloc can hold most of the map and little of the money — then a ranked,
  browsable roster of every nation, then a dossier on whichever you pick.
  - **Four rankings, none of which filter:** cheapest to flip (the delivered
    price times the distance to the join gate), closest to joining (the same
    distance without the price — a nation two points away can be the dearest on
    the board, and a bargain can still be thirty points out), under flashpoint,
    and already a member. The metric on each row changes with the
    active sort, so a ranking can always be checked rather than trusted. Absent
    data sorts last — an unpriceable nation must never read as "cheapest".
  - **Two of the four narrow rather than reorder.** Cheapest and closest answer
    "who should I go after", where hiding a nation would hide an option, so they
    show the whole roster. "Under flashpoint" answers "where is influence worth
    most right now" and shows only nations with an open crisis, soonest to
    settle first — the raised ceiling you are about to lose. "Already a member"
    answers "who am I about to lose" and shows only your own bloc, weakest hold
    first. Neither question can be answered by a list padded with countries you
    have no relationship to. An empty result explains itself per mode rather
    than rendering a blank panel.
    It replaced a per-turn "slipping away" trend that looked right and was
    useless — on turn one nothing has a previous turn, so every trend was null
    and the mode collapsed into an alphabetical list of non-members.
  - **The dossier** shows the nation's shares as the same `ShareBar` the Ledger
    uses, marked with the join (60) and leave (40) gates — both thresholds on a
    share. The locked gate is deliberately absent: it is a threshold on _lead_,
    and drawing it on a share axis would place it somewhere it does not mean.
    Alongside it, what is actually acting on the nation: half-strength
    resistance, a raised flashpoint ceiling, and any bloc whose sanctions are
    eroding its own standing there.
  - **Rival activity, without the money.** The dossier names the bloc that
    pushed and the points it landed, never the spend — a rival's fund depth is
    their position, not their action. That is enforced where the query is built
    rather than in the component, and a test asserts the rendered intel contains
    no currency at all, so it cannot be "helpfully" added back later.
  - `ShareBar` moved out of the Ledger into `@/components/alignment`, and the
    per-nation projection both screens rely on is now one shared function
    (`projectNationStanding`), so the Ledger and this tab cannot describe the
    same country differently. The tab itself shrank from 297 lines to 210 while
    gaining all of the above, because the three panels became their own
    components.
- **The Influence tab quotes a price per nation.** With a flat rate a player could
  memorise one number; now every target costs something different, so the tab
  shows the per-point price in each option and, for the selected nation, what a
  full turn's movement costs. Members that pay tribute render as members marked
  _no vote, pays tribute_ — not as an error and not as a blank row.
- **Measured against the live testing world before shipping the rates.** A bloc's
  per-turn income (~$448M for NATO there) buys roughly 3.7 points against a
  mid-sized target and ~0.01 against a superpower, so money binds only at the top
  of the board and the 5-point cap binds everywhere else — which is the intended
  shape, and no rate needed changing. The probe also turned up 21 stale
  `countryAlignments` rows on that database still keyed by the pre-rename
  `countryId`; the current code reads `entityId`, so they were already inert.
- Warsaw-Pact tier gets a real 1953: authored 1953 Layer-1 census bundles for
  Poland, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Byelorussia
  and the Baltics (they previously reused the 1979 census, so Stalin's last year
  was seeded with Brezhnev-era urbanisation and schooling), a Stalinist position
  and turnout table that actually separates the uncollectivised countryside from
  the plan's industrial towns, and an era-specific command-economy metric
  baseline (reconstruction growth, pre-mass-schooling attainment, the terror
  state's incarceration and transparency levels).
- Fixed Belarus seeding against `BY_BEL` instead of its real region id
  `BLR_BEL`, which left `BLR_BEL` with no demographics, metrics or baselines in
  either Cold-War era. The whole bloc now has a test asserting every census,
  metric and baseline id is a real region.
- **1953 Layer-1 demographics realism (UK/DDR/USSR).** UK 1953 census shares
  recalibrated to 1951 Churchill geography; adult senior share and `no_qualifications` brought
  in line with 1951 census. USSR university shares tightened and the BEL/BLT header
  corrected. (The DDR census tightening was dropped — it compressed the
  derived economic spread below the DD 1953 calibration floor.) Guarded by new UK 1953 era-fact
  anchors in `uk.test.ts`.

  (`householdConsumptionEnabled`, default-off).** Wires the household signals the

- **Non-US corporations could not build or split a sector at all.**
  `expandSectorSchema.stateId` was refined against `STATE_IDS` — the 50 US
  `HOUSE_SEATS` keys — so every non-US region id (`SEE`, `LON`, `CEN`, …) was
  rejected by zod with `stateId: Invalid state ID` before the route ever ran.
  Both the build and split flows POST the same endpoint, so a UK/RU/DE/CN/JP
  corp was locked out of both. The gate was redundant as well as wrong:
  `expandSector` already resolves the id against `states` scoped to the
  corporation's own country, which proves more than an allowlist can. `stateId`
  is now shape-only, matching every other multi-country schema in the repo. The
  same dormant US-only gate on `executeActionSchema.targetState` was removed
  for the same reason (and it now rejects the empty string it used to admit).
  Fixed twice in parallel — ticket-1022 landed the same schema change on
  `development` — and the two were reconciled at merge. That merge caught a
  real defect in the shipped version: its `.max(12)` shape bound silently
  excluded Nigeria's `NORTH_CENTRAL`, the longest seeded region id at 13
  characters. The bound now lives in one place as `MAX_REGION_ID_LENGTH` (15),
  measured against the seeds and shared by all four region-id schemas, so two
  authors cannot pick two different caps again. Pinned by a test naming the id.
- **Staff could not relocate a non-US player.** The same `STATE_IDS` refine sat
  on `homeState` in both `admin/characters/update-state` and
  `moderator/characters/update-state` ("homeState must be a valid US state
  code"), and both already did the authoritative country-scoped `states`
  lookup underneath — the sibling `update-country` route was written that way
  from the start. Both are now shape-only. The admin panel behind them carried
  a hardcoded 50-entry US state table and rendered a homeState dropdown only
  when the country was `US`, so the gap was invisible from the UI: it now reads
  regions from `GET /api/country/[code]/states` (the endpoint that already
  backs corporation relocation) with a country picker on the Change Home State
  card. `loading` is derived from the loaded country rather than set in the
  effect, so switching country can never render the previous country's regions.
- **Relocating a player to a non-US country was impossible from the admin
  panel.** Found while auditing the above. The Change Home Country card gated
  its region picker on the same `countryHasStates(id) => id === "US"`, so a move
  to any other country sent no `homeState`; `update-country` then falls back to
  `character.homeState` — a region of the country they are LEAVING — and its
  `{ _id, countryId }` lookup can never match, so every such move 400'd with
  `No state "CA" in country "UK"`. The picker is now country-driven, and Update
  Country is blocked while regions load or while a region-bearing country has
  none selected, so the unsubmittable state is unreachable rather than merely
  reported. Covered by a component regression test.

## Platform, performance & infra

- **Android WebView.** Stopped zstd re-compression breaking WebView fetches with
  a `no-transform` sweep across page/RSC, character-creation, and client-nav
  responses (#3085, #3122, #3128, #3211); never load AdSense in the native-app
  WebView (#3181).
- **Egress & retention.** Private ETag/304 on the hottest polled endpoints
  (#3036); R2 archive + downsample pipeline for history collections (#3046).
- **Turn perf.** Coalesced per-phase telemetry writes + optional batched fund
  dividends (#3365); admin reset drops runtime collections to fix the reset
  timeout (#3373).
- **1999, 2007 and 2023 were silently seeding 2019's parliaments.**
  `getPresetSeats` matches on preset id and has a `default:` branch commented
  "Default to 2020 if unknown preset" — so those three, which have no case, were
  handed the entire 2020 chamber roster: 1,004 seats byte-identical to
  2019-default, with nothing anywhere saying so. Downstream, 192 Japanese and 187
  German seats named parties whose `validForPresets` excluded those eras, so
  `resolvePartyId` found nothing, its regex fallback found nothing, and the seats
  folded to "independent" — the same failure the `de_spd` regression test guards
  for 1991. The fallback now records itself through the same mechanism
  `selectPresetBundle` uses, so a seed run's closing "⚠ N seed lane(s) had no
  bundle and used 2019 data" line names it instead of the world merely looking
  authored. Party era-validity is corrected for the parties that demonstrably
  existed — the LDP (1955), Kōmeitō (1964) and the Greens (merged 1993) now carry
  1999/2007/2023, and the CDP, Ishin, DPFP, Die Linke and AfD now carry 2023 —
  which fixes 2023 completely and takes 1999/2007 from 379 mis-seeded seats to 128. The 128 that remain name parties that genuinely postdate the era (AfD
  2013, Ishin 2012, CDP 2017, DPFP 2018, Die Linke June 2007); those need real
  1999/2007 chambers authored and are pinned in the test suite until they are.
- **The reset spent most of its time waiting for the network, one document at a
  time.** 12,261 of a bootstrap's 15,490 commands were single-document updates
  issued from plain `for` loops — mean batch size 1.16. `seedUnownedSectors`
  alone was 3,842 round trips at ~15ms each, 22% of the run, and that 15ms is
  essentially pure latency, not work. Thirteen measured call sites now
  accumulate their upserts and issue one `bulkWrite(ops, { ordered: true })`.
  Ordered, deliberately, and not the repo's usual `ordered: false`: several of
  these loops revisit a key (`statePolicies` takes 1,743 writes to 1,319
  documents), so ordered execution is what preserves the sequential semantics
  exactly — same application order, and a failure still skips the remainder.
  Two seeders were also creating their upsert-filter index _after_ the loop that
  filtered on it, which made every upsert a collection scan; latency had been
  hiding that, and batching would have exposed it, so the indexes now come
  first. A full admin reset goes **16,177 commands / 273.2s → 6,853 / 126.6s**,
  and a bootstrap **15,656 / 270.0s → 6,420 / 116.8s**, with all twelve affected
  collections landing on byte-identical document counts.
- **46% of the stock exchange was priced at 1:1 with the anchor.** "Has a
  currency code" and "has an exchange-rate row" are different sets:
  `COUNTRY_CURRENCY_MAP` assigns a currency to 28 countries, `FOREX_ACTIVE_COUNTRIES`
  lists 18. The six Warsaw-Pact members in that gap are deliberately budget-only
  — `seedExchangeRates` is _meant_ to skip them — but their corporations carry a
  real `liquidCurrencyCode`, so every FX helper took the convert path, found no
  rate document, and returned 1.0. Measured on a seeded 1953 world: **62 of the
  exchange's 136 listings were bloc-currency, and every one had
  `totalRevenueAnchor` exactly equal to `totalRevenue`** — converted at 1.0 —
  while the controls came out right (GBP 0.357, DDM 4.2, SUR 9.0). A
  Czechoslovak corporation's 25,714,260 Kčs of revenue read as ₳25,714,260
  instead of ₳952,380, a 27× overstatement, and it ranked against every other
  corp on the exchange at that inflated figure. The authored rates already
  existed for exactly this era; nothing consulted them.
  There are now two FX maps and the difference is load-bearing:
  `loadValuationFxRates` back-fills a missing currency from the authored table,
  for anything that displays or ranks a value; `loadFxRatesByCurrency` leaves it
  missing on purpose, so settlement paths such as share-listing refunds keep
  failing closed rather than moving money at a turn-0 anchor. The seed
  diagnostic's forex check, which iterated only the forex-active countries and
  so was structurally blind to all of this, now covers the others too.
- **"Reset Only" no longer leaves a world with no currency system.**
  `resetGameWorld` deletes `exchangeRates` and `centralBanks`, and `seedForex` is
  their only re-seeder — but it sat _below_ `bootstrapGameWorld`'s `seedOnly`
  early return. Both the "Reset Only" and "Delete All Data" buttons post without
  `bootstrap: true`, so both took that path and left a world with no exchange
  rates, no central banks and no money-supply baselines, recoverable only by
  pressing a different button. (`tradeHistory` and `currencyOrders` lost their
  indexes there too, since `createForexIndexes` is called from `seedForex`.) The
  old comment said it had to follow `initializeGameState`; only the closing
  feature-flag write touches gameState, and every path into bootstrap already has
  that document. Now measured after a seed-only reset: 18 exchange rates, 18
  central banks, 24 money-supply snapshots.
- **The turn-1 stock exchange no longer prices most of the world at a 1:1 rate.**
  `generateStockExchangeSnapshots` ran _before_ `seedForex`, and it converts each
  corporation through `getCorpFxRate`, which returns 1.0 when no rate document
  exists. Only Nigeria had one at that point (written early by `seedNgBudgets`),
  so the opening snapshot mixed converted and unconverted values — a JPY-
  denominated corp reading ~360× its real anchor. It did not self-heal on any
  useful timescale either: `/api/stock-exchange` serves that document directly,
  and both rebuild paths (the turn and the 15-minute cron) skip while paused —
  which a freshly reset world is, sealed in maintenance mode, exactly when an
  admin is checking the market page. Moving `seedForex` above the seed-only
  return fixed both orderings at once.
- **`POST /api/seed?reset=true` no longer deletes 23 countries.** `runSeed` is
  the United States pack — every other country is seeded by its own module, each
  of which already scopes its own reset with `deleteMany({ countryId })`. But
  `runSeed`'s reset branch dropped 21 collections outright and re-seeded only the
  US, and the two standalone callers (`POST /api/seed?reset=true`, guarded by
  SEED_SECRET, and `scripts/seed.ts --reset`) never reach the orchestrator that
  would rebuild the rest. Measured at **1,995 documents lost, `states` 24
  countries → 1**, with `statePartyOrg` 591 → 100 and `politicalParties` 71 → 2 —
  and the region-keyed collections surviving at full count, so `unownedSectors`
  3,842 and `congressionalDistricts` 435 were left pointing at a 51-row `states`.
  Every drop is now scoped, and two things `runSeed` cannot rebuild are no longer

## Authentication & identity

- **Shared identity broker.** A standalone service now centralizes game-session
  verification and adds Discord OAuth2 as a second identity provider, replacing
  three independent implementations that had drifted apart. In-memory
  single-use handoff codes and OAuth state (60s TTL, atomic redemption); fails
  closed when unconfigured.
- **Hardened JWT verification.** The shared game-JWT verifier pins
  `alg: "HS256"` explicitly before checking the signature, closing an
  algorithm-confusion class of vulnerability. Signature comparisons are
  timing-safe throughout, and `nbf` is honored alongside `exp`.
- **Identity is not authorization.** The broker returns only a normalized
  identity shape and never resolves staff roles, bans, or entitlements; those
  are resolved downstream, so identity proof alone can never grant staff
  access.
- **Discord sign-in.** The account portal now offers "Sign in with Discord"
  alongside the existing game handoff. Discord identities are never silently
  merged into an existing account by email match, avoiding an account-takeover
  class of bug.
- **Return-URL allowlist hardening.** Cross-origin handoff targets are
  validated against an explicit host allowlist (exact or wildcard-scoped),
  rejecting credentialed URLs, non-default ports, and subdomain-suffix
  confusion.

## Alt detection — behavior tracking & confidence metrics (forensics-v2 Wave 3)

- **Two behavior-only signals.** Every existing signal correlates an identity
  token (fingerprint, cookie, IP, OAuth id, email) and is therefore defeatable
  by an operator willing to use a fresh browser, a fresh device, and a VPN.
  `session_handoff` and `activity_rhythm` correlate _when a human is at the
  keyboard_ instead, and keep working against a clean operator.
  `session_handoff` (0.45) fires on sessions that alternate tightly — one
  account stops, the other starts within ten minutes — across 4+ transitions
  with effectively zero concurrent online time; concurrency refutes it
  outright, which is what separates "one operator alternating logins" from
  "two friends who play together". `activity_rhythm` (0.25, deliberately weak)
  compares hour-of-day and day-of-week histograms, but only for accounts with
  25+ events whose activity is _concentrated_ — a player spread across the
  whole day matches everyone and is excluded by an entropy guard. New pure
  module `src/lib/altDetection/behavior.ts`; profiles are memoized per facet
  so the pairwise sweep stays O(candidates × events), not O(pairs × events).
- **The two unimplemented behavioral kinds now have data behind them.**
  `AltBehavioralObservation` declared `common_attack_victim` and
  `ap_dump_overlap` but only `party_bloc_switch` was ever populated.
  `ap_dump_overlap` now reads `actionLogs.targetState` (both accounts pouring
  action points into the same states); `common_attack_victim` reads
  `actionAuditLog` counterparties, scoped to contested categories only
  (governance/election/party/corp — a shared _market_ counterparty is just
  "both traded the same stock"). Both require an absolute floor of 3 shared
  targets _and_ 30% Jaccard overlap, so neither a small-sample coincidence
  nor two high-volume accounts brushing past each other qualifies.
- **A denser activity timeline.** Facets now carry `activityTimestamps`
  assembled from logins **plus** `actionLogs` and audited API calls. The
  login log alone was too sparse for session/rhythm analysis: an account that
  stays logged in for a week yields one login and hundreds of actions.
- **Confidence calibration** (`src/lib/altDetection/calibration.ts`, `GET
/api/admin/alts/calibration`). The tuning report already asked "which
  signals discriminate?"; nothing asked the prior question about the number
  the whole system rests on — _when we say a ring is 80% likely, is it?_
  The new report bins dispositioned rings by predicted confidence and plots
  predicted against observed confirm rate, and reports a Brier score, a
  **skill score** against the base-rate prior (at or below zero means the
  confidence is not beating a naive guess), expected/maximum calibration
  error, signed bias (over- vs. under-confident), and per-signal precision
  and lift. A precision/recall/F1 sweep says whether the 0.6 auto-open gate
  is set in the right place; ties break toward the _currently configured_
  threshold, so an already-optimal setting is never reported as something to
  change.
- **Longitudinal link tracking.** `altLinks` rows now carry
  `firstDetectedAt`, `peakConfidence`, `previousConfidence`,
  `observationCount`, and `escalatedAt`. Each hourly run previously
  overwrote `confidence` outright, making a link that climbed 0.20 → 0.95
  overnight indistinguishable from one that had sat at 0.95 for a month. All
  five fields are optional; pre-existing links simply don't carry them.
- **Run telemetry** (`altScoringRuns`, `GET /api/admin/alts/metrics`).
  `runAltScoring` swallows every throw so it can never break the cron — which
  also made its failures silent: a run scoring zero links because an upstream
  field stopped being populated looked exactly like a quiet hour. Each pass
  now leaves a record (candidate count and whether the pool truncated,
  confidence histogram, mean/p95, per-signal fired-vs-guarded-to-zero counts,
  escalations, new links, duration, error), TTL'd at 90 days. The metrics
  report compares recent runs against a prior window and warns explicitly
  when a signal that used to fire has **gone silent** — the specific failure
  this exists to catch.
- **Admin "Detection health" view.** New panel behind a toggle on the Alts
  tab (admin-only, off by default — triage remains the primary flow) showing
  the reliability plot, the headline calibration figures, the threshold
  sweep with the current setting marked, signal precision, and pipeline
  health including the confidence histogram and declining-signal trends.
- Both new endpoints are `requireAdmin` and read-only end to end; they never
  write `gameConfig` or `altClusters`. Report-only doctrine is unchanged
  throughout — nothing here auto-bans, auto-throttles, or auto-tunes, and
  every recommendation is applied by hand via `PUT /api/admin/alts/config`.

## Corporate M&A and index-fund payout correctness

- **Take a corporation private past an index-fund holdout (#71, PR #3452).** When
  the only non-CEO shareholders are index funds there are no eligible voters, so
  the privatization buyout vote was structurally unwinnable (it failed after 24
  turns and set a 96-turn cooldown). Now, when the only holdouts are index funds
  and there is no public float, the funds are bought out from the corporate
  treasury at fair market value and the corp goes private directly. Reuses the
  tested `sellFundHoldingShares` path, so no new cross-currency money math.
- **Pay index-fund shareholders on take-private and wind-down (#3450 PR #3454,
  #3451 PR #3464).** `allocateShareholderPool` counted fund shares in the
  denominator but emitted no fund row, so a fund's pro-rata slice was drained
  from the payer and distributed to no one, a conservation leak. Fund holders
  are now paid their ₳ slice (`cashAnchor`) and their stale holding dropped,
  across the passing-vote take-private buyout, voluntary dissolution, and
  whole-corp nationalization payouts. Shared helper `payFundShareholderRows`.
- **Agreed corp-to-corp acquisitions, behind `corpDealsEnabled` (PR #3470,
  default OFF).** A CEO can offer to buy another corporation outright from a new
  CEO-only "Deals" tab (name-search target picker + accept / reject / withdraw).
  On accept the target folds into the acquirer: sectors re-parent haircut-free
  and currency-re-denominated (`moveSectorToCorp`), the target's cash relocates,
  shareholders are cashed out via the fund-correct `payShareholders`, and the
  shell is deleted. Contained blast radius: the live hostile-takeover path is
  untouched, and the whole subsystem is inert until an admin enables the flag. v1
  blocks bonded targets and targets holding cross-corp equity.

## NPP autonomy v3/v4 — a smarter, less predictable brain (and rails so it isn't annoying)

The v3/v4 tiers had full agency but a thin brain. `decideNppAction`
(`src/lib/npp/actionAi.ts`) chose from four actions using a fixed weight table,
three personality thresholds and a 2×2 archetype bucket — with no knowledge of
the world. This pass gives it context, real per-NPP variation, and a hard
restraint layer for everything a player has to absorb.

- **v4 correctness (three latent bugs).** `NPP_AUTONOMY_LEVEL_RANK` +
  `nppAutonomyLevelAtLeast` are now exported from `nppAutonomy/featureFlag.ts`
  and `nppActionProcessing`'s private duplicate is gone. Two call sites gated a
  v3 feature with strict equality — `billSponsorship.ts` (archetype-scaled
  sponsor cooldown) and `savingsInterestTurn.ts` (NPP savings interest, an
  entire economic subsystem) — so raising the world to **v4 silently switched
  them off**. Both now compare by rank, and a source-scanning regression test
  (`__tests__/autonomyLevelRank.test.ts`) fails the build if the pattern
  reappears. Bill sponsorship also honours the ordinary per-country gate at v4
  instead of the legacy non-player-only rail, so "full agency, globally" is
  actually global.
- **Context-aware scoring.** New `npp/actionSignals.ts` assembles election
  proximity, candidacy/contest status, government status and party-treasury
  health in three fixed queries before the NPP cursor opens — never a round trip
  per NPP. The brain now damps saturated actions to zero, which ends NPPs
  campaigning at influence 100 (hard-capped) and advertising at favorability 100,
  both of which burned AP and funds on literal no-ops every cycle. Election
  proximity ramps campaigning up and war-chest building down; contested seats
  raise urgency; a poor party attracts more donations. **v0–v2 remain
  byte-identical**, covered by a table-driven compatibility test.
- **Unpredictability that isn't noise.** `careerArchetypeModifiersContinuous`
  bilinearly blends the four archetype rows over (ambition, stubbornness), so
  ambition 51 and ambition 99 stop being the same politician. `v3/nppIdiosyncrasy.ts`
  derives stable ±10% per-NPP quirks from a hash of `sequentialId` folded with a
  ~200-turn career phase — persistence with no collection, no migration and no
  write amplification, and slow drift that reads as a career arc. Action
  selection is now temperature-weighted (stubborn NPPs decisive, flexible ones
  erratic) with intra-cycle commitment to the previous action. All of it
  reshapes weights rather than drawing extra randomness: **RNG consumption is
  unchanged call-for-call**, so replay safety holds, and a test asserts it.
- **Smarter economic sweeps.** Bond selection ranks by current yield and samples
  the top few instead of always taking the highest coupon; there is now a yield
  floor below which an NPP simply holds cash. Stock buying uses the
  already-projected-but-unused `fundamentalSharePrice` as a value signal and
  samples across the top corps by size, replacing "every NPP in the country buys
  the single largest corp". Selling responds to price rather than a flat 3% coin
  flip (take profits, hold when underwater). Party-org building samples its
  organiser instead of always picking the lowest `_id`, and its per-row
  `npps.find()` loop collapses to one grouped query. `saveInvestAppetiteMult` —
  defined, clamped, tested and read by nothing — now sizes allocations.
- **Player-impact rails (`nppAutonomy/playerImpactBudget.ts`).** A single
  reviewable place for how much autonomous behaviour a human absorbs. Bill
  sponsorship in a player country is capped at 1 active bill with a 36-turn
  cooldown (tighter than the 2/12 NPP-only pair). Primary challengers are capped
  per country per pass, and a **player candidate can no longer be challenged
  repeatedly** — previously the pool filter compared NPP ids against a player
  candidate's absent `nppId`, excluded nothing, and would have produced
  unbounded challenges the moment v4 let the pass into a player country.
  Corporate attacks gain a defender-side cooldown (`lastAutoAttackedTurn`) and a
  same-pass guard, so several NPP corps can no longer dogpile one player — the
  existing cooldown was per-attacker only. Bond and share sweeps leave a 25%
  float reserve untouched so players are never quietly bought out of a market,
  and attack notifications are budgeted per player per window (the wire headline

- **Sim harness.** `forceFullAutonomy(db, level)` and `runWorld.ts --autonomy=v4`
  — the harness hardcoded v3, so the one thing v4 changes could not be simulated

## Campaign-strength repricing, congress leadership & staff tooling

- **Presidential campaign strength is priced per point bought, not per click
  (#3838).** The old cost was `sqrt(currentCS + added)` in campaign funds plus a
  flat 10 actions — both charged per click and blind to the quantity bought.
  Because a click yields `nationalInfluence * 0.75`, the price per POINT of CS
  scaled inversely with influence: saturating the bonus cost ~12,900 CF / 500
  actions at 4,000 NPI but ~516,000 CF / 20,000 actions at 100 NPI, for an
  identical result. The marginal price is now
  `PRICE_PER_POINT * (1 + (CS / TAU)^2)` with `PRICE_PER_POINT = 83`, and a
  purchase is charged the EXACT integral across `[current, current + added]`
  rather than a midpoint sample — for a quadratic the midpoint rule undercharges
  by `added^3 / (12 * TAU^2)`, an error proportional to step size that would have
  quietly restored the large-click discount. Totals are now identical to
  floating-point precision across 3,000 / 750 / 75-point steps. Calibrated
  against the campaign economy (L10 fundraising $5M/turn, L10 upgrade $10M, full

- **Armed forces for 18 more countries.** RU, DD, PL, CS, HU, RO, BG, YU, NG,

  random `3 + rand(3)` units per branch for those countries. Composition only —
  names, veterancy, readiness, posture, XP and equipment stay RNG-derived, so

## Buildable sectors — the `plants` market tier (PR #3969, flag off)

Ships behind a sixth market tier, `plants`, with `live: false`. No world runs
it; production stays on `capital`. Origin: OGBuildz in #gameplay-advisors
(Jul 29-31) — "I don't understand the point of nameplate revenue if realized
revenue is calculated completely independently." He was right, and the
arithmetic genuinely did not close: the sectors table printed nameplate while
profit was computed from realized, so revenue × margin ≠ profit on screen.

Design report (rev 3, public):
(internal report) inversion.** Under `plants`, capacity is authoritative and revenue is
derived. `capitalStock` (units/day) is bought with cash, depreciates, and gates
production; `producedUnits = capacity × constraints`; clearing offers those real
units; `revenue = Σ soldUnits × clearedPrice`. Nameplate compounding
(`sectorTurn.ts` revenue anchor) stops. The flip is a per-sector no-op by
construction: `capacity := max(capitalStock, impliedOutputUnits(revenue))` and
the derived revenue equals the pre-flip realized revenue exactly, pinned to 1e-6
across ten adversarial states (strike, embargo, depleted deposit, active crisis,
negative margin, mid-strategy-transition, governor mid-ramp).

- **Build primitive.** `buildCapacity` (build / cancel / mothball / reactivate)
  with a build queue, `CAPACITY_BUILD_TURNS` lead time, and capitalized
  construction-in-progress — cash out, CIP asset in, never through
  `earningsHistory` (a lumpy build annualized ×48 would have nuked share price
  and zeroed dividends). CIP enters tangible book, credit equity, listing
  valuations and transfers, with its own shadow-ledger capex legs.
- **Growth retired.** The slider, `targetGrowthRate`/`currentGrowthCost`, the
  region `adjust-growth` route and the bulk growth path all go. Dominance and
  prime-rate multipliers move onto the build price (antitrust priced at decision
  time; the CB keeps its lever over investment). Accrued in-flight ramps convert
  to build credit at the era price rather than being confiscated.
- **Unowned sectors inverted.** They supplied zero commodities
  (`commodityPriceTurn.ts`: "Only OWNED sectors count") and paid no tax; they
  were phantom revenue doing three load-bearing jobs. Now `headroomUnits` —
  unmet demand in units, tracking state GDP — feeding a capacity-based
  market-share denominator. Fixes two conservation bugs: NPP expansion minted
  sector revenue without decrementing the pool, and `expandSector` created
  supply from nothing while the pool stayed full.
- **Physical P&L.** `costs = revenue × (1 − margin)` is replaced by real lines:
  inputs at actual lagged prices, labor, upkeep including idle plant, compliance,
  and a calibrated `otherOpexPerUnitAnchor` residual. Commodity input modifiers
  become the real input cost; commodity _surplus_ modifiers are deleted outright
  (clearing already prices a glut on the revenue side — the original
  double-count). Margin becomes a derived output. Calibration is exact by
  construction, so the first physical-P&L turn is byte-identical.
- **Crises gain physicality.** Six templates (blackout, input shortage, strike
  disruption, bridge collapse, port closure, industrial accident) now cut
  tonnage; twelve stay financial. Effects are snapshotted at spawn, so crises
  already live in a world keep legacy behavior.
- **Extraction has one capacity system.** Geological capacity becomes a hard
  min on produced units (no 0.5 floor, no per-plant 240-turn grace) and deposits
  deplete against a derived reserve.
- **GDP impulse** switches from the vestigial growth-rate average to the
  region's annualized realized-revenue delta, snapshotted on the state doc in
  every mode so a live flip has a baseline. Build spend routes into the existing
  `corpGrowthInvestmentAnchor`, so plant construction raises national potential
  growth with no new wiring.

**Review record.** An independent branch audit ran between every wave and caught
a P0 in each: realized revenue being persisted back into `revenue` (compounding
every realization leg, −94% over 50 turns, dragging world commodity supply with
it); the launch-guard kill switch silently disarmed by the new tier's mode
enumeration; capex ledger legs built but never wired; a composed flip-identity
break invisible to every per-agent test; a 327× capacity re-pricing across a
strategy retool; an active crisis on flip day worth 8% of revenue in free
profit. A four-round loop that stopped converging was resolved by naming the
root cause — control flow keyed on `revenue` where the authoritative quantity is
capacity — and running one systematic sweep (95 sites triaged, 11 fixed). The
recurring defect shape was a fix applied to one member of a duplicated-formula
pair; that is now closed structurally by shared helpers rather than by more
audit rounds.

A final hostile review by a fresh agent with no context, briefed to assume
everything was wrong, returned **do not merge** and four blockers that six
friendly rounds had missed. All fixed here:

- **Exit money-printer.** Book value priced capacity at list while builds were
  charged at founding/acumen/tech/host discounts, so found-then-restructure
  returned 8.5× the spend, credited rather than paid by a counterparty. Book now
  carries the cash actually paid (`capacityBookAnchor`), maintained across
  build-landing, depreciation, flip seeding, attacks, sheds, merges, carves,
  nationalization haircuts and directed credit. Free R&D capacity dilutes the
  basis rather than minting book. No-mint invariant pinned across all seven
  acquisition paths.
- **The turn erased charged builds.** The processor `$set` the whole
  `buildQueue` from a pre-turn snapshot, so an order placed mid-turn was billed
  and deleted. It now writes only the delta it owns.
- **Contract damages were an uncapped wire.** Free instant cancellation made the
  shortfall penalty opt-out, and the damages `$inc` had no cap or solvency
  floor. Now: four-turn cancellation notice with settlement still applying,
  damages capped at 0.25 of contract notional, payer floored at available cash.
- **Intel leaks.** The new physical fields bypassed private-corp redaction
  entirely (including a corp-level aggregate the review did not name), and the
  public fog was invertible three ways — produced/sold shared one factor so the
  ratio was the exact fill rate, the raw build-queue summary sat next to its own
  fogged value, and `fogFactor` was served outright.

Also fixed from that pass: a fully halted plant earned full baseline revenue at
zero cost (~6× profit pump — the governor's `<= 0 → baseline` substitution was
written for factors and misapplied to amounts), and the ±15% governor cap never
released, which made the physical economy cosmetic beyond that band. The cap now
widens with the ramp: identical at λ=0, exactly as tight at λ=0.5, inert once
fully ramped.

What the hostile pass **could not** break, having tried: long-run stability over
120+ turns across strategies and through a live flip, dimensional consistency,
money and capacity conservation, and the flip identity itself.

## Ticket 974 follow-ups: signup navigation, Volkskammer elections, cabinet identity, mobile (PR #3975)

## East Germany could not declare an offensive (bloc truth from live membership)

A player reported that the Central Front's Command panel refused every target

**Root cause.** `blocOf` in `src/lib/military/theaters.ts` resolved a country's

entry, which is not a `CountryId` at all.

Eighteen of twenty-seven countries were missing, so DD, PL, CS, HU, BG, RO, YU,

## Ticket 0974 follow-ups: modernisation cost, dead terrain, general corps roster

Three more items off the same East German ticket as the bloc fix above. A fourth
— "how do I promote generals?" — needed no code: promotion is automatic on
battle XP and the profile page already explains it. Those generals were stuck at
Brigadier because the bloc bug blocked every offensive, and offensives are the
only XP source in the game.

**Modernising was free, and paid twice.** "I don't think upkeep went up when I
clicked the modernize and cutting edge button" — it could not.
`computeEffectiveUpkeep` took `upkeepBase`, posture, country scale and the
force-tier setting; `techTier` was not an input. `upkeepBase` is written only at
recruit and seed and never mutated, and the upgrade route only `$set` the new
tier with no treasury charge. Meanwhile a tier is worth +8% power via
`techPowerMult`, plus combat-card stats, plus `modernSig` feeding the
`defense.defenseIndustry` political family. Seeded forces carry a random tier
0–3, so a fresh world could be taken to Cutting-Edge across the board for
nothing but ministerial actions.

Now it costs twice, mirroring recruitment:

- `unitUpgradePrice` charges the treasury through the same GDP-share model as
  `unitPurchasePrice` — same divisor, same country cost scale — so building and
  modernising are quoted in one unit and can be compared. `UPGRADE_COST_SHARE`
  escalates by target tier (0.25 / 0.35 / 0.50), making Cutting-Edge the
  expensive half of a formation's life; all three steps come to 1.10× a fresh
  build. Soft spend: overspending is debt, not a refusal. A null GDP refuses
  rather than pricing at zero.
- `TECH_UPKEEP_MULT` [0.85, 1.0, 1.2, 1.45] enters `computeEffectiveUpkeep`.
  Deliberately steeper than the power curve: Legacy → Cutting-Edge is ×1.30
  power for ×1.71 upkeep, so a modern division is better than an old one and
  worse value than two of them. Cheap legacy kit stays viable for mass.

The unit card quotes both the price and the upkeep it will move to before the
click, computed with the same pure helper the server charges with.

**This is retroactive.** Upkeep is derived on read, so every existing unit
reprices on deploy — national upkeep rises ~12% on average, unevenly by country.
Intended: a Cutting-Edge division should always have cost more to run. Note
upkeep is not a cash drain — it sets `gap` against the defense envelope
(budgetBalance) and `spendSig` into defenseIndustry, so it is partly
self-offsetting. The up-front price is the hard bite.

Re-keyed by terrain FAMILY, matched off the front's own `terrain` string with the

## Advisor report: a founded sector priced its output at $0 forever (plants tier)

A sandbox advisor posted a Pennsylvania sector reading **3,772 offered / 3,772
sold / 100% sold / $0 per unit / $0 revenue / $0 every cost line / $0 profit**. A
live sector, physically producing and clearing its whole book, with no economics
attached to it at all.

**Root cause: `plantsMixPrice` resolved 0/0 to 0, and revenue 0 was absorbing.**
`sectorTurn.ts` priced a unit of output as

    plantsMixPrice = preFlipNameplateRevenue / nameplateUnits

and `nameplateUnits` is `impliedOutputUnits(revenue, rates) = revenue × Σ(rate/base)`.
So the numerator and denominator carry the SAME revenue factor, which cancels
the price is scale-free, exactly as the comment beside it already claimed
("`plantsMixPrice` is 1/Σ(rate/base), independent of the revenue it was measured
from"). But written as a ratio it is 0/0 at revenue 0, and the code took the
zero branch.

That made `revenue === 0` a state nothing could leave, and founding a sector
under plants walks directly into it:

1. `expandSector` creates the sector with `capitalStock: 0` and the founding
   build in `buildQueue`, correct, it owns nothing yet.
2. First turn: the nameplate is restated from owned capacity,
   `plantsNameplateRevenue = plantsCapacity × mixPrice = 0 × mixPrice = 0`, and
   that is persisted to `sector.revenue`.
3. Second turn reads `revenue: 0` back, so `nameplateUnits` is 0, so `mixPrice`
   is 0, so the restatement falls through to `newRevenue`, which is also 0.
   Locked.
4. The starter build lands. Capacity is real, `producedUnits` is real, and
   `plantsDerivedHourlyRevenue = producedUnits × 0`. $0 forever. Because
   maintenance and labour are derived off revenue, every cost line reads $0 too,
   which is why the panel showed a perfectly balanced ledger of nothing.

`plantsTransition.ts` already names this exact condition a hard NO-GO blocker
(`"no-priced-output-mix"`, "capacity NEVER binds output for this sector"), but
the preflight only guards the flip turn. Nothing guarded sectors founded _after_
the flip, which is every sector an advisor has founded since the sandbox went to
plants at turn 412.

**Fix.** Derive the price from the unit yield directly
`1 / unitYieldForSupply(strategy.supply)`, the function `capacityEconomy.ts`
already exports for this. It is identically equal to the old expression for every
sector carrying positive revenue, so the flip identity and every live sector are
byte-unchanged; it simply keeps returning a real price at revenue 0.

**No backfill needed.** Stuck sectors heal on their next turn on their own:
capacity is intact, so `capacity × mixPrice` restores the nameplate, and the
launch governor passes derived revenue through unclamped against a zero baseline
(the amount variant has no anchor to govern against). A test pins that.

**Why the existing tests missed it.** `sectorTurn.foundingNewborn.test.ts` ran
each turn from a _fresh_ newborn carrying its founding nameplate, so a persisted
`revenue: 0` was never fed back in, and the feedback loop is the entire bug. The
new cases run the sector forward through its own written document. All three fail
on the old formula.

## Ticket 0974 round 3: skill-point economy, head of state, mobile tabs, combat legibility

Six more reports off the same East German ticket. Three were real bugs.

**Doctrine node cost was enforced nowhere.** `TraitTreeNode.cost` is authored on
all 115 nodes and printed twice in the UI — the node header and the Train button
— but `trainNode` checked `pts >= 1` and deducted a flat `1`, and the client's

capstone came at the same price as a 1-point basic, and a general holding a
single point could take anything in the tree. `ag4 "Risk-Taking Breakthroughs"`
is cost 3, which is exactly what the reporter trained with one point. Both layers
now charge `node.cost`, and the refusal names the shortfall.

**Which made the second complaint worse, so the economy moved with it.** A
career was 4 starting + 1 per promotion × 4 = 8 points — 8 nodes while cost was
ignored, and under 4 once it was not. Modelling the horizon that was asked about
(2400 turns ≈ 100 real days) showed tenure at a point per game year would supply
71% of a career by itself, and uncapped at a faster rate would finish the whole
tree without a battle.

**The chain of command is visible now.** Three questions arrived from one player in
one sitting — "where can I see if I am part of a battle as a general, not SoD?",
"where do I assign more troops to the battlefield as SoD?", "is it user error when I
made my division a logistics command?" — and they are one gap, not three. Authority
over a war is split across three seats and no page said so:

1. The DEFENSE HOLDER builds force structure: commands, the units in them, and each
   command's Commanding General.
2. The COMMANDING GENERAL employs it: posts the generals under them to Conflicts and
   names one Theater Commander.
3. UNITS FOLLOW THEIR GENERAL (`theaterOfUnit`). Nobody moves units to a front.
4. The THEATER COMMANDER — or the defense holder while none is designated — declares
   offensives.

Step 3 is why "where do I assign more troops to the battlefield" has no answer: there
is no button, and there should not be one. It needed a sentence.

`resolveCommandChain` is pure and decides what to say from the flags the conflict page
already resolved (plus one new query: whether the viewer leads a Command). It returns
the seat held, what it may do, and for everything it may not, WHO does it and a link
there. A "YOUR ROLE HERE" panel renders it above COMMAND — the panel that says whether
you may give orders now precedes the one that takes them. The SoD Commands tab carries
the same rule at the point the chain starts.

Precedence is most-specific-seat-wins, so a defense holder who is also posted here
reads as the general at the front rather than the minister at a desk. A viewer with no
character gets no panel — a logged-out reader of the public record does not need to be
told they hold no seat.

⚠️ The panel MIRRORS `canActAtTheater` rather than sharing it; the routes still enforce
authority server-side. If that rule moves, both must move.

**"Clicking the third command shows both clicked and shows the first one."** Not a
rendering bug — colliding ids, and it was corrupting saved data.

`createCommand` built ids from a module-level counter that reset to 0 on every page
load, and ignored the `_existing` parameter it was handed (the underscore was the
tell). So a player who created commands in one session got `cmd1`/`cmd2`, and the
first command created after a reload got `cmd1` again. Everything keys on id:

- `CommandRoster` marks `selected = c.id === selectedId`, so BOTH rows highlight.
- `commands.find((c) => c.id === selectedId)` returns the older one, so the detail
  panel opens the wrong command.
- Every `commandId` action — `ASSIGN_UNIT`, `TOGGLE_REGION`, `SET_POSTURE`,
  `ADD_COMMANDER`, `STAND_DOWN` — is a `.map` over `c.id === action.commandId`, so it
  edited BOTH commands at once. `useDebouncedSave` then persisted that.

Three parts to the fix, because the first alone would leave existing saves broken:
`nextCommandId` derives the id from the commands already held (deterministic without a
global); `dedupeCommandIds` re-keys collisions at seed time, keeping the FIRST holder
so the older command with units attached stays put; and the PUT schema refuses a body
with duplicate ids so the shape cannot reach the database again. Nothing outside this
state references a command id — `requireCommandingGeneral` resolves by
`commandingGeneralId` — so re-keying is safe.

⚠️ The existing "assigns unique ids to successive commands" test passed `[]` as
`existing` to BOTH calls and still passed, because the counter was global. It asserted
uniqueness while proving nothing about the case that broke. Rewritten to thread the
first command into the second call.

**"This should show who won the battle, not just 'resolved T#'."** RECENT OFFENSIVES
on the conflict page listed `T446 · DD → US … resolved T448` — which records that the
turn processor reached the declaration and nothing about what it did. The row now
names the outcome (`Decisive Victory · T448`), coloured from the VIEWER's side rather
than the declarer's, because `listDeclarationHistory` is not filtered by country and
both sides' offensives appear in the same list.

The walkover is why this mattered most. `buildRecordExtras` builds the engagement list
from `reports.filter((r) => r.result)`, and `result` is null on a no-contact report —
so a player who took ground against an undefended front saw ENGAGEMENTS say "the front
has not been contested" beside three offensives that said only "resolved". Taking
ground unopposed is a real outcome; `declarationOutcome` reports it as one, and
distinguishes it from a no-contact that moved nothing, which stays uncoloured because
it settled nothing.

Declarations join their report on `(turn, declarer, target)`. A merged offensive writes
ONE report under its principal, so an ally who joined someone else's push matches
nothing and falls back to "resolved" rather than borrowing a result that is not theirs.
`declarationOutcome` takes the full `BattleDeclarationStatus` union rather than the
resolved/fizzled pair the history query happens to return, so widening that query later
cannot silently relabel a pending offensive as resolved.

**"Three successful offensives, no XP."** The same reporter commanded a theater
through three wins and their general gained nothing. Not a crediting bug in the
strict sense — `applyOutcome` credited only generals with units ASSIGNED TO THEM
at that front, so directing a battle was worth exactly zero by design. That is
indistinguishable from broken progression from the player's side, and it made the
Theater Commander billet worth nothing to the person holding it.

A Theater Commander now earns `THEATER_COMMAND.xpShare` (0.5) of what a general
leading ONE AVERAGE FORMATION at that front earned, for every battle in their
theater, whether or not they led any units in it. If they also led units they are
paid for both roles — they did both jobs.

The measure matters more than the fraction:

- Not the front's TOTAL, which would let a superpower's commander level many times
  faster than a small nation's for identical work.
- Not the MEAN OVER GENERALS PRESENT either, which was the first attempt and is
  gameable: it moves when you reshuffle which general holds which unit, so command
  structure could be rearranged purely to farm XP. A test pins the invariant —
  four formations under one general and under four generals pay the commander the
  same.

Per-formation average reads only the fighting. In a coalition the billet can belong
to an ally, whose general is absent from this contingent's `assignments`; they earn
through their own nation's contingent, and `theaterCommanderOf` returning null here
is correct rather than a miss. ⚠️ A TC whose nation contributed no units to the
battle still earns nothing — their contingent never resolves.

The promotion help now states the rule, since the reporter had no way to tell a
design from a defect.

**Both devs fixed the enforcement bug independently, and the resolution took the
other branch's pricing.** `fbc03e3c1` flattened every node to 1 point via the
`N()` factory — authored costs remain in the source as arguments, so only the
factory keeps them uniform, and a test now guards that. Flat pricing is the
better model: specialisation is bounded by how many points a career yields, not
by which nodes happen to be expensive, and no node can be priced out of reach of
a general who saved for it. The economy was rebalanced around cost 1, which
makes points and nodes the same currency and the whole budget legible:

- `POINTS_PER_PROMOTION` 1 → 4. Level caps at 5, so this is paid exactly four
  times: 16 points.
- Tenure accrual: 1 point per 24 turns — a real day — capped at 20 over a career,
  reached around turn 480. Battles were previously the ONLY source, so an officer
  in a nation at peace never developed at all.
- Post-Field-Marshal progression: one point per 200 XP earned past the rank-5
  ceiling, capped at 15. A maxed general otherwise had nothing left to earn from
  campaigning. The cap is the correction to the other branch's uncapped version —
  "should still have an upper limit on points to really force people to
  specialize", and an uncapped track hands a late-game commander the whole tree.
- `WIN_BONUS_XP` 30 → 45, `LOSS_BONUS_XP` 12 → 20. The per-unit share is
  `(16 + ratio×20) × natMods.xp`, so the flat bonus dominates for a nation
  fielding one or two formations and barely moves for a superpower leading a
  dozen — it speeds the ladder where it was slowest.

Since every node costs 1, the budget counts directly: 4 starting + 16 promotion +
20 tenure + 15 post-Field-Marshal = **55 of 115 nodes** at absolute most. A
peacetime general tops out at 24. Fighting is worth 31 nodes over waiting — 56%
of a maximal career — and past the tenure cap it is the only source left.

`accrueTenurePoints` pays every whole interval elapsed and advances its marker by
exactly those, so a stalled cron cannot swallow what it owed nor pay it twice on

the XP behind them, so the ceiling is expressible and re-running the award grants
nothing further. A profile with no marker starts its clock rather than being

## Corporation founding blocked in 1953 (era-blind request schema)

**Symptom.** Founding a corporation in a 1953 world failed for every wealth
tier with `startingCapital: Starting capital must be at least 1,000,000`,
while the modal correctly offered a $14,333–$716,667 range.

**Cause.** `foundCorporationSchema.startingCapital` validated against the
MODERN `MIN_CORPORATION_STARTING_CAPITAL` (1,000,000) /
`MAX_CORPORATION_STARTING_CAPITAL`. Zod runs before the route body, so the
request died at `parseJsonBody` and never reached the route's already-correct
`getEraFoundingBounds(worldPreset, ...)`. 1953 scale = 387e9/27e12 = 0.014333
→ era min 14,333, era max 716,667. The route computed those bounds but never
actually enforced them: the schema was the only guard, and it was the wrong
one.

**Fix.**

- Schema validates SHAPE only (`.int().positive().optional()`). Era-dependent
  magnitude is not knowable at that layer.
- Route enforces `eraBounds.min` / `eraBounds.max` after resolving the preset,
  quoting the era's own numbers in the error. This also closes the gap where
  no server-side bound existed at all once the schema stopped covering it.
- Modal passed no `preset` to `computeFoundingCosts`, so it diffed an era
  treasury against the modern baseline: "Extra treasury (personal) −985,667"
  (14,333 − 1,000,000). The TOTAL was accidentally right because the unscaled
  fee (1,000,000) cancelled the error exactly; only the line items were wrong.
  Now passes `worldPreset`. Post-success treasury reset also re-seeds from
  `eraBounds.baseline` instead of the modern constant.

**Why the existing tests passed.** `eraMoneyScale.test.ts` already asserted
"a high-income character can still found a corporation in 1953" — but only
across the CONSTANTS layer (`getEraFoundingBounds` + `computeFoundingCosts`).
Nothing exercised the request path, so the schema floor was invisible to it.
Same shape as the scoped-verification lesson: the math was right and the door
was locked. Added: schema-level era-min acceptance tests, a middle-income
affordability assertion across all four presets, and a regression asserting
`extraCapitalOverBaselineAnchor === 0` at the era baseline.

**Affordability after the fix** (1953, anchor ₳): min founding cost 14,333;
low 14,333 (exactly at the floor), middle 35,833, high 71,667. Middle and
high clear it in US/UK at all times, as required. Only 1953 is affected —
1979/1991 have no GDP entry, so their scale is exactly 1.

**KNOWN, NOT FIXED HERE — founding share price floors in 1953.**
`initialSharePrice = max(MIN_SHARE_PRICE 0.01, round(capital/10M × 100)/100)`.
With the era baseline 14,333 over a fixed `CEO_INITIAL_SHARES` of 10,000,000
the true price is 0.0014, so it floors at 0.01 and the corp opens at a
100,000 market cap against a 14,333 treasury (~7x). Modern is exact
(1,000,000/10M = 0.10). This bites any founding under 100,000, i.e. most of
the realistic 1953 range, and at founding-IPO the float is sold at the
inflated floor. Fixing it means era-scaling `CEO_INITIAL_SHARES` or allowing
sub-cent prices — both ripple through ownership %, IPO issuance and the
wealth list, so it is a balance call for the owner, not a silent change.

## Era-scaled share base: founding market cap now equals treasury in every era

Follow-up to the 1953 founding fix. Owner call: scale the share count down
rather than allow sub-cent prices.

**The defect.** `CEO_INITIAL_SHARES` (10,000,000) was era-blind while the
treasury backing it was not. A 1953 founding seeded ~14,333 over 10M shares =
$0.0014/share, which floored at `MIN_SHARE_PRICE` ($0.01) and opened the corp
at a ~$100,000 market cap on a $14,333 treasury (~7x). Modern is exact by
construction (1,000,000 / 10,000,000 = `DEFAULT_SHARE_PRICE` $0.10). At
founding-IPO the float was sold at the inflated floor, so subscribers overpaid
~7x — a real value-extraction path, not just a cosmetic price.

`sectorSeedEra.ts` (delegates to `getEraNominalAmount`, so ONE scale governs
money and share counts alike). 1953 → 143,333 shares; 14,333/143,333 = $0.10,
market cap == treasury. Exact no-op for 2019/2023/unknown/absent preset.

**Applied at every MINT site** (audited by grepping `CEO_INITIAL_SHARES`):

- `api/corporations/route.ts` — player founding (price, IPO base, founder
  holding, supershares, totalShares)
- `FoundCorporationModal.tsx` — preview must equal the charge
- `admin/spawnNppCorporation.ts` — NPP corps (already era-scaled its capital;
  the share base was the missing half)
- `nationalization/privatizationShares.ts` + `privatizeAsset.ts` — spin-out
  cap table (inflated float proceeds before)
- `corporations/subsidiaries/commands/spinOff.ts` — minted 10M at a FLAT
  `DEFAULT_SHARE_PRICE`, so a 1953 subsidiary was born at a modern-sized
  ~$1,000,000 cap
- `imf/seedImfInstitution.ts` (totalShares now `halfShares * 2` so the two
  holders sum exactly — an odd era count previously left a phantom share) and

**NOT applied to read fallbacks.** `corp.totalShares ?? CEO_INITIAL_SHARES` in

**Residual found and fixed in the same pass.** `SHARE_CONSOLIDATION_MIN_TOTAL_SHARES`

## Defence budgets become a real account

`spending.byCategory.defense` was a number nothing ever spent: it fed
`resolveDefenseEnvelope` as a comparison value, upkeep was never debited from
anything, and recruiting borrowed against the national treasury without limit.
It is now an account.

**The pot.** `federalBudget.defenseAppropriation { balance, accruedThroughTurn,
arrearsRatio }`, accruing `resolveDefenseLine / TURNS_PER_YEAR` each turn from
the _live_ enacted line — no fiscal-year gate, so a law that changes defence
spending bites on the next tick. **Not a second charge:** `processTreasuryTurn`
already deducts the whole annual line from `treasuryBalance` at 1/48 per turn,
so the pot is a sub-ledger of money already spent. The one thing that does hit
the treasury is the overdraft, which is spending _beyond_ the enacted line.

**Upkeep is charged, and denominated against the appropriation.** `upkeepBase`
is an abstract unit shared by every country (a British and an American division
both cost 70), so it cannot be compared to money directly. A GDP-keyed divisor
was tried and rejected: the defence-line-to-GDP ratio swings hugely by era (US
13.6% in 1953, ~3.4% in 2019), so one calibrated in 1953 puts the US at ~3.2x
arrears on the live 2019 preset while looking correct where it was measured.
Instead `upkeepShare = 0.55 x (liveRoster / seededRoster)` — a nation at its
historical starting force pays 55% of its appropriation; double the roster and
it pays 110%.

**The seeded-roster denominator is derived, not authored.**
`seedRosterUpkeepFor` measures `buildCountryRoster` directly (memoised per
preset). A checked-in table would go stale the moment an archetype's upkeep or
an authored order of battle changed, silently shifting every country's share.
`scripts/calibrate-defence-upkeep.ts` prints the same numbers for review.

**Overdraft, then arrears.** The pot may go one year's accrual negative, drawn
as national debt. Past that, `arrearsRatio` suppresses the readiness baseline
units drift toward (`ARREARS_READINESS_WEIGHT` 0.35) — a moved target, not a
decay counter, so it is fully reversible and can never destroy a unit. On a
one-hour turn clock an irreversible drain would punish sleeping.

**Procurement moves off the treasury.** Recruit and upgrade debit the pot via a
guarded atomic `$inc` (so a concurrent order 409s instead of overdrawing) and
refuse outright when short — no overdraft for new orders, which closes the
unlimited-borrow hole. `borrowingOutlook` is retired; there is no borrowing left
to report.

**Prices are anchored so growth pays off.** Price was `gdp x k` and accrual is
`line / 48`, so units-per-year reduced to `defenceBurden / k` — economic size
cancelled entirely, and doubling GDP doubled unit prices. Now priced against
`militaryPriceAnchor(gdp, baselineGdp) = baseline x (gdp/baseline)^0.5`, so the
defence line outruns prices and a growing economy buys a bigger army. Flat
pricing was rejected on hard grounds: GDP is stored in local currency and
cross-country comparison is invalid by guard-tested design, and six Eastern Bloc
countries have no exchange-rate document at all.

**Reach.** The sweep runs for every country with units, seat or not — following
`applyReinforcement`, not `applyMilitaryForceEffects` (which returns early with
no defence seat). A nation fed manpower but never charged for it would be a
standing asymmetry.

**Readiness drift moved to the sweep, for the same reason.** Review caught that
drift still lived inside `applyMilitaryForceEffects` and so inherited that step's
seat gate. The eleven seatless nations — France, Italy, Spain, Sweden, Turkey,
Brazil, Greece, Finland, Austria and the two devolved UK members — were charged
upkeep and booked an `arrearsRatio` that nothing ever collected on: an unfunded
French army of 22 units held full readiness indefinitely. `applyReadinessDrift`
now runs from the appropriation sweep, which also means it reads the ratio the
same step just computed instead of depending on two turn phases staying adjacent.

**The settlement is an `$inc`, not a `$set`.** Writing back the absolute closing
balance was a read-modify-write over money: a player recruiting between the
sweep's read and its write had their debit reverted, minting the unit for free.
The turn's net change is applied atomically instead, under a
`accruedThroughTurn: { $lt: turn }` filter that makes the whole write idempotent
at the database rather than only in the caller's pre-check.

**The defence envelope is retired; budget balance now means budget balance.** The Defense
seat carried a third budget-shaped number — a synthetic "discretionary envelope" of
`clamp(line x 3%, 18,000M, 50,000M)`. On the live board **26 of 27 countries sat exactly on
that floor**: the US 3% slice is $1.59B against an $18.0B floor, the UK's is $37M. So the
`governance.budgetBalance` metric it divided by was a constant, and the metric contained no
budget term at all — it moved only with roster size. Russia, whose defence line is sized for
its 49 units, read the WORST balance on the board (0.122) for the crime of having a large
army, while Japan's 8 units against a floor 2,400x its real slice read 0.919.

Both Defense metric consumers now read the **upkeep burden** — the share of a turn's defence
income the standing force consumes — which reduces to `0.55 x (liveRoster / seedRoster)`.
Both terms are the same country's own money, so it is comparable across nations and eras
without any shared constant. A force at its historical starting size reads +0.45 anywhere;
it takes ~1.82x the seeded roster to reach zero and 3.64x to bottom out. A country with no
usable defence line contributes nothing rather than reading as a free force.

⚠️ **This moves live metric values on every country**, in the direction of removing free
positive metric that a country-independent floor was handing out. `FORCE_EFFECT.budgetWeight`
is the dial if it bites.

**Every money-shaped number on the Defense masthead is now money.** The "Upkeep allowance"
tile is gone. "Force upkeep" was the abstract `upkeepBase` aggregate rendered through a
millions formatter — a 4,400-point index printed as "$4.4B" beside a $52.8B pot, implying
the force ate 8% of the account per turn when it actually ate 1.1%. It now shows the real
per-turn charge and its share of income. The Budget tab's branch and category breakdowns are
scaled onto the same real figure rather than left in index units.

**A contract is now an offer, not an order.** A minister awards it and it sits `pending`,
building and billing nothing, until the supplying CEO accepts. Accept and decline live on the
corporation's Defence tab, the CEO gets an inbox notification naming the buyer, the quantity,
the component and the rate, and a declined order is recorded distinctly from one the minister
withdrew — the order book shows who walked away. Both transitions are guarded on `pending`, so
a double-click, or an accept racing the minister's cancel, resolves to exactly one winner
rather than reviving a withdrawn order. Ministers can now withdraw an unanswered offer too,
not only a live one.

**Contracted plants no longer get paid twice for the same output.** A plant under contract had
been supplying its FULL production to the commodity market and collecting the contract price
on top, with nothing anywhere deducting the materiel it shipped — a money pump that scaled
with however many contracts a friendly minister wrote. Output sold to the state does not also
reach the market: a delivery stamps the plant with the share of its output that left, and that
share comes off both the goods (its supply contribution) and the cash (a realization leg on
`hourlyRevenue`). Military output is keyed to the nameplate rather than realized revenue,
because otherwise a fully-contracted plant would oscillate — deliver everything, earn nothing,
therefore produce nothing, therefore deliver nothing, therefore earn again.

**Procurement got its two missing surfaces.** The award endpoint had no UI consumer at
all: a minister could see an empty arsenal, an empty contract list, and the message
"without one, the arsenal never fills" — with no way to award anything. The Arsenal tab now
carries the award form, fed by a new supplier list whose filters are a deliberate mirror of
the award route's own rejections, so the picker cannot offer a plant the server would refuse.
It quotes lots × the going rate off the same anchored GDP the route bills against, and says
how many turns the chosen plant needs to fill the order.

**Corporations can see their order book.** `/api/corporations/[id]` had been serving a
`defenceContracts` block since C2 that nothing rendered. A supplying CEO now gets a Defence
tab: outstanding lots, throughput, earnings, the delivery grade their research has reached,
and a warning on any live contract delivering nothing because its plant was re-tooled off
the contracted component. The tab is gated to corporations with defence as their primary or
secondary focus — anything else can never hold a contract, so the tab would be permanently
empty.

**Deliveries unwind after a failed write.** Payment precedes recording, so
everything past the debit had to be able to give the money back and could not.
A failed arsenal deposit now refunds the whole cost; a failed supplier payment
reclaims the lots and refunds exactly what the country did not keep. One broken
contract stalls instead of taking down the sweep for every other country.

**Two bugs found by the audits, not the plan.**

- `UnitCard` was a fourth caller of `unitUpgradePrice` and would have quoted an
  unanchored price against an anchored bill.
- `seedRosterUpkeepFor` originally fell back only to _earlier_ presets. DD, DE,
  AT and NG seed nothing in 1953 (NVA 1956, Bundeswehr 1955, Bundesheer 1955,
  Nigerian independence 1960) but can still acquire units there — every one
  would have fielded a permanently free army, and the live testing world already
  had two such DD units. It now falls forward as well, with an invariant test.

**Migration** `scripts/migrations/2026-08-08-seed-defence-appropriation.ts`
seeds every pot with one year of accrual and captures the price baseline.
Idempotent, dry-run by default, and it REFUSES to apply if any country would
reach arrears inside a game year. Dry run on the testing world: 24 budgets, none
in arrears.

**Conflict record.** `readinessBaselineOf` gained the arrears ratio, and every
projection that quotes recovery at the player moved with it —
`forceReadiness`, the new `recoveringCount`, and `OrderOfBattlePanel`'s
per-formation `turnsToFull`. Otherwise the record would promise a recovery the
turn processor never delivers.

## The national arsenal: defence corporations start supplying the army

C1 made the defence budget an account. C2 makes it buy materiel from somebody, and
in doing so fixes a field that has been read on every combat calculation since it
was written and that nothing in the game could ever move.

**`equipment` was a dead seam.** `computeEffectivePower` folds `1 + eqAvg × 0.03`
and six of the eight unit-dossier stats read the three tracks — but `equipment`
was written exactly twice, at seed (random 0–2) and at recruit (hardcoded
`{1,1,1}`), and no code path anywhere raised it. Same class as W7's discovery that
generals never affected combat.

**Per-domain arsenal.** `nationalArsenal` holds lots and a volume-weighted grade
per `UnitDomain` — the six values units already carry, so a unit knows which store
feeds it by construction. This is the good half of the original "0–10 component
score" proposal (a starved navy is legible) without the decaying counter, which on
a one-hour turn clock would have punished sleeping.

**Certification is the strategy the CEO already picks.** Rather than adding a
`certifiedComponent` field beside `strategyId` — the overlapping-knob mistake the
first triage rejected when "corps pick a focus" turned out to be `techDecadeLane` —
the arsenal component is derived from the production strategy. The five existing
defence lines only reached ground and air, so three were authored:
`naval_systems`, `missile_systems`, `aerospace`. `cyber` maps to NOTHING on
purpose: it supplies electronics and software, not materiel, and a bucket no unit
could draw from is exactly what sank the proposal's "Surveillance" component.

**Rates were measured, not chosen.** `scripts/calibrate-arsenal.ts` projects the
DELTA against what defence sectors run today — an absolute footprint would have
misled, since `standard` (which all 37 run) demands no energy while
heavy_armor/munitions/directed_energy all do. First pass flagged five rows moving

> 5% of a short market; re-rated naval freight/iron/construction, missile
> rare_earth, aerospace freight. Steel demand went UP on both (D/S 0.558, heavily
> oversupplied, and both lines were reducing demand against it). What remains is
> favourable: aerospace relieves electronics (1.322) as a net avionics supplier,
> naval relieves vehicles (1.324), and missile's −43% chemicals swing corrects an
> oversupply rather than causing a shortage.

**Grade and fill are orthogonal.** Grade — what a corporation's defence R&D can
BUILD, reusing `techDecadeLane`'s existing specialist/generalist split — sets
`techTier`. Fill — how much ARRIVED — scales the equipment tracks. Advanced
industry with no throughput fields good-tier units with empty racks; mass
production with no R&D fields fully-kitted obsolete ones. A `fill > 0` guard on
tier was added after a test caught that a drained arsenal keeps its last grade,
which would have stamped that tier on a unit issued nothing.

**Delivery pays before it records.** Three caps in order: what the plant built,
what the order has outstanding, what the appropriation covers — and procurement
still has NO overdraft, so a country that cannot pay takes fewer lots and the
contract waits. Recording first would have needed a rollback, and
`advanceContract` clamps negatives to zero, so that rollback would have been a
silent no-op crediting a buyer with materiel they never paid for.

**Domestic suppliers only, on a hard finding.** `liquidCurrencyCode` is absent on
pre-forex corps and documented "treat as USD", and six Eastern Bloc countries have
no exchange-rate document at all — so paying across a currency boundary would move
roubles into a dollar balance with no conversion anywhere. Refused at AWARD, not
discovered later as a contract that mysteriously never delivers. Arms exports need
their own design, starting with that question.

**An empty arsenal never blocks recruitment.** A unit raised against nothing
arrives hollow — real personnel, no kit — and the new per-turn refit brings it up
as deliveries land, nearest-to-complete first so scarce lots produce some
combat-worthy formations rather than raising everything a little.

Also: `maxTechTierForPreset` exported from the seeder so the era ceiling that stops
a 1953 world fielding modern kit governs contracts too; both new collections
registered `runtime` in `seedManifest` (a prior audit found all seven conflict
collections missing from it, silently surviving resets); and the corp-side view
lives in its own module rather than extending `corporationDetail.ts`, which is
2,000+ lines and grandfathered into the size allowlist with an explicit "do not
extend for new code".

## Fighting now destroys equipment

C2 gave `equipment` a way up. C3 adds the way down, closing the C1→C2→C3 loop: a
war drains the arsenal, which drains the appropriation through contracts, which is
what a legislature votes on. Until now a campaign cost a nation men and readiness
but not a single tank.

**`UnitResult` gains `materiel`** — equipment tracks destroyed — computed in
`unitOutcomes` from the SAME `intensity` that drives casualties. Every modifier
already folded in there (force ratio, unit role, morale, the general's mods, the
rear/reserve sustain term, and the lighter punishment of a retreat) therefore
applies to materiel for free, through one expression. A second attrition formula
would be a parallel balance surface that could drift out of agreement with the
first, and there is no design reason a rout should cost proportionally fewer tanks
than it costs men.

**Armour is the one term divided back out.** `armorMit` reduces casualties because
armour protects the crew — but the vehicle IS the materiel, and a knocked-out tank
is destroyed equipment whether or not its crew walked away. Carrying armour through
would make heavy formations both harder to kill and cheaper to re-equip, which is
backwards. A test pins that the armour advantage does not carry into materiel.

**Losses are whole tracks.** `UnitEquipment` is three integers and every other path
that moves it (`equipUnit`, the refit step) assumes that, so a skirmish costs 0 and
a mauling 1–2. `MATERIEL_LOSS_SCALE` is the single dial, set so an ordinary
engagement rounds to nothing and a rout costs one track — larger and one battle
would strip a formation that took a game year of contracts to equip.

**All three tracks lose together**, because splitting by what was hit would need a
damage model the engine does not have.

`persistSide` now writes `equipment` alongside personnel/readiness/xp/vet — without
that the computed loss would be discarded on write and the whole mechanic would be
inert. Units are still never destroyed, only stripped, consistent with the rest of
the engine.

⚠️ **This is a live combat-balance change.** Equipment feeds `effPower`, so a
stripped unit is genuinely weaker in its next battle. That compounding is intended —
losing should make the next fight harder — but it is the thing to watch on a live
world, and `MATERIEL_LOSS_SCALE` is the number to turn.

- **UK Commons winner's bonus reworked: the organized duopoly gains together,
  third parties get the squeeze (ticket #1032, PRs #4324, #4325).** The FPTP
  boost no longer keys on pooled party votes, which let three mid-tier
  candidates out-pool one race leader and then have the pool lead amplified.
  Players correctly read that as neither FPTP nor PR. Now the two parties
  that benefit are the two best-organized parties in the region
  (`statePartyOrg`), boosted together against every other party by the
  square law and splitting the gain in plain proportion to their compared
  vote scores. There is no amplification inside the pair, so a race
  leader's share is never amplified away, and a minority duopoly is clamped
  at its proportional share, never below it. To give the squeeze someone to
  squeeze, the historical Commons eligibility gate drops from 20% to 10%
  while the bonus is active (pre-1999): a 1950s Liberal at 11-13% now holds
  a squeezed handful of seats instead of zero, which is also the historical
  shape. Modern (1999+) worlds keep the 20% gate and proportional
  allocation, byte-identical. Largest Remainder rounding and exact seat
  conservation are unchanged. Strategy consequences: candidate-stacking
  buys nothing beyond your proportional share, and region organization is
  what earns a party membership in the boosted pair.

- **Projected seats now match the seats you actually win (ticket #1032,
  PR #4329).** The election page and election night were running two
  different seat engines. The projected-seats panel gated candidates
  individually instead of pooling a party's candidates the way the resolver
  does, and its fallback re-admitted every candidate whenever fewer of them
  cleared the gate than there were seats. Multi-seat chambers always have
  more seats than candidates, so that fallback fired in 11 of the 12 UK
  Commons regions and the panel ran with no threshold at all, handing seats
  to parties polling 1.7-6.6% that the resolver zeroes. The two surfaces
  disagreed by up to 16 seats nationally, which is why the same race showed
  different totals depending on which page you opened. The panel now uses
  the resolver's exact eligibility rules, and a parity test pins the two
  engines together on the shapes that used to diverge.

- **The region's leading party always holds a seat in the boosted pair
  (ticket #1032, PR #4333).** Choosing both boosted slots on party
  organization alone had two faults. A party could win an outright majority
  of a region's votes and still be left out of the pair because its machine
  ranked third, and because organization moves from turn to turn the pair
  could change while the vote shares stood still — in the North East it
  flipped from Conservative+Liberal to Labour+Conservative on flat shares
  and swung four seats in a single turn. The first slot now belongs to the
  region's leading party by votes and organization decides the second, and
  the two slots are always different parties. Seat counts no longer lurch
  when an organization ranking reshuffles. On the current UK tally the
  national result is unchanged at Labour 311 / Conservative 338 /
  Liberal 1; this only removes the cliff.

- **Northern Ireland gets a nationalist bloc in the 1953 seed (ticket #1032,
  PR #4337).** The 1951 regional polling table gave Northern Ireland a single
  entry, `uk_conservative: 63`, and nothing else — the file's own header noted
  that the nationalist and NI Labour vote had "no seeded default party" and
  were omitted. Every other region sums to 99 or 100. Because registration is
  seeded straight from these shares and then normalized against the other
  parties in the region, the missing 37 points did not sit unaligned: they
  inflated the Unionists from an intended 63% to roughly 100% of Northern
  Ireland's registered support, which is why an NPC Unionist was polling 87%
  and sweeping all 18 Commons seats. The region now seeds Unionists 63,
  nationalists 30 and NI Labour 7, which returns 12 Unionist and 6 nationalist
  seats. Sinn Féin carries the nationalist bloc because it is the only Irish
  nationalist party the 1953 roster seeds; that is a deliberate stand-in, since
  the 1951 nationalist vote went to the Nationalist Party and Sinn Féin did not
  contest Westminster until 1955. Guard tests now require every region to
  account for a full electorate and reject any region that normalizes to a
  one-party sweep. This is a seed change, so it applies to worlds created or
  reset from here on and does not alter a world already in progress.

- **Every build now delivers progressively, not just Build-dialog expansions
  (PR #4338).** Smooth per-turn delivery shipped on 08-10, but three writers
  still queued all-at-once orders: the founding starter build on a newly
  expanded sector, both NPP construction paths, and the plants-flip growth
  credit. A brand-new sector's first build, the first thing a new corp
  watches, still landed as one lump at the end of the bar. All four sites now
  stamp orders smooth, and the retroactive conversion for orders placed
  before 08-10 has been applied to the live world, so in-flight builds ramp
  in a slice per turn instead of arriving all at once.

- **The whole commodity ledger now counts in one unit system, which unpins
  the floor-price cluster (PR #4345).** Phase 2 of the ticket #1027 re-anchor.
  Under plants on an era world, supply is physical units on the era basis,
  but every dollars-derived leg of the ledger, meaning the inputs sectors buy
  from each other, the macro service demand that scales with GDP, and the
  legacy nameplate supply used for unowned buckets and extraction, was still
  divided by the modern price table. On a 1953 world that undercounts those
  legs about 70x against plants supply, which is why 20 of 29 commodities sat
  glutted at 0.44x to 0.68x of base price while 77% of plants sold less than
  half their output. All of those legs now carry the era unit scale, so B2B
  demand is real again. The rescaled demand is capped per commodity at 1.5x
  world supply, the same bound the household re-anchor uses, so markets that
  flip from glut to shortage carry a clear build-here signal instead of a
  pinned price, and a commodity that was already in genuine shortage on the
  old basis keeps its full pressure rather than being cut to the cap. Modern
  worlds and worlds below the plants tier are unchanged.
- **The sector page now tells the truth about low fills (PR #4344).** Under the
  plants economy the sector page computed its headline margin against sold
  revenue only, so a sector selling 15% of its output could show a 45% margin
  next to a bank balance going nowhere; on live worlds 77% of sectors sell
  under half their output. The Market and money panel now leads with three
  numbers: the share of output actually selling (with a per-product breakdown
  in the tooltip), what a produced unit earns against what it costs to make
  (wages, materials, upkeep on idle capacity and overheads included), and a
  break-even readout that says how many turns of profit at today's rate pay
  back the money still sunk in construction, or "profitable now", or "not at
  current fills". The margin breakdown panel and the corporation overview's
  sector rows now lead with a fill-adjusted margin (realized profit over the
  cost of everything produced) and demote the sold-units-only effective margin
  to a secondary row. Build orders in the queue now show units delivered so
  far, the slice arriving each turn and the turn they finish, instead of
  rendering the smooth ramp as a binary wait. When under half the output
  sells, the market panel says plainly that the market is oversupplied and
  extra sales must be won from rivals on price. All figures are computed in
  the query layer from telemetry the engine already writes; nothing about how
  the engine computes or stores margins changed, and the exact new ratio is
  withheld from outside viewers the same way the exact fill rate already is.

- **New wiki guide: Running a Corporation under the Plants System (PR #4351).**
  A player-facing walkthrough of the physical-capacity economy, seeded at
  /wiki/plants-corp-guide and code-backed so its numbers can never rot: every
  quoted constant (idle upkeep fraction, mothball upkeep, cancel refund,
  per-sector build times, dominance thresholds, bond caps and cooldowns) is
  interpolated from the live constants at render time. Covers founding a corp
  and the starter plant's per-turn ramp, reading the sector page's three
  headline numbers (selling share, per-unit earns vs costs, break even), why
  output goes unsold in a min(supply, demand) market, when to mothball versus
  run lean, bond financing, and the dominance build-cost ramp, with real
  production screenshots committed under public/static/wiki/plants-corp-guide/.

- **Removed the foreign-policy and culture party axes (ticket #1032,
  PR #4354).** Parties carried four ideological axes, but only economic and
  social were ever read by anything. Foreign policy and culture were written
  by every party seed, by charter ratification and by the position-shift
  proposal, and consumed by no engine, driver or appeal calculation — the
  party type's own comment admitted it was "scaffolding only". The cost was
  not just dead storage: the shift proposal offered all four axes as
  identical choices, so a party could spend a committee vote and a 336-turn
  cooldown moving a number that could not affect anything, and the charter
  screen warned you about this while the shift screen did not. Both axes are
  now gone from the party model, charters, schemas, the proposal flow and
  every country seed, and a migration unsets them from existing parties and
  charters. Historical proposals that targeted a retired axis are deliberately
  left in place — they still render, labelled as retired, and applying one is
  now a no-op instead of an error.

- **Era worlds no longer clear every commodity in one worldwide order book
  (PR #4359).** The corporate clearing pass handed every seller on the planet a
  pro-rata claim on global lagged demand: a Donbas kombinat and a Connecticut
  mill sold steel into the same book, with no trade barrier between them, which
  is why unrelated sellers of a commodity all reported the same sold fraction.
  On era worlds each seller now clears in its home country's reachable book:
  domestic demand net of import competition, plus the export volume the live
  trade graph lets that country place abroad. The graph is the one the world
  trade snapshot already runs, so embargoes block lanes, embargo caps clamp
  them, tariffs and geography drag, and legislated autarky lands as the walls
  it enacts. A country whose surplus cannot leave sees its own sellers' fills
  and prices pinned instead of quietly exporting the glut into every open
  economy. Auto-postured sellers also read their own market's balance, so an
  unmanaged corp in a walled-off glut undercuts off the book it can actually
  sell to. Modern worlds keep the single worldwide book unchanged.

- **The 1953 iron curtain now exists in the ledger, not just the flavour text
  (PR #4362).** Era worlds seeded no trade restrictions at all, so the trade
  graph the new market partition reads was fully open. 1953 worlds now seed
  the era's opening trade policy as durable embargo lanes: strategic goods
  (steel, electronics, vehicles, ordnance, chemicals, plastics, oil, natural
  gas, rare earths) are blocked both ways between the CoCom eight and the
  Comecon seven, and the PRC sits behind the stricter CHINCOM wall in which
  nothing ships from CoCom members to China at all. Raw materials and
  services stay open, because Soviet timber and coal really did reach Western
  markets, and the neutrals (Yugoslavia after the split, Finland, Austria,
  Sweden, Spain, Ireland, Brazil, Nigeria) carry no walls in either direction
  and become the era's leak routes on purpose. The lanes are organization
  policy, so countries can still lift or override them through play, and
  modern worlds seed nothing.

## Onboarding "Take charge of a company" ignored player-founded corps (ticket #1056)

`loadOnboardingSignals.hasCompany` queried `{ ceoId, ceoType: "character" }`, but
`POST /api/corporations` never stamped `ceoType` on the inserted doc (the type
documents it as defaulting to `"character"` when absent). Mongo equality does
not treat a missing field as that default, so founders stayed incomplete on
Your First Moves step 7 despite owning and running the corp. Founding now sets
`ceoType: "character"`; the checklist query matches allocate-stats and treats
absent `ceoType` as character. Tests cover both.
