title: "1.1.11: Companies buy from the same market they sell into"
summary: >-
  Company revenue has always been cushioned against extreme prices: when a
  good is scarce, the price a seller realizes is dampened and capped. Input
  bills were not. A company paid the full raw market ratio for every input,
  so in a world where freight, vehicles and ordnance trade at 2.5 to 3 times
  base price, any company with an input-heavy recipe lost money no matter how
  well it was run. Mining companies were hit hardest and several went
  insolvent through no fault of their owners. Input bills now price through
  the same dampened, capped function as revenue, so both sides of the market
  obey the same physics.
tags: [corporations, economy]

## Fixed

- Input bills now price through the same realization function as revenue
  (dampened square root, floored at 0.7x and capped at 1.5x of base price).
  Previously revenue was capped while costs were not, which made input-heavy
  sectors structurally loss-making whenever world prices inflated. In live
  data this was bankrupting extraction companies whose owners had already cut
  every budget to zero.
- Static validation against the live world: 253 of the 298 income-negative
  companies flip to profitable under the symmetric rule, including 155 of
  174 player-run companies.
