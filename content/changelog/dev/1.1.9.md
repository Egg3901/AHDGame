## What changed

The Workers figure on a sector page was tied to revenue at a rate that made an
entire regional industry look like it employed a few hundred people. A top
sector pulling hundreds of millions a day reported around 600 workers, which
read as far too small for a whole industry in a whole region.

The headcount now scales one hundred times higher, so the same sector reads in
the tens of thousands and a busy state's industries add up to a believable
labour force. Capacity build previews use the same anchor, so the "workers this
capacity needs" figure moves up in step and still matches the sector total.

## What did not change

Nothing in the economy moves. The worker count is a display figure: labour cost
is driven by revenue, wage and automation effects use the headcount only as a
relative weight (so the scale cancels out), and national employment is never
built from these numbers. Wages, margins, strike thresholds, dividends, and
prices are all exactly as before. Existing sectors pick up the new figure on the
next turn with no migration.
