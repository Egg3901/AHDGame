Cut from development.

## Fixed

- An application to join a bloc could sit through its whole voting window and be
  refused with every vote cast in favour of it. China and North Korea both
  applied to the Warsaw Pact, both were backed by every member that voted, and
  both were turned down without a single vote against.
- Admissions, trade agreements and votes to enter a war all need every member of
  the roll to agree, so a member who says nothing blocks the measure as firmly as
  one who votes no. Governments the game runs itself decide once every six turns
  and took one diplomatic action when they did, which a bloc vote had to win
  against tariffs, embargoes and the conduct of any war already under way. Over a
  voting window that came to four chances to vote, and a member that spent all
  four elsewhere vetoed the measure by accident.
- Voting is no longer one of those actions. A foreign ministry now casts every
  ballot in front of it and still takes its diplomatic action for the cycle, so
  measures are decided by what members think rather than by what else was on
  their desk that week.
- Members the game runs are also no longer counted on the ballots where silence
  blocks. They keep their vote on everything decided by a majority, where not
  voting costs a measure one yes and nothing worse.
- The bloc panel counted a different roll from the one the game used to decide
  the vote. It reported the Warsaw Pact admission as one vote short of unanimous
  when it was five short, so there was no way to see what was actually holding
  the application up. Every panel now shows the roll that will decide its own
  measure.

## Changed

- Trade agreements between members were affected by the same fault and could not
  be ratified. Several agreements were open in Comecon with a single vote each
  and no way to reach the rest.
