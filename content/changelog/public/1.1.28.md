## The curtain closes

Until now, planned economies could sell their surpluses into Western markets
through ordinary trade routes. Millions of units of Eastern food were arriving
as Western imports every turn, holding prices down for Western producers.

No longer. Trade between the planned bloc and the open world is closed, both
directions. The bloc trades freely within itself; the open world trades
freely within itself; nothing crosses.

What this means for you:

- **Western prices are Western now.** Food and other commodities the bloc was
  exporting reprice on what the open world actually produces and needs. The
  West is short of food — prices rise, and farming there pays again.
- **Imports you were relying on may vanish.** If your market was buying
  Eastern goods, that supply is gone. Shortage is now a real opportunity:
  build the capacity to fill it.
- **The bloc runs on the plan.** Inside planned economies, prices are
  administered by the state rather than set by their (now landlocked)
  surpluses. Their economies keep functioning in isolation.
- **History takes its course.** The curtain lifts country by country on its
  historical marketization date — China in 1978, most of Eastern Europe in
  1989-1990, the Soviet republics in 1991. When trade opens, it will mean
  something.

Corporations are unaffected: if you operate sectors on the other side of the
curtain, they keep running. Only the goods stopped moving.
