## Costs reach prices

Until now a commodity's price came only from supply and demand. What it cost
to make was never part of it. That could trap a whole industry: fertilizers,
plastics and freight all in world shortage and far above normal price, while
food — which needs all three — sold into a surplus below its normal price.
Most of the world's farms were losing money every turn with nothing a player
could do about it.

Now, when the things needed to make a commodity get expensive, part of that
cost lifts the commodity's own price. Producers still eat some of the squeeze
— passing on half, capped — and cheap inputs never push a price below what
the market says. Gluts still behave like gluts.

For farmers specifically: food prices will climb over the coming turns as the
input squeeze passes through, and margins recover with them. Expensive inputs
now also fix themselves faster — higher output prices draw in supply.

## Steadier stock prices

Share valuations discounted earnings by the central bank rate at that exact
moment, so every rate meeting jolted the whole exchange within the hour. The
market now responds to the rate trend over several turns instead of the
latest vote. Loans and bond coupons still reprice immediately — those really
do follow the current rate.
