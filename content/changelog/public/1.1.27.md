## Your prices, your market

Until now, half of every local price came from a single worldwide
supply-and-demand number — including supply from economies that cannot trade
with yours at all. Millions of units of food sat in closed economies,
unreachable by any buyer, and still pushed world food prices far below normal
while Western countries could not feed themselves from their own farms.

Prices now come from the market your country can actually reach: your own
production, real imports that can arrive, and real export routes. Supply
locked behind closed borders prices its own market, not yours.

What you will see over the coming turns:

- **Food prices rise in food-short countries** — most of the West. Farms
  there become profitable again on real scarcity.
- **Closed economies drown in their own surplus** — their internal prices
  fall. Overproduction with nowhere to sell now has consequences at home.
- Other commodities adjust the same way wherever trade barriers matter.

Combined with the recent input-cost pass-through, farming in a food-short
country is worth doing again. If you mothballed or held off on agriculture,
it may be time for another look.
