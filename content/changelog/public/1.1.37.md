## Phantom cost after a recipe change

Changing a plant's recipe changes what "one unit of output" means. The
command you use already kept leftover operating costs in line with that, so
the bill did not jump. Computer-run firms were skipping that step, so a
mine that retooled onto a scarcer mineral could suddenly look far more
expensive to run.

They now follow the same rule you do. The leftover operating cost moves
with the recipe, and the total bill stays put.

## Home-market prices for computer-run mines

Computer-run mines were still looking at the world price when deciding
whether to switch minerals. In a split market that can chase a shortage
they cannot actually sell into. They now read the prices in the country
they operate in.

Computer-run factories that are not mines were already making that call
from the reachable market. The extra global pass that could lock them into
a cooldown first is gone, so that decision is no longer fighting itself.

## Your companies

A strategy you set is yours. Computer-run recovery is gradual: one sector
per firm, a staggered cohort, and a twelve-turn transition. Watch
transitions and the P&L, not a single date on the clock.
