## What changed

- **Two prices, not one.** Selling settles at the market's bid, buying at its
  ask. The gap is the dealer's cut: one percent each way on government paper,
  two on corporate.
- **A wary market pays less.** When the market is short of cash, or a
  government's debt looks shaky, both prices slide down. Strong issuers earn
  a small premium.
- **You can see the depth.** The bond page shows the bid, the ask, and how
  many units the market can buy at once. The trade window caps a sale at
  that number and tells you why.
- **The market grows with the economy.** Each currency's bond market sizes
  itself to broad money, takes in savings when it runs low, and returns cash
  when it has more than it needs.
