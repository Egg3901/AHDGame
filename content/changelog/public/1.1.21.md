## The income statement adds up now

If you read down your corporation's income waterfall and the numbers did not
reach the total at the bottom, you were right. Two cost lines were missing.

Your **wage bill** had no row. For some corporations it is the biggest cost they
pay, and it was charged against income without ever being shown. **Regulatory
compliance** was missing the same way.

The bottom line was also worked out separately from the rows above it, so a
missing cost changed nothing about the total. Now the total is the rows: gross
revenue minus everything listed. If any part of your income cannot be explained
by the rows, it appears as an **Other** line rather than disappearing.

If your corporation earns more from bond coupons than it pays in interest, that
income is now shown as money coming in instead of being left out.

## Your dividend rate shows what you actually pay

The dividend tile showed the rate you set. Some legal structures require a
minimum payout that is higher, and the income statement was charging that higher
rate. A US LLC requires 20%, so the tile could show 0% while 20% was taken.

The tile now shows the rate that is actually charged, and tells you when your
legal structure has raised it above your setting.

## Two labels corrected

The income figure on your corporation's header is the money kept after the
dividend is paid, so it is now labelled **Retained**.

The UK Public Limited Company description said it was Japan's standard company
type. It now describes the UK.
