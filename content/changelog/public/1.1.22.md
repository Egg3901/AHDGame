## Sector margins are real now

Your corporation screens computed each sector's margin from a formula that did
not know what your sectors really cost to run. The engine — the thing that
actually moves your money every turn — uses a margin built from your real
costs: wages, upkeep, inputs.

The gap could be enormous. A sector genuinely losing money each turn could
display a strong positive margin, and everything downstream inherited the
error: projected income ran ahead of what you were actually paid, and the
balance sheet valued your sectors well above what they earn.

Every money screen now uses the engine's margin. **Balance sheets and
projected income will drop for many corporations — sometimes a lot.** Nothing
was taken from you: the difference was never real, and your share price never
included it. The market has been pricing your company on the real numbers all
along, which is why a stock could fall while its screens looked healthy. Now
the screens and the market finally tell the same story.

If a sector shows a negative margin now, it is really losing money every turn,
and it was before too — the screen just hid it. That is a problem you can act
on: raise the margin, change strategy, or sell the sector.

## Borrowing is priced on real earnings

Bond issuance capacity and credit ratings valued your sectors at a flat
standard margin regardless of what they earned. Loss-making sectors counted as
healthy collateral, and unusually profitable ones counted for less than they
deserved. Both directions are fixed: your borrowing room now reflects what
your company really earns.

We checked every corporation with bonds outstanding before shipping this: no
existing position is forced into anything by the change.
