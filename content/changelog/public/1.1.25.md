## Your business income counts again

The market applies a valuation discount to corporations that live off bond
coupons rather than running a business — coupon income is fragile to interest
rates, so it is worth less per dollar than operating profit.

That discount was hitting the wrong companies. If you borrowed heavily, your
interest payments shrank your net income, and the formula then compared your
coupon income against that shrunken figure — concluding you were mostly a bond
fund. Your actual business earnings could be discounted to nothing. The more
debt you carried, the harder it hit.

Now the market looks at your NET bond income: coupons received minus interest
paid. If you pay more interest than you collect — true for almost every
company that borrows to grow — you have no bond reliance at all, and your
business earnings are valued in full. Pure coupon funds are treated exactly as
before.

If your corporation carries debt and earns real income, expect your share
price to correct upward on the next market pass.
