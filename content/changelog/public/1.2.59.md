## Shareholder charts read as your company

- **Slices are shades of your corporation's colour.** The ownership and voting
  charts used a fixed set of twelve colours that had nothing to do with the
  company you were looking at. They now tint every holder off your own brand
  colour, so the chart looks like your company at a glance. The shades are
  spread far enough apart to tell one holder from the next.
- **Every new company is given a colour.** Companies founded from now on are
  assigned one at founding, including spin-offs and companies run by
  non-player parties. Older companies without one keep a fixed colour worked
  out from their identity, so a company never changes colour between visits.
- **Large cap tables stay readable.** A company can have hundreds of
  shareholders, and the chart drew a slice and a legend entry for every one of
  them. It now names the ten largest holders and totals the rest into a single
  slice. Shares held by the public float are still shown on their own, in grey.

## Building capacity is quicker to drive

- **Type the number you want.** The count field can be cleared and typed into.
  Before, it snapped back to a minimum of one on every keypress, which ate the
  first digit of anything you tried to enter.
- **Plus and minus ten.** New buttons either side of the count move it ten at a
  time, next to the existing single steps.
- **Hold a key to move faster.** Holding shift multiplies any step by ten, and
  holding ctrl multiplies it by a hundred. That works on the buttons and on the
  arrow keys, so ctrl and the plus ten button move a thousand at once.
