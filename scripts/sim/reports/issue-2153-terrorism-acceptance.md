## Durable costs and independent decisions

- Read-side option availability and the real command reject military action before attribution reaches 45. The aggregate resolver applies the same guard. An ineligible ordinary character is rejected separately.
- Only the country submitting a commitment gains troops or emergency authority. In the intervention case Germany withholds troops throughout while US and UK commitments accumulate. National treasury costs are debited on submission; duplicate submissions leave balances unchanged.
- Emergency authority persists by country until that government restores ordinary law. It imposes standing due-process and participation offsets. These are the game's emergency-authority state and political consequences; this replay does not claim a parliamentary bill, court hearing or election result was simulated.
- Standing counterterrorism spending uses whole national currency and GDP: at most 1% of GDP for a full deployment plus 0.2% for maximum emergency authority. The intervention case reaches **66,960,000,000 US currency units/year** in the retained economy. Drawdown and legal restoration bring that line back to zero.
- Every paired ordinary treasury sample matches annual counterterrorism spending divided by 48 within one currency unit. The standing line therefore reaches actual treasury cash; it is not just a displayed track.
- Policing and drawdown recover from intervention-led insurgency. A country can remain exposed under deliberate inaction, but active strategy can move the world out of high threat and insurgency into settled normalization. Routine vigilance preserves settled status until renewed threat warrants reopening.

## Acceptance mapping

- Threat exists without a forced historical attack: opening, inaction and varied attack timing.
- Preparedness can disrupt, reduce or displace plots: prevention, limited severity and victim-selection rules; partial preparedness still permits harm.
- Durable legal, fiscal, political and security costs: persistent national authority/deployments, actual budget and treasury, standing board effects, insurgency and repeal/drawdown.
- Independent coalition commitments: real country-specific commands and Germany's withheld deployment.
- Prevention, limited/catastrophic harm, policing and intervention blowback: five real subsystem continuations plus focused rules/response tests.
- No trivial disappearance or forced permanent maximum threat: unresolved inaction and successful recovery/normalization under changed policy.
