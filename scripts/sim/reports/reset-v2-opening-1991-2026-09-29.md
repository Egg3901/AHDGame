# Reset v2 opening books: 1991 source audit and 240-turn stress

This report covers the **new-world 1991 v2 opening and its live runtime**. It does not migrate an existing save. Game-calibrated estimates remain visibly identified wherever reviewed historical values are unavailable.

## Final implementation status

Status checked on 2026-10-01. The scoped implementation is feature complete for fresh 1991 US, UK, and JP worlds. `src/lib/resetVersions/availability.ts` releases independent Metrics, Legislation, and Cabinet v2 admin selections. The active world remains on v1 until an admin selects versions for the next reset and that reset writes verified, world-bound seed receipts. Unsupported countries remain on v1. Existing-world migration is deliberately deferred at the player's request.

### Google Fonts are not an implementation blocker

The current worktree passes `npm run verify:build`. The v2 implementation adds no font dependency and does not require a runtime connection to Google Fonts. The shared layout continues to use the repository's established `next/font/google` definitions, which Next bundles into the build. Earlier restricted-run font failures were environmental and are removed from the v2 blocker list.

### Completed runtime scope

- **Metrics:** all 58 replacement metrics have opening observations or clearly labeled proxies, live owner refresh, law-delivery pressure, Cabinet-action overlays, demographic cohort integration, descriptors, direction-aware display, and reset receipt validation.
- **Legislation:** national and regional reviewed catalogs feed the Guided Path proposal flow, legacy tax and tariff rate controls remain intact, current law is non-selectable, bills persist reviewed snapshots, enactment is transaction-safe and retry-reconciled, and national plus regional budgets settle actual implementation factors.
- **Cabinet and treasury:** world-bound department accounts receive only paid sovereign authority; coupon and maturity shortfalls create an emergency advance and crisis while holders remain paid. Cabinet allocations, delivery receipts, temporary actions, expiry, per-holder charges, cooldowns, and metric overlays are persisted without a country-wide simultaneous-action cap. Regions have no Cabinet and settle fixed law allocations against their own budgets.
- **Version isolation:** the three admin selectors target the next reset, depend on verified seed receipts, leave the running v1 world unchanged, and route non-US/UK/JP countries to v1.
- **Player surface:** Option A, Guided Path, is the v2 national and regional proposal modal. It supports Domain or Metric entry, five ideological positions plus eligible Leave it to the States, current-law markers, agencies, fixed allocations, budget and metric deltas, multi-provision drafts, and return-to-overview flow.

### Final verification

Formatting, strict TypeScript, lint, the portable-rules architecture audit, and the optimized production build pass. A bounded-worker full repository run completed 44,618 passing tests with no failures; the repository's intentional skips and one existing todo remain. The reset simulation set passes 36/36 scenarios across treasury cash, regional law delivery, option price grids, multi-law portfolios, health proxies, department openings, and Cabinet actions. ### Provisional option-price source audit

The prototype's base GDP fractions and asymmetric five-level factors are now copied into the **non-selectable** workbench. Comparing each center design amount with the already-booked 1991 family claim exposed a dangerous mismatch: the center design amount is under one-quarter of the booked source for 4 US, 12 UK, and 12 JP funded families. For L08 alone, a raw center-price replacement would turn the US $117.262B fiscal source claim into $372M, the UK £14.474B claim into £36M, and the JP ¥23.932T claim into ¥28.2B. That would manufacture a budget windfall without an actual repeal of protected obligations.

The provisional pricing rule now uses the **booked family claim as the calibration anchor when one exists**, with each level scaled relative to the center factor. A family with no booked source uses the original GDP-based new-program estimate. This correction prevents the raw prototype cost from silently wiping out existing expenditure; it does not assert that the 1991 current law is legally the proposed center level. Exact supersession, accrued liabilities, tax expenditures, capacity, and option-level prices remain to be reviewed. Before the further repricing below, a stress case with every family selecting far left implied anchored agency allocations of approximately 33.0% of US GDP, 85.5% of UK GDP, and 64.4% of JP GDP. Those extremes indicated real budget pressure, not a recommendation or an approved playable balance.

## Opening sovereign maturities and institutional demand audit (2026-10-02)

The reset no longer treats the complete opening sovereign stock as if it were issued on reset day. The portable opening-maturity rule preserves the approved 25% one-year, 35% two-year, and 40% five-year tenor mix, but reconstructs an established quarterly ladder. Large opening books produce 32 cohorts with remaining maturities from turn 12 through turn 240. Each cohort retains a historical issue turn consistent with its contractual tenor, and largest-remainder allocation preserves the opening face exactly to the bond unit. Production's existing quarterly issuance path already includes bonds maturing in the next quarter when it calculates rollover supply, so the ladder changes timing rather than bypassing the market or creating a separate repayment rule.

`resetSovereignMaturity240.ts` is a deliberately narrow liquidity sensitivity. It applies the production opening planner and the production 90% primary-pool commitment ceiling, then redistributes each quarter's funded rollover through the production sovereign tenor profile. It excludes ordinary deficits, coupons, player orders, changing yields, debt ceilings, and Cabinet claims, so it is not a second fiscal forecast. The institutional arms model secondary purchases as recycled pool liquidity. The observed arm uses the aggregate institutional ownership share measured on live; the 5% arm is a clearly labeled provisional comparison and is not an enacted target.

A separate read-only aggregate audit against the live world confirms that autonomous institutional bond buyers are real, but economically small and difficult for players to see. At turn 1,303, 296 of 459 active sovereign issues had a named holder. Individual NPPs held 0.014%, other corporations 0.077%, characters 0.005%, and the bond-market pools carried 93.708%. The audit found 526 recorded NPP-corporate purchases and 12 active bond funds with cash. Live had the enhanced index-fund liquidity gate enabled and the broader domestic-coverage gate disabled.

This explains why the behavior was not obvious in ordinary play. The execution paths were functioning, but the bond-detail API discarded holders identified by `fundId` or `nppId`, and it omitted central-bank ownership from the held total. The player surface now resolves and labels index funds, NPP investors, and central banks and links funds and NPPs to their existing pages. No new synthetic buyer, institutional allocation target, or live balance constant is activated by this pass. A larger institutional target should remain a separately simulated balance decision rather than being inferred from the visibility repair.

The audit was repeated at turn 1,304 with country-level maturities, pool liquidity, and conservation checks. ### Treasury reserve-investment boundary

A Treasury Cabinet must not purchase bonds issued by its own sovereign. A domestic transaction is a debt-management buyback and retires principal; representing the same government as both debtor and departmental investor would create circular demand and let operating appropriations escape their legal purpose. Central-bank domestic purchases remain monetary operations.

A later foreign-sovereign reserve capability is compatible with this design only as a separately authorized Treasury reserve portfolio. It must exclude the home country, preserve an operating-cash floor, apply issuer and currency concentration limits, use the ordinary bond market and FX ledgers, and return coupons, maturities, gains, and losses to that reserve account. Ordinary department and program balances remain unavailable for investment. This is a new mechanic requiring its own issue and simulation; it is not silently included in the present reset implementation or in the provisional 5% institutional-demand arm.
