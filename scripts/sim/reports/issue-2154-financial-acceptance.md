## Sovereign-bank feedback and member decisions

The euro continuation reserves **3,000 already issued DE sovereign units**, pays **€2,923,800** from the bank vault to the actual bond market pool, and records the same position in its portfolio. Public float plus holder units remains **857,500,000**. Both cash legs conserve funds. Existing bank-owned sovereign holdings remain included.

A disclosed deficit appropriation changes prospective spending and financing needs, not cash or debt balances. The existing failed primary-fill history and current financing needs produce a real sovereign decision; no new issuance or fabricated failed-fill row is used. Exposure is computed from conserved bond-holder records in a common currency. Unbacked legacy prop-book entries do not qualify. Disabling euro membership makes the euro exposure observation exactly zero.

The lower chamber advances the public restructuring proposal, but the upper chamber **rejects** it. The ordinary rejection/repudiation path reduces DE sovereign principal to zero and marks the purchased position from **€2,923,800 to €150,000**. Bank vault, bond pool and issuer treasury cash remain unchanged: a security write-down is not a second cash debit.

A public austerity response reduces prospective annual spending from **€2,379,955,493,781.036 to €1,861,836,624,587.21**, preserving contractual debt service. The ordinary government commodity-demand consumer records lower healthcare/service orders. After 53 real recovery passes, fiscal discipline returns the sovereign state to `normal`. This is a different outcome from the ratified US restructuring; no consent or winner is forced.

The sample observes the ordinary engine's combined inherited dynamics and financial transmission, not a causal estimate attributing every GDP/unemployment change to the loan loss. The crisis demand component is bounded at 0.8–1.2 and decays after credit stabilizes. No new flat GDP penalty substitutes for the balance-sheet, spending, commodity and producer paths.
