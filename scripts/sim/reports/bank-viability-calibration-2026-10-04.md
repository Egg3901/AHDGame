# Bank viability and funded-bill calibration, 2026-10-04

## Finding

The current balanced retail-bank settings do not meet the 8 to 15 percent neutral ROE target in this deterministic rule model. At an 8.5 percent prime rate, 4.2 percent inflation, 20 percent reserve requirement, and the modern corridor midpoint, the 480-turn model ends with $0.71 million equity and a negative 25.61 percent annualized ending ROE. Moving the deposit rate to the current legal minimum improves ending ROE to 6.45 percent, still below target. Pushing the lending rate to the corridor maximum shrinks the rule-priced book and yields negative 5.43 percent ROE.

An APY-relative deposit rate of central-bank savings APY plus 2 percentage points produces 11.66 percent modeled neutral ROE, but at these inputs that rate is 4.15 percent, 0.35 percentage points below the current legal minimum of 4.5 percent. A 4.4 percent rate yields 7.94 percent. This identifies a narrow candidate corridor adjustment to test, not a production calibration: the model has no observed sovereign inventory and therefore gives banks zero bills and zero coupon income.

The aggressive book fails the recession viability check. With the current midpoint deposit offset held constant and prime rising 5 percentage points at turn 240, ending equity falls from $10 million to about $124,000 and annualized ending ROE is negative 77.67 percent. Under the APY-plus-2 offset, the same shock still loses money at negative 40.41 percent ROE. The stress loss computed from the production band weights rises from 5.2 percent for a balanced book to 11.08 percent for the aggressive mix.

## Reproducible inputs and rules

Run `npx tsx scripts/sim/bankViabilityCalibration.ts` from the AHDGame checkout. It reads no database and performs no world simulation. It prints the scenario JSON below. Loan-book targets use `bandOriginationTargets` and the production `fundedNpcFlowDelta`, with current production credit-band default rates. Deposit capture uses `computeNpcDepositShare`. Interest uses 48 turns per year and USD cent rounding. New positive loan principal pays the current 1 percent origination fee through `quoteLoanOrigination`; fees are counted only on newly funded increases. Insurance uses `computeInsurancePremium` and its current 0.4 percent annual base rate with its existing reserve-coverage weight. Deposits capitalize their interest liability, loan interest enters vault cash, defaults reduce loan assets, and premiums leave vault cash.

The model assumptions are $10 million opening equity, $1 trillion external household cash, a 20 percent reserve requirement, no player deposits, and a ten-year horizon of 480 turns. The deposit ceiling applies the production 14 times equity leverage limit against that cash pool. Prime and inflation remain at 8.5 and 4.2 percent except for the stated recession shock. The lending offset is the modern corridor midpoint unless specified otherwise. The model's ending ROE annualizes the last modeled turn's net income against ending equity. Cumulative fees and premiums are horizon totals, not annual rates.

The 4.15 percent APY-plus-2 neutral case reaches the desired range only by crossing today's 4.5 percent legal minimum. It is a reasonable bounded next test for the deposit-corridor owner, paired with additional prime, inflation, and cash-pool cases. The 2.4 percent APY-plus-0.25 case is not a viable proposed setting: its 37.48 percent ending ROE and multi-billion deposit balance show that this model is sensitive to the leverage cap and that a small premium over savings APY is too low as a universal floor.

## Treasury bills and executable liquidity

The base model explicitly reports zero eligible funded bill units, zero held units, and zero funded coupon cash. The approved read-only analytics catalog does not expose sovereign `publicFloat`, bank-specific same-currency holdings, or bond-pool cash at the 1991 seed. The model's $1 trillion household pool is an assumption, not evidence of bill inventory. No viability result below treats requested bill notional as eligible supply.

Unit economics use the production sovereign coupon rule, market-price formula, and pool bid/ask quote. At an 8.5 percent prime rate, a one-year sovereign has an 8.5 percent coupon and par mid. With neutral pool cash equal to target, its $1,000 face unit quotes at $990 bid and $1,010 ask. The immediate purchase-to-executable-bid mark gap is $20. If the issuer actually funds all scheduled coupons and principal through maturity, first-year cash above the $1,010 purchase cost is $75 per unit, or $75,000 per 1,000 units. This is conditional cash yield, not current model income.

If the bond's market yield rises 5 percentage points and pool cash is zero against a $100 million target, the model prices the bill at a 0.9559 mid and a $917.70 executable bid. That is a $92.30 loss per $1,010 original ask, before considering whether the pool has cash to buy any units. Mark value and liquid sale proceeds must remain separate. The quote's executable unit depth is bounded by the real public float and the pool's actual funding. These inputs remain unverified for the 1991 seed, so a funded bill yield contribution cannot yet be added to the bank viability claim.

The conservative cash floor for a sample bank with $100 million cash-backed NPC deposits is $22,640,625: $20 million required reserves, $2.5 million from the existing 2.5 percent one-turn household flow cap, and $140,625 of next-turn deposit interest at 6.75 percent. This sample has no borrowing, so discount-window, CB margin, and interbank interest add nothing; actual outstanding amounts must be included per bank. Bills do not meet this cash floor.

## Insurance pricing limit and next decision

At exact required reserve cover, the current formula charges 0.4 percent annually on insured deposits before risk weights, so $100 million insured balances imply $400,000 per year at parity. The risk weight ranges from 0.5 to 3 under the existing formula. That is an arithmetic reference, not a claim-cost calibration. Historic aggregate claims and premiums show a funding mismatch but do not supply a bank-year insured exposure denominator, recovered-asset path, or cohort failure frequency. Raising or lowering `BASE_PREMIUM_ANNUAL` from that aggregate would therefore be ungrounded.

Keep premium calibration separate from treasury asset work. Before changing the premium constant, build a private, identity-free bank-year loss table with insured exposure, premiums, failure date, cash and asset recoveries, insurance payments, and funded backstop principal and interest. Then price the premium against observed loss per insured balance and test adverse cohorts. The public model should continue to exclude any backstop debt proceeds from bank income.

The next bounded viability test is a 4.15 percent neutral deposit-rate candidate at multiple prime and inflation levels, with the aggressive shock required to remain loss-making. It needs an owner decision because it crosses the current corridor by 0.35 percentage points. Independently, capture actual eligible 1991 bill units and pool cash through an approved read-only source, then rerun with each bank's finite same-currency offer depth and funded coupon cash. Until both checks are complete, retain current fees and premium constants and make no viability claim based on projected sovereign coupons.
